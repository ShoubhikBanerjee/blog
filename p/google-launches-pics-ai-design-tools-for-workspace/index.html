Google launches Pics AI design tools for Workspace

Google has introduced a new suite of creative design tools for Workspace users called Google Pics, aimed at simplifying the creation and editing of professional-grade AI images for businesses.

🔍 Overview

Google Pics is a new image generation and editing app designed to bring graphic design capabilities directly into Google Workspace. It addresses challenges businesses face in creating consistent, high-quality marketing imagery, presentations, and product storytelling.

🧩 How it works

  • Built around the Gemini and Nano Banana generative AI models.
  • Allows granular control over prompt-based image creation and manipulation.
  • Users can tap on specific objects or text within an image and describe desired changes.
  • Supports generating images from scratch or editing individual objects using descriptive prompts.
  • Enables changing, reformatting, and translating text within images.
  • Includes tools to upscale images to 2K or 4K resolutions.
  • Provides quick cropping for web, social media, print, or digital formats.
  • Supports collaboration by sharing designs with teammates.

⚙️ Key details

  • Launches as a standalone Workspace app and as an integration for Google Docs and Slides.
  • Planned rollout to Google Drive in the coming weeks.
  • For integrations, users can select an image in a document or slideshow to edit it with Pics’ full suite of precision AI tools.

🚀 Availability

  • Now rolling out for Workspace users on Business Standard, Business Plus, Enterprise Standard, and Enterprise Plus plans.
  • Also available for Google AI Pro and Ultra personal plans, and the Google AI Pro for Education plan.
  • Previously launched for testers following Google I/O in May.

💡 Why it matters

Google states that while AI image generation has been popular for personal use, businesses have struggled with inconsistent results, trial-and-error prompting, and cumbersome workflows across multiple apps. Pics aims to streamline these processes within Workspace, reducing the need for third-party services for basic creative work.
