Former EPA Officials Warn of Health Risks From AI Data Center Deregulation

A group of former Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) officials warned this week that President Donald Trump is weakening environmental regulations to accelerate the construction of AI data centers, raising health risks for Americans. In a briefing and new report, the former officials urged the administration to adopt a “Data Center Health Protection Pledge” to signal that the environmental threat is being taken seriously.

⚙️ Key Policy Changes

Former EPA employees who are part of the independent organization Environmental Protection Network (EPN) released a report identifying 30 different federal actions since January 2025 that they say exacerbate health risks stemming from data center pollution.

  • The AI Action Plan: Released by President Trump in July 2025, this plan recommends “streamlining or reducing regulations” under the Clean Air Act, Clean Water Act, and Superfund law to expedite permitting for data center projects and chip factories.
  • Targeted Deregulation: Of the 30 policy changes included in the report, 17 specifically mention AI or target data centers.
  • EPA Rollbacks: Since the start of his term, EPA Administrator Lee Zeldin and the White House have rolled back dozens of rules on top of cutting staff and funding at the agency.
  • Fossil Fuel Expansion: The administration has laid the policy groundwork for new gas projects to power energy-hungry data centers, ranging from turbines installed on-site to power plants built specifically to serve tech companies.

⚠️ Environmental and Health Risks

As a result of the Trump administration’s deregulation and attacks on renewable energy, the wave of new AI data centers is increasingly powered by dirty energy. The former EPA officials warn that this physical infrastructure can impact public health far beyond the immediate location of the data centers.

  • Air Pollution and Premature Deaths: The new report cites a study from UC Riverside, Caltech, and the Rochester Institute of Technology that found air pollution associated with AI could lead to up to 1,300 premature deaths and more than $20 billion in public health costs by 2028. This toll could be even larger after factoring in the policy changes outlined in the EPN report.
  • Water and Chemical Waste: Demand for advanced chips needed for generative AI is reviving the production of “forever chemicals” that do not break down easily and build up in the environment and human bodies.

🛡️ Proposed Safeguards

According to the EPA alumni, the US can avoid these public health and environmental losses by restoring clean air and water standards, as well as rebuilding the capacity of federal agencies to enforce those rules.

Marc Boom, EPN’s senior director of public affairs, explained that “EPA should be measuring how this buildout is changing pollution, who is being exposed, what it means for their health, and making all of that information public. That is what a Data Center Health Protection Pledge should mean in practice.”

💬 Key Perspectives and Responses

  • Lynn Goldman (pediatrician and former EPA assistant administrator): “You would think that an industry buildout of this magnitude, billed as a revolution, would mean more vigilance from EPA. Instead, EPA is doing just the opposite, thinking first about industry’s needs and weakening or reconsidering limits on toxic chemicals and harmful pollutants.”
  • Current EPA Employees: Last year, hundreds of current EPA employees fired off a public letter addressed to Lee Zeldin saying, “EPA under your leadership will not protect communities from hazardous chemicals and unsafe drinking water, but instead will increase risks to public health and safety.”
  • EPA Spokesperson Cora Mandy: Responding to the report, Mandy stated that “In each action EPA has taken, the agency has returned regulations to the best reading [of] the Clean Air Act after years of overreach by previous administrations.” She also reiterated the agency’s commitment to “Protect Human Health and The Environment” while simultaneously prioritizing “Making America the AI Capital of the World.”
