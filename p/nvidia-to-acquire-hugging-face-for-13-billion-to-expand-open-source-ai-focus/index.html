NVIDIA to Acquire Hugging Face for $13 Billion to Expand Open-Source AI Focus

On Thursday, American chipmaker NVIDIA announced the acquisition of the artificial intelligence library Hugging Face for $13 billion (€11.2bn). The deal marks an increased focus on open-AI models for NVIDIA, which is currently the largest publicly traded company in the world by market cap following a surge in demand for GPUs in AI data centres.

⚙️ Key details

  • Acquisition Price: $13 billion (€11.2bn).
  • Platform Role: A premier database for hosting open-source and open-weight AI models.
  • Company History: Hugging Face transitioned from an age-appropriate chat app for teenagers to a central AI ecosystem player.
  • Workforce: While the deal is based in the United States, the founders are French and half of the employees are located in Paris.
  • Strategic Goal: Hugging Face aims to remain a key part of the open-source AI ecosystem in Europe.

🔍 Overview

NVIDIA has significantly increased its embrace of open AI models over the last year, offering dozens of models for free and leading policy efforts to ensure competition among LLM providers. For European innovators who lack the capital to train models at the level of Anthropic or OpenAI, Hugging Face platforms provide valuable tools for local solutions. Open models also allow governments and agencies to host custom versions locally, utilizing sensitive or protected data without relying on external infrastructure.

💡 Why it matters

Open-source AI is presented as a solution to the transparency issues found in proprietary systems. Hugging Face co-founder Clément Delangue stated in a 2024 interview with Le Monde that “proprietary models are black boxes that make it difficult to analyse their bias or sources of truth.”

In a 2023 testimony before a U.S. House Committee, Delangue further explained, “Open science and open source AI distribute economic gains by enabling hundreds of thousands of small companies and startups to build with AI. It fosters innovation, and fair competition between all.” This accessibility allows researchers, artists, and small companies to fork, tune, and upgrade models at little or no cost.

⚖️ Regulatory landscape

The size of the deal is expected to draw concern from European Union regulators regarding digital and AI sovereignty. Competition regulators and industry watchers in Paris and Brussels may seek a review of the deal terms and enforcement of the EU AI Act. Key concerns include vendor lock-ins and the need to de-risk European systems from reliance on American or Chinese infrastructure. While building EU data centers is a priority for regulators, the software used within those centers is considered equally critical.
