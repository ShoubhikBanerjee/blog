Science in the Age of Foundation Models

Exploring how foundation models are revolutionizing scientific computing, from weather prediction to computational fluid dynamics, while addressing unique challenges in data scarcity and physical constraints.

Oct 28, 2025
3 minute read

🚀 Science in the Age of Foundation Models

💡 The Foundation Model Revolution

Deep learning’s transformative impact has become widely evident with the advent and rapid adoption of large language models (LLMs). LLMs and other so-called foundation models are potentially powerful tools for advancing a wide range of both pure and applied sciences. However, despite the role foundation models have played in language and computer vision, there has been a slower rate of adoption in scientific domains, such as computational fluid dynamics (CFD).

This raises a critical question: What would it take for deep-learning foundation models to play a more significant role in scientific applications?

🔬 Bridging Science and Machine Learning

We explored a similar idea in 2023 when we observed that deep-learning (DL) methods had “shown promise for scientific computing, where they can be used to predict solutions to partial differential equations (PDEs)” but required hard constraints for physically meaningful predictions. A year later, at ICLR 2024, we hosted a workshop aimed at bridging the gap between scientists interested in using machine learning (ML) to solve real-world problems and ML researchers who have attempted to apply ML methods to scientific problems but require more real-world test cases.

📊 Probabilistic Time Series Forecasting

Foundation models show particular promise in probabilistic time series forecasting for both univariate (one-dimensional) and spatiotemporal (two- and three-dimensional) data in scientific domains. This approach differs significantly from traditional methods by providing distributions of future points rather than single predictions.

🎯 Key Advantages

  • Enhanced uncertainty quantification for robust decision making
  • Adherence to physical laws through constraint satisfaction
  • Specialized forecasting techniques that overcome data scarcity
  • Scalable solutions for complex scientific problems

⚙️ Critical Differences from LLMs

Scientific foundation models face unique challenges compared to traditional language models:

  1. Data Scarcity: Limited training data compared to language applications
  2. Physical Constraints: Must strictly adhere to scientific laws and principles
  3. Uncertainty Quantification: Essential for scientific validity and safety-critical applications
  4. Domain-Specific Requirements: Need specialized architectures for scientific phenomena

🌍 Applications in Weather and Climate

Recent advances in deep-learning weather prediction (DLWP) models have reached the point where they rival traditional numerical weather prediction (NWP) models. This progress demonstrates the potential for foundation models in:

  • Short-term weather forecasting
  • Climate modeling and prediction
  • Extreme weather event detection
  • Long-range atmospheric simulations

🚗 Computational Fluid Dynamics

Foundation models are showing promise in aerodynamics and CFD applications, particularly for:

  • Automotive design optimization
  • Aerospace engineering simulations
  • Industrial flow modeling
  • Real-time design iteration support

The release of high-fidelity datasets like DrivAerML, WindsorML, and AhmedML has been crucial for advancing these applications.

🔐 Physical Constraints and Validation

For foundation models to gain widespread adoption in scientific domains, they must:

  • Satisfy conservation laws (mass, energy, momentum)
  • Respect boundary conditions and physical constraints
  • Provide reliable uncertainty estimates for decision-making
  • Maintain scientific rigor while enabling rapid innovation

🏁 Conclusion

For foundation models to achieve widespread adoption in scientific applications, ensuring reliable physical-constraint satisfaction and robust uncertainty quantification are essential to gain trust from domain scientists. The potential for these models is limitless when developed through interdisciplinary collaborations between scientists and ML experts.

The future of scientific computing will likely see foundation models playing increasingly important roles, but only when they can demonstrate the reliability, accuracy, and physical consistency that scientific applications demand.
