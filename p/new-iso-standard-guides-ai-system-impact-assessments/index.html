New ISO Standard Guides AI System Impact Assessments

Researchers and industry proponents are focusing on a key role in the AI workforce—‘facilitators’—who translate AI capability into practical deployment. To support this group, attention is being drawn to the new international standard ISO/IEC 42005:2025, which provides guidance on systematically integrating AI system impact assessments into an organization’s broader governance ecosystem.

🔍 Overview

With generative AI adoption moving faster than the personal computer or the internet and global AI-related investment in 2025 representing $581.69 billion, organizations must position their workforce to use AI responsibly. Researchers affiliated with the AI Adoption Initiative argue that the workforce relates to AI through an AI Labor Stack comprised of AI innovators, facilitators, and users. Facilitators are often ’the missing middle’ in national AI strategies, though they are responsible for translating AI capability into practical deployment across firms, sectors, and public services. The new ISO standard aims to support this facilitator population.

🧩 Key Features of ISO/IEC 42005

  • Provides explicit guidance on how to integrate AI system impact assessments into existing impact assessment processes within an enterprise, which often include different kinds of impact assessments for IT systems (e.g., risk, privacy, cybersecurity).
  • Unlocks a more integrated AI governance process for customers.
  • Specifies the specific information that should be documented in an AI system impact assessment via a templated assessment approach.
  • Explains how to leverage other risk assessments the enterprise may already be conducting to avoid duplication.

⚙️ How It Works

The standard covers the full assessment life cycle, including:

  • Scoping and execution
  • Analysis and reporting
  • Ongoing monitoring and review

It also identifies:

  • When an AI system impact assessment should be conducted
  • How comprehensive it should be
  • How to establish reassessment triggers

When establishing the timing for assessment and reassessment, the standard recommends considering triggers such as:

  • Applicable legal requirements
  • Contractual obligations
  • Internal policies
  • Customer expectations
  • Changes to the AI system, its operational environment, or its context
  • Other factors

The standard explains how to conduct a lighter AI system impact assessment triage—a quick classification to identify whether a more comprehensive assessment is needed or not (depending on level of risk)—before committing to a full assessment or reassessment.

💡 Why It Matters

An AI system impact assessment is a documented process of AI system risk identification by which organizations developing, providing, or using AI systems consider impacts to the organization, individuals, communities, groups, and societies. The process channels its outputs, such as identification of privacy impacts, discriminatory impacts, or performance impacts, into the organization’s risk management decisions. Using an AI system impact assessment process helps organizations responsibly manage their AI deployments, choosing appropriate guardrails to manage identified risks. AI system impact assessments are an integral part of an organization’s overall risk management process.

🚀 Practical Implementation Options

The standard provides two approaches:

ApproachDescription
Integrated (Annex D)Provides a process organizations can use to simplify impact assessments and avoid duplication, helping coordinate relevant reviews (e.g., risk, legal, security, privacy, procurement, or architecture).
Standalone (Annex E)Provides a ready-to-use template for self-contained implementation.

Organizations such as Amazon Web Services (AWS) offer tools to support governance work aligned with ISO/IEC 42005 and other responsible AI best practices, including an ISO/IEC 42001:2023 AI Management Systems (AIMS) implementation guide on AWS and the Well-Architected Framework: Responsible AI Lens.
