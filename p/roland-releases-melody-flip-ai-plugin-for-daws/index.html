Roland Releases Melody Flip AI Plugin for DAWs

Roland has entered the generative AI music space with Melody Flip, a DAW plug‑in that creates short musical loops to spark creativity.

🔍 Overview

  • Roland’s new Melody Flip tool marks the company’s foray into generative AI music.
  • Available as a plug‑in for digital audio workstations (DAWs).

🧩 How it works

  • Users can start from scratch and ask the plugin to generate any combination of melody, chord progression, bassline, or drums.
  • Alternatively, a reference track can be provided; the tool builds off its melodic ideas.
  • Outputs are simple musical loops intended as a creative spark, not fully polished songs.

⚙️ Features

  • Around 250 “Palettes,” themed collections of musical ideas sorted by genre.
  • Genre selection is fine‑grained: from standard styles like “80s disco” and “90s R&B” to niche categories such as “Kawaii Future Bass” and “Anime World.”
  • Users choose genre, note density, BPM, and key; there is no text‑prompt interface.
  • Preset sounds are basic MIDI tones similar to those found in 1990s video games.
  • Intended workflow: export MIDI data from Melody Flip into a DAW and pair it with more powerful synth plugins.

🚀 Availability

  • The plugin is released for DAWs (no specific platforms are listed in the available evidence).

💡 Why it matters

  • Melody Flip differs from services like Suno or Udio, which output fully arranged songs with vocals.
  • The limited controls and generic sound palette suggest the tool is meant for ideation rather than final production.
  • Given the mixed reception of generative AI in the music community, the plugin is unlikely to generate widespread goodwill.
