2026 U.S. Election Websites See Traffic Surge, Minimal DDoS Activity

Visitors to U.S. government election websites surged 363% on November 3, 2026, the day of the midterm elections, while Cloudflare recorded no major DDoS attacks on protected sites.

🔍 Overview

  • Traffic to state and local election websites reached about 602 million requests on Election Day, up from 130 million on November 1.
  • Overall traffic across Cloudflare’s global network rose only 12% during the same period.
  • Attacks focused on gaining access to internal systems and data rather than taking sites offline.
  • Major DDoS activity was absent on Election Day; only one significant DDoS occurred on November 2 targeting a California House candidate with 520,000 requests.

⚙️ Key details

  • Website vulnerability attempts were 180 times more frequent than DDoS attacks and ten times the rate seen across Cloudflare’s broader customer base.
  • Vulnerability exploit attempts rose from 200,000 per day in January to 2 million per day in October.
  • In the 12 months before the election, Cloudflare mitigated 392 million website vulnerability attacks on government election websites.
  • Cross‑site scripting attacks inject malicious code into web pages to capture administrator credentials or deface site content.
  • SQL injection manipulates database queries to potentially extract voter records or alter data.

🚀 Availability

  • No escalation in attack volume and zero major DDoS incidents were detected across the 1,400 protected election-related websites on Election Day.
  • The only significant DDoS attack occurred on November 2, targeting a California U.S. House candidate and totaling 520,000 requests.
  • Traffic to state and local election websites was higher on Election Day than any other day in 2026, reaching approximately 602 million requests.
  • By contrast, traffic across the rest of Cloudflare’s network increased only 12% over the same period.

💡 Why it matters

  • Cloudflare’s free election protection programs (Athenian Project, Cloudflare for Campaigns, Project Galileo) helped protect more than 1,400 election‑related websites belonging to governments, candidates, parties, advocates, journalists and civil‑society groups.
  • Cloudflare launched its first election protection program in 2016 after widespread reports of cyberattacks targeting the U.S. elections.
  • Despite the surge in traffic and increased vulnerability attempts, cyberattacks did not disrupt the 2026 election, underscoring the effectiveness of the protective measures.
