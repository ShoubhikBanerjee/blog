Amazon Introduces Connect Talent for Evidence-Based AI Hiring

Amazon has introduced Connect Talent, a service that applies established hiring science to AI-led interviews at scale. The tool is designed to help organizations gather consistent, job-related information while providing more candidates the opportunity to demonstrate their strengths.

🔍 Overview

Connect Talent builds each interview around the competencies required for the role. It uses job requirements and established occupational frameworks to create behaviorally anchored scorecards. Each scorecard defines specific strength and concern signals, and every interview question targets the behaviors it aims to uncover. The service evaluates transcribed candidate responses without using video, voice tone, or physical appearance to calculate competency scores. It also excludes candidate identity information from scoring and evaluates each competency separately.

🧩 How it works

The AI interviewer adapts to each response by checking whether the candidate has provided enough evidence for the competency being assessed. When details are missing, it asks targeted follow-up questions. Connect Talent separates the interview conversation from the evaluation: the AI interviewer gathers evidence but does not assign scores. Afterward, a separate evaluation process identifies which behaviors the candidate demonstrated and cites the supporting transcript passages. The service calculates each competency score from 1 to 5 using a fixed formula. When the transcript lacks sufficient evidence, Connect Talent flags the competency instead of producing a score. Hiring teams can review the evidence behind each rating, and missing evidence does not automatically count against the candidate. Additionally, hiring teams can review, select, and edit the competencies, scorecards, and questions before using them.

⚙️ Key details

Amazon engaged an independent I-O psychology firm to evaluate Connect Talent’s AI-generated interview content. The evaluation found that Connect Talent retained critical job information in 100% of the job descriptions reviewed. Of the competencies generated, 91% were clearly traceable to the job description. The firm’s psychologists also found that 98% of interview questions targeted the intended competencies, and 94% of rubric signals clearly measured the intended competency. An independent I-O psychology firm reviewed 36 interviews assessing whether candidates demonstrated specific strengths and concerns, creating 252 rubric signals. Connect Talent evaluated the same transcripts, reviewed hundreds of rubric signals, and aligned with expert scoring decisions in 89.3% of cases. Scoring alignment reached 97.6% when candidates provided clear evidence and 92.9% when only considering candidate concern signals, which identify behaviors that may indicate a poor fit for the intended role.
