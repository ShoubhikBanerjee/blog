Amazon Updates Alexa for Shopping to Detect and Verify Impersonation Scams

Amazon has launched a new feature for its AI assistant to combat impersonation scams. The update allows users to ask the assistant to determine whether an email, text message, or phone call actually came from the company.

🧩 How it works

When a user receives a message, they can ask Alexa for Shopping to verify its authenticity. The assistant analyzes the message using AI through several methods:

  • Comparing the message against a record of every message Amazon has sent.
  • Analyzing the message’s contents.
  • Checking the message’s formatting.
  • Reviewing the sender details.

As noted by Amazon, the assistant will only confirm that a message is real if it is “completely certain.”

⚙️ Key details

  • Handling Genuine Messages: In an example shared by Amazon, a customer asks, “Did Amazon just text me an OTP from 98626? Message came around 4:50pm.” Alexa for Shopping responds by confirming, “This was a genuine One Time Password from Amazon.”
  • Handling Suspected Scams: If Alexa for Shopping determines a message did not come from Amazon, it will instruct customers to check their orders in the app and contact Amazon’s support team directly.

💡 Related updates

This verification tool is part of Amazon’s broader efforts to protect customers and expand the capabilities of Alexa for Shopping:

  • verify@amazon.com: Earlier this year, Amazon launched a similar feature that lets you forward suspicious messages to “verify@amazon.com” to check if they are real.
  • Update Me When: In another recent update, Amazon added an “Update Me When” feature to Alexa for Shopping, which helps users track new product releases.
