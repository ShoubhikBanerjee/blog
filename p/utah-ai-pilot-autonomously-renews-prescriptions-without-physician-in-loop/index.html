Utah AI Pilot Autonomously Renews Prescriptions Without Physician in Loop

On January 6, 2026 the Utah Office of Artificial Intelligence Policy (OAIP) publicly launched Doctronic’s autonomous AI prescription pilot – the first known instance in the United States where an AI system renews medications without a physician directly involved.

🔍 Overview

  • The AI Prescription Pilot operates under Utah’s Artificial Intelligence Policy Act (Title 13, Chapter 72) as a regulatory “sandbox.”
  • The sandbox allows companies to test innovative AI products in the real world for a limited time, with temporary relief from specified state rules, but it does not waive federal law or provide a general license to practice medicine.
  • Participants sign a regulatory mitigation agreement negotiated by the OAIP with the relevant state agency; the agreement can waive or modify specified state enforcement but cannot grant a medical license.

🧩 How it works

PhaseProcessCurrent Status
Phase OneA licensed physician must review and authorize every AI‑recommended prescription renewal before it is sent to a pharmacy.Ongoing – no medication group has reached Phase Two.
Phase TwoThe AI may submit approved renewals directly to the pharmacy; pharmacists retain authority to escalate to a Doctronic physician.Not yet activated – each medication group must independently reach 250 filled prescriptions before OAIP approval.
  • The AI can process 30‑, 60‑, or 90‑day renewals of existing prescriptions only.
  • It may not issue new prescriptions, handle controlled or addictive substances, or change treatment plans.
  • The pilot covers renewal of 191 medications for chronic conditions such as hypertension, diabetes, and depression.

📊 Performance (May 2026 OAIP report)

  • AI recommended renewal in 72 % of cases; the remaining 28 % were escalated to a physician.
  • Reviewing physicians agreed with 91 % of the AI’s renewal recommendations (rising to 97 % when a second physician’s opinion was considered).
  • Physicians judged 69 % of escalations to be warranted, labeling the rest as overly cautious.
  • The data were reported by Doctronic’s own physicians; the OAIP has launched an independent review.

🚨 Regulatory response

  • Utah’s Medical Licensing Board demanded immediate suspension of the pilot activities, even as the program continues beyond the pilot phase and Doctronic eyes expansion to other states.
  • The OAIP tightened Phase Two criteria: the original agreement allowed advancement after 250 total prescriptions were filled; the new rule requires each medication group to independently hit 250 filled prescriptions.
  • The underlying mitigation agreement runs for a 12‑month term (≈ Oct 2025 – Oct 2026). Extensions can be requested no later than 30 days before term end, with a maximum of two 12‑month extensions.
  • Participants must promptly report consumer harm, privacy breaches, or unauthorized data use, which could trigger removal from the program.
  • The sandbox does not resolve whether an AI system performing the clinical determination to renew a prescription constitutes the unlicensed practice of medicine under Utah Code § 58‑67‑301 and related statutes.

🌐 Future outlook

  • Doctronic is in active discussions with regulators in Arizona, Texas, and Wyoming about similar programs.
  • A second AI company, Legion Health, has signed an agreement with the OAIP for a renewal pilot limited to non‑controlled psychiatric medications; its demonstration period has not yet begun.
  • No medication group has yet moved the pilot into Phase Two, and the OAIP’s independent review is pending.
  • If extensions are granted, the pilot could continue through October 2027 or October 2028, subject to compliance with reporting and safety requirements.
