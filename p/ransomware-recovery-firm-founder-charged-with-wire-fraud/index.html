Ransomware Recovery Firm Founder Charged With Wire Fraud

Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar, co-founder of the Florida-based ransomware recovery firm RansomwareHelp, has been charged with wire fraud after an FBI sting operation revealed that the company routinely paid ransomware attackers for decryption keys while claiming to victims that it used proprietary cryptographic tools.

🔍 The Allegations

The indictment, returned in Rhode Island, alleges that the 52-year-old Miami-based co-founder routinely paid ransomware groups for decryption keys and charged victims a significant markup for work that was never actually performed. RansomwareHelp, a six-year-old Florida business, had claimed on its website that it used “advanced cryptographic tools” to restore 100 percent of data, servers, and systems. The company explicitly stated it did not promote, intermediate, or pay ransom to threat actors on behalf of its clients.

⚙️ FBI Sting Operation

The FBI set up a sting operation in which investigators posed as both a ransomware victim company and a fictitious ransomware group. Investigators, acting as victims, supplied the company with a phony ransomware note and expressed a desire not to pay a ransom. RansomwareHelp initially reiterated its policy against negotiating with or making ransom payments.

However, Palacio soon contacted the fake ransomware group run by the FBI and requested the lowest possible price for a decryptor. FBI investigators responded with a price of 0.2 bitcoin, or around $13,000. Less than 48 hours later, Palacio allegedly informed the fake victim company that the diagnostic assessment was successful and quoted a decryption cost of $13,260. The following day, the indictment states, Palacio messaged the fictitious group to reduce the ransom demand to $4,000 for the purchase of a decryption tool. The agreed-upon price was around $6,000 in bitcoin, yet Palacio charged the FBI’s fake victim firm $16,010, including a $2,750 diagnostic fee and the $13,260 full fee.

💡 Real-World Impact

In 2024, Paul Ihme, co-founder of the security firm Soteria, heard from a small accounting firm that had paid RansomwareHelp a diagnostic fee to recover from a Lockbit ransomware infection. RansomwareHelp sent a proposal for a recovery price of $438,880, claiming this included “professional configuration of forensic servers” and “expert engineering hours of programming to configure decryption algorithms.”

The proposal claimed the recovery process involved reverse engineering the ransomware encryption using “510 preconfigured forensic servers of the maximum quantitative capacity located in different parts of the world.” Ihme believes RansomwareHelp insisted on unrestricted remote access into the victim’s network using Anydesk remote desktop software in part to delete copies of the ransomware notes left by the attackers. A review of the network found only one copy of the ransom note, unearthed from a backup snapshot. When the security firm connected to the Lockbit chat server using the provided user ID, they discovered RansomwareHelp had already been negotiating with the Lockbit extortionists for several days, pleading not to reveal their involvement to the victim.
