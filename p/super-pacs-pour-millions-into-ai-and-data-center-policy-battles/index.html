Super PACs Pour Millions into AI and Data Center Policy Battles

A wave of money‑driven campaigning is shaping AI and data‑center policy ahead of key 2026 elections.

🔍 Overview

  • Super PACs backed by tech investors and AI companies are spending tens of millions to influence both Democratic and Republican‑aligned groups.
  • Data‑center development has become a wedge issue in multiple swing races, with candidates proposing tax changes, environmental safeguards, and even a new “AI Dividend.”

🏛️ State‑Level Legislative Moves

Texas – Senate Race

  • James Talarico (Democratic) introduced the Hold Data Centers Accountable Plan (July). It would:
    • End state sales‑tax incentives for data centers.
    • Give communities more say in data‑center development.
    • Require a closed‑loop water system in new data centers to limit consumption.
    • Create federal standards to protect the energy grid and environment.
  • Talarico also proposed an AI Dividend funded by a tax on “AI‑attributable profits.”
  • Ken Paxton (Republican) unveiled the Texas First Data Center Plan (August). It would:
    • Repeal sales‑tax exemptions for data centers.
    • Require data centers to pay for the electricity they use and add to state capacity.
    • Prevent AI data centers from building in rural parts of the state.
    • Co‑sponsor a bill easing connection to off‑grid power systems, introduce legislation banning Chinese technology in U.S. data centers, and hold data‑center companies criminally liable if their AI chatbots undermine children’s safety.

Texas – Governor Race

  • Gina Hinojosa (Democratic challenger) accuses incumbent Greg Abbott of “flip‑flopping” on data centers after Abbott’s office celebrated a $40 billion Google investment but later announced an audit that could slow new facilities connecting to the state energy grid. Hinojosa has called for a statewide moratorium on data‑center development.
  • The Cook Political Report shifted the governor’s race rating from “likely Republican” to “leans Republican.”

Florida – Senate Race

  • David Jolly (Democratic) pledged a one‑year ban on hyperscale data centers on his first day in office and has not ruled out a permanent extension; an ad pairs data centers with crime.
  • Byron Donalds (Republican), while no longer calling for an outright ban, introduced the Protecting Ratepayers Act, requiring data‑center developers to fund the energy and water needed to run their operations.

📊 Election Impact

  • Texas is a deep‑red state but has the second‑highest number of planned data‑center projects in the country after Virginia, making data‑center policy a key wedge issue.
  • The Senate race is rated a “toss‑up” by the Cook Political Report, reflecting the competitive climate around AI and data‑center legislation.

⚖️ Why It Matters

  • Large sums from venture‑backed super PACs are directing the national conversation toward AI safety and data‑center regulation.
  • Proposals ranging from tax reforms to an “AI Dividend” illustrate how policymakers are trying to balance economic growth, environmental concerns, and national security in the age of AI‑driven infrastructure.
