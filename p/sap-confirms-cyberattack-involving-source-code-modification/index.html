SAP Confirms Cyberattack Involving Source Code Modification

SAP has confirmed taking urgent action to protect its customers after detecting suspicious activity in its IT environment, a cyberattack the company tracks under the code name “Project Granite.”

🏢 Company Profile

SAP is one of the world’s largest enterprise software vendors, based in Walddorf, Germany. Key operational details include:

  • Employees: 110,000 across 157 countries
  • Subscribers: 300 million
  • Annual Revenue: Over $40 billion

🔍 Incident Details

According to reports from Der Spiegel, which accessed an internal SAP memo, the attackers breached SAP’s GitHub environment, potentially with the aid of AI tools. Insider sources indicated that authentications tokens were stolen from SAP employees and used to copy and modify source code on GitHub.

AspectDetail
Internal Code NameProject Granite
Breach VectorGitHub environment
Suspected AidAI tools
Actions Taken by AttackerStole authentication tokens; copied and modified source code

🛡️ Offical Response

SAP stated that it prompted all employees to keep project details confidential and implemented necessary measures to preserve service security. A company representative told BleepingComputer:

“SAP has identified suspicious activity and promptly implemented the necessary measures to protect its customers and preserve the security of its services.”

Regarding the scope of the compromise, SAP noted:

“Based on our investigation to date, we have found no evidence of unauthorized access to customer business data.”

“Our customers trust us to protect their data, and that remains our highest priority. In a highly dynamic threat environment, we will continue to strengthen our security to provide the secure and resilient environment our customers depend on.”

Despite this, SAP has not specified which environments were observed having suspicious activity, nor has it advised customers downloading code or performing updates from specific repositories to take action. A spokesperson declined to share further information on this matter.

☣️ Context

The incident follows a public warning from Germany’s Federal Office for Information Security (BSI) regarding agentic AI tools that empower cyberattacks, such as ARTEX, CAIRN, and PentAGI. This follows reports from Swiss and Italian national cybersecurity agencies regarding the first detections of large-scale AI-driven cyberattacks targeting critical infrastructure.
