MIT Tech Review: LLMs lack reasoning, agentic web rises

Recent coverage from MIT Technology Review highlights a critical reassessment of large language models and the emergence of the agentic web. New research suggests that current AI systems are not utilizing the underlying mechanisms that enabled past breakthroughs, while simultaneously acknowledging that the internet is now designed for non-human agents.

🔍 Overview

A narrative shift is occurring in AI development, characterized by skepticism toward current model capabilities and recognition of new infrastructure trends. The evidence indicates that recent assertions about advanced AI capabilities do not hold up to detailed analysis.

  • Ten years after AlphaGo’s match against Go champion Lee Sedol, today’s AI is not tapping into the machinery that made that win possible.
  • Breathless claims about AGI and new capabilities fall apart quickly under scrutiny.
  • The summer of AI hype is contrasted with the reality that LLMs do not reason.

🧩 Research Findings

A specific development has drawn attention from the AI safety community, described as an “explosive claim” that has upended the field.

  • An exclusive interview with researcher Nick Cammarata follows his publication of a 26,000-word blog post.
  • The post has generated celebration among AI safety researchers.
  • The central finding of the research is that the field has been looking at the problem “wrong.”

🚀 The Agentic Web

While debating internal reasoning, the practical landscape of the internet is changing with the rise of personal AI assistants.

  • The “agentic web” is described as being here.
  • The internet is no longer exclusively for humans.
