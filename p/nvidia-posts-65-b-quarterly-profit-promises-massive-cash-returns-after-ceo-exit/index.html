Nvidia Posts $65 B Quarterly Profit, Promises Massive Cash Returns After CEO Exit

Nvidia reported a record‑breaking quarter of profit and cash flow, and interim CEO Colette Kress announced plans to return all excess free cash flow to shareholders following co‑founder Jensen Huang’s resignation.

📊 Financial Highlights

  • Nvidia is “raking in $700 million in profit a day on average.”
  • Quarterly profit reached $65.3 billion, outpacing the $37.7 billion, $29.8 billion and $55.3 billion profits of Micron, SK Hynix and Samsung respectively.
  • Annual “pure profit” exceeds $200 billion.
  • “That’s how much pure profit Nvidia now makes.”
  • Projected revenue for the next quarter is $130 billion.
  • Gross margins are expected to dip to 71–72 %, with a target of 73 % next year.
  • Market‑cap fell from around $5 trillion to $3.6 trillion.
  • Share price is expected to clear $45,000 and change.
  • A $1,000 investment ten years ago is now worth over $67,000; a $15,000 investment then would be over $1 million today.
  • Nvidia could give a $1 million bonus to each of its 42,000 employees every three months without exhausting cash.

🗣️ Executive Statements

  • “Going forward, we intend to return all excess free cash flow net of strategic uses to shareholders,” Colette Kress announced.
  • “All” is “a lot more than the 50 percent baseline that Jensen promised five months ago.”
  • “From a business perspective, it’s business as usual,” she said.
  • She added that Nvidia has secured backing from customers and suppliers, “including TSMC, the all‑important firm that actually fabricates Nvidia’s chips.”

⚔️ Competitive Landscape

  • “I want to show you that Nvidia has risen so far, so fast, that rival chipmakers AMD, Intel, and Qualcomm barely qualify as competition now.”
  • In the DRAM market, Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron control nearly 90 % of capacity, yet each falls short of Nvidia’s quarterly profit.
  • Both Micron and SK Hynix lost money in 2023 because they “produced too much memory.”

💡 Why It Matters

  • The scale of Nvidia’s cash generation enables unprecedented shareholder returns and employee bonuses.
  • Even after a sharp market‑cap decline, the company’s profit growth shows the AI‑driven data‑center demand remains strong.
  • The firm’s relationship with TSMC ensures continued supply of its leading‑edge GPUs.
