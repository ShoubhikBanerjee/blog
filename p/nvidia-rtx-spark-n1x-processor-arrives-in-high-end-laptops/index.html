Nvidia RTX Spark N1X processor arrives in high-end laptops

Nvidia’s RTX Spark N1X processor is finally here, and we’ve been testing a range of powerful laptops equipped with this new ‘superchip.’

🧩 Overview

Nvidia’s RTX Spark is designed as a high-performing Arm-based System on a Chip (SoC) for laptops and small desktop computers, positioning itself as a competitor to Apple, Intel, AMD, and Qualcomm. The chip features many CPU cores, Nvidia RTX Blackwell graphics, and unified memory capable of running sizable local AI models.

⚙️ Key details

  • The RTX Spark is exclusively available in high-end laptops
  • All tested models feature 20 CPU cores and 6,144 GPU CUDA cores in the flagship version
  • Lower cost configurations have 18 cores and 5,120 CUDA cores
  • Unified memory ranges from 32GB to 128GB across different models
  • Pricing starts at $2,599.99 for the 18-core version and goes up to $6,999.99 for the 20-core version

🚀 Availability

The RTX Spark is currently available in several premium laptops:

  • Microsoft Surface Laptop Ultra (128GB)
  • Asus ProArt P16 (both 64GB and 128GB configurations)
  • Lenovo Yoga 9n 2-in-1 (64GB)
  • HP OmniBook Ultra (32GB)

💡 Why it matters

The processor is aimed at developers, coders, and content creators who need the ability to run local AI models and utilize the latest gaming technologies like DLSS 5. Windows on Arm has improved significantly since previous generations, with native Arm apps working well and many x86 applications running through Microsoft’s Prism emulation layer.

Antonio Di Benedetto has made an RTX Spark his daily driver, running typical work and personal applications without issues. The chip handles daily tasks well but shows more variability in gaming performance.

Gaming performance

  • Black Myth: Wukong: 70fps at 3270 x 2180 with medium settings and DLSS
  • Cyberpunk 2077: 66fps with medium settings and DLSS
  • Forza Horizon 6: 57fps with medium settings and DLSS
  • Returnal: 60fps at 1440p with high settings
  • Jedi Survivor: 60fps with DLSS upscaled to 1200p and medium settings
  • Shadow of the Tomb Raider: 60fps at 1800p with highest settings

Performance varies significantly by game and settings, with some titles like Helldivers 2 and Star Wars Zero Company being more CPU-bound and showing lower frame rates.
