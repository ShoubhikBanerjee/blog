Epic Games previews AI‑assisted, faster launcher with Linux support

Epic Games announced today a preview of a refreshed Epic Games Launcher that is “way faster” and adds Linux support.

🔍 Overview

  • The new launcher is described as “way faster” and “more responsive than the current one, and has a new look.”
  • It now supports Linux alongside Windows and Mac.
  • Epic says the refresh was “vibe coded.”

🛠️ AI involvement

  • Epic has been “making a big push into AI‑assisted development,” and this approach includes the new launcher.
  • Martin Keely, Epic’s VP and GM of the Epic Games Store and Epic Online Services, said “We used AI as a tool for this.”
  • AI has been “super helpful” for testing and for “replicating the core functionality from the existing launcher.”
  • Keely added that AI also enabled offering the launcher on Linux.
  • The team “definitely had agents write and review all of the code,” and “used it to replicate the core functionality.”
  • The development process was “managed that process” and “supervised the entirety of what we did.”
  • While the team has not adopted the “no more hand‑written code” model reported by Bloomberg, the effort “is on the same train” as the company’s broader AI pivot.

🚀 Availability

  • The new Epic Games Launcher preview is available on Windows, Mac, and Linux beginning today; the old version will remain available.
  • Epic also announced plans to bring Fortnite to Linux through the app.
  • No specific timeline for a full migration; Keely says the full launch will happen when the update feels “stable” and has parity in “all places that we need” with the current launcher.

📈 Why it matters

  • CEO Tim Sweeney announced a “huge pivot underway” to “move developers around to retool for maximum AI acceleration.”
  • He emphasized the goal is “not to do the same stuff with fewer people, but to enable everyone here to build MORE and BETTER stuff, and to bring the benefits to all teams using Unreal.”
  • Sweeney noted the recent March layoffs of more than 1,000 staffers are “not related to AI,” and added, “To the extent it improves productivity, we want to have as many awesome developers developing great content and tech as we can.”
