World Bank Update Highlights AI Growth and Policy Needs in India

The World Bank released its India Development Update, highlighting AI as a key development opportunity for the country.

📈 Economic Context

  • India’s economy is projected to grow at 7.1% in FY27 supported by robust domestic demand and strong exports, despite global headwinds.
  • It remains one of the fastest‑growing major economies and a key contributor to global growth, according to the World Bank’s latest economic update.
  • The South Asia Economic Update projects regional growth of 6.9% in 2026, keeping South Asia the strongest‑growing region.

🤖 AI Investment and Workforce Trends

  • Private AI investment rose three‑fold, from $1.2 billion in 2024 to $4.1 billion in 2025.
  • Global Capability Centers (GCCs) employed 1.9 million professionals in 2024, increasing to 2.36 million in 2025.
  • India is listed among the top 10 emerging‑market performers on AI readiness.

🛠️ Policy Recommendations

  • The Update calls for cross‑cutting actions to deepen AI‑enabling infrastructure, improve the business environment, broaden AI access, and enhance labor capacity so benefits are broadly shared.
  • Recommended measures include:
    • Strengthening workforce skills.
    • Establishing a more business‑enabling environment.
    • Improving physical and digital infrastructure.
    • Reducing barriers for small firms, fostering local AI innovation, and creating a clear regulatory framework that safeguards data security and privacy.

🌾 Small AI for Development

  • The report stresses that AI should be used as a development tool, not just a technology, to boost productivity and improve public services.
  • Through AgriConnect, the World Bank Group is investing in ‘small AI’: targeted, locally‑adapted tools that ensure gains are accessible and widely shared while supporting workers through the transition.

🌏 Regional Perspective

  • For most of South Asia, the greatest gains lie in adopting AI and adapting it to local conditions, including small AI applications suited to low‑resource settings.
  • “The adoption of AI has the potential to transform South Asia’s development trajectory by boosting labor productivity, expanding export opportunities, and improving public service delivery. But to reap these benefits, governments need to address the foundational gaps that hold back adoption.” – Franziska Ohnsorge, World Bank Group Chief Economist for Asia
  • “India is one of the top 10 leading emerging-market performers on AI readiness**.” – Paul Procee, World Bank Acting Country Director for India

⚠️ External Risks

  • The Update notes elevated external risks, including downside risks from global oil prices, El Niño, and stock‑market corrections that could cause capital‑flow volatility.
