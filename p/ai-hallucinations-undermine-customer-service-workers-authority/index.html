AI Hallucinations Undermine Customer Service Workers’ Authority

Generative AI tools are increasingly causing customers to challenge the expertise of service workers, leading to dangerous situations like ignored allergy warnings and dismissed sommeliers.

🔍 Overview

A growing number of customer service workers report that patrons are trusting AI-generated information over the advice of human experts, a trend exacerbated by AI hallucinations. This phenomenon is affecting restaurants, national parks, childcare facilities, and other service industries.

🧩 How it works

Customers use generative AI tools like ChatGPT to answer questions or plan experiences. When the AI produces incorrect information — known as a hallucination — some users insist the AI is correct, even when confronted by human professionals.

Examples from the evidence include:

  • A server in New York City, Madison, has guests push back when she tells them a dish contains an allergen, saying “ChatGPT disagrees.”
  • One diner with a shellfish allergy ordered a fish dish with a shellfish-based broth because they believed ChatGPT over the server’s warning.
  • Sommeliers at a Michelin-recommended Italian restaurant are being dismissed from tables as guests prefer to ask ChatGPT for wine recommendations.
  • Customers have requested bottles that the restaurant doesn’t carry — or that don’t exist at all.

⚙️ Key details

The issue extends well beyond restaurants:

  • River rangers: Jason, a river ranger on federal lands in the Mountain West, says visitors arrive with nonsensical AI-generated itineraries that include hallucinated campsites or schedules out of geographical order. Some groups remain skeptical even when rangers explain the errors while pointing at a map.
  • Childcare professionals: Casey, an after-school program teacher in New York, reports parents and caretakers turning to ChatGPT or Claude for childhood development advice. One family insisted that ChatGPT’s recommended sleep schedule was fine, even though the child needed two to three more hours of sleep a night based on his behavior.
  • General customer service: Workers at movie theaters, coffee shops, and Apple Stores report customers asking for products or features that don’t exist.

💡 Why it matters

“Mass adoption of generative AI tools is rewriting the old adage that has long been a thorn in the side of restaurant and retail workers: ‘The customer is always right,’” according to the evidence. Now, workers must contend with customers who trust AI chatbots over human expertise.

To “soften the blow” of AI hallucinations, Madison and her colleagues soften their language, saying things like, “We have this really hyper-specific wine list, maybe it’s showing you a French wine, and we don’t carry French wine,” instead of telling the customer the AI made up a wine brand.

The launch of a new AI agent, Muse from Meta, is expected to worsen the problem, as are similar tools from other companies.
