Qualys TotalAppSec Achieves FedRAMP High Authorization

🔎 Overview

Qualys TotalAppSec has been granted FedRAMP High authorization on the Qualys Government Platform (FedRAMP Certified Class D, package FR2231052341). The authorization positions the service within the federal high‑impact security boundary and enables agencies to use its continuous application‑security capabilities.

⚙️ What TotalAppSec Provides

  • Maintains 99.2%+ CISA KEV coverage with a 16‑hour median from CVE disclosure to detection.
  • Generates ATO and Continuous Monitoring (ConMon) evidence from within an authorized High boundary.
  • Detects and reports vulnerabilities at a cadence that meets the daily detection requirement for High‑impact assets.
  • Supports application‑layer asset tagging as required by CISA BOD 26‑04.

📜 Policy and Compliance Backdrop

  • NIST SP 800‑228 (updated March 2026) treats APIs as a distinct control surface, calling for inventories that include shadow and zombie APIs, runtime discovery, and reconciliation of declared specs against live traffic.
  • CISA BOD 26‑04 (issued June 10 2026) mandates risk‑based remediation and application‑layer asset tagging by December 7 2026. The directive replaces flat patch deadlines with urgency calculations that factor in exposure, KEV status, exploit automation, and technical impact.
  • For High‑impact findings, agencies must assess reachability and exploitability for each finding and conduct at‑least‑daily detection.
  • Non‑compliant offerings lose their authorization after March 7 2027, removing the right to sell to the government.

📈 Security Impact

  • Mean Time to Exploit (MTTE) is now estimated at minus seven days (M‑Trends 2026), indicating adversaries often act before a vulnerability is disclosed.
  • Agencies cannot out‑patch an adversary who starts before disclosure, underscoring the need for rapid detection.
  • Public‑facing applications are now the leading initial‑access vector (40 % of incidents) and 56 % of tracked vulnerabilities require no authentication to exploit.
  • Federal AI use cases have more than doubled to 3,611 across 56 agencies, with 445 designated high‑impact AI subject to OMB M‑25‑21 risk‑management practices. AI coding assistants have cleared FedRAMP authorization, and the GSA is asking agencies to build Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers.

🚀 Availability and Next Steps

  • TotalAppSec’s FedRAMP High status enables immediate use by federal agencies seeking to meet the CISA BOD 26‑04 remediation timeline.
  • Agencies should integrate TotalAppSec into their application‑layer asset tagging processes and ensure continuous API discovery to satisfy NIST SP 800‑228 requirements.
  • Ongoing monitoring will be required to maintain daily detection and reporting compliance through the December 7 2026 deadline and beyond.
