Netflix’s New Cliff Booth Film Faces AI‑Usage Allegations

Early screenings of David Fincher’s latest film, The Further Mis‑Adventures of Cliff Booth, have raised questions about whether generative AI was used in the movie.

🔍 Overview

  • Early screenings of David Fincher’s latest film, The Further Mis‑Adventures of Cliff Booth, have raised questions about the use of generative AI.
  • Critics and audiences complained about uncanny, inconsistent visuals that make it feel more like slop than a polished film.
  • Patrick Tomasso, a YouTuber/director, tweeted that he walked out of his screening feeling “completely gutted” and that the film featured “some of the most blatant and egregious AI use” he has ever seen from a major studio.
  • Tomasso also insisted that AI‑generated footage can be seen in the trailers posted online and that the film is filled with “slop as you know and loathe it. The shit you can pump out of Higgsfield.”
  • Other critics echoed his sentiment, saying “the amount of AI in the CLIFF BOOTH movie is astounding to an absurd degree” and vowing “never watch another Fincher, Pitt or Tarintino [sic] movie again.”
  • Some viewers suggested the perceived AI might be bad CGI instead.

🧩 How it works

  • Netflix has incorporated AI into movies and series for some time.
  • The company acquired Ben Affleck’s startup InterPositive for around $600 million.
  • InterPositive’s AI models generate assets based on footage captured during a film’s production.
  • The models are designed to tweak details like lighting or remove unwanted background elements.
  • Netflix claims the models are highly customizable and can replicate a human director’s signature style.

🎬 Film Context

  • The story picks up in 1977, about a decade after Once Upon a Time in Hollywood.
  • Cliff Booth, played by Brad Pitt, has left stunt work to become a fixer for Hollywood elites.
  • He also works as a projectionist and bartender.
  • The plot focuses on his romance with Karen Crosby (Elizabeth Debicki) and his relationship with TV actor Charlie Peters (Scott Caan) who needs him to protect his wife (Carla Gugino).

📢 Reactions

  • Patrick Tomasso posted on X: “I walked out of my screening feeling ‘completely gutted’ and that the film featured ‘some of the most blatant and egregious AI use’ he has ever seen from a major studio.”
  • Tomasso also claimed that AI‑generated footage can be seen in the trailers.
  • Other critics echoed his sentiment, calling the amount of AI “astounding to an absurd degree” and vowing to never watch another Fincher, Pitt or Tarantino movie again.
  • Some viewers countered that the perceived AI might be bad CGI instead.

🏢 Netflix’s Involvement

  • Netflix has neither confirmed nor denied AI use in the film.
  • Netflix’s involvement in production and recent AI investments has fueled speculation.
  • Fincher’s recent interview with the Los Angeles Times said, “I’m not a Luddite, so I don’t fear the unknown.”
  • Fincher also said: “Is everyone satisfied that we have figured this art form out enough that what we really need to do is go back to celluloid? How long have people painted? 40,000 years? We’re talking about a thing that’s a hundred years old and everyone’s going, ‘Stop. Pencils down. No more iterating. It’s not necessary to have at your fingertips the ability to change the color of that person’s eyes.’ Well, it exists. Should we not use it?”
