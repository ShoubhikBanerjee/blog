Lawmakers Hold Hearing on Flock AI Surveillance Network and ALPRs

Washington lawmakers recently held a hearing to examine the “AI Surveillance Network” operated by Flock and the use of automated license plate readers (ALPRs). While the CEOs of Flock and peer companies Axon, Motorola Solutions, and Verkada declined to testify, the hearing featured testimony from legal and cybersecurity experts, a sheriff, and a private citizen.

⚙️ Key details

Lawmakers expressed several specific concerns regarding the technology and its implementation:

  • Data Collection: Concerns were raised about ALPRs that “hoover up location data for Americans who haven’t been accused of crimes,” with information sometimes stored for a full month.
  • Security: Cybersecurity engineer Benn Jordan testified that he previously accessed Flock’s image trove with “minimal technical maneuvering,” though he noted security has improved from a year ago.
  • Access and Use: Lawmakers questioned the ease of officer access to data and the ability of the system to construct dossiers on individual vehicles, which they noted is distinct from traditional license plate readers.
  • Legal Loopholes: Senators Alex Padilla and Dick Durbin questioned how federal immigration authorities could be barred from the data, with one witness suggesting a sympathetic local officer could perform searches on their behalf.
  • Civil Liberties: Sen. Cory Booker asked if the technology could be used to track people seeking reproductive care across state lines, while Sen. Josh Hawley noted similar risks for gun owners near ranges or stores.

💡 Why it matters

Testimony from the hearing highlighted the real-world impact of the technology, including a Florida woman who stated she spent 13 days in jail after Flock cameras incorrectly identified her car as being connected to a crime scene. Additionally, one sheriff testified that his department ended its contract with Flock after receiving “slippery answers” regarding its AI use.
