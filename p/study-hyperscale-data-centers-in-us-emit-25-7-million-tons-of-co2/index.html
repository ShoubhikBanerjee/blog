Study: Hyperscale Data Centers in US Emit 25.7 Million Tons of CO2

A new paper published in the journal Nature Sustainability reveals that fossil fuels remain the biggest source of electricity for hyperscale data centers in the US, resulting in significant greenhouse gas emissions.

🔍 Overview

The study focuses on hyperscale facilities—massive structures spanning millions of square feet built largely to support generative AI. Researchers from the team of Guidi used satellite imagery to identify 403 hyperscale data centers in the US, estimating each facility’s power consumption based on its electrical capacity. Using data from the Environmental Protection Agency, they approximated carbon pollution for the period between May 2024 and April 2025.

⚙️ Key details

  • Total Consumption: The 403 facilities likely consumed around 82 terawatt-hours of electricity, representing about 1.8 percent of all US electricity consumption.
  • Emissions: Electricity demand from these centers produced 25.7 million metric tons of CO2 emissions.
  • Global Comparison: This consumption level exceeds the individual electricity burn of about 148 nations, and the emissions exceed the total carbon emissions of about 113 nations.
  • Energy Source: About 54 percent of the electricity consumed likely came from gas and coal.
  • Regional Variance: The study showed how the local electricity mix impacts environmental outcomes; for example, generating electricity in coal-reliant Wyoming led to roughly seven times more carbon emissions per kilowatt-hour than in hydropower-rich Oregon.

💡 Why it matters

Fengqi You, a professor in energy systems engineering at Cornell University, states that the results highlight the problem of “long-term carbon lock-in,” noting that power plants built to meet demand may operate for decades and shape emissions well into the future. Both You and Guidi pointed out that researchers had to use satellite imagery to estimate energy consumption because data center operators do not disclose that information. To address this, Guidi and his colleagues are using their research to build a website to track the environmental impact of individual data centers, while a bipartisan bill in Congress aims to mandate greater disclosures nationally.
