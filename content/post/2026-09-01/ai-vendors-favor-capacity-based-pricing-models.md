# AI vendors favor capacity-based pricing models

A Bain & Company report finds that around 80% of software vendors introducing AI pricing are adopting capacity-based models over pure consumption-based pricing.

## 🔍 Overview
Capacity-based pricing is emerging as the preferred model for AI software vendors, with approximately four in five opting for fixed commitments rather than charging based on actual usage.

## ⚙️ Key details
- Capacity models allow customers to commit to a fixed amount of AI usage for a set period, with no refunds or carry-forward for unused capacity.
- This approach provides customers with predictable budgets and vendors with stable revenue.
- The model retains economic advantages of traditional seat-based pricing, as vendors receive revenue from committed entitlements even if unused.
- Procurement teams and CFOs benefit from defined commitments, avoiding open-ended variable costs.

## 📊 Pricing trends
- About 20% of AI-native software companies still rely mainly on per-seat licensing, often supplemented with usage entitlements.
- Most companies expanding beyond seat-based pricing are adding new pricing meters rather than replacing seats entirely.
- Among hybrid AI pricing models:
  - Output-based: ~55%
  - Effort-based: ~35%
  - Outcome-based: ~10%

## 💡 Why it matters
- Outcome-based pricing, where vendors are paid for business results, remains limited in scope and is most suitable when outcomes are observable, uniquely attributable to AI, and clearly agreed upon.
- Direct usage pricing continues to have a role, particularly in infrastructure software and products sold to technical buyers.
- Capacity-based pricing is likely to remain the favored approach for most enterprise applications.
