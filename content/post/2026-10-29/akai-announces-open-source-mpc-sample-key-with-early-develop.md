# Akai Announces Open-Source MPC Sample Key with Early Developer Access

Akai is previewing an upcoming 37‑key version of its budget‑friendly MPC Sample, called the **MPC Sample Key**. Although the device has not been formally announced, Akai is already sharing vibe‑coded mods and has committed to an open‑source firmware and early access for developers.

## 🔍 Overview
- The MPC Sample Key is an open‑source, 37‑key take on the MPC Sample.
- It **does not include pads**; instead, the keys are used to trigger samples.
- Akai plans to give hackers and custom‑firmware developers early access to the device.

## 🛠️ Open‑Source Strategy
- The firmware will be open source and is expected to run a version of the software found on the MPC Sample, with tweaks for a traditional keyboard, pitch wheel, and mod wheel.
- Akai will provide an SDK and will seed early devices to developers, as explained by CEO Jack O’Donnell to *The Verge*.

## 🎹 Device Details
| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Keys | 37‑key keyboard (open‑source version of MPC Sample) |
| Pads | None (unlike the higher‑end MPC Key 37) |
| Sample playback | Keys trigger samples |
| Firmware | Open‑source, based on MPC Sample software with keyboard, pitch, and mod‑wheel tweaks |
| Mod support | Vibe‑coded mods can add synths such as Native Instruments Massive, virtual‑analog, and FM synths |
| Custom OS demo | A reel shows a completely custom OS running several synths |

## 🎛️ Vibe‑Coded Mod Demonstrations
- Instagram Reels posted by Akai display mods that add synths, including **Native Instruments Massive**.
- Other mods showcase virtual‑analog and FM synthesis capabilities.
- One reel features a device running a completely custom operating system with “several different synths in it.”
- Similar community‑built synth features appear on the regular MPC Sample, built by Claude.

## 🚀 Early Access & Community Plans
- Akai is explicitly embracing the explosion of hackers and custom firmware developers by providing early access devices.
- The company plans to open‑source parts of the MPC Sample platform, potentially the entire system.
- An SDK will accompany the open‑source software to aid developers.

## 💡 Why It Matters
- By open‑sourcing the firmware and offering early developer kits, Akai signals a shift toward a more collaborative hardware ecosystem.
- The vibe‑coded mods demonstrate how the community can extend the instrument far beyond its original sample‑playback purpose, adding full synth engines and even custom operating systems.
- This approach could set a precedent for other music‑hardware manufacturers to engage directly with the modding community.
