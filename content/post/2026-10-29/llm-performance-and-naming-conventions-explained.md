# LLM Performance and Naming Conventions Explained

On October 28, 2026, Ted Sanders provided insights into the complexities of LLM performance and OpenAI's model naming conventions.

## 🔍 Overview

In general, LLM performance is incredibly multidimensional. There's no guarantee of a fixed ordering where the model that is best at math is also the model that's best at jokes. Despite that complexity, much of the variation in performance is explained by just a single variable: model size.

## ⚙️ Key Details

In general, models that are bigger will:

*   (a) be more performant / reliable
*   (b) cost more per token
*   (c) have better recall of esoteric facts

As you note, our models are ordered:

Luna < Terra < Sol < Astra

These days, version number signifies a few things at once: the order of release, the relationship to other releases, and the magnitude of improvement. So something like 6.1 would indicate it was launched after 5.6, may share some similarities to other 6.1 models in terms of training data / personality (which is why it's not 5.7), and isn't a giant enough leap to merit the headlines and expectations that would come with a 7.

— Ted Sanders, explaining OpenAI's model naming conventions
