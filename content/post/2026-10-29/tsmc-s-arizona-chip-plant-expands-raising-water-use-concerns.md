# TSMC’s Arizona Chip Plant Expands, Raising Water Use Concerns

TSMC has finished its second semiconductor fab in north Phoenix and is building a third, while its water consumption and the broader demand for power and land are sparking a growing backlash in Arizona.

## 🏭 Overview
- The Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC) is the world’s leading producer of microchips.
- Its Arizona operation grew from a pre‑pandemic gamble to revive Phoenix’s sluggish economy.
- The complex now "looms over the city’s outskirts" and is described as massive, squat, and endless.
- Inside, engineers create the force behind phones, computers, appliances, streaming services, search engines, chatbots, and apps.

## 💧 Water Usage
- The desert plant draws **nearly 5 million gallons of water a day**, sourced from Phoenix’s municipal supply.
- Industry has drained the state’s water reserves for decades; the state will soon lose access to **nearly 30 percent of the water it receives from the Colorado River**.
- Newer developments demand more water, power, and land than Arizonans are willing to give.

### Daily Water Use Comparison
| Facility | Daily Water Consumption |
|----------|--------------------------|
| TSMC Arizona complex | ~5 million gallons |
| Google data center (The Dalles, OR) | 1.5 million gallons |

## 📈 Economic Impact
- In 2020 TSMC announced plans that initially projected **$12 billion** in investment by 2029.
- The projection has grown to **$265 billion**, touted as the largest foreign direct investment in U.S. history.
- Arizona now hosts the largest chip factory this side of the Pacific **and 172 data centers**, which initially faced little pushback.

## ⚡ Energy & Land Demands
- New projects from Phoenix to Tucson require **more water, more power, more land**.
- A grassroots, bipartisan coalition of residents is uniting against further data‑center construction.
- "Data centers are the breaking point," reflecting growing concern over resource strain.

## 🌐 Why It Matters for AI
- The chips produced at the Arizona fabs power the hardware behind AI‑driven services such as **chatbots, streaming platforms, and search engines**.
- As AI workloads expand, the demand for high‑performance silicon—and the water and electricity to produce it—intensifies the environmental debate in the desert Southwest.
