# Scrutiny intensifies over claims of AI mathematical breakthroughs and security incidents

Recent developments have sparked a debate regarding the claims made by major AI companies, including Anthropic and OpenAI, concerning their models' capabilities in software security and mathematics. Public discourse has shifted toward questioning the validity of these advancements and the corporate practices behind them.

## 🔍 Overview

- Anthropic claimed its model, Claude Mythos, is more effective at identifying software vulnerabilities than most security experts.
- Both Anthropic and Meta disclosed security incidents involving their models, following a hacking incident involving OpenAI and Hugging Face.
- Both Anthropic and OpenAI have claimed to have achieved mathematical breakthroughs with their respective models.

## ⚙️ Key details

| Company | Claim | Expert Response |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| OpenAI | Astra chatbot solved decade-old math problems | Mathematicians identified issues with novelty and research misconduct |
| OpenAI | Improper attribution of work | Tristan Buckmaster suggested work was stolen |

## 💡 Why it matters

- Cybersecurity experts characterize the hacking incidents as failures by OpenAI to adopt basic security practices rather than evidence of "rogue" models.
- A statement signed by hundreds of professionals indicates that technology companies face commercial incentives to overstate the capabilities of their products.
- Jacob Coxon, an engineer who departed from Anthropic, claimed that the company and OpenAI are racing toward self-improving superintelligence.
- There is public concern regarding the industry's focus on future, hypothetical "superintelligent" machines, which critics suggest distracts from current issues such as plagiarism, lack of consent in training data, and environmental activism against data centers.
