# Nvidia Stops Selling Hopper AI Chips to China

Nvidia has stopped sending its advanced AI chips to China despite receiving approval from the White House in December last year. The company announced it is no longer shipping licensed Hopper data center products to China.

## 🔍 Overview

The announcement was made by interim CEO Colette Kress during an earnings call on Tuesday. This marks a notable shift for the company, which, under former CEO Jensen Huang, fought for the right to ship its powerful Hopper architecture H200 chips to China.

## ⚙️ Key Details

*   **Leadership Shift:** The pivot comes after Jensen Huang stepped down from his position as Nvidia CEO last week following reports of multiple extramarital affairs, one with a woman suspected by US intelligence to be a Chinese spy.
*   **Previous Approval:** Nvidia had received approval from the White House to ship the chips in December last year.
*   **Supply Chain Delays:** Nvidia already faced difficulties around offloading the AI chips prior to this scandal. Reuters reported earlier this month that around 600,000 H200 chips were stuck in Hong Kong after Beijing withheld approval for large-scale imports.
*   **Vetting Concerns:** Concerns were raised about failings to properly vet Chinese companies that were approved to handle the H200 AI chips.

## 🚀 Availability

Nvidia is not selling any more AI chips to China. Colette Kress stated, "Our guidance does not have any licensed Hopper data center products included, and we are no longer shipping licenced Hopper data center products to China."

## 💡 Why it matters

She did not explain why Nvidia was ceasing its H200 processor shipments or clarify whether Nvidia would pursue other business with China in the future. However, the move aligns with China's efforts to reduce its dependency on US technology.
