# Pupavision: AI-powered instrument for fruit fly pupariation monitoring

Pupavision is a low-cost, AI-powered instrument designed to monitor and record pupariation in fruit flies, addressing limitations in biology experiments.

## 🔍 Overview
- Pupavision is a small physical AI instrument built from a single-board Linux computer and a day/night camera.
- It is designed to monitor the timing of pupariation in the fruit fly Drosophila melanogaster, providing biologically relevant information about growth regulation.

## 🧩 How it works
- A frontier coding agent runs its agentic harness directly on the tiny board.
- The model thinks in the cloud, but the agent works on the device.
- The agent assists image capture, writes code to repair errors, and checks the experiment every day to keep raw data capture reliable.
- If recording stops, the researcher gets a ping on their mobile phone.
- A simple deterministic capture service records one image every ten minutes and never depends on the agent.

## ⚙️ Key details
- Pupavision offers a blueprint for do-it-yourself agentic instruments that put frontier intelligence on a tiny board, with deterministic code underneath.
- It is built for roughly 45 to 75 US dollars in parts.
- A researcher-in-the-loop workflow then produces per-vial pupariation curves by tracing selected pupae backward in time from a late reference frame.

## 🚀 Availability
- Pupavision is presented as an early case study from a pilot lab deployment.
- It was tested for 26 days in a fly incubator, including eleven days in which the agent was logged out and capture continued, and a 51-hour outage.

## 💡 Why it matters
- Pupavision contributes lessons learned toward a future where scientific domain experts are empowered by DIY agentic hardware.
- Future work will validate the assay for lifecycle tracking accuracy against expert-labeled data and expand real-time computer vision analysis.
