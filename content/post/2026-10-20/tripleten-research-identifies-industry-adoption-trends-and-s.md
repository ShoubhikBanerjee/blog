# TripleTen Research Identifies Industry Adoption Trends and Skill Development Gaps in Artificial Intelligence

Research from TripleTen in 2026 indicates that tech and finance sectors are leading AI adoption, with healthcare, education, and retail identified as the next sectors for growth. While 79% of workers across all surveyed industries report being asked to learn new skills, the level of exposure and financial investment in training varies significantly between sectors.

## 🔍 Overview

According to the TripleTen data, the tech industry maintains the highest adoption rates, while the finance sector has emerged as a close second. The research highlights a professional advantage for early adopters; when employees lead AI innovations, 88% of those processes become standard company practice. Ana Riabova, AI strategy and enablement lead at TripleTen, notes that the advantage of early adoption is the ability to expand what a worker can do, allowing them to handle tasks they previously had to hand off without being restricted by their core skills.

## ⚙️ Key details

AI exposure and adoption rates differ by industry, with finance workers seeing significantly higher requirements for automation than those in healthcare or education.

| Industry | Asked to Use AI Chatbots | Asked to Automate Workflows |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Finance | 48% | 32% |
| Education | 25% | Not specified |
| Healthcare | 18% | Not specified |

Workers also show varying levels of out-of-pocket investment in professional development based on their industry:

| Industry | Average Out-of-Pocket Spend | Percentage Spending $0 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Tech | $1,758 | 13% |
| Finance | $1,664 | 24% |
| Healthcare | $990 | 45% |
| Education | $883 | 42% |

## 🧩 How it works

Tech workers are utilizing AI tools differently than their counterparts in other fields to accelerate their own learning. 

* Tech workers are nearly twice as likely as the general workforce to use AI chatbots as learning tools (48% vs. 27%).
* Compared to workers outside the tech sector, tech employees are more than twice as likely to use AI to acquire new skills (48% vs. 21%).
* Despite the availability of AI, 31% of workers trust a peer currently doing the job most for learning new skills, while 19% trust certification programs, 16% trust AI tools, and 6% trust university professors.
* Only 30% of managers and owners prioritize the ability to judge whether AI output is correct, ranking it sixth on a list of seven essential skills.

## 💡 Why it matters

As the AI adoption curve remains steep, the ability for individual workers to introduce automation processes provides significant professional leverage. Employees who adopt these tools early can expand their capabilities regardless of their seniority level. However, the data shows that nearly half of workers in healthcare and education spent nothing on professional development last year, even as non-tech industry adoption rates continue to increase.
