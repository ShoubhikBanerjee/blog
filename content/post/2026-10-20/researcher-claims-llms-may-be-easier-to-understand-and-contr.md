# Researcher Claims LLMs May Be Easier to Understand and Control

Nick Cammarata, a researcher at the AI startup Goodfire, has published a 26,000-word blog post claiming that Large Language Models (LLMs) may be far easier to understand, monitor, and control than previously thought.

## 🔍 Overview
Cammarata utilizes an approach known as mechanistic interpretability, which MIT Technology Review named one of the 10 hottest technologies of 2025. This method involves peering inside models and using tools to probe individual neurons to determine what they represent.

## ⚙️ Key details
While working at Goodfire, Cammarata has spent most of the last year looking at neurons for approximately 10 hours a day, seven days a week. His process includes:
* Building new tools to analyze neural networks.
* Deploying teams of AI agents to help make sense of the data.
* Mapping out whole circuitry within the models.

## 🧩 How it works
Cammarata applied his current approach and agents to Inception v1, a small image-recognition model he had previously studied in 2020. 

* **Previous Finding:** In 2020, Cammarata and colleagues discovered a neuron that responded to both images of cats and cars, a phenomenon called "superposition" where models fold unrelated concepts into single neurons.
* **New Finding:** By investigating what those unrelated things had in common, Cammarata determined the commonality was a "V shape."
