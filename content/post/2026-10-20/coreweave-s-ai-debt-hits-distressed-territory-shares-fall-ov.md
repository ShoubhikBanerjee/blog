# CoreWeave’s AI Debt Hits Distressed Territory, Shares Fall Over 20%

CoreWeave, an AI‑focused cloud provider, saw its bonds slip into distressed territory and its shares tumble more than 20% after a wave of commentary about a potential private‑credit bubble.

## 🔍 Overview
- Its bonds slipped into distressed territory by a common definition — when investors demand 1,000 basis points more in yield than they’d get from government bonds.
- Its shares are down more than 20 percent since Tuesday’s close.
- The company lost more than a billion dollars in the first half of the year.

## 📊 Debt Profile
- As of its second‑quarter earnings, CoreWeave owed more than $35 billion.
- Earlier this year, CoreWeave secured its first investment‑grade chip loan.
- Co‑founder Brannin McBee told the Financial Times the company wants its debt to be investment‑grade “so that it can borrow from insurers, as their capital is, in his words, \“unlimited.\”"

## 📈 Market Reaction
- Last Tuesday Chamath “SPAC King” Palihapitiya noticed the distressed debt and asked whether the AI buildout was a private credit bubble.
- John Yovanovic, co‑head of leveraged finance at MetLife Investment Management, said: “The problem is not that credit profiles have changed in the last year, it’s that we’ve got another year and a half of supply coming.”
- Oracle’s shares have fallen almost 10 percent in the same timeframe, and there’s been a sell‑off in Oracle bonds.

## 🤝 Customer Base
- CoreWeave’s biggest customers are Microsoft, OpenAI, and what is probably Nvidia, though the third‑biggest customer is not disclosed.
- Those three generated almost three‑quarters of the company’s second‑quarter revenue.

## 📅 Future Obligations
- The answer is “remaining performance obligations” from its customers — $103.7 billion of them as of the second quarter.
- About 40 percent of that money is due to CoreWeave in 2028, and the companies who’ve agreed to these contracts can’t cancel them except under very limited circumstances.
