# US fossil fuel infrastructure surge as renewables slow and AI data center demand rises

## 🔍 Overview
The new report from the Environmental Defense Fund and Atlas Public Policy shows the United States is building more fossil fuel infrastructure than it has in a decade while renewable energy growth slows down.  Data centers are demanding more energy, and the Trump administration is making sure fossil fuels meet the moment.

## ⚙️ Key details
- "The US is building more fossil fuel infrastructure than it has in a decade while renewable energy growth slows down, a new report shows."
- Electricity demand is climbing faster than it has in more than a decade with more data centers, factories, and electric vehicles plugging into the grid.
- Plans for new fossil fuel infrastructure climbed from just 5 % of all new capacity in 2024 to 30 % by the second quarter of 2026.
- If the trend continues, fossil fuels could make up 70 % of the development pipeline by the end of the decade.
- Planned fossil capacity would reach 68 GW by 2030, a 45 % jump from construction plans six months earlier.
- Renewable projects are being cancelled at a faster rate than fossil projects (4.9 % vs. 2.9 %).
- Many of those plans could still fall through, particularly if forecasts for AI data center energy demand are overblown.

## 📊 Energy mix and costs
- The U.S. still generates 60 % of its electricity from fossil fuels, but wind and solar produced more electricity together than coal for the first time in 2024.
- Solar was the fastest‑growing source of new electricity generation in 2024, accounting for 81 % of added capacity.
- New utility‑scale solar and wind farms cost $68–$69 per MWh on average, compared with $90 per MWh for new combined‑cycle natural gas plants.
- Fossil fuels are subject to increasing price volatility, particularly since the US instigated war with Iran.

## 💡 Why it matters
- "The U.S. power sector is at a new inflection point, with decisions made today shaping the cost, reliability, and emissions profile of the grid for decades to come," the report says.
- If all gas projects in the development pipeline are completed, they could lock in more pollution and higher electricity prices for decades.
- Gas and coal plants emit air pollutants and greenhouse gases, leading to higher health costs.
- Electricity bills have risen across the nation as utilities upgrade grids and build new energy infrastructure to meet rising demand.
- The administration’s policies have sought to hobble wind and solar while promoting fossil fuels, including issuing stop‑work orders, blocking permits, and removing tax incentives.
- The forecasted surge in AI data‑center energy demand is a key driver behind the renewed focus on fossil‑fuel‑intensive power generation.

---
*The report was published last week and analyzes data from the U.S. Energy Information Administration.*
