# US Approves Nvidia AI Chip Sales to Empty Chinese Firm New Matrix

The U.S. Commerce Department has cleared Nvidia’s H200 AI chips for sale to New Matrix, a Chinese firm whose physical presence appears to be largely nonexistent.

## 🔍 Overview
- Concerns are being raised that neither the Commerce Department nor Nvidia are doing their due diligence when it comes to vetting Chinese firms cleared to handle the company’s H200 AI chips.
- The government is approving sales of Nvidia’s AI chips to Chinese firms with ties to blacklisted companies.

## ⚙️ Key details
- The New York Times reports that New Matrix is on the list of approved firms, yet its offices are empty, "a hollow gray shell with wires dangling from the ceiling where lights should be."
- The paper could find little evidence that the company exists in any meaningful way.
- Only scant information can be found online about New Matrix, which was created 17 months ago.
- At the building that New Matrix lists as its address, a company directory does not include the firm.
- At the nearby property‑management agency for the industrial park, a receptionist and a manager both said they had no record of the company.
- Its owners have ties to a Chinese private‑equity firm that has been blacklisted by the U.S. government since 2024.

## 📊 Approval pattern
- According to the Times, this is not a rare lapse in oversight, but a troubling pattern of companies connected to entities with national‑security‑related trade restrictions being approved to purchase Nvidia chips.

## 🏭 New Matrix specifics
| Company | Approval status | Notable concerns |
|---|---|---|
| New Matrix | Listed as approved for Nvidia H200 chips | Empty office described as a hollow gray shell; owners linked to a blacklisted Chinese private‑equity firm |

## 📅 Policy background
- Nvidia had been restricted from selling chips to China during the Biden presidency.
- The Trump administration relaxed those limits after Nvidia agreed to give the government a 15‑percent cut of all sales.
- Nvidia’s CEO Jensen Huang launched an intense lobbying campaign and was apparently targeted by China’s Ministry of State Security in a spying plot.
