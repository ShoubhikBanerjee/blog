# Midterm Election Shifts AI Landscape Amid Big Tech Loss and Monetization Uncertainty

On Tuesday, millions of American voters went to the polls and voted overwhelmingly for Democrats and against AI. The results have major implications for the technology sector, especially AI development and data‑center investments.

## 🗳️ Election Outcome
- "On Tuesday, millions of American voters went to the polls and voted overwhelmingly for Democrats and against AI."
- "Big Tech lost big in the 2026 election"

## 🏗️ Data‑Center Fight
- "The fight against Big Tech is about to come roaring back, and the fight over data centers is looking even harder for the folks trying to build them."

## 📻 The Vergecast Takeaways
- "On The Vergecast: blue waves, the confusing business of AI search, and the many thoughts of RAM Altman."
- "On this episode of The Vergecast, Nilay and David discuss what the 2026 midterms mean for the tech industry going forward."
- "They dig into the self-quarantining echo chamber that is X, the congressional energy likely to be pointed at tech CEOs and AI regulation, and whether anything is actually likely to get done in the next two years."
- "After that, the hosts turn to AI."

## 💰 AI Monetization Questions
- "Specifically, whether anyone has figured out how to make money on AI."
- "Specifically, whether OpenAI’s explosive growth in web search means much, when even Google can’t figure out how to make money on AI web search."
- "Businesses are scaling back; agents don’t appear to be taking over."
- "There’s a lot of money flowing around the AI world, but is there enough flowing into it to keep everything going?"
- "That’s the hard part."

## 📈 Outlook
The conversation highlights a growing uncertainty: while AI continues to attract capital, questions about sustainable revenue models and the ability to expand critical infrastructure such as data centers remain unanswered.
