# Voters Oppose AI Data Center Expansion in 2026 Midterms Prompting Policy Shift

Following widespread voter opposition to AI data center expansion in the 2026 midterm elections, tech giants including OpenAI and Amazon are altering their community engagement policies and non-disclosure agreement practices.

## 🔍 Overview

Data centers serve as the physical foundation for tech companies' AI initiatives, but expansion efforts faced severe pushback during the 2026 midterms. Of voters polled by NBC News on Election Day, 76 percent stated they opposed data centers in their communities. Dozens of local communities voted directly to restrict data center development through ballot measures, delivering a major blow to AI infrastructure expansion.

## ⚙️ Key details

The election results resulted in several key leadership changes and policy commitments across politics and the tech industry:

| Person / Organization | Role or Action | Stance on Data Centers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gina Hinojosa | Texas Democratic Governor-elect | Supports a moratorium on data center development |
| Abdul El-Sayed | Michigan Democratic Senator-elect | Supports a moratorium on data center development |
| Sherrod Brown | Democratic Senator-elect (Ohio) | Defeated Republican Jon Husted, whom Brown labeled "the face of data centers" |
| James Talarico | Texas Democratic Senator-elect | Favors ending tax incentives and creating an "AI dividend" for Americans |
| OpenAI | AI Development Company | Announced commitments to eliminate standard NDAs with public officials and cover local energy costs |
| Amazon | Technology Company | Announced in October it would no longer use NDAs with government agencies |
| Lisa Su | AMD CEO | Stated, "We are a data center first company" |

Following the midterm elections, OpenAI stated it will restrict NDA usage to narrow protections covering legitimate security information, trade secrets, or proprietary and competitively sensitive information. Maine previously attempted to pass a state-level moratorium on data centers, though the governor vetoed the measure.

## 💡 Why it matters

Alongside local opposition, legislative efforts and environmental concerns are reshaping the data center landscape:

* **Permitting Reforms:** A massive bipartisan permitting bill is advancing to speed up energy projects and potentially lower utility bills. However, it limits environmental reviews and prevents communities from delaying projects in court.
* **Environmental Impact:** Advocates express concern that developers will take advantage of streamlined permitting to avoid public scrutiny and rely more heavily on fossil fuels to satisfy increasing electricity demand.
* **Economic and Product Criticisms:** On The Vergecast, Nilay Patel noted that Big Tech lost big in the 2026 election, pointing out that AI products currently cost more to run than they earn, while failing to convince voters that the benefits outweigh local disruptions.
