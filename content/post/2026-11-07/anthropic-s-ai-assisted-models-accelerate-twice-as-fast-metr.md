# Anthropic’s AI‑Assisted Models Accelerate Twice as Fast, METR Report Finds

Anthropic is using AI to build more powerful AI, and a new METR investigation shows the effort is yielding rapid progress.

## 🔍 Overview
- Anthropic’s internal research uses AI to improve its own models.
- METR (the AI safety research organization) completed an 11‑week investigation inside the company and published its report on Thursday.
- The probe is separate from a program that gives safety researchers “employee‑like” access to systems after recent hacking incidents.

## 📈 Acceleration of Model Performance
- Models are improving roughly twice as quickly as a year ago; benchmark scores suggest “roughly two years of historical progress over the last year.”
- METR’s researchers are confident the acceleration is **not** simply due to scaling up computing power.
- They attribute the majority of the recent speed‑up to AI itself, with its contribution growing over time.
- No clear signs of diminishing returns or observable bottlenecks were found.

## 🤖 AI‑Assisted Research
- Employees using Anthropic’s most capable internal model self‑reported being about **12×** as productive as without AI assistance, roughly double the uplift reported a few months earlier with Mythos 5.
- The current acceleration appears to stem mainly from AI assisting human researchers, though METR warned that “there are some signs that fully autonomous contributions will play a larger role soon.”

## 📊 Model Development Timeline
- Over the past six months Anthropic finished training three unreleased models:
  * **Model 2** – completed training in July.
  * **Model 3** – completed training in August‑September.
  * **Model 4** – completed training in September‑October.
- METR also identified an unreleased model from 2022, called **Unreleased 2022**.

## 🔒 Transparency and Redactions
- Anthropic voluntarily gave METR a large amount of non‑public information, surpassing the original project scope.
- The company exercised its right to redact and did so extensively; a majority of the most informative evidence was removed from the public version of the report.
- METR described its information pipeline as “relatively narrow,” noting that several data requests were denied.
- Redactions were justified by Anthropic on the grounds that releasing the information could benefit its rivals, leaving the public to rely heavily on METR’s judgment.

## 💡 Why It Matters
- The findings arrive as Anthropic prepares to go public, despite its CEO Dario Amodei publicly urging labs to “pump the brakes” on AI development.
- Rapid AI‑driven acceleration could influence ongoing discussions about AI safety and the pace of future research.
