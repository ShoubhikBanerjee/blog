# AI-Powered Automation Inverts Cyber Kill Chain Leading to Large Scale Tech Breaches

The technology industry is currently experiencing a period of continuous security breaches, characterized by the automation of the traditional cyber kill chain through AI. According to Alex Stamos, chief security officer at Cognition and lecturer at the Stanford University Cyber Policy Center, AI-enabled systems are now capable of finding vulnerabilities and writing exploits in real time, shifting these activities from a long-term research phase to immediate execution.

## 🧩 How it works
The traditional cyber kill chain involves upfront research, finding vulnerabilities, and writing exploits well before an attack occurs. AI has inverted this process through the following capabilities:
* AI systems autonomously scan targets for bugs in real time.
* The systems identify vulnerabilities and write functional exploits immediately.
* Open-weight models have reached a level of sophistication that allows for the automation of most of the kill chain.

## ⚙️ Key details
Recent events illustrate how these automated attacks are being chained together across the tech industry, affecting neoclouds and AI-related companies:

| Incident or Tool | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| Binance Hack | Described as the largest heist of anything in human history. |
| Fireworks | A prominent AI service provider that was breached; stolen API keys were used to access other systems. |
| Cursor Incident | Attackers used stolen keys to push a bad plugin to 5,000 companies, resulting in 5,000 potential breaches. |
| ARTEX | A Chinese open-source tool, available with an English translation on GitHub, used to automate attacks. |
| Obliterated GLM-5.3 | A specific model that can power tools like ARTEX to break into a large number of organizations. |

## 💡 Why it matters
Alex Stamos suggests that the multibillion-dollar cybersecurity industry is overdue for disruption as AI-boosted attacks increase. State actors, including Russia and North Korea, are currently utilizing AI to detect and exploit vulnerabilities simultaneously. While Stamos is not a long-term doomer, he indicates that the industry is entering a dark age as it struggles to adapt to these new adversary capabilities and the resulting waves of hacks.
