# Nikon Disqualifies Small World in Motion Winner for Generative AI Use

Nikon has removed the original first‑place video from its Small World in Motion contest after determining it violated the competition’s rules on generative AI.

## 🔍 Overview
- The video that initially won first place was created by Dr. Ning Xu and claimed to show “tiny, hair‑like structures called cilia moving in the airway of a child with the respiratory condition PCD.” (BBC)
- Nikon announced that the entry “did not comply with the competition rules regarding generative AI.”
- After online skepticism, Nikon said it was reviewing the video and later confirmed the disqualification.

## 📹 What Happened
- Nikon said last week it was reviewing the video following skepticism online about its authenticity.
- Dr. Xu later admitted to using AI in a LinkedIn comment, stating:
  > “An unsupervised neural‑network method was subsequently used for AI‑assisted post‑processing to distinguish and visualize features in the reconstructed grayscale images from the super‑resolution optical imaging.”
- The video has now been disqualified and removed from Nikon’s website.

## ⚖️ Rules and Response
- Nikon announced it will “revisit rules and evaluation procedures for future competition entries.”
- In the same statement Nikon added the decision “should not be interpreted as a judgment of the entrant’s professional reputation, scientific contributions, or intent.”
- The company also said it is “revisiting its competition rules after the original winner’s video was too good to be true.”

## 📈 Aftermath
- A video from Nguyen Nam Nhat now holds first place, according to the contest’s updated rankings.

## 💡 Why It Matters
- Highlights the need for clear guidelines on AI‑generated content in scientific imaging contests.
- Shows how contest organizers may need new verification steps when entries appear “too good to be true.”
- Underlines the growing scrutiny of AI‑assisted post‑processing in published research visuals.
