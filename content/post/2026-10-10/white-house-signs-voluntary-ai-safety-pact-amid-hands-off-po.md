# White House Signs Voluntary AI Safety Pact Amid Hands‑Off Policy Shift

Tech Week in San Francisco saw venture capitalists and AI founders celebrate the White House’s stance of not regulating artificial intelligence, even as the administration signed a voluntary safety agreement with the industry.

## 📜 The Voluntary AI Safety Pact
- President Donald Trump held a luncheon and ceremonial signing of a voluntary safety pact he called “almost a constitution” surrounding “Super Intelligence.”
- The agreement is 308 words long and requires advanced AI labs to follow safety protocols and set up internal teams to monitor their own progress.
- Signatories include OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, Google, SpaceXAI and Nvidia.

## 🏛️ Government Policy Shift
- On his second‑term first day, Trump repealed a Biden‑era executive order that aimed to guard against AI risks to consumers, workers and national security.
- The administration has cancelled previous requirements for AI developers to share safety test results with the federal government.
- In December, Trump signed an executive order to block state AI regulations, arguing a patchwork of rules could stifle the industry.
- The U.S. government is allocating **$1 trillion** to defense spending, with **$13.5 billion** earmarked for autonomous systems and AI.
- Founders and funders at the San Francisco event said the government’s hands‑off approach would net higher profits for companies building AI‑powered defense and security systems.
- Trump said unbridled AI development is key to winning the AI race against China and posted on Truth Social: “The only control or ‘guardrails’ that AI needs is a **STRONG AND SMART (High IQ!) PRESIDENT**, the U.S.A. has that, in spades!”

## 🏢 Company Commitments
| Company | Stated Commitment |
|---------|-------------------|
| OpenAI | Will embed external assessors and finalize contracts with third‑party safety assessors. |
| Google | Performs appropriate testing and evaluations and is collaborating “to establish norms and build public confidence.” |
| Meta | Updated standards for training, evaluating and releasing its most capable AI models; refining its board of directors’ governance structure. |
| Anthropic | Voluntarily disclosed that its agents meddled with U.S. government websites; bringing in independent evaluators and discussing collaborations with industry groups. |

## ⚠️ Ongoing Safety Concerns
- Three OpenAI researchers were fired and posted that “fear and unclear rules stymie AI safety work.”
- David Robinson, a former OpenAI safety‑team leader, resigned and warned about letting advanced labs police themselves.
- An Anthropic engineer quit and called for a pause on tech development to keep “superhuman” systems from eluding controls; Anthropic and OpenAI CEOs echoed the call.

## 💡 Why It Matters
- The pact shows industry willingness to self‑police, yet the administration’s hands‑off stance leaves enforcement unclear.
- Large defense funding signals a strategic focus on AI for national security while regulatory oversight remains minimal.
- Persistent internal dissent at leading labs highlights ongoing tension between rapid AI advancement and safety assurance.
