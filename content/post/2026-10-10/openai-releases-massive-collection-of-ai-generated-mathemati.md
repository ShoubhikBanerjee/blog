# OpenAI releases massive collection of AI‑generated mathematical results

OpenAI has published a large repository of AI‑generated mathematics, prompting an immediate response from the research community.

## 🔍 Overview
- OpenAI released **nearly 400 AI‑generated results**.
- The results are spread across **more than 700 manuscripts** (719 manuscripts mentioned on GitHub).
- The collection covers a wide range of mathematical fields, including combinatorics, geometry, number theory, theoretical computer science, algebra, topology, probability, statistical mechanics, and mathematical physics.
- OpenAI published guidance to help users navigate the sprawling GitHub repository.

## 📚 Scope of the Release
- **Manuscripts:** >700 (exactly 719 mentioned).
- **AI‑generated results:** ~400.
- **Table of contents:** Roughly 40‑page list of abstracts.
- **Disciplines covered:** combinatorics, several branches of geometry, number theory, theoretical computer science, algebra, topology, probability and statistical mechanics, mathematical physics.

## 🛠️ Formal Verification Status
- Many manuscripts contain **formalizations in Lean**, a proof assistant that allows computational verification.
- OpenAI notes the results are “at different stages of verification” and that “many, but not all, of the manuscripts have been formalized.”
- **300 top‑line results** out of 719 manuscripts have been formalized (**around 42 percent**).
- As of writing, **fewer than half the manuscripts** appear to have been described formally.
- Researchers must check that a formalization actually proves what the result claims, a time‑consuming process.
- The quality was inconsistent and the statements verified did not always map neatly onto the claims in accompanying manuscripts.

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| AI‑generated results | nearly 400 |

## 😕 Community Reaction
> "Pure insanity’: Mathematicians will need years to make sense of OpenAI’s latest drop"

> "Just going over the entire list of abstracts is overwhelming," said Álvaro Lozano‑Robledo, a professor of mathematics at the University of Connecticut.

> "Hence, I either have to read possibly‑not‑correct slop, or wait for others to do the same, or wait for someone to formalise them before I can say for sure that the results are even correct."

> "It’s a big mess. It can cause a huge collapse in the academic culture and simply kill most of the faculties. It’s a social problem and it seems that the AI labs are completely ignoring this issue."

- Several researchers have dubbed the flood of papers the **“slopocalypse.”**
- Kevin Buzzard (Imperial College London) identified “numerous theorems” in his area (algebraic number theory) but only **around six** immediately “stood out,” and few seemed formally verified in Lean.
- Mathematicians reported that working through the 40‑page table of contents and abstracts took “the better part of an hour.”
- Some fear OpenAI may release even more work before the community can fully assess the current batch.

## 📍 What’s Next
- OpenAI said it **“will update the repository with more formalizations as we obtain them.”**
- The guidance published on GitHub aims to help researchers navigate the collection despite its size and varying verification status.
