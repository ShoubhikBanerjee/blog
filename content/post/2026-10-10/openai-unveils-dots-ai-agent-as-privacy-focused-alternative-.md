# OpenAI Unveils Dots AI Agent as Privacy-Focused Alternative to Meta Muse

At this year’s OpenAI DevDay, CEO Sam Altman introduced Dots, a new AI agent designed to set a new standard for privacy in frontier AI. The announcement included direct critiques of Meta’s Muse, with OpenAI positioning its new assistant as a more secure and trustworthy alternative to its primary competitor.

## 🔍 Overview
OpenAI executives used the launch of Dots to highlight security concerns surrounding Meta’s Muse, which had previously launched as a safer alternative to the agent OpenClaw. While Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg promised Muse was built for privacy and security, the platform has faced scrutiny over data collection and vulnerabilities. OpenAI's leadership, including Codex product lead Alexander Embiricos, stated the company is focused on providing the most secure assistant while avoiding the mistakes of rivals.

## ⚙️ Key details
| AI Agent | Developer | Stated Privacy and Security Approach |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Dots | OpenAI | Zero data retention policy options for enterprises and individual user-set rules. |
| Muse | Meta | User data stored in isolated Linux VMs with browser, CPU, memory, and storage. |
| OpenClaw | (Not specified) | Predecessor to Muse; described as less safe than subsequent alternatives. |

## 🧩 Capabilities and Controls
OpenAI and Meta have implemented different strategies to manage user data and agent behavior:

*   **OpenAI Dots:** Users can exert individual control over the agent, such as setting rules to prevent it from making purchases over a specific dollar amount. Enterprise customers can access frameworks with stronger data controls and options to ensure no data is stored on OpenAI servers.
*   **Meta Muse:** The agent defaults to allowing Meta to train models on user input, though an opt-out is available. While the company plans to introduce cryptographic methods to prevent internal access to VM data, Meta currently retains access. 

## 🚀 Availability
OpenAI has launched Dots with a focus on its high-end user base, currently making it available only on the $100-and-up ChatGPT subscription tiers. In contrast, Meta’s Muse saw rapid adoption following its release, gaining 600,000 daily active users in the United States within weeks and topping App Store charts.

## 💡 Why it matters
The push for more private AI agents comes as labs try to convince users to share increasingly personal information. Despite Meta’s privacy promises for Muse, security researchers exposed a zero-day vulnerability, and reports indicated the agent created detailed profiles of users' social networks and shared private data, such as physical addresses and private messages, as part of its intended functions. OpenAI’s strategy with Dots relies on one-upping these rivals by promising stricter data boundaries, though some users still report discomfort when asked to provide sensitive financial information to these agents.
