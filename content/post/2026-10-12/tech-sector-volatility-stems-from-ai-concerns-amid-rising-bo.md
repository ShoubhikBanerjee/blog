# Tech Sector Volatility Stems From AI Concerns Amid Rising Borrowing Costs

The week‑end markets showed a clear split: broad indices posted modest gains while the technology sector faced selling pressure linked to worries about artificial intelligence and higher borrowing costs.

## 🔍 Overview
- Volatility was mainly driven by selling pressure in the tech sector due to concerns around AI and borrowing costs.  
- The NZX 50 gained approximately **0.50 %** over the week.  
- The All Ordinaries rose about **0.26 %**, with Australian REITs among the strongest sectors while technology shares declined.  
- In the United States, the Dow increased **0.93 %** and the Nasdaq **0.64 %**.  
- The FTSE 100 climbed **0.85 %**, helped by easing UK government bond yields and strong mining shares.  
- The Shanghai Composite fell **0.74 %**, trading only two sessions after the Golden Week holiday.

## 📉 Market Impact of AI Concerns
- Tech‑focused investors reacted to headlines about AI, contributing to a sell‑off despite overall market upticks.  
- The broader market sentiment remained relatively subdued, with the NZX 50 only gaining around half a percent and the All Ordinaries showing modest growth.
- Mixed sector performance highlighted that while some areas (e.g., REITs, mining) performed well, technology lagged due to the AI‑related risk perception.

## 💡 Why It Matters
- **Investor Sentiment:** The AI narrative is now a factor in short‑term market moves, influencing risk appetite in the tech segment.
- **Borrowing Costs:** Rising interest rates amplify concerns, as higher borrowing costs can affect AI‑driven business models that often rely on significant capital investment.
- **Future Outlook:** Continued monitoring of AI news and monetary‑policy signals will be crucial for gauging whether tech volatility persists or eases.
