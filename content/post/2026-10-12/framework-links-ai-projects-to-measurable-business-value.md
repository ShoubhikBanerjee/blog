# Framework Links AI Projects to Measurable Business Value

A practical framework has been introduced for moving from technology activity to measurable business value, helping leaders determine whether digital transformation and AI initiatives are delivering a competitive edge.

## 🔍 Overview
- Digital transformation promises a competitive advantage: faster decisions, more personalized customer experiences, more productive employees, smarter use of data and AI.
- Key questions: “How do you know your digital transformation is creating a competitive edge?” and “But how do you know whether that advantage is actually materialising?”

## ⚙️ The Three‑Level Model
| Level | Focus | Typical Question |
|-------|-------|-------------------|
| Transformation activity | What have we implemented? | Go‑live dates, usage levels, adoption rates |
| Adoption & behavioural change | Are people using the new capabilities as intended? | Login rates, actual usage patterns |
| Business outcomes | What measurable value has been created? | Revenue growth, customer retention, operational efficiency, employee productivity |
- The first two levels help explain whether the transformation is progressing.
- The third determines whether it is succeeding.

## 📈 Measuring What Matters
- Most organisations can report when a new platform went live, how many employees completed training, or how many processes were automated.
- Far fewer can confidently explain how those activities improved revenue, customer retention, operational efficiency, or employee productivity.
- A high login rate does **not** automatically mean employees are more productive.
- An automated process does **not** necessarily create a better customer experience.
- An AI pilot does **not** prove that AI is delivering commercial value.

## 🎯 Choosing the Right KPIs
- Start with a business priority that leaders recognise: winning profitable business, reducing customer effort, improving service reliability, or bringing cash into the business sooner.
- Making that priority explicit gives teams a shared basis for deciding what to change, where to invest, and which results deserve management attention.
- Choose KPIs based on the desired outcome, not on the technology.
  - **Customer loyalty** → outcomes: increased retention, customer lifetime value; supporting KPIs: customer effort, response time, first‑contact resolution, digital self‑service adoption.
  - **Operational efficiency** → outcome: lower cost to serve; supporting KPIs: process cycle time, automation rate, error reduction, amount of manual work removed.

## 💡 Why It Matters
- A competitive advantage is created when digital capabilities produce measurable improvements that are difficult for competitors to replicate.
- Those improvements could be responding to customers faster, making better use of data, bringing new services to market sooner, or operating at a lower cost.
