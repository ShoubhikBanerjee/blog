# US Reverses Ban on Sharing AI Models with Britain’s AI Security Institute

The United States has reversed a previous decision that barred American AI developers from giving Britain early access to their newest models for safety testing.

## 🔍 Overview
- The change was reported by the *Telegraph* and credited to a shift in the Trump administration’s stance.
- It allows developers such as OpenAI and Anthropic to share new AI models with Britain’s AI Security Institute without violating U.S. policy.

## 🛠️ What Changed
- Previously, the administration had asked developers not to share new AI models with Britain’s AI Security Institute before release.
- The reversal removes that restriction, giving U.S. developers greater latitude to cooperate with the British institute.

## 👥 Who Is Involved
- **U.S. side**: The Trump administration and its newly launched AI task force, which requires developers to report security incidents.
- **Developers**: OpenAI and Anthropic are specifically mentioned as the types of firms affected.
- **British side**: The AI Security Institute, created after the world’s first AI safety summit in 2023, tests advanced AI models for capability and misuse risk.
- Spokespersons for Trump, OpenAI and Anthropic did not immediately respond to Reuters requests for comment.

## 📈 Why It Matters
- The reversal signals a softer U.S. stance on international AI safety collaboration.
- It enables British authorities to evaluate cutting‑edge AI systems earlier, potentially improving global risk assessment.
- The move aligns with the administration’s new requirement that developers report AI security incidents.
