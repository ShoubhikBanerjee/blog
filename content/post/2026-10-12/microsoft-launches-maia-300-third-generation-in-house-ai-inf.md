# Microsoft launches Maia 300, third‑generation in‑house AI inference chip

Microsoft announced its third‑generation **Maia 300** AI chip today, positioning it as the primary AI inference system for the company’s products and services.

## 🔍 Overview
- Maia 300 is designed to improve the cost and efficiency of running AI workloads in the cloud.
- It is built to handle the latest reasoning models and agentic tasks.
- The chip reduces Microsoft’s reliance on Nvidia GPUs and AMD solutions.
- Production roll‑out is planned for **2027**, with multi‑gigawatt Azure deployments beginning the same year.

## 🧩 How it works
- Microsoft uses a **chiplet architecture** to gain efficiency gains.
- The chip is fabricated on **TSMC’s N2P process node**.
- Maia 300 separates **compute, memory controller, and I/O functions** into distinct chiplets, which *"increases the silicon area dedicated to compute performance and allows each function to use the technology best suited to its role,"* said the Azure hardware and infrastructure team.

## ⚙️ Key details
- **Performance**: *"We expect Maia 300 to deliver 40 percent more tokens per megawatt compared to leading GPU‑based systems,"* said Scott Guthrie, EVP of Microsoft’s cloud group.
- **Scale**: In a full 96‑accelerator configuration, Maia 300 delivers *up to 7× the rack‑scale token throughput of Maia 200*.
- **Workload focus**: *"Maia 300 is a complete AI system designed for the new types of AI workloads emerging today: Long‑context reasoning, latency‑sensitive conversational experiences, and sustained, multi‑step agentic tasks,"* according to Microsoft.
- **Successor**: Maia 300 follows Maia 200, which was announced in January and is already running production workloads such as Microsoft Copilot, GitHub Copilot, Microsoft Foundry, and Microsoft’s own AI models.

| Chip | Current Use | Notable Claim |
|------|-------------|---------------|
| Maia 200 | Production workloads (Microsoft Copilot, GitHub Copilot, Microsoft Foundry, internal models) | *"Maia 200 is the best performing AI infrastructure in our fleet, measured in real‑world metrics for AI services,"* – Scott Guthrie |
| Maia 300 | Planned production rollout in 2027; primary inference system for Microsoft services | *"40 percent more tokens per megawatt"* and *"up to 7× rack‑scale token throughput"* compared with Maia 200 |

## 🚀 Availability
- Maia 300 will start rolling out to production AI workloads in **2027**.
- Microsoft plans to scale the Azure Maia family to **multi‑gigawatt deployments** starting in 2027, making the chip the primary inference platform for its services.

## 💡 Why it matters
- The efficiency improvements allow Microsoft to rely mainly on its own AI chips rather than third‑party GPUs.
- Owning the silicon stack enables tighter co‑design with Microsoft’s AI models, a priority expressed by AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman: *"We have to work across the full stack, all the way down to the silicon… running our Microsoft AI models on top of the Maia chip is a critical focus for our team."*
- The chip is intended to help Microsoft catch up with leading AI research organizations such as Google DeepMind, OpenAI, and Anthropic.
