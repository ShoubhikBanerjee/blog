# Global Survey Shows Consumers Trust AI Agents Only With Clear Limits

A new random double‑opt‑in survey of 11,000 consumers across 12 countries highlights a growing trust challenge as AI moves from answering questions to acting on consumers’ behalf.

## 🔍 Overview
- The shift to AI‑driven actions requires organisations to handle more personal data and digital account access, yet confidence in data management remains low.

## 📊 Survey Scope & Key Stats
- **Sample size:** 11,000 consumers
- **Geography:** 12 countries
- **Trust in data handling:** Only 12% of global consumers fully trust organisations to manage identity data, down from 17% in 2025.

## 🤝 Trust & Autonomy
- Consumers are open to AI assistance but set strict limits on autonomous actions.
- **Support preferences:** 56% comfortable using AI for low‑stakes support (27%) or recommendations (29%).
- **Action with approval:** Only 5% comfortable with AI taking action after their approval.
- **Fully autonomous actions:** Just 3% comfortable with routine actions or transactions without final approval.

## 💰 Financial Boundaries
- 42% would allow an AI agent to spend no more than **$100**.
- 22% would not permit any financial transactions at all.

## 🧭 Representation & Oversight
- 71% worry AI agents acting on their behalf could misrepresent their personality or values.
- 47% say constantly having to check an AI agent’s work is a bigger concern than the agent making a mistake.

## 📢 Transparency Demands
- 56% say knowing whenever AI is being used is more important than getting the fastest possible result.
- **Potential fallout:** 20% would leave a website immediately if they suspect undisclosed AI, and another 20% would avoid that brand in the future.

## 🌐 Regional Variations
| Country | Percentage willing to let AI decide independently |
|---------|---------------------------------------------------|
| India   | 70% |
| Indonesia | 61% |
| Sweden  | 21% |
| Netherlands | 19% |
| Australia | 18% |
- While 68% of Australian, 63% of U.S., and 57% of UK consumers want to be notified whenever AI is used, 62% of Indonesian respondents prioritize speed and accuracy over disclosure.

## 🗣️ Expert Perspective
> “Consumers are open to AI doing more, but they want a say in how far it can go,” said Darryl Jones, Vice President of Consumer Segment Strategy at Ping Identity.
>
> “The more responsibility we give AI, the more important it becomes to be clear about what it can do, what it can access and who remains in control.”

According to Ping Identity, as AI moves from assistance to action, **secure and governed AI** is critical to building consumer trust. Clear permissions, transparency, and accountability can give consumers confidence that AI operates within defined boundaries while people remain in control.
