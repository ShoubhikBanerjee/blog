# CIOs Report AI Adoption Outpacing Governance, Highlight Authority‑Accountability Gap

New research from Thoughtworks shows that AI adoption is moving faster than governance structures can keep up, creating a widening gap between authority and accountability for CIOs across North America.

## 🔍 Overview
- Surveyed 3,200 CIOs across 10 countries.
- **91%** say AI adoption is outpacing governance.
- The study highlights a growing mismatch between who has authority over AI decisions and who is held accountable for the outcomes.

## ⚖️ Authority vs. Accountability
- **Responsibility shift**: 92% of North American CIOs now spend more time redesigning workforce workflows and labor models than managing core IT infrastructure (94% in the U.S., 91% in Canada).
- CIOs are being held personally accountable for AI outcomes they cannot fully influence.
- **Incidents**: 37% report security incidents involving AI systems; 35% cite data‑privacy breaches.
- **Perceived lack of authority**:
  - Workforce disruption: 32%
  - Regulatory/compliance failures: 32%
  - Demonstrating ROI on AI: 31%
- *“AI governance is also a workforce design issue,”* said **Rachel Lycock**, Chief Technology Officer at Thoughtworks.
- *“Authority over AI is distributed, but accountability hasn't always moved with it,”* said **Mike Sutcliff**, CEO of Thoughtworks.

## 📊 Governance & Budget Structures
### Budget Responsibility Models (North America)
| Model | Share of CIOs |
|-------|---------------|
| Budgets managed centrally by IT/technology | 21% |
| Budgets controlled independently by business units | 21% |
| Responsibility shared between IT and business | 20% |
| Model still evolving | 20% |
| Budgets managed centrally at executive/board level | 19% |

- 92% of CIOs (94% U.S., 89% Canada) believe central IT will ultimately be held responsible for security or compliance failures caused by AI tools purchased by business units.
- Thoughtworks emphasizes that the solution is not to re‑centralise every decision or to place a single executive in charge; instead, organizations need clearer decision rights and skilled people to make informed choices.

## 👥 Leadership & Roles
- **Chief AI Officer (CAIO)** adoption:
  - 67% of surveyed organizations have already hired a CAIO.
  - 28% are planning to appoint one.
- Relationship between CAIO and CIO/CTO is unclear:
  - 38% see the CAIO as an extension of the CIO/CTO’s centralized strategy.
  - 34% view the CAIO as operating independently with equal or greater enterprise influence.
  - 28% describe the relationship as a source of friction or unclear boundaries.
- *“At Thoughtworks, our experience has been that AI transformation is a team sport, from defining enterprise AI strategy and architecture to embedding AI into internal platforms and day‑to‑day operations.”*
- *“The question isn't who owns AI, but how leadership collaborates to create business value responsibly and at scale.”*

## 📈 Next Steps & Confidence
- Despite the gaps, 97% of CIOs consider their organization prepared to govern AI consistently across multiple business functions.
- 66% describe their organization as **very prepared** (U.S. 83%, Canada 49%).
- **Planned actions** (next 12 months):
  - 56% are establishing a formal AI governance and operating model.
  - 52% are embedding a dedicated technology lead within each business unit.
  - 48% are expanding the CIO’s role to oversee enterprise‑wide AI strategy and implementation.
