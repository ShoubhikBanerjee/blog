# AI‑Native Ordering Startup Bites Challenges DoorDash with Flat‑Fee Model

Bites, a pre‑seed food‑delivery startup operating in the Bay Area, attracted DoorDash’s attention after the larger platform warned restaurants that they might be listed on Bites without consent. Bites markets itself as an “AI native” service that lets diners order through ChatGPT and charges a flat $1 surcharge per order, aiming to keep menu prices lower than DoorDash’s commission‑based model.

## 🔍 Overview
- DoorDash processed **970 million orders** in Q2 and generated **$4.5 billion** in revenue.
- DoorDash commissions for restaurants range from **15 % to 30 %** per delivery order.
- Bites has around **300 restaurants** signed up in the Bay Area and is still in pre‑seed stage.
- In August, DoorDash sent a form‑letter to Bay Area restaurants warning that they may have been added to Bites without agreeing to its terms, pricing, or service standards.

## 🧩 How it works
- Bites offers a traditional mobile app **and** an AI‑native interface: diners can place orders directly in **ChatGPT**, which forwards the order straight to the restaurant instead of a third‑party platform.
- The pricing model is a **flat $1 surcharge per order**. Diners typically pay this fee, though some restaurants choose to cover it for customers.
- By removing variable commission fees, Bites claims restaurants can keep item prices lower for consumers.

## ⚙️ Key details
| Platform | Pricing Model | Typical Fee |
|----------|---------------|------------|
| DoorDash | Commission per order (15‑30 % of order value) | Variable, up to 30 % |
| Bites    | Flat surcharge per order | $1 (paid by diner or restaurant) |

- DoorDash’s warning letter said restaurants may lack control over **menu accuracy** or **hours of operation** on Bites and that such practices could be illegal in some states. The email provided instructions for contacting Bites to request removal.
- Bites founder and CEO **Bala Subramaniam** said the company heard from **14 restaurant partners** who received the DoorDash letter and that a handful of businesses not yet listed wanted to join after learning about Bites.
- Some restaurants asked to be removed; Bites acknowledged they were listed from old demos.
- Co‑founder **Matt Maloney** (also co‑founder of Grubhub) stated that Bites can keep prices lower because the pricing structure on larger platforms is outdated.

## 💡 Why it matters
- Restaurant owner **Jay Jayaraman**, who has 13 restaurants on Bites, reports that before joining Bites **80 %** of his orders came from DoorDash. After adopting Bites, he estimates **65 %** now come through Bites, **25 %** from DoorDash, and a small share from Uber Eats and Grubhub.
- Jay says his margins are higher with Bites orders and notes, “**We are seeing a huge drop or a huge shift away from DoorDash, which I personally welcome because it’s adding to my bottom line**.”
- If Bites’ flat‑fee model scales, it could pressure the dominant platform’s commission‑based pricing and influence how restaurants manage delivery costs.
