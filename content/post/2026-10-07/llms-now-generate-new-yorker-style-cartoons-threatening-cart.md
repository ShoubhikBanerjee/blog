# LLMs now generate New Yorker‑style cartoons, threatening cartoonist jobs

Large language models have reached a point where they can produce cartoons that mimic the style of *The New Yorker* and even attach the original cartoonist’s signature.

## 🔍 Overview
A recent story highlighted that various LLMs are now churning out simulacra of New Yorker cartoons. You can ask for a cartoon about cats in the style of, say, Emily Flake, and the model will loyally produce it, complete with the artist’s signature.

## 🎨 Capabilities
- Generate New Yorker‑style cartoons on command.
- Replicate the visual humor and drawing style of specific cartoonists.
- Add the original cartoonist’s signature to the output.
- Produce content that is “an uncanny simulacrum of an impression of a joke,” as described by a long‑time New Yorker cartoonist.

> “It’s an uncanny simulacrum of an impression of a joke,” said Jason Adam Katzenstein, who has been publishing cartoons in *The New Yorker* since 2014.

> “But it’s not the point for it to actually be funny or good. The point is that it’s trying to deal a death blow to labor. It’s trying to render individual cartoonists obsolete because we cost money and take time.”

## ⚠️ Why it matters
- The technology is explicitly aimed at making creative labor—specifically cartoonists—redundant.
- It exemplifies broader concerns that AI could “kill labor” in many creative sectors.
- Prominent voices have warned that such capabilities contribute to existential risks, with Bill Gates noting it would be “pretty hard” for AI to kill everyone, estimating a “mere billion deaths.”
- Sam Altman’s suggestion to “accept some bad things” from new AI technology has been compared to the satire of *Dr. Strangelove*.

## 🌍 Context
The rise of such AI capabilities occurs alongside other major concerns:
- Data centers that power AI “pollute air and water, and suck up vast amounts of electricity” at a time when clean power is needed for climate transition.
- The greenhouse effect is described as “by far the biggest threat that world has faced.”
- AI is also seen as a “remarkable threat to the jobs on which many of us depend,” echoing broader anxieties about automation.

These intersecting issues underline why the emergence of AI‑generated cartoons is seen as a significant development in the ongoing debate over AI’s societal impact.
