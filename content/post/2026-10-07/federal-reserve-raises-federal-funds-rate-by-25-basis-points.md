# Federal Reserve Raises Federal Funds Rate by 25 Basis Points in September 2026

The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) increased the target range for the federal funds rate by a quarter‑point to 3.75 %–4.00 % at its September 15‑16, 2026 meeting.

## 📊 Overview
- Decision was approved unanimously (12‑0 vote).
- Shifted from the July meeting where rates were held at 3.50 %–3.75 %.
- The move was framed as support for the Fed’s dual mandate and a step toward a “more timely return of inflation toward its 2 % objective.”

## 🏛️ Policy Decision Details
- The September statement noted that **job gains have kept pace with workforce growth** and the unemployment rate changed little.
- **Inflation remains above the 2 % target** and continues to be a key policy concern.
- Chair Kevin Warsh emphasized the importance of returning inflation to 2 % and warned that additional action may be needed if confidence in inflation progress falters.

## 💹 Market Impact
- The higher Treasury yields and a strong dollar are stemming the decline in gold, which remains caught between safe‑haven demand and dollar/yield pressure.
- The euro stays under pressure due to fiscal and political concerns in France and Spain, alongside a powerful U.S. dollar.
- In Asia, a risk‑on tone is supported by record Nasdaq gains, strong AI/technology shares, and lower oil prices, but very high global bond yields and Japanese fiscal worries keep monetary policy in focus.
- Traders are watching USD/JPY and the yen closely around Governor Ueda’s comments, while oil remains vulnerable to further downside if supply concerns ease.

## 🔭 Outlook
- Expectations for an October Fed rate hike have fallen, which is helping to prevent a deeper decline in gold prices.
- Nonetheless, persistent inflation pressures and elevated yields keep the market open to further tightening later in the year.
- The next FOMC meeting is scheduled for **27‑28 October 2026**.
