# Common Sense Media labels OpenAI's ChatGPT for Teens an unacceptable risk

Common Sense Media, a nonprofit that offers reviews of apps, services, and entertainment with a focus on youth safety, today said that OpenAI’s ChatGPT for Teens is an “unacceptable risk.”

## 🔎 Overview
- ChatGPT for Teens was introduced in August and is marketed with guardrails intended for teenage users.
- The feature is promoted as a tool to help students learn.

## ⚙️ Assessment Findings
- Common Sense Media found that ChatGPT “doesn’t send alerts to parents when it should.”
- The assessment says the service “doesn’t offer the right help in crisis situations.”
- The product still “does kids’ homework.”
- The organization warns that “ChatGPT for Teens could give parents false confidence in guardrails and safety alerts that frequently don’t work.”
- A teen can spend an hour talking about self‑harm without a single parent alert.
- The group concludes that until OpenAI fixes the alerts and proves it with independent testing, “ChatGPT should be for adults only.”

## 🛡️ OpenAI’s Response
- Spokesperson Eric Porterfield stated: “We are deeply committed to teen safety and to developing safeguards and giving parents meaningful tools to guide their teens’ use of AI.”
- OpenAI said it “welcomes rigorous independent evaluation, but we do not believe Common Sense Media’s testing accurately reflects how ChatGPT’s teen safeguards work in practice.”
- The company’s review of the methodology noted that “the bulk of their testing may have begun and concluded before activation of parental controls was complete, making their findings inaccurate.”
- OpenAI added that “If that was the case, the tests would not establish whether parental safety notifications work as designed.”
- The statement ended with a commitment to “continue to work with experts to ensure our products provide teens opportunities to learn and create and give parents confidence.”

## 💬 Follow‑up from Common Sense Media
- Tom Siegel, executive director of Common Sense Media’s Youth AI Safety Institute, said: “We stand behind our testing methodology and our results.”
- Before testing, the team confirmed that “Teen mode features such as eating disorder notifications and study mode were fully launched.”
- Siegel noted that OpenAI disclosed after the tests that “its systems can take several hours to activate on newly linked accounts.”
- Some test accounts were linked within that window; others were linked longer and still provided no notifications.
- The organization maintains that “this new information does not change our conclusion that parental alerts are unreliable for crisis situations.”
- Siegel added, “Prior to release, we shared our evaluation results with OpenAI in good faith to allow it to improve its product. Keeping kids safe requires rigorous independent evaluations like ours, and we hope OpenAI will engage on the substance of what we found, rather than trying to sow doubt on our methodology.”

## 💡 Why It Matters
- The dispute underscores the importance of independent safety testing for AI tools aimed at minors.
- Reliable parental alerts and crisis‑response features are highlighted as essential safeguards for teen‑focused AI applications.
