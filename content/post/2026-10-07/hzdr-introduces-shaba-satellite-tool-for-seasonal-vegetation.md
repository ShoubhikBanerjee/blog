# HZDR Introduces SHABA: Satellite Tool for Seasonal Vegetation Analysis

Researchers at the Helmholtz‑Zentrum Dresden‑Rossendorf (HZDR) have unveiled SHABA – Seasonal Harmonic Anomaly Break Analysis – a satellite‑based remote sensing tool designed to analyze spatial and temporal vegetational dynamics (DOI: 10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2026.07.001).

## 🔍 Overview
- SHABA is a satellite‑based innovative tool for studying vegetation.
- It was created by a research team at HZDR to better assess the spatial extent of mining facilities and their environmental impact.

## 🧩 How it works
- The method factors in seasonal fluctuations and trends.
- By incorporating these seasonal harmonics, SHABA can reliably detect and quantify changes in vegetation over time.

## ⚙️ Key details
- **Satellite‑based**: Uses remote‑sensing data from Earth observation satellites.
- **Seasonal analysis**: Explicitly models seasonal patterns to avoid misinterpreting normal cycles as anomalies.
- **Anomaly detection**: Identifies breaks in expected seasonal behavior, signaling real vegetation changes.
- **Application focus**: Tailored for evaluating mining‑related environmental impacts.

## 🚀 Availability
- The tool is described in a peer‑reviewed article (DOI: 10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2026.07.001).
- It represents a new capability for researchers and regulators to monitor vegetation dynamics.

## 💡 Why it matters
- Enables more precise assessments of how mining activities affect surrounding ecosystems.
- Provides a systematic way to separate normal seasonal variation from actual environmental change.
- Supports policymakers and industry with reliable data for environmental decision‑making.
