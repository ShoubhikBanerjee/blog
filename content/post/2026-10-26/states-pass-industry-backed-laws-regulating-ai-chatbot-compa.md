# States Pass Industry-Backed Laws Regulating AI Chatbot Companions

Legislators in multiple U.S. states have enacted new laws that set basic safety standards for AI chatbots, but the measures are largely shaped by industry influence.

## 🔍 Overview
Lawmakers are passing industry‑backed bills to guard against the worst outcomes, rather than addressing more common problems.

## 🏛️ Legislative Landscape
- This year, for example, Google backed bills to regulate AI chatbots in several states.
- The legislation mandated basic protections against sexual content and promotion of self‑harm that tech companies should have established regardless, but with broad carve‑outs that could exempt some of the most popular services.
- Passing these bills allowed legislators to claim a safety win while efforts to promote stronger legislation—laws designed to prevent children from becoming emotionally dependent on AI—failed.
- This playbook worked in states like Nebraska, producing legislation heavily influenced by industry at the expense of bills that would have done more to hold companies accountable.
- Sam Hiner is the cofounder and executive director of the Young People’s Alliance, where he coordinated a coalition of advocates to pass laws regulating AI companions in eight states in 2026—laws such as California’s SB1119.

## 🤖 Industry Response
- An AI company promises that its chatbot will not encourage suicide.
- But companies can clear these low bars while continuing to make products that leave young people dependent on simulated friendships or trapped in feeds built to keep them scrolling.

## 👶 Youth Impact
- A third of teens now choose to discuss serious matters with AI companions instead of other people.
- MIT researchers found that young people using ChatGPT to write essays showed weaker brain connectivity during the task, and their evidence indicated that “cognitive debt” persisted even after the task was complete.
- Companies should be prohibited from designing companions that pretend to be conscious or profess love to users, and they should face independent scrutiny of how their systems affect relationships and people’s ability to think for themselves.

## 📚 Calls for Stronger Action
- Technology companies and school districts should have to show beyond a reasonable doubt that AI improves learning and critical thinking before making it a fixture in classrooms.
- We should demand safety as a minimum, yes—and then demand considerably more.
- The companies that have inserted their products into nearly every part of modern life have earned obligations far greater than avoiding the next terrible headline.

## ❓ What Remains Unknown
- There is plenty we still need to learn about the long‑term effects.
- We already have reason to ask whether technology marketed as making us more capable is eroding the habits and relationships that help us develop capabilities in the first place.
