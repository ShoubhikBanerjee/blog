# RIAA CEO Mitch Glazier Calls for Action on AI-Generated Music and Copyright

In 2026, Mitch Glazier, the chief executive and chairman of the Recording Industry Association of America (RIAA), is joining music fans to push back against big AI companies that train on artists’ work without permission and flood platforms with low‑quality output.

## 🔍 Overview
- "That’s because here in 2026, Mitch, the RIAA, the record labels, the artists, and ordinary people are all pretty unhappy with the big AI companies in the same way, for training on everyone’s work without permission and flooding our feeds with slop."
- The dispute echoes the early‑2000s clash over Napster, when the RIAA was seen as the villain for suing students for downloading music.

## 🤖 AI Legal Challenges
- The conversation explored "the legal complexity of things like AI deepfakes, how banning them might affect other kinds of expression, and how the AI detection and labeling systems that we all seem to want might actually work in practice — especially if big platforms like Spotify and YouTube don’t get on board."
- Issues discussed include:
  - Protecting copyrighted music from unauthorized AI training.
  - Preventing AI‑generated “slop” from overwhelming listeners.
  - Designing detection and labeling mechanisms that platforms might adopt.

## ⚖️ RIAA’s Role
- "And since the RIAA is both the group that lobbies for changes to the law and also the group that files lawsuits about things that affect record labels and distributors, Mitch is a pretty busy guy these days."
- The RIAA continues to lobby for legislative reforms while also filing lawsuits to enforce rights.

## 🎤 Why It Matters
- Artists and fans alike feel the economic value of art is being siphoned by AI services and large tech platforms that collect only fractions of a penny from each use.
- The alignment of the RIAA with music‑loving consumers marks a shift from the adversarial stance of the Napster era to a unified front against AI‑driven infringement.
- "This conversation is a real ride through how AI, the law, art, and commerce might all intersect."
