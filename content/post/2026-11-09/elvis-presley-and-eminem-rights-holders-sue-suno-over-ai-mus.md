# Elvis Presley and Eminem Rights Holders Sue Suno Over AI Music Clones

AI music company Suno is facing two new lawsuits from rights holders of Elvis Presley's music and the publisher of some of Eminem's biggest records. The lawsuits claim that Suno illegally generates music and lyrics that mimic the two artists.

## ⚖️ Claims and Legal Arguments

The lawsuits allege that Suno creates imitations of artists using their identities and work without permission. Lawyers representing Presley's rights holders argue that Suno violated Tennessee's Ensuring Likeness Voice and Image Security (ELVIS) Act, which makes it illegal for an AI company to clone a singer's voice.

In the Presley complaint, lawyers argue:
* "Suno has mechanically encoded itself with artists’ identities, hard-work, creativity, and time, and harnessed them all for a consistent conveyor belt of cold hard cash."
* "Suno’s ravenous race to the bottom aims to make human creativity obsolete, coldly replicating without a license in seconds what takes years to learn, struggle with, maintain, and endlessly attempt to make better and perfect."

These filings mirror a suit brought in September by Jason Isbell and others, which similarly alleged Suno created imitations of plaintiffs without directly asserting copyright infringement. Suno currently faces additional legal action from Sony, UMG, Isbell, Reservoir Media, and others.

## 🛡️ Suno's Guardrails and Response

In response to the litigation, Suno spokesperson Cat McCormack told The Verge that "Suno exists to help people create new, original music, not to trade on anyone’s name or likeness, and we prohibit any unauthorized use of an artist’s work."

To prevent misuse, Suno maintains the following platform safeguards:
* Prompts requesting specific artists' names and songs are prohibited.
* Third-party providers screen uploaded audio files and lyrics for potential misuse.
* The company is working to strengthen existing guardrails and add new safeguards to protect artists and users.
