# Google’s AI‑powered satellite sends first haiku from orbit

Google’s AI chip‑equipped satellite transmitted its first response from space – a haiku written by the on‑board Gemma 4 model.

## 🔍 Overview
- The satellite is part of **Project Suncatcher**, an experimental effort that could eventually host AI data centers in space.
- It was launched into orbit earlier this month.
- After successfully booting its Tensor Processing Units (TPUs), the system generated a space‑themed haiku and sent it back to Earth.

## 🛰️ Test details
- Google’s blog post on Friday confirmed that the TPUs were powered up and the Gemma 4 model responded to a prompt for a haiku about being in space.
- The haiku received on the ground reads:
  
  > **Blue marble below**  
  > **Data streams in silent grace**  
  > **Starlight feeds my core. ✨**
- Google also ran other space‑themed limericks and telemetry prompts during the test.

## ⚙️ Technical specifics
- The satellite hosts AI‑optimized TPUs that execute workloads in orbit.
- The first successful transmission demonstrates that TPUs can operate under space conditions, a prerequisite for future on‑orbit AI services.

## 🚀 Next steps
- Google plans a series of tests to evaluate TPU performance under thermal stress and radiation exposure in orbit.
- Verifying reliable TPU execution is aimed at enabling a **twin interconnected satellite** architecture slated for 2027.

## 💡 Why it matters
- Proving that AI workloads can run on hardware in space opens the possibility of satellite‑based AI data centers.
- Such capability could reduce latency for Earth‑bound services and enable new types of on‑orbit processing.
