# AI‑Generated Mia Goth Image Spread via Verified Fan Account, Instagram Removes It

An AI‑generated image of actress Mia Goth wearing a T‑shirt that read **MY EX IS MY BIGGEST FAN** was posted on Instagram shortly after she filed for divorce from Shia LaBeouf. The post quickly went viral and was eventually taken down by the platform.

## 🔍 Overview
- The image was created with artificial‑intelligence tools, as reported by The Verge.
- It featured a Beyoncé song, “Single Ladies (Put a Ring on It),” added to the post.
- AI photos and videos of celebrities are racking up millions of views on social media.
- The Instagram account @miagoth_ had more than one million followers and displayed a blue “verified” checkmark.

## 📲 How the AI Image Gained Traction
- The post garnered nearly 500,000 likes within days, including likes from peers in the entertainment industry, despite being labelled a fan account.
- The account had previously been complained to by Goth’s representative for posting “counterfeit, AI images” of her.
- Meta’s policy allows users to purchase blue verification checkmarks even when the account is not an official page.
- The news of Goth’s divorce caused the post to be mentioned by celebrity‑gossip outlets, which in turn drove traffic through Google search.
- Searches for the actress and the shirt produced an AI‑generated overview stating Goth was taking “a clear swipe at her ex, Shia LaBeouf, following their recent divorce filing,” and cited the same blogs.
- A Google search result section titled “Mia Goth turns heads on Instagram by wearing a sassy ‘My ex is my biggest fan’ t‑shirt” was featured prominently, linking to social‑media discussions of the image.

## 🛑 Platform Response
- Instagram eventually removed the @miagoth_ account for impersonating a public figure.
- Christine Tripicchio, a representative for Goth, said: 
  > “The fact that this fan account meets Instagram’s criteria to qualify for a ‘blue check’ is disconcerting on a fundamental level.”
  > “Beyond that, it is incomprehensible that Instagram evidently prioritizes the dissemination of forged and manipulated images over the protection and integrity of a public figure’s likeness.”
- Platform policy statement from Dziedzan: 
  > “We do not permit accounts to use someone’s likeness to pose as that individual, regardless of whether the content is authentic or AI‑generated.”

## 💡 Why It Matters
- The incident highlights how purchased verification can lend credibility to fan or impersonator accounts that circulate deep‑fake content.
- It demonstrates the rapid amplification of AI‑generated celebrity material through gossip outlets and search engines.
- The case adds to a growing list of celebrity actions against non‑consensual AI portrayals, such as Scarlett Johansson’s legal team response to an OpenAI voice and Ariana Grande blocking an AI‑content account.
- Legislative context: the U.S. Take It Down Act (signed in 2025) criminalizes the distribution of non‑consensual intimate imagery, including AI‑generated content, and requires platforms to remove it within 48 hours.
- Together, these factors underscore the need for clearer verification standards and more aggressive enforcement against unauthorised AI‑generated likenesses.
