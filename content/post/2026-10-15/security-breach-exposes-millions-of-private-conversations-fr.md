# Security Breach Exposes Millions of Private Conversations from Eiliko Robot Toys

Energize Lab’s “Eiliko” digital pet robot has been found to have severe security vulnerabilities that expose private user data and conversations. While the AI companion tells users their secrets are safe, researchers discovered that the company stores millions of unprotected transcripts, personal profiles, and unencrypted photos on Chinese servers accessible to hackers.

## 🔍 Overview

Eiliko is a marshmallow-shaped robot designed to change its cartoony expressions to match a user's mood. However, conversations held over Wi-Fi are transmitted to servers in China where they are stored in a completely unprotected state. Researcher Azdoufal found that the service’s lack of security allowed access to 13 million conversations and the entire active worldwide fleet of 68,000 robot toys.

| Data Category | Details and Findings |
| :--- | :--- |
| **User Profiles** | Includes name, age, birthday, profession, family, friends, and personality assessments. |
| **Technical Data** | Emails, IP addresses, and searchable full-text databases of all user conversations. |
| **Media Storage** | Unencrypted photos of children and short audio recordings stored at public web addresses. |
| **Fleet Scale** | 68,000 active robots, with over 31,000 profiles belonging to children in the United States. |

## ⚙️ Key Details

*   **AI Profiling:** The service analyzes conversations to create psychological profiles. One log described a 52-year-old user as someone who "values friendship" but "has difficulty trusting family members."
*   **Vulnerability Reports:** Nine official CVE vulnerability reports are being published by Azdoufal and Tod Beardsley regarding Energize Lab’s security failures.
*   **Security Practices:** Many admin passwords used by the company were found on "most common passwords" lists.
*   **Corporate Response:** Energize Lab has not addressed the breach effectively, providing only robotic automated replies to repeated inquiries from researchers and journalists.

## 💡 Why it matters

Beyond data theft, the robot’s memory and Large Language Model (LLM) can be remotely hijacked. During testing, a researcher from across the globe successfully changed a toy's personality and memory. The hijacked robot began telling the user they had a different name, claimed their parents had died in an accident, and stated that the Earth is flat. While the LLM maintains some basic guardrails—such as warning against getting into a stranger's car—the ability to bypass its intended personality poses significant risks to users.
