# Google Antigravity and ProtNLM Accelerate Discovery of Novel Antimicrobial Peptide

Google Antigravity, an agentic science workbench, together with the ProtNLM model, enabled biologists to computationally identify a novel antimicrobial peptide candidate from *Streptococcus pseudopneumoniae*, which is now undergoing wet‑lab validation.

## 🔍 Overview
- Antimicrobial resistance could cause an estimated 10 million deaths annually by 2050.
- Antimicrobial peptides (AMPs), especially bacteriocins, are a promising alternative because they target fundamental bacterial vulnerabilities.
- The UniProt database contains millions of protein sequences with unknown function, constituting a large “dark matter” of potential AMPs.
- Google Antigravity provides a single‑session workbench that merges database search, structural analysis, and literature synthesis.
- ProtNLM predicts biological function directly from raw amino‑acid sequences, annotating uncharacterized UniProt entries.

## 🧩 How it works
- The researcher defines a focused goal: discover novel AMPs in *Streptococcus pseudopneumoniae*.
- In an interactive multi‑turn session, the AI agent queries UniProt, runs ProtNLM predictions, and retrieves structural models from the AlphaFold Database.
- The Biomolecular Structure Viewer visualizes 3D structures; the researcher can highlight residues, and the agent updates its reasoning accordingly.
- The agent synthesizes evidence, ranks candidates, and presents a biologically plausible lead for experimental testing.

## ⚙️ Key details
- **Agentic workflow**: Turns days of fragmented database triage into a single collaborative session.
- **ProtNLM**: Deep‑learning model trained on the evolutionary “grammar” of protein sequences to assign high‑confidence functional labels.
- **Integrated 3D viewer**: Shows structures, highlights functional regions, and synchronizes annotations back to the AI.
- **Cross‑database orchestration**: Automatically pulls data from UniProt, AlphaFold, and literature sources.
- **Outcome**: A novel AMP candidate identified and sent for wet‑lab validation.

## 💡 Why it matters
- Rising antimicrobial resistance is a global health crisis, underscoring the urgency for new therapeutics.
- AMPs like Nisin, Darobactin, Dynobactin, and Lariocidin have already demonstrated the potential of genome‑mined peptides.
- By rapidly annotating the “dark matter” of protein databases, Antigravity narrows the search space, reducing the resource‑intensive burden of wet‑lab validation.
- The platform lowers the barrier for everyday biological workflows, enabling researchers to explore diverse microbes and filtering criteria at scale.
