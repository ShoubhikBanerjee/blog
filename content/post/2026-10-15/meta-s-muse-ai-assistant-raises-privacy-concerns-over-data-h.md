# Meta's Muse AI Assistant Raises Privacy Concerns Over Data Harvesting

Meta’s recently released AI personal assistant, Muse, has sparked debate regarding the collection of user and third-party data. Since its release last month, the agent has reached No. 1 on Apple’s App Store with millions of downloads, utilizing a virtual avatar to request access to sensitive information including bank accounts, passwords, contact lists, and chat logs.

## 🧩 How it works

Muse is designed as an autonomous helper intended to perform menial tasks such as filling out invoices and sending emails. To function, the agent ingests large amounts of personal context, which Meta states is gathered from public information and data the user chooses to share. Security researchers have discovered that the agent maintains an internal process that updates profiles of every person in a user’s life on an hourly basis.

## ⚙️ Key details

*   **Data Scope:** The agent accesses emails, messages, contacts, and photos, often including information belonging to the user's friends and family.
*   **User Interface:** Critics suggest the interface uses dark patterns to encourage users to share more data than they may realize.
*   **Permissions:** Meta maintains that data access is opt-in. For example, the company stated that full disk access on a Mac is required for the agent to access text messages.
*   **Incidents:** Users have reported issues such as the exposure of a tech reviewer's home address and the potential for the agent to access text messages, which Meta attributes to misconfigured app permissions.

## 💡 Why it matters

Experts from the Signal Foundation and the Electronic Frontier Foundation have raised concerns that these AI agents extend mass surveillance to sensitive data. Because the agent processes information about the user's contacts, individuals who did not consent to using the service may still have their personal data ingested. There is currently no meaningful way for those individuals to opt out of this data collection process.
