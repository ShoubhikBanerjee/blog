# Benn Jordan Testifies on Flock Safety ALPR Surveillance

Benn Jordan, musician‑turned‑investigative journalist, has become a leading voice exposing the private‑sector surveillance network built around Flock Safety’s automatic license‑plate readers (ALPRs).

## 🔎 Overview
- Jordan has shifted from an experimental music career to investigating Flock Safety’s ALPR systems.
- His work has prompted congressional testimony, government scrutiny, and contract cancellations.

## 🎤 Who is Benn Jordan
- Musician, YouTuber, researcher, and investigative journalist.
- Known for a prolific discography of experimental music dating back decades.
- In the last couple of years, he has become a high‑profile face in creator and media spaces for his work investigating Flock Safety.

## 📡 Flock Safety’s ALPR Network
- Flock manufactures automatic license‑plate readers and the software that powers them.
- Atlanta has more Flock cameras than anywhere else per square mile.
- Jordan first noticed the cameras about two years ago, initially thinking they were traffic cameras.

## 🏛️ Congressional Testimony and Political Response
- Jordan recently testified before the Senate Judiciary Committee’s Subcommittee on Crime and Counterterrorism on Flock and other ALPR systems.
- Hundreds of cities, counties, and other local governments have begun canceling contracts with Flock.
- Republican Senator Josh Hawley has opened an investigation into Flock.

## 📉 Business Impact
- Reuters reported that Flock plans to cut about 18 percent of its workforce.

## 🛠️ Jordan’s Accountability Approach
- He does not hate Flock as a company; he says its posture toward critics is a PR failure.
- He talks directly to Flock’s CEO Garrett Langley and reports security vulnerabilities when he finds them.
- His YouTube videos and collaborations with other researchers, civil‑rights groups, and journalists serve as an accountability function that forces the conversation to the forefront.

## 📢 Why It Matters
- The issue extends beyond Flock to the interlocking relationship between private industry, government, and law enforcement that prioritizes secrecy over transparency.
- Lack of transparency allows a network of cameras to be deployed without public consent, raising concerns about abuse by police officers.
- Jordan emphasizes that society never voted for an insecure network of cameras on every street corner.
