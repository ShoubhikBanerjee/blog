# YouTube adds AI disclosure label to music tracks

YouTube is rolling out a “Made with AI” label for music tracks that were altered or fully generated using generative AI, beginning in the YouTube Music app and later expanding to the main YouTube app.

## 🔍 Overview
- The label appears on tracks that were “altered or fully generated” with AI.
- It mirrors YouTube’s existing process for applying AI labels to videos.
- The goal is to give listeners clear information about AI‑generated music.

## ⚙️ How it works
- Music partners must disclose generative AI use when uploading songs.
- If no disclosure is provided, YouTube may use “other signals” to decide if a song is AI‑generated.
- YouTube combines partner disclosures with its own detection tools to identify AI‑generated tracks.
- When AI played a significant role, a label is shown directly on the player page.
- Tapping the label opens a simple menu that shares more information.

## 🚀 Availability
- The label is rolling out starting today in the YouTube Music app.
- It will expand to the main YouTube app later.

## 📊 Industry context
| Platform      | AI Label Action |
|---------------|-----------------|
| Spotify       | Launched a voluntary AI label earlier this year and said it wouldn’t recommend songs from “AI personas.” |
| Apple Music   | Reportedly getting an AI tag. |
| Deezer        | Added a switch for filtering out AI tracks. |
| Bandcamp      | Banned AI music outright earlier this year. |

## 💡 Why it matters
- YouTube wants it to be as easy as possible for listeners to have the right information and clarity when it comes to the music they listen to.
- The label helps differentiate AI‑generated content from human‑created music, supporting informed choice.
