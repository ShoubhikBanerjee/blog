# US Elections Bring New Moratoriums on Data Centers, Challenging AI Industry Growth

Data center developers are poised to face a more restrictive building environment after voters across the country elected candidates who favor moratoriums and ballot measures that put more obstacles on their expansion, delivering a stinging blow to a central pillar of the AI industry.

## 🔍 Overview
- Midterm and general election outcomes showed a clear voter rejection of unrestrained data‑center development.
- Exit polls reported that **76 %** of voters opposed data centers in their communities.
- Voters were asked directly about data centers in **45** local ballot measures nationwide; **33** of the results will restrict development.

## 📊 Ballot Measures Impact
| Community | Restriction |
|------------|-------------|
| Town of Wells, Maine | 180‑day moratorium on large data centers, backdated to mid‑June 2026 |
| Several cities in Ohio | Ban on data centers larger than **25 MW** |
| City of El Dorado, Kansas | Ban on data centers with peak electrical load over **10 MW** for **10 years** |

## 🗳️ Election Results That Shift Policy
- **Gina Hinojosa** (Democratic) will take over the Texas governor’s seat from Republican incumbent Greg Abbott and supports a statewide moratorium on data‑center development.
- **Abdul El‑Sayed** (Democratic) won a Senate race in Michigan and calls for a pause on new data‑center developments until they can be done safely with federal protections.
- **Rob Sand** (Democratic) won the Iowa governor’s race and opposed a moratorium that his challenger favored.
- **Byron Donalds** (Republican) was elected governor of Florida, opposed an outright ban, but introduced the **Protecting Ratepayers Act** requiring developers to cover their own energy and water needs.
- **Josh Shapiro** (Democratic incumbent) retained the Pennsylvania governorship, signed an executive order barring nondisclosure agreements for data‑center projects and removed AI data‑center proposals from the fast‑track.
- **Sherrod Brown** (Democratic) reclaimed his Senate seat in Ohio, labeling former incumbent Jon Husted “the face of data centers.”
- **James Talarico** (Democratic) flipped a Texas Senate seat, both he and opponent Ken Paxton wanted to remove some tax exemptions for data centers; Talarico emphasized environmental impact, while Paxton focused on keeping Chinese tech out.
- **Jon Husted** (Republican) previously helped install data‑center tax breaks and later introduced the **Ratepayer Protection Act** in Congress to force large data centers to cover infrastructure upgrades and energy costs.
- **Stacy Garrity** (Republican challenger in Pennsylvania) supports a development pause of up to three years.
- **Mike Rogers** (Republican) was defeated by Abdul El‑Sayed, who had early pledged a pause on data‑center development.

## ⚙️ Policy Tools and Proposals
- **Protecting Ratepayers Act** – introduced by Rep. Byron Donalds, would make developers pay for their own energy and water needs.
- **Ratepayer Protection Act** – introduced by Jon Husted in Congress, aims to force large data centers to cover infrastructure upgrade and energy costs.
- Statewide moratoriums – advocated by Texas Gov‑elect Gina Hinojosa and Michigan Sen‑elect Abdul El‑Sayed.
- Audit of data‑center connections to state energy grids – announced by Texas Gov‑incumbent Greg Abbott.

## 💡 Why It Matters for AI
- Data centers are described as a “central pillar of the AI industry.”
- Restrictive local measures and new political leadership demanding moratoriums or higher cost responsibilities could slow the deployment of the massive compute infrastructure that modern AI models require.
- Legislative actions that shift cost burdens to developers may increase operational expenses for AI firms, potentially influencing where future AI workloads are sited.
