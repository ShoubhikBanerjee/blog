# Tech Giants Issue Multi‑Billion Dollar Bonds as AI Build‑Out Drives Debt Markets

Large technology firms are tapping debt markets with multi‑billion‑dollar bond offerings to fund AI projects, while leading investors flag the scale of financing as a potential bubble risk.

## 🔍 Overview
- Bloomberg reported a roundup of comments from the 2026 Global Financial Leaders’ Investment Summit in Hong Kong titled “Wall Street CEOs, Big Money Managers Divided Over AI Risks.”
- The “astronomically expensive AI buildout” is overwhelming debt markets and making it harder for companies to raise cash.

## 📊 Financing Scale
- PIMCO CEO Emmanuel Roman estimates the AI buildout could require **$2 trillion** in financing over the next five years.
- The total U.S. bond market is around **$12 trillion**.
- Wall Street banks were arranging **$63 billion** in debt for Anthropic, a non‑hyperscaler AI firm.

## ⚠️ Investor Concerns
- PIMCO’s Emmanuel Roman publicly warned that the financing surge could lead to a bubble, even as PIMCO sold a “bunch of its AI debt.”
- PIMCO was part of the first CoreWeave chip‑based financing in 2023, made a **$2 billion** paper gain from **$29 billion** in Meta bonds last year, and bought **$4 billion** of SpaceX bonds last month.
- Apollo Global Management’s President Jim Zelter said AI will need “dramatically more computing power,” but stressed uncertainty about pricing and economics: “What we don’t know is how pricing and economics will evolve.”
- Roman identified **U.S. rate hikes, war, or a eurozone crisis** as potential triggers that could flip the market.

## 📈 Market Context
- The huge order flow on Meta’s bonds suggests the market is not yet at its peak.
- Reports from China claim its AI bubble burst earlier.
- Despite the risks, financing activity remains vigorous, illustrated by the $63 billion in Anthropic debt and the large bond orders for Meta.
