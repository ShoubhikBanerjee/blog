# Google’s AI‑Mode Search Boosts Queries but Slows Ad Revenue Growth

Google reported that search queries are growing at their fastest rate in five years, but the monetization of those queries is lagging as the company rolls out AI‑Mode.

## 📈 Search Growth vs. Revenue
- Queries are up at the fastest pace in five years, according to Alphabet’s earnings report.
- Search revenue growth slowed to **12 % YoY in Q3**, down from roughly **17 %** in the prior quarter.
- Growth in paid clicks fell from **13 %** to **8 %**.

| Metric | Q3 YoY | Prior Quarter |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| Search revenue growth | 12 % | ~17 % |
| Paid‑click growth | 8 % | 13 % |

## 🤖 AI‑Mode Impact
- AI‑Mode queries are growing strongly but are **monetizing at a lower rate** than traditional searches.
- Chief Business Officer Philipp Schindler says most AI‑Mode queries are **"incremental"** – additional questions rather than replacements for existing searches.
- Many AI‑Mode queries are **upper‑funnel**, meaning users are researching potential purchases before they know exactly what they want.
- Google is expanding ads in **AI Overviews** and **AI‑Mode** internationally and across multiple turns of U.S. AI‑Mode conversations.
- The company has floated bringing ads to its **standalone Gemini app**, leveraging formats developed for AI‑Mode, though no timeline has been announced.

## 💰 Financial Reaction
- Alphabet’s shares have fallen about **14 %** since the earnings report, as investors question the costs and returns of the company’s AI ambitions.
- Alphabet plans to spend roughly **$200 billion** this year on infrastructure, largely to support AI.
- Schindler cites **currency effects** and **changing advertiser spending** in specific industries as the biggest drivers of the slowdown.

## 📊 Advertiser Perspective
- Tinuiti’s same‑store advertisers increased Google paid‑search spending **14 % YoY** in Q3 while maintaining their return‑on‑ad‑spend targets.
- Andy Taylor (Tinuiti) notes, “We’re still seeing a good return, and we’re not seeing performance take a hit.”
- However, Google does **not let advertisers separately target AI‑Mode inventory** or break out its performance.
- “If people are clicking through less often, that is a challenge that they need to figure out,” says an industry representative.

## 🛠️ Analyst Views & Open Questions
- Nate Elliott (eMarketer) calls this “the first major disruption that Google is facing to its core business in decades.”
- Elliott adds, “It’s not a slam dunk that it’s going to turn into more effective advertising.”
- An October working paper found that U.S. visits from **ChatGPT ads** generated **64 % less revenue per session** for retailers than visits from Google Search ads ($0.89 vs. $2.45). The paper measures retailer sales, not platform revenue, and does not establish how Google’s own AI ads perform.
- Both Google and competitors like OpenAI are still figuring out how to make ads work in AI contexts—delivering results for advertisers while keeping users satisfied.

## 🔍 Why It Matters
- AI is reshaping how people find information and whether they click on the ads that fund Google’s business.
- The shift toward AI‑Mode could alter the balance between query volume growth and ad revenue, influencing Google’s long‑term profitability and the broader digital‑advertising ecosystem.
