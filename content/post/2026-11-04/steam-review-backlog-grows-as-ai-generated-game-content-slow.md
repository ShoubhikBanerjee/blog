# Steam review backlog grows as AI-generated game content slows approvals

Indie developers are reporting unusually long wait times for Steam store and page approvals, causing missed launch dates.

## 🔍 Overview
Steam’s game‑store review process is currently behind schedule, with some developers waiting weeks for final approval. The delay is affecting indie launch plans.

## 📈 Reported Delays
- Studio Daimon’s *Food Devils* was scheduled for early‑access launch on the day of submission (Oct 12) but is still “awaiting approval” according to director Alfred Kamon.
- 2 Left Thumbs missed the planned launch of *Wheel Make It* by nearly two weeks because of a long build‑review wait.
- One developer has been waiting for weeks on approval for a game it planned to release today.
- Multiple other recent social‑media posts describe similar build‑review wait times.

## ⏳ Expected Review Times vs. Reality
- Valve’s developer site states a typical review takes **3‑5 business days**, and recommends submitting at least **7 business days** before the desired live date.
- In a new post, Valve acknowledges a “significant increase in submissions for store and page review” and says reviews are now taking **up to 12 business days**.

## 🤖 AI “slop” Impact
- Kamon and 2 Left Thumbs suspect the longer waits are due to “slop” slowing down the process and say, *“We can only assume it’s because Steam’s review queue is clogged up by AI slop, but the whole process is now taking several weeks for some developers.”*
- Valve notes that much of the delay is “having to re‑review games,” implying additional scrutiny of AI‑related content.
- A Reddit analysis found **49.9 %** of games released since Valve began requiring AI disclosure in 2024 now include an AI disclosure.

## 🚧 Platform Responses
- Valve has updated its release checklist, now requiring developers to run a game on Steam for at least five minutes, and says further improvements to the build‑review process are forthcoming.
- Itch.io reports a doubling of daily game‑page creations over the past two months and recently “deleted a couple terabytes of ai slop.”

## 📊 Summary of Affected Developers
| Developer / Studio | Game | Planned Launch | Reported Delay |
|--------------------|------|----------------|----------------|

These facts illustrate a growing bottleneck in Steam’s approval pipeline, linked to the surge in AI‑generated game content.
