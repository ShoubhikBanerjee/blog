# Uber reports only four rides and 500 deliveries placed by third‑party AI agents in Q3 2026

Uber disclosed that third‑party AI agents arranged just four rides and roughly 500 Uber Eats deliveries in the third quarter of 2026 – a minuscule share of its 320 million total rides and food‑delivery transactions.

## 🔍 Overview
- Analysts have warned that AI agents could disintermediate platforms like Uber and DoorDash, letting customers order food or book rides without opening the native app.
- Uber executives said the actual impact is still “very small” and that the company sees “very, very early” adoption of such agents.
- This is the first time Uber has published the numbers of rides and deliveries generated by third‑party AI agents.

## 📊 Scale
- **Total platform transactions:** 320 million rides and Uber Eats deliveries.
- **Rides arranged by AI agents:** 4 (Q3 2026).
- **Food deliveries arranged by AI agents:** ~500 (week of Halloween).

| Metric                          | Count (Q3 2026) |
|--------------------------------|-----------------|
| Rides from third‑party AI agents| 4               |
| Uber Eats deliveries from AI agents| ~500          |
| Total platform transactions      | 320 million     |

## 🗣️ Executive Perspective
- CEO Dara Khosrowshahi said, “We hope that there will be more traction.”
- He added, “We do think that we want to make services that are available to consumers in a delightful, easy way. We think our direct services are going to be the most delightful and easy, but we will work with these third parties.”
- Khosrowshahi also noted, “It is just very, very early, and we want to make sure we are not dependent on any one player, and I think we are very well positioned in this environment going forward.”
- Executives expressed openness to collaborating with third‑party AI companies while remaining confident that customers will continue preferring the Uber app directly.

## ⚖️ Why it matters
- Experts caution that the “first wave of AI agents still have a long way to go before people begin seeing them as a viable way to order food or book a ride.”
- There are “a lot of friction points,” and platform operators like Uber have “little incentive to allow these agents to barge in and replace their own customers.”
- Nonetheless, AI companies view platform ordering as a primary use case and will likely keep pursuing disintermediation, even though “getting Uber to agree to more disintermediation will be a tough ask.”

## 🤖 Uber’s own AI response
- Earlier this year Uber launched a **Cart Assistant** for Uber Eats, an AI bot that can build a grocery list from an image of a recipe or a text prompt, aiming to keep the ordering experience within Uber’s own ecosystem.
