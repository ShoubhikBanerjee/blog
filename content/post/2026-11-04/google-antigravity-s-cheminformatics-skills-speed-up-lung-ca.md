# Google Antigravity’s Cheminformatics Skills Speed Up Lung‑Cancer Drug Design

Designing new medicines typically takes multiple days for a single iteration, requiring curating complex data from charts, figures and literature, training predictive models, and proposing candidate molecules to synthesize.

## 🔍 Overview
- Google Antigravity, equipped with new Cheminformatics Skills, accelerates drug discovery workflows.
- In a real‑world lung cancer treatment scenario, an agent searches through thousands of compounds, performs matched molecular pair analysis, and proposes novel rigidified macrocycles with synthetic routes in just an hour.

## 🧩 How it works
- **Division of labour**: the language model handles workflow orchestration, database navigation and scientific reasoning, while deterministic chemistry tools execute molecular calculations.
- **Data integration**: built on RDKit and connectors to curated resources from ChEMBL and PubChem, the skills unify millions of structures from journal articles and vendor catalogs and encode domain best practices.
- **Interactive workbench**: the agent extracts molecules from literature, associates them with reported data, launches an interactive viewer with customizable scatter plots (e.g., potency vs. rotatable bonds, colored by macrocycle status).

## ⚙️ Key details
- **Rigidification**: adding a second bond to tie two substructures sharply limits conformational freedom, "paying the entropic cost in advance" and can dramatically boost potency.
- **Case study**: crizotinib (first‑generation TKI) has five major rotatable bonds; lorlatinib (third‑generation TKI) is cyclized to reduce the effective number of rotatable bonds to zero, leading to higher potency.
- **Matched molecular pair analysis** compared many pairs that differ by cyclization; results show cyclization reduces rotatable bonds while generally increasing potency.
- The agent also built a large number of potency models using varying feature sets and model architectures, validated them on a held‑out set.

## 💡 Why it matters
- Chemistry records are rich but error‑prone; a single atom or bond change describes a different compound, making accuracy essential.
- By handling data wrangling and orchestration, the Cheminformatics Skills let scientists focus on hypotheses rather than manual preprocessing.
- Accelerating the workflow from days to an hour opens the possibility of faster iteration cycles in medicinal chemistry.
