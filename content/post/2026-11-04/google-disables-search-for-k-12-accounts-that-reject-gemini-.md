# Google disables Search for K‑12 accounts that reject Gemini AI

Google announced that any K‑12 school that opts out of its Gemini AI suite will lose access to Google Search. The policy applies automatically unless administrators explicitly enable the AI features.

## 🔍 Overview
- Access to Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Google Search will be completely off for student accounts unless turned on by administrators.
- Students in schools that reject the AI will see a notice that Search is unavailable.
- Administrators cannot turn off Gemini‑powered features without also disabling Search.

## ⚙️ Key details
| Service | Availability for K‑12 student accounts |
|---------|-------------------------------------------|
| Gemini AI | Off by default; must be turned on by admin |
| Gemini Notebook | Off by default; must be turned on by admin |
| Gemini in Classroom | Off by default; must be turned on by admin |
| Google Search | Off unless any Gemini feature is enabled |

- Chris Phillips, Google’s vice‑president of education, said Search and AI are deeply integrated, so disabling AI removes Search access.
- Google’s official statement claims the change “gives schools more choice, not less,” and notes that schools can toggle each service individually.
- The update follows a prior rollout that set Gemini AI on by default inside Google Classroom, a platform used by more than 150 million teachers and students worldwide.

## 🗣️ Reactions
- Sen. Bernie Sanders labeled the move “extortion.”
- NYC Mayor Zohran Mamdani warned that no corporation should dictate how children are taught.
- Rohit Chopra, California’s Secretary of Business & Consumer Services, argued that Big Tech should not be able to “strong‑arm schools.”
- Lawmakers had earlier pressed Google on the “potential harms” of AI in classrooms.
- Google did not immediately respond to a request for comment from The Verge.

## 💡 Why it matters
- The policy adds to growing skepticism about AI tools in education, amid reports of declining test scores and concerns that AI may encourage shortcuts.
- Google is testing a “Guided Learning” feature embedded in AI Overviews in Search, designed to help students work through problems rather than rely on shortcuts. Testing begins in select U.S. schools this month.

## 📅 Timeline
- Announcement made in a broader education update (Tuesday).
- Update added on November 3 with additional statements from lawmakers and Google.
- Guided Learning testing starts later this month.

*The situation remains fluid as schools weigh the trade‑off between AI capabilities and access to basic search functionality.*
