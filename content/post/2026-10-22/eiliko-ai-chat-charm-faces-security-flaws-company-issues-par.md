# Eiliko AI Chat Charm Faces Security Flaws, Company Issues Partial Fixes

Eiliko, a cute robot charm you can hang on your bag with built‑in AI conversations, turned out to be a security nightmare.

## 🔍 Overview
- The device tells owners that “your secrets are safe with me.”
- The Verge and security researcher Sammy Azdoufal spent over a month trying to contact Energize Lab’s CEO and founder Shaw Yeung, but “nobody would answer our questions.”

## ⚠️ Security Issues
- Hackers could easily read complete transcripts of everything spoken to the toy.
- The company was still storing audio recordings and photos of customers at unsecured public web addresses with no protection whatsoever.
- A link to an owner’s face could be accessed directly without authentication.
- Azdoufal still has full SSH access to the robots and can theoretically read transcripts and modify the toy’s behavior.
- Claims that “closed all external access” do not appear accurate.

## 🛠️ Company Response
- Energize Lab confirmed “the core issues identified in your reporting and are addressing each of them.”
- The firm will hire an independent security firm to conduct a full review of its systems.
- A dedicated inbox, **security@energizelab.com**, has been set up and is staffed and monitored.
- As of Wednesday the company says it has “closed all external access to our backend systems” and “disabled the interface that allowed a robot’s responses to be modified remotely.”
- It pledges to “no longer store conversation records or the user profiles generated from them,” and will delete the conversation records, user profiles, and “avatar images” already stored.
- The long‑term memory feature has been taken offline.
- The app now offers built‑in avatars only.
- “The long‑term memory and custom avatar features will not return until that review confirms they are secure.”
- The company apologized, stating “we know that words alone will not earn that trust back” and that it intends to earn trust “through what we do in the coming weeks.”

## 📌 Ongoing Concerns
- The company is not promising to eliminate data collection permanently.
- Despite the announced shutdown of external access, security researcher Azdoufal confirms the systems remain insecure.
- The long‑term memory and custom avatar features may be re‑enabled after the security review, implying future personal data storage.
