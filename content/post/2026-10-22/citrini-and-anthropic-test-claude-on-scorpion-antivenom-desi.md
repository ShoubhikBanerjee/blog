# Citrini and Anthropic Test Claude on Scorpion Antivenom Design

An investment research firm wanted to prove AI could revolutionize drug development. They teamed up with Anthropic to see whether an unreleased version of Claude could make scorpion antivenom. The effort did not produce a drug.

## 🔍 Overview
- Investment research firm (Citrini Research) wanted to prove AI could revolutionize drug development.
- Partnered with Anthropic, weeks before its IPO, to access an unreleased Claude model.
- Goal: use Claude to design proteins for a scorpion antivenom.

## 🧪 Experiment Design
- Team built a 35‑page prompt and launched about 300 subagents to discover suitable proteins.
- Anthropic reported Claude generated nearly 320,000 candidates, narrowed to 180 designs after discarding those resembling known toxins or existing proteins.
- Anthropic added 31 designs, total 211.
- Process took 64 hours.
- Compute and token bill just shy of $50,000, covered by Anthropic.
- Designs sent to Swiss biotech Adaptyv Bio and US‑based Twist Biosciences for testing, costing about $150,000.

## ⚙️ Results
- Seventeen proteins passed muster, collectively binding to four of the five scorpion toxins the team hoped to target.
- No protein bound more than a single toxin.
- Binding to a toxin does not mean the protein can neutralize it; Citrini notes “no experiment has yet tested whether they actually stopped the toxin working.”
- Experts tell The Verge the AI “couldn’t” make a drug and “came up with some promising starting points, but nothing close to an actual drug.”

## 💭 Takeaways
- Citrini admitted “none of us could claim to know anything about AI as it related to biology.”
- The experiment shows that, as of now, Claude could not create a functional antivenom from scratch.
