# Celonis chief scientist stresses operational context for scaling enterprise AI

Enterprise AI is moving from experimental pilots toward everyday operations, but leaders are discovering that without operational context, AI agents often miss critical business constraints. Devavrat Shah, chief scientist of enterprise AI at Celonis, calls the needed approach “decision intelligence.”

## 🔍 Overview
- Enterprise AI can generate recommendations, automate tasks, and help organizations make decisions faster.\
- Moving AI beyond experimentation requires connecting agents to operational context and identifying the business processes where they can deliver the greatest value.\
- For Devavrat Shah, chief scientist of enterprise AI at Celonis, that context is central to what he calls “decision intelligence.”

## ⚙️ How Contextual AI Works
- The solution begins with connecting AI to the processes and data enterprises already use.\
- A contextual layer can help agents understand workflows, operate within defined guardrails, and produce recommendations that people can review and approve.\
- AI agents can support individual tasks within larger decision‑making processes, but they need to understand the role each task plays and the business conditions surrounding it.\
> "You can't just do that in a vacuum," he says.

## 📈 Benefits & Practical Steps
**Benefits**
- Faster, more accurate decision making.\
- Automation of repetitive, manual work.\
- Coordination of tasks across teams, freeing staff for more complex strategic decisions.\
- With the right context, AI could help organizations evaluate options and make consequential decisions more quickly.

**Implementation steps (Shah’s advice)**
1. Start with a specific business decision.\
2. Identify its most difficult steps.\
3. Test whether AI can improve those steps.\
4. Measure gains in speed and value to decide if scaling is worthwhile.

## 🚧 Risks Without Context
- Even powerful models can miss the constraints and relationships that determine whether a decision is the right one.\
- Without the right operational context, agents risk overlooking important constraints, making costly mistakes, or consuming excessive computing resources as they try to work out what they need to know.\
- Applying generalized principles to specialized scenarios leads to performance deficits, leaving business leaders unconvinced and AI's potential unrealized.

## 🔮 Future Outlook
- Shah sees opportunities extending from routine operational tasks to enterprise‑wide planning in finance, workforce management, marketing, and sales.\
- With the right context, AI could reduce the time spent on resource planning and help people make the right choices quickly.\
> "Now, maybe [resource planning] should not take that much time, and people are actually making the right choices quickly."
