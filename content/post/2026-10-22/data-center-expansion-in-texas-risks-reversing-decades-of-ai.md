# Data Center Expansion in Texas Risks Reversing Decades of Air Quality Gains

New research from the Houston Advanced Research Center (HARC) indicates that a surge of data centers in Texas could return air pollution to levels not seen since the 2000s. As Texas becomes a primary hub for generative AI infrastructure, the massive electricity demands of these facilities threaten to erase decades of air quality progress.

## ⚙️ Key details
Texas currently leads the data center market with 26 gigawatts of existing and under-construction capacity. According to a March Wood Mackenzie report, Texas is also preparing for four times more proposed data center capacity running on onsite power generation than any other state.

## 💡 Why it matters
The environmental impact depends on how developers and utilities meet electricity demands. A HARC report warns that if all planned projects use the anticipated grid mix, the results could be severe:

* **Fine particle pollution (soot):** Could more than triple, reaching the highest level since 2006.
* **Smog-forming NOx emissions:** Could increase 136 percent, approaching 2004 levels.
* **Carbon dioxide pollution:** Could climb 90 percent above 2025 levels.

John Hall, HARC CEO, stated, “There is a good chance that our children and grandchildren will breathe dirtier air than we did,” adding that progress made by the state, the transportation sector, and other stakeholders is now at risk.

## 🧩 How it works
The level of pollution is determined by the power sources chosen by developers:

| Power Source | Environmental Impact |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Onsite Renewables** | NOx emissions could be up to 10 times lower when paired with grid power. |
| **Grid Mix** | High levels of soot and smog-forming pollutants. |
| **Onsite Gas Engines** | Potential for the highest pollution levels possible; often used to avoid grid connection wait times. |
| **Gas Turbines** | Less efficient technology used to speed construction, increasing pollution. |
