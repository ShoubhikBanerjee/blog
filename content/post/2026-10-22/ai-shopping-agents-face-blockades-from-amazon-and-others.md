# AI Shopping Agents Face Blockades from Amazon and Others

AI agents are ready to shop, but their real‑world rollout is hitting a wall of blocks and odd user experiences.

## 🔍 Overview
- Meta, Google, OpenAI, and others have introduced agentic shopping tools that promise to make buying things even easier than it already is.
- Demos show AI agents that can seamlessly book flights and hotels, browse billions of products to find the right one, and secure tickets to sporting events on a human’s behalf.

## 🤖 How the agents work
- The agents require an inordinate amount of instructions and specifications.
- In practice, using an AI agent to shop is described as “a strange experience” and likened to “sending an anxious and apologetic teenager out to the store.”
- They often complete a task, but not the specific one asked, and not in the way a human would have approached it.

## 🚧 Roadblocks & Blocking
- **Amazon** – the most prominent retailer refusing AI agents. Amazon sued Perplexity for ignoring its request to stop the agent from shopping on the site and more recently blocked Meta’s Muse agent from shopping on Amazon.
- **JPMorgan Chase** – CEO Jamie Dimon said the bank wouldn’t allow outside agents to access accounts, calling it “too risky.”
- **Delta & Amtrak** – some users report they’ve been unable to use agents to book trips from these providers.
- **Target** – Muse was caught in Target’s bot check one day but got through the next; later Muse was blocked again when asked for mattress recommendations, despite being able to pull from the site minutes earlier. Target has AI shopping partnerships with OpenAI, Google, and Microsoft.
- General bot‑blocking systems can make agents inconsistent, reducing usefulness for the tasks tech companies tout as the future.

## 💬 Real‑world experiences
- When asked for the best per‑piece granola‑bar price, Muse offered options from Sam’s Club, Walmart, and Costco, but noted that Amazon might have had the lowest price, yet it was “hard‑blocked” from browsing so Muse couldn’t confirm.
- Muse suggested a listing from a grocery chain that doesn’t ship to the user’s address while searching for the best price on granola bars.
- On The RealReal, a subscription gives shoppers early access to new items; placing an item in the cart reserves it for 20 minutes. The author instructed Muse to bulk‑add the newest 10 listings from a favorite brand, effectively blocking other shoppers.
- Even when they work, the agents “suggest a more annoying future for humans.”

## 🛒 Amazon’s own agentic feature
- Amazon runs its own on‑platform agentic shopping feature that monitors prices, automatically reorders items, and purchases products based on rules set by human users.
- A separate Amazon feature that uses an agent to purchase products from other sites without leaving Amazon angered smaller businesses, who say they didn’t consent to having their storefronts scraped and featured on Amazon.
- Amazon spokesperson Maxine Tagay confirmed the company “requested that Meta remove Amazon” from Muse and stated that third‑party applications “should operate openly and respect service provider decisions about whether or not to participate.”

## 🔮 Why it matters
- AI agents are getting blocked left and right, indicating a clash between the promise of democratized labor and the technical, legal, and business realities of e‑commerce platforms.
- The friction observed—technical problems, frequent blocking, and unintended consequences like reserving inventory for a bot—highlights the challenges ahead for making agentic shopping a seamless part of everyday life.
