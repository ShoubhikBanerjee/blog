# The Seattle Times and Newsday Sue OpenAI and Microsoft Over Copyright Infringement

The Seattle Times and Newsday have filed a lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft, alleging copyright infringement. The publishers are the latest in a series of media organizations to take legal action against the technology companies over the unauthorized use of their intellectual property.

## 🔍 Overview

The two news outlets state that OpenAI used their journalism as training data for its artificial intelligence models without permission. Additionally, the suit alleges that the AI models frequently reproduce passages of their reporting in response to user queries. Microsoft is named as a defendant alongside OpenAI because its Copilot assistant is built on OpenAI's technology.

## ⚙️ Key details

This legal action follows similar lawsuits and industry-wide concerns:
* Similar copyright lawsuits have previously been filed against OpenAI by The New York Times, Ziff Davis, Merriam-Webster, and Encyclopedia Britannica.
* The Seattle Times and Newsday join a group of nearly 400 local newspapers that recently sued both OpenAI and Microsoft.
* The publishers argue that chatbots reduce user traffic to their websites for reporting and answers, costing them valuable subscription revenue.
* OpenAI and Microsoft did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

## 💡 Why it matters

The plaintiffs are seeking severe remedies that could impact how these AI models operate. Specifically, The Seattle Times and Newsday are seeking:
* The destruction of any copies of their works currently held by the companies.
* The destruction of training datasets that incorporate their works.
* The destruction of the AI models built on and incorporating their works.
