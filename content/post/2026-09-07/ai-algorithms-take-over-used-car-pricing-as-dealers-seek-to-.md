# AI Algorithms Take Over Used Car Pricing as Dealers Seek to Protect Margins

Automated pricing algorithms are beginning to play a larger role in setting prices for used cars, replacing traditional manual methods with data-driven decision-making. As dealers navigate rising vehicle prices, tightening affordability, and slim margins, these automated systems cut prices before a vehicle goes cold online and quietly loses money.

## 🔍 Overview

The average list price of a used car today is above $27,000. Highlighting this trend, Carvana's most recent quarterly report showed its average retail price rising by more than $4,000 between the first and second quarters of this year. Tightening affordability makes precise pricing discipline essential, yet many dealers still wait too long to adjust prices and cut them too severely once a vehicle has gone cold.

Historically, dealers relied on instinct and spreadsheets to catch these shifts. Today, automated pricing serves as a key test of whether artificial intelligence can turn everyday retail discipline into profit.

## 🧩 How it Works

Cox Automotive frames the transition to automated pricing as an evolution rather than a machine takeover. Depending on the dealership, automated pricing takes one of two primary forms:

* **Rules-Based Automation**: The dealer establishes the guardrails, including when the first price change occurs, the size of the discount, and how often the vehicle is repriced. The system adjusts prices within these rules and can pause scheduled discounts if the market day supply jumps or if the vehicle starts generating leads.
* **Fully Autonomous Pricing**: The system recommends an opening price and manages the entire cycle from appraisal to inventory to final sale. It uses data science to continuously adjust the price until the vehicle sells.

Even with autonomous systems, dealer judgment remains central. According to Patrick Janes, assistant vice president of vAuto Inventory Solutions at Cox Automotive, customers still want to choose or approve the opening price, track and modify prices as market conditions change, and let the system manage the exceptions.

## ⚖️ The Debate

The expanding role of algorithms in pricing has opened a broader debate regarding where automation should stop.

* **Opposing Views**: Urs Buscke, Senior Legal Officer at the European Consumer Organisation (BEUC), argues that automated pricing crosses a line.
* **Regulatory Measures**: Several U.S. states have already begun banning surveillance pricing. In the European Union, BEUC argues that the upcoming Digital Fairness Act should implement similar bans.

## 💡 Why It Matters

Implementing automated pricing requires trust and a clear understanding of a dealership's current pricing practices. However, successful automation can significantly improve operational efficiency. Used car managers typically handle a wide array of tasks simultaneously, including:
* Pricing
* Merchandising
* Vehicle preparation and reconditioning
* Sales oversight

Patrick Janes notes that dealers want technology that helps them sell more cars, protect margins, and free up manager time. If AI can handle pricing more efficiently, dealership staff can redirect their focus toward other value-adding tasks.
