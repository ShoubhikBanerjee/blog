# Google Researcher Uses AI Agents to Find Maya Site, Then Discovers It Was Already Known

Google researcher Pietro Miotti recently claimed to have discovered a potentially unmapped Maya site in Mexico using AI agents and lidar technology. However, subsequent verification revealed the site had already been documented in an academic journal.

## 🔍 Overview

*   **Researcher:** Pietro Miotti, a Google researcher.
*   **Goal:** To find undocumented Maya sites in Mexico.
*   **Initial Finding:** A major village that appeared not to be registered in existing records.
*   **Government Response:** Mexico’s National Institute of Anthropology and History (INAH) confirmed they had no record of a registered site at that location.
*   **Correction:** The site was already discovered and published in an academic journal more than a year earlier.

## 🤖 Methodology

*   **Technology:** Lidar (laser-based mapping) to produce detailed three-dimensional images.
*   **Agents:** A team of 48 AI agents.
*   **Data Source:** NASA lidar data.
*   **Process:** Agents combed through data for signs of undocumented sites and translated correspondence into Spanish.
*   **Timeline:** The process took less than 72 hours from a random thought to an official government reply.

## ⚠️ Verification and Correction

*   **Misconception:** Miotti noted that not being registered in a database does not mean a site has not been mapped.
*   **AI Limitations:** Miotti stated, "My agents and I missed it." He added that he had not realized a site not being registered wasn't the same as it not having been mapped.
*   **Verification Process:** Archaeologists do not publish the coordinates of sites. Therefore, checking whether a site is already known requires comparing images against figures in papers, not searching a database.

## 💡 Expert Perspectives

*   **Sarah Parcak:** Stressed the importance of peer review. She noted that just because the Mexican government doesn't have something in its database doesn't mean it is unknown or unmapped.
*   **Elizabeth Graham:** Stated that the "problem" is not discovering new sites, as most Maya sites are known but unexplored. She highlighted that local communities are often aware of sites and that funding is the bigger obstacle to research.
