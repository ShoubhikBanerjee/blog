# UN diplomats agree to begin discussions on autonomous weapon regulations

During a week‑long UN diplomatic meeting in Geneva, representatives discussed rules for autonomous weapons, often called “killer robots.” The talks ended without a binding agreement, but countries agreed to “start discussing options” and set a two‑year window to propose regulations.

## 🔍 Overview
- The agenda included working towards rules regarding the use of autonomous weapons.
- Negotiations were largely fruitless, but a compromise was reached to begin discussions.

## 📅 Timeline & Options
- Countries will have **two years** to propose regulations.
- Proposed regulations may include **optional** provisions.
- Negotiations on the content and enforcement are expected to extend well beyond the two‑year period.

## 🤝 Key Players & Obstructions
- **International Committee of the Red Cross** urged legally binding rules within a year.
- The call was backed by the **U.N. secretary‑general** and **Pope Leo**.
- **Russia** delayed proceedings by objecting to the meeting’s agenda.
- The **United States** proposed a five‑year window for nonbinding, optional guidelines.

## 📈 Why it matters
- Humanitarian organizations and some countries argue urgent legally binding rules are needed because semi‑autonomous weapons and drones have caused civilian deaths in **Ukraine, Gaza, and Sudan**.
- Without clear rules, the risk of civilian casualties from “killer robots” remains high.
