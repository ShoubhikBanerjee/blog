# Cleverbridge Breach Exposes Customer Payment Card Data

Cleverbridge, an e-commerce platform that provides services to software and SaaS companies, disclosed that a security breach resulted in the theft of customers' payment card information. The stolen data includes credit card numbers and CVV security codes.

## 🧩 Company Background

Cleverbridge enables software and SaaS companies to sell products, process payments, and manage subscriptions. The platform lists numerous technology brands among its customers, including:

* Red Hat
* SUSE
* Tenable
* Veeam
* Jamf
* Sony
* Parallels

## 🔍 Incident Timeline

According to a security incident disclosure, the company detected unauthorized activity on November 4, 2026. Systems were temporarily taken offline to contain the breach and were brought back online the same day. The investigation subsequently determined that a third party copied payment card data stored on the platform.

Affected customers began receiving notifications on November 20, 2026.

## ⚙️ Data Stolen

A breach notification shared with BleepingComputer reveals that the stolen information includes:

* Payment card numbers
* Expiration dates
* CVV/CVC security codes

The notification explicitly states that no other personal data, such as names, contact details, or billing information, were copied. The notice specifies that the affected card ends in a placeholder sequence.

## 🚫 What Was Not Involved

Cleverbridge confirmed that the incident was not a ransomware attack. Attackers did not encrypt any of the company's systems. The company has not disclosed how the attackers gained access, how long they remained in the systems, or the total number of affected customers.

## 💡 Response and Remediation

Cleverbridge has notified data protection authorities, law enforcement, payment card networks, and payment partners regarding the incident. The company is working with TransUnion's Cyberscout to distribute breach notifications and provide affected customers with complimentary credit monitoring and fraud assistance. Customers are advised to review their payment card statements for any unauthorized transactions.
