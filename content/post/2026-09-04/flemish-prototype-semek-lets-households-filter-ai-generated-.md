# Flemish prototype Semek lets households filter AI-generated online content

A new Flemish prototype called **Semek** has been installed between the internet and the home Wi‑Fi network, giving users direct control over which online content reaches their devices.

## 🔍 Overview
- The device aims to let users decide which online content they see.
- It can label, blur, or block AI‑generated images.
- Explicit images or information from certain sources can also be prevented from passing through.

## 🧩 How it works
- Semek sits between the internet connection and the home Wi‑Fi router.
- All analysis is performed locally on the device using a specialised AI chip.
- Personal data does **not** need to be sent to an external AI cloud for processing.

## ⚙️ Key features
- Users can set which content is allowed through or filtered out.
- The device can label, blur, or block AI‑generated images.
- It can block explicit images or information from specified sources.
- Local processing demonstrates that dependence on foreign tech companies is not inevitable.

## 💡 Why it matters
> "AI should give users more control over which information is relevant to them, rather than leaving that selection entirely to platforms, providers, or algorithms." – Verborgh

> "With local processing, we aim to demonstrate that dependence on American and other tech companies does not have to be inevitable," says Aaltra CEO Pieter‑Paulus Vertongen.
