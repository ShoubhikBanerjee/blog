# New Survey Highlights Gaps in AI‑Driven Data Center Energy Optimization

Data centers are increasingly optimized by artificial intelligence and, at the same time, increasingly loaded by it. A newly submitted paper systematically surveys the literature, uncovers methodological gaps, and proposes a unifying framework.

## 🔍 Overview
- Title: *Artificial Intelligence for Energy Optimization in Data Centers* (submitted 3 Sep 2026).
- Scope: Examines the disconnect between control‑oriented studies (which treat workload as exogenous) and sustainability studies (which treat infrastructure as a fixed multiplier).
- Method: Screened ~194 papers via a documented protocol, fully coded 63 papers, and derived statistics and a reporting schema.

## 📊 Key Findings
- **Study composition**: 28 primary control‑oriented studies identified.
  - 18 validated **only in simulation**.
  - 5 reached **physical hardware or a production facility**.
  - **None** accounted for **water withdrawal** or **embodied carbon**.
- **Performance reporting**: Savings intervals across four technique families overlap almost completely, preventing the field from ranking methods.
- **Identified gaps**: Ten recurring gaps were scored for consequence and tractability.

## 🛠️ Proposed Framework – CLEAR‑DC
CLEAR‑DC is an **architectural and methodological proposal** (not a trained system) that:
- Couples a **control‑policy branch** to a **workload‑demand branch** via an explicit **elasticity term**.
- Outputs **net benefit** rather than direct benefit.
- Emits a **schema‑conformant record** covering:
  - Energy
  - Carbon
  - Water
  - Embodied share
  - Validation venue
- Serves as a **reporting schema** derived from the corpus analysis.

## 📑 Contribution
- The primary empirical contribution is the **corpus analysis** and the **reporting schema** derived from it.
- All coding sheets, derived statistics, and result artifacts are made available at the provided URL.

## 💡 Why It Matters
- Highlights the **lack of holistic sustainability metrics** in current AI‑driven data‑center optimization research.
- Provides a **structured pathway** for future studies to capture comprehensive environmental impacts.
- Offers a **common reporting format** that can improve comparability across techniques.
