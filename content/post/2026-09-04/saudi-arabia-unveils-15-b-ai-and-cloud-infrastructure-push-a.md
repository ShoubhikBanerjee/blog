# Saudi Arabia unveils $15 B AI and cloud infrastructure push at LEAP 2026

At LEAP 2026 in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia announced a multi‑billion‑dollar surge of AI‑related investments, partnerships, and infrastructure launches, signaling the Kingdom’s move into a new phase of its technology strategy.

## 🔍 Overview
- LEAP 2026 highlighted the Kingdom’s expanding digital economy across cloud, AI, telecom, venture capital, and skills development.
- By the end of the opening day, more than **$15 billion** in AI and advanced‑technology launches, investments, and partnerships had been announced.
- The initiative is framed as part of a broader, ambitious technology strategy built around artificial intelligence, advanced infrastructure, national talent, and an investment ecosystem.

## 💰 Investment totals by day
- **Day 1:** > $15 B across AI, cloud, data centers, semiconductor, energy, and industry‑specific applications.
- **Day 2:** > $2.5 B aimed largely at expanding data‑center capacity and advanced‑technology infrastructure.
- **Day 3:** $857 M in technology and manufacturing investments, including cloud infrastructure and data‑center manufacturing projects.

## ☁️ Cloud & AI infrastructure milestones
- **AWS Saudi Cloud Region** – on track to launch **December 2026**, representing **$5.3 B** in planned cloud‑infrastructure investment.
- The region will let developers, startups, government entities, and companies run workloads from Saudi data‑centers.
- **AWS‑HUMAIN partnership** – plans up to **50 MW** of capacity in Saudi Arabia’s first AI Zone by **2028**, using AWS Trainium chips and NVIDIA infrastructure.
- **HUMAIN’s Arabic LLM “ALLAM”** to become available via **Amazon Bedrock**; the HUMAIN Fabric data platform will be offered through **AWS Marketplace**.

## 🤝 Partnerships and platform expansions
- **DataVolt + HUMAIN** – jointly developing **100 MW** of the first **360 MW** phase of an AI‑ready data centre at Oxagon in NEOM; the first 100 MW expected **2028**.
- **AMD, Cisco, HUMAIN** – production AI computing infrastructure now live, powered by AMD Instinct MI355X GPUs, AMD EPYC CPUs, and Cisco networking. Plans for an additional **250 MW** beginning **2027**, targeting **1 GW** of AI infrastructure by **2030**.
- **HUMAIN AI Cloud** – powered by NVIDIA HGX B300 Blackwell Ultra systems, currently delivering **1.1 MW** of operational AI capacity with >90 % utilization; slated to expand to **35.1 MW** by **2027**.
- **Strategic investment in Arabic.AI and Tarjama** – to boost Arabic‑language translation, document intelligence, and enterprise AI capabilities (translation management, digitizing legacy archives, AI agents for contracts, tenders, filings).
- **Reflection AI partnership** – deploying Reflection’s first generally available model on HUMAIN infrastructure later this year, with joint work on model customization, sovereign AI deployment, and compute for future training.
- **KORA Systems collaboration** – co‑developing **HUMAIN OS**, an AI‑native Linux operating system that interprets user intent to select the appropriate model, application, or environment.

## 📊 Capacity roadmap
| Initiative | Capacity (MW) | Target year | Notes |
|------------|--------------|------------|------|
| AWS AI Zone (with HUMAIN) | 50 | 2028 | Uses AWS Trainium chips & NVIDIA infrastructure |
| HUMAIN AI Cloud (NVIDIA) | 1.1 (operational) → 35.1 | 2027 | Current utilization >90 % |
| DataVolt‑HUMAIN Oxagon phase 1 | 100 (first tranche) | 2028 | Part of 360 MW NEOM AI‑ready data‑center phase |
| AMD/Cisco/HUMAIN joint venture | up to 250 additional (on top of existing) | 2027 | Aim for up to 1 GW AI infrastructure by 2030 |

## 🌍 Why it matters
- The Kingdom’s technology ambitions are framed around measurable impact on people: better connectivity and faster digital services.
- Building AI‑ready infrastructure, localized large‑language models, and an ecosystem of partners positions Saudi Arabia to develop home‑grown AI products and companies with global reach.
- The massive investment in cloud and AI hardware creates a foundation for talent development, venture capital activity, and downstream industry applications across energy, manufacturing, and services.
