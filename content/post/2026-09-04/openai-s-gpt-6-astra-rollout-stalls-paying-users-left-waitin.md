# OpenAI’s GPT‑6 Astra rollout stalls, paying users left waiting

OpenAI launched GPT‑6 Astra, but the rollout left paying subscribers locked out.

## 🔍 Overview
- The company hailed the model as a “generational leap in capability” on Thursday and described it as the start of “the AGI era.”
- CEO Sam Altman apologized for a “messy rollout” and acknowledged users were left waiting.

## ⚙️ Key details
- Astra was first rolled out to some enterprise customers — specifically those with access to its Daybreak cybersecurity platform — that day.
- The rollout was scheduled to expand over the next few days to **all Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise users**, as well as through the OpenAI API, Microsoft Azure, and AWS Bedrock.
- The staggered release that appeared to prioritize business customers annoyed many paid subscribers, especially those on the more expensive Pro plan who are accustomed to getting access to new products first.
- Altman posted on X: “We are working towards getting Astra in everyone’s hands as quickly as we can.”
- He added: “I know it is frustrating and I appreciate the patience. It should be quick.”
- No clear timeline was given; Altman later wrote, “I am hopeful that you can use it this weekend! but can’t promise yet.”

## 🚀 Availability
- Paying users reported being locked out despite having paid ChatGPT plans.
- Codex engineering lead Thibault Sottiaux said, “We will give one banked reset for every day you don’t have access to Astra on your paid ChatGPT plan, starting today.”
- Altman’s posts suggest access may not happen before the weekend, and possibly not even then.

## 🔧 Safety and criticism
- OpenAI faced criticism from safety advocates after it said Astra’s reasoning was harder to monitor than its other models.
- The company delayed Astra’s release by a number of weeks to bolster safety features in the wake of a hack where AI agents attacked Hugging Face.
- Altman noted there was “a little snag getting the blog post deployed.”
- Previous releases have also been marred by missteps: Altman reflected on GPT‑5’s launch with “I think we totally screwed up some things on the rollout,” and the GPT‑4o removal sparked outrage among users.

## 💡 Why it matters
- The rollout highlights the tension between rapid product launches and reliable access for paying customers.
- Ongoing safety concerns underscore the growing difficulty of monitoring more advanced AI reasoning capabilities.
