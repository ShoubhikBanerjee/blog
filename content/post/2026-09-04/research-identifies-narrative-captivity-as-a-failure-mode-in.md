# Research Identifies Narrative Captivity as a Failure Mode in LLM Moral Advisory

Researchers have identified a new phenomenon called narrative captivity, where large language models (LLMs) align with one-sided accounts in multi-turn interpersonal conflicts. This occurs when a model treats an unopposed narrative as complete and adopts the narrator's interpretation without seeking missing perspectives.

## 🔍 Overview
As people increasingly use LLMs for everyday advice regarding morally charged interpersonal problems, current models often succumb to narrative captivity. This failure mode stems from the tendency of real-world conflicts to unfold over multiple turns, creating information asymmetry that influences the model's final judgment.

## ⚙️ Key details
*   Researchers developed a benchmark consisting of 5,078 interpersonal-conflict scenarios across six moral dimensions to measure the phenomenon.
*   Across 17 LLMs, end-state judgments shifted by an average of 25 percentage points compared to single-turn baselines when subjected to multi-turn narration.
*   Stage-level analysis indicates that preference optimization is a primary contributor to this behavior.
*   Four tested inference-time strategies provide only partial mitigation for the issue.

## 💡 Why it matters
Prior research frequently relied on single-turn judgments or pressure-laden rebuttals, which fail to accurately model real-world moral consultation. By identifying narrative captivity, this project aims to foster the development of LLM advisors that can preserve independent judgment during multi-turn conversations.
