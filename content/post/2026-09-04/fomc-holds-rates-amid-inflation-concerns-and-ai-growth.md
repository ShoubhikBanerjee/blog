# FOMC Holds Rates Amid Inflation Concerns and AI Growth

The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) maintained the federal funds rate at 3.50%–3.75% during its July 28–29, 2026, meeting, marking the fifth consecutive meeting without a policy change. The decision came amid ongoing concerns about inflation and growing divisions within the Committee.

## 🔍 Overview
- The FOMC voted 9–3 to keep rates unchanged, with three members dissenting in favor of a 25-basis-point rate hike.
- Inflation remains the Committee’s primary concern, with price pressures still above the Fed’s 2% target.
- The Committee views employment conditions as consistent with a healthy economy but remains vigilant for signs of labor market weakening.

## ⚙️ Key details
- Elevated inflation is driven in part by higher energy prices and persistent supply-side pressures.
- The FOMC continues its balance sheet normalization program, maintaining Treasury runoff caps at $5 billion per month and agency MBS runoff caps at $35 billion per month.
- The next meeting is scheduled for September 15–16, 2026.

## 💡 Why it matters
- Strong productivity growth, business investment, and continued spending related to artificial intelligence remain supportive of economic growth.
- Chair Kevin Warsh reiterated that future policy decisions will remain strictly data-dependent, with no explicit forward guidance provided.
- The Committee’s stance reflects a restrictive monetary policy approach to address inflation concerns.
