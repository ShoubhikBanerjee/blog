# ARIA bans fully AI-generated songs from official Australian music charts

Australia’s official chart body, ARIA, has changed its rules so that recordings created primarily by artificial intelligence are no longer eligible to appear on the ARIA Charts.

## 🔍 Overview
- AI‑generated music has pushed the music industry into new territory.
- While many countries are introducing copyright, transparency and labeling rules, ARIA has taken the most explicit step by drawing a clear line for chart eligibility.

## ⚙️ Rule specifics
- The restriction applies when AI is responsible for the **main creative performance** of a track.
- Examples of ineligible recordings:
  - A song with an AI‑generated lead vocal.
  - A track where a major instrumental performance is generated by AI.
  - Recordings created almost entirely from AI prompts.
- AI can still be used for **supporting tools** such as mastering, stem separation, or other production assistance.
- The rule does **not** prevent artists from using AI at all; it only bars fully AI‑created works from chart competition.

## 🌍 Global context
- Europe has introduced transparency requirements for AI‑generated content.
- The UK is discussing copyright and AI training, and the US is moving to protect artists’ voices and identities.
- Music organisations are adding clearer labeling for AI recordings and setting new chart‑qualification conditions.
- Spotify plans to display **AI Persona** badges on artist profiles built around AI identities.
- Data company **Luminate** has launched a framework within its CONNECT platform to identify AI‑generated songs and artist profiles, reporting thousands of such recordings already in its database.
- These initiatives show the industry is shifting from treating AI music as a side note to actively tracking and measuring it.

## 📊 Impact
- Despite new rules elsewhere, AI‑generated tracks can still appear on many official charts.
- ARIA’s rule is currently the clearest line, ensuring that only music with a primary human creative performance competes on its charts.
- The change may influence other markets to adopt similar eligibility standards.
