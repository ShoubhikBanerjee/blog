# Prompt Engineering Variability Limits LLM Accuracy in Drug Toxicity Prediction

In a newly submitted computer‑science paper (3 Sep 2026), researchers analyse how prompt phrasing affects large language model (LLM) performance for predicting drug toxicity.

## 🔍 Overview
- Title: *Analysis of Prompt Engineering for Drug Toxicity Prediction*.
- Field: Computer Science → Artificial Intelligence.
- Goal: Investigate the importance of prompt phrasing for drug toxicity prediction.

## 📊 Why drug toxicity prediction matters
- Clinical trials in the UK can cost up to **£1.3 million**.
- Approximately **90 %** of drug candidates fail.
- **Toxicity** is a major contributing factor to these failures.
- Testing is both **time‑ and cost‑intensive**.

## 🛠️ Methodology
- LLMs were prompted to identify chemical properties relevant to toxicity.
- Prompts were varied to explore three dimensions:
  - **Job role**
  - **Prompt structuring**
  - **Rule interpretation**
- The identified features were used to generate datasets that were then fed to traditional machine‑learning algorithms.

## 📈 Findings
- Natural variance inherent in LLM outputs **exceeds** any performance gain from fine‑tuning prompts.
- Substantial performance improvements were observed when **chemoinformatic code** extracted features, rather than using LLM‑generated values.
- The proposed analysis method can be applied to a wide range of prompt types across different bioinformatics areas.
