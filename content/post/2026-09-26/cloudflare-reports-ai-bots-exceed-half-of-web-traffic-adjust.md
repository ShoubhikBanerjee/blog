# Cloudflare Reports AI Bots Exceed Half of Web Traffic, Adjusts Controls

Cloudflare announced that, as of June, bots account for more than half of all internet traffic. The surge is linked to AI companies scraping the web and AI agents acting on behalf of users. In response, the company is expanding its traffic‑control options for site owners.

## 🔍 Overview
- Cloudflare found in June that bots made up more than half of internet traffic.
- The proportion is rising as AI companies and AI agents increase their activity online.
- Cloudflare positions itself between websites and those agents, offering owners granular control.

## 📈 AI Traffic Impact
- Bots include both malicious scrapers and legitimate AI tools.
- Site owners can **block all AI tools**, **allow all**, or **allow only those that might pay for access**.
- AI is also “upending everything inside of Cloudflare.”

## 🛡️ Cloudflare's Controls
- The network spans **over 350 cities worldwide** and thousands of data centers.
- Services aim to keep applications and content **safe** and **fast**.
- Matthew Prince has described Cloudflare as “a service that makes the internet faster and protects it from bad guys.”

## ⚙️ Internal AI Changes
- Earlier this year Cloudflare laid off **more than a thousand people**, about **20 %** of its workforce.
- Matthew Prince wrote an op‑ed in the *Wall Street Journal* titled **“How I choose which Cloudflare employees to replace with AI.”**

## 💡 Why It Matters
- The shift in traffic composition forces a rethink of the internet’s business model, which has been dominated by advertising for the past 30 years, largely shaped by Google.
- Cloudflare is exploring how to shape a healthier future business model for the internet while managing the growing influence of non‑human traffic.

## 🗣️ Public Discussion
- At Web Summit, Prince was asked when non‑human traffic might surpass human traffic online, highlighting industry curiosity about the trajectory of AI‑driven web activity.
