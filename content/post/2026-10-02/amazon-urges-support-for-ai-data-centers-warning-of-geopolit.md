# Amazon Urges Support for AI Data Centers Warning of Geopolitical Risks

Amazon Web Services CEO Matt Gorman has published a blog post calling for public support of AI data center projects, warning that blocking them could cause irreparable harm to the US economy and national security. The publication comes as public support for data centers has plummeted, with communities across the United States raising concerns about jobs, environmental impact, power demands, and AI safety.

## 🔍 Overview

The push from Amazon comes amid rising local backlash and over 100 data center moratoriums currently being considered across the country. AWS CEO Matt Gorman warned that the United States cannot afford to lose the geopolitical race for AI dominance, stating that enacting these moratoriums would write a "losing ticket to this race, and the consequences would last generations." Gorman also argued that some public opposition is fueled by myths, social media, 24/7 news, and reports of foreign nations intentionally seeding misinformation in the US to trick communities into slowing down the build-out.

## ⚙️ Key details

To address local friction and establish clearer practices, Amazon is codifying several measures under a new "Data Center Commitment." Additionally, the company has altered how it interacts with local government agencies:

* **Community Investment**: Amazon has pledged to invest more than $1 billion to support communities neighboring its data centers.
* **Economic and Utility Safeguards**: The commitment includes practices around creating jobs and preventing increases to local energy rates.
* **Elimination of NDAs**: Amazon will no longer use nondisclosure agreements with government agencies. This policy change follows a probe by Democratic lawmaker Jamie Raskin into tech companies' secret deals with local officials.
* **Shift toward Transparency**: Commenting on the change, Amazon chief global affairs and legal officer David Zapolsky noted that while the industry has a history of secrecy, "it just didn't make sense" to continue requiring NDAs when community leaders need to share information with constituents.
