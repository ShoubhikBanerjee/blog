# US urges Europe to tap diesel reserves to lower fuel prices

The United States is pressing European governments to release a portion of their strategic diesel reserves in order to ease price pressures and address supply disruptions.

## 🔍 Overview
- The American Secretary of Energy, Chris Wright, proposed a coordinated plan to release diesel from European strategic reserves.
- The proposal targets 120 million barrels of diesel over 180 days.
- This amount is more than one‑third of the roughly 315 million barrels that EU countries held in reserve in June.
- US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent also supported the measure in a post on X.
- President Donald Trump indicated he could ask Europe to tap their diesel reserves during a visit to Texas.

## ⚙️ Key Details
- **Release target:** 120 million barrels of diesel.
- **Time frame:** 180 days.
- **Share of EU reserves:** > 33 % of the 315 million barrels reported in June.
- **Winter timing:** The minister said, "It is time to bring the diesel stocks to market in a coordinated manner," as winter approaches.

## 🗣️ Political Statements
- Chris Wright told Fox News, "Europe can also contribute to improving the situation."
- Scott Bessent wrote on X, "Our European partners should accelerate the implementation of their existing commitments and make additional supplies immediately available to address ongoing disruptions."
- President Donald Trump said in Texas, "We could do that. They have diesel," indicating he could ask Europe to release reserves.
- On Thursday, Trump’s tone was described as less forceful.

## 💡 Why it matters
- Releasing diesel could help lower fuel prices in the United States and Europe.
- Coordinated market entry aims to stabilize supply ahead of the winter heating season.
- The request reflects ongoing tensions over President Trump’s earlier threat to impose an export ban on US diesel.
- European participation would demonstrate trans‑Atlantic cooperation on energy security.
