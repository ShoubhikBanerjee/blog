# Judge Dismisses Chegg and PMC Antitrust Lawsuits Over Google AI Search

Federal Judge Amit Mehta dismissed the antitrust lawsuits brought by Chegg and Rolling Stone parent Penske Media Corporation, which alleged that Google's AI‑powered search features siphoned traffic from their sites.

## 🔍 Overview
- Chegg and PMC claimed Google abused monopoly power by coercing publishers into providing content for AI Overviews for free, or risk disappearing from search results.
- The lawsuits, filed last year, asserted that Google's AI search features diverted traffic away from publishers, hurting revenue.

## ⚖️ Legal Ruling
- Judge Mehta wrote that the plaintiffs’ claims “don’t stand up to antitrust law.”
- He noted the plaintiffs have only “pleaded an ‘expectation’ that Google will send them search traffic if they make their content available for free.”
- “But an expectation is not an agreement. It is simply how a general search engine works,” Mehta wrote.
- The court is “not ‘unsympathetic to the situation publishers now find themselves in,’” but said antitrust rules cannot replace legislative action on the economic impact of “new innovation.”

## 📉 Impact on Publishers
- Publishers continue to see a steep dive in traffic to news outlets and small websites after Google’s AI‑powered search overhaul.

## 💰 Google Pilot Payments
- This week, The Information reported that Google is paying **around 100 publishers** for their contributions to AI Overviews, AI Mode, and Gemini as part of a pilot program.
