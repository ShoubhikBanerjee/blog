# Microsoft expands biomimicry in data centers as AI growth raises community concerns

Microsoft announced that it is expanding its use of “biomimicry” at data centers while facing increased pushback from local residents over construction, pollution, and noise.

## 🔍 Overview
- Data centers have evolved from “relatively unassuming office buildings” in the early 2000s to massive “hyperscale” facilities spanning millions of square feet that house the physical infrastructure for AI.
- Communities across the United States are protesting the impact of these facilities on quality of life, pollution, water, and electricity bills.
- Tech companies, including Microsoft, have responded with promises to mitigate harm by paying for infrastructure upgrades needed in local water and power systems.

## 🌱 Biomimicry Strategy
- “Biomimicry is sort of a catch‑out term for many of the ways technologies take inspiration from nature, from ‘painless’ needles that imitate a mosquito’s proboscis to naturally ventilated buildings modeled after termite mounds.”
- Microsoft describes its approach as “blending our datacenters into the natural landscape” to “create resilient ecosystems that support biodiversity, strengthen community ties, and help our datacenters become better neighbors.”
- The company is adding more gardens and native trees on‑site and investing in “400+ acres of woodland and pollinator prairie restoration” with the goal of leaving the land “more productive than we received it.”
- It is also funding projects that repair damaged ecosystems, such as a $4.2 million creek‑restoration effort in 2023 and outfitting stormwater ponds with forebays to prevent sediment escape.

## 📍 Local Impact – San Antonio
- San Antonio City Councilmember Ric Galvan recalls the first data centers in his city and notes that there are now “more than a dozen data centers in Galvan’s roughly 55‑square‑mile district,” with an expectation of around 20 if proposed projects are completed.
- In 2022, Microsoft cut down more than 2,600 trees across a 33‑acre site to build a data center, sparking controversy over loss of canopy that helps mitigate heat.
- Residents have complained about traffic, tree removal, constant bright lights, and noise that “concentrate nuisances in the same neighborhoods.”
- Galvan said, “that [feeling] gets taken away pretty quickly when you make a gigantic data center.”
- He added, “Maybe … they’re trying to just make sure that they’re covering their bases,” and, “but also, I think if that’s the case, that’s fine,” provided Microsoft and other operators address concerns around pollution, water use, and destruction of green spaces.

## 📈 Scale & Reach
- Microsoft operates “more than 300 data centers in more than 34 countries.”
- The company started biomimicry projects at a data center in the Netherlands in 2022.
- It now plans to bring “nature‑inspired solutions to restore and enhance natural ecosystems” to more than 20 sites in the US and Germany, and to apply the same approach to all new projects in the US and a growing number worldwide.
- In the United States, Microsoft is repurposing the shuttered Foxconn LCD‑factory property in Mount Pleasant, Wisconsin, for its own data centers.

## 🤝 Community Response & Mitigation
- Across the country, communities are rising up against hulking data warehouses.
- Microsoft’s stated mitigation includes ecosystem restoration, woodland and prairie projects, and customized habitat‑enhancing solutions for each site.
- According to a Microsoft spokesperson, the company is “investing in enhancement projects that includes 400+ acres of woodland and pollinator prairie restoration” with the aim of leaving land “more productive than we received it.”

## 🔧 Key Facts
- Hyperscale data centers now span millions of square feet and power AI workloads.
- More than a dozen data centers exist in San Antonio’s 55‑square‑mile district; projected growth to ~20 centers.
- Microsoft is spending “hundreds of millions of dollars” to build several new data centers in the Greater San Antonio area.
- Biomimicry initiatives involve on‑site gardens, native trees, ecosystem restoration, and stormwater management.
- Microsoft operates >300 data centers in >34 countries and has launched biomimicry projects in the Netherlands, the US, and Germany.
- Community concerns focus on traffic, noise, light pollution, tree loss, heat mitigation, and water usage.
