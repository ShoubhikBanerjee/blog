# US House to Vote on Bipartisan Bill Protecting Households from Data Center Power Costs

The US House of Representatives is expected to vote on the bipartisan 'Ratepayer Protection Act' this Wednesday. The legislation aims to prevent the soaring costs associated with data center expansion and power needs from being passed on to average household electricity bills.

## 🔍 Overview
Introduced by Representative Kathy Castor (D-FL) and Representative Gabe Evans (R-CO), the bill seeks to ensure that large data centers pay their fair share of the costs for expanding the electricity grid. The proposal comes as government officials face pressure to manage the increasing energy demands of the AI industry while protecting consumer affordability and grid reliability.

## 🧩 How it works
*   **Cost Allocation:** State utility regulators would be required to consider whether large electricity users should pay for the infrastructure needed to serve them.
*   **Capacity Threshold:** The proposed rules would require new data centers using at least 20 megawatts to cover the additional power infrastructure costs they create.
*   **Rate Reductions:** Regulators would have the authority to charge large data centers extra to help lower the bills of other utility customers.
*   **State Authority:** The bill would give states more freedom to impose conditions, such as requiring data centers to secure new power supplies or placing limits on their electricity usage.

## 💡 Why it matters
While data centers have existed for decades, the growth of AI has significantly increased the demand for new power plants and grid upgrades. This shift has raised concerns regarding higher costs for households. The bipartisan support for the bill arrives ahead of the November midterm elections, highlighting the political priority of grid reliability and consumer costs. While Amazon suggests the bill could accelerate infrastructure investment, the Data Center Coalition is currently reviewing the legislation. Microsoft, Google, and Nvidia declined to comment, while Meta did not respond to requests for comment.
