# Anthropic flags Chinese open model GLM-5.3 as major cyberattack risk

Late last month, Anthropic released a report arguing that the model GLM-5.3 poses a major cyberattack risk.

## 🔍 Overview
- GLM-5.3 is a powerful new model released in August by the Chinese company Z.ai.
- The model is **open**: it can be freely downloaded, its weights are publicly available, and anyone can modify it.
- Anthropic’s report warns that this openness creates a significant risk of misuse for cyber‑attacks.

## ⚙️ Open vs. Closed Models
| Feature | Open Model | Closed Model |
|---|---|---|
| Distribution | Direct download of model weights | Access only through website, app, or API |
| Transparency | Internal structure observable; can inspect the model’s “chain of thought” | Researchers can only send prompts and view responses |
| Customisation | Can be retrained, fine‑tuned, combined with other models, or compressed | Limited to provided interfaces |
| Cost (per Mozilla) | Typically cheaper | Generally more expensive |

## 📊 Current Landscape
- As of publication, the top eight models on the Artificial Analysis LLM leaderboard are all **closed**.
- Open models are only a handful of months behind in development, indicating rapid progress but still lagging behind the most capable closed systems.

## 💰 Cost & Deployment
- Open models can be run on local hardware, allowing businesses to keep sensitive or proprietary data on‑premise.
- Even when using an external provider for inference, open models tend to cost less than closed alternatives.

## 🛡️ Safety Concerns
- Open models can be easily misused by bad actors because they are downloadable and modifiable.
- Anthropic’s Dario Amodei, whose company could be undermined by cheap, powerful models, calls for a more cautious approach.
- Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, who benefits from continued hardware sales for running open models, advocates for the United States to develop more of them.
- The clearest argument for open models is preventing the mass concentration of power, framing the technology as a public good.

## 🌐 Strategic Implications
- The world’s most sophisticated hacking models are currently all made in the United States, putting the decision to share such technology in the hands of U.S. companies and the U.S. government.
- This dynamic could lead to a scenario where the United States can hack any other country, while no one can hack the United States.
- If every individual had a loyal, safe AI system running locally, the risk from centralized, inscrutable government AI would be reduced.
