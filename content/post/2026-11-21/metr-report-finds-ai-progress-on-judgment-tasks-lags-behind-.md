# METR Report Finds AI Progress on Judgment Tasks Lags Behind Coding Capabilities

The AI evaluation nonprofit METR has released a new report showing that while some AI capabilities continue to improve along an exponential trajectory, models remain weak at tasks requiring judgment. The report, which serves as an update to METR's widely shared time horizon plot, incorporates new open-ended and judgment-heavy evaluations to assess whether progress in highly verifiable domains like coding and math carries over to areas where success is harder to measure.

## 🧩 How it works

METR evaluates models by testing them against a large suite of tasks that have also been completed by human testers:

* **Task Difficulty:** Each task is scored based on the amount of time it took human testers to finish it.
* **Model Rating:** AI models are rated according to the length of the tasks they can successfully complete.
* **Progress Trajectory:** In the original time horizon plot, the task length that frontier models could complete doubled every four months. The updated plot adds tasks with much longer estimated completion times and more open-ended requirements.

## ⚙️ Key details

The update includes tasks designed to test judgment and open-ended performance. The following table describes how models fared on these new tasks:

| Task Name | Task Description | Model Performance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| AI Evaluation Design | Design new AI evaluations for METR to use in its research, scored by human METR employees | No AI-designed evaluation was considered useful by METR employees, though Opus 5.5 achieved "borderline above the bar" performance. |
| Scoring Philosophical Arguments | Score philosophical arguments to capture the extent to which models can exercise good judgment | Models fell short of human performance. |

## 💡 Why it matters

Tasks that require judgment are essential if AI systems are to automate research and development, but they are difficult to train using reinforcement learning because success is harder to define and measure. 

Commenting on the update, Sayash Kapoor, a computer scientist and incoming professor at UC Berkeley, said: "We’re at the point where AI evaluations need to get more creative. Trying to push the envelope of the space of evaluations is a welcome change." Najoung Kim, a linguist and computer scientist at Boston University, added: "The improvement we observe, I think that does tell us something for the current moment."
