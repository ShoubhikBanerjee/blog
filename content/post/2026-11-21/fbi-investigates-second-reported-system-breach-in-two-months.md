# FBI Investigates Second Reported System Breach in Two Months

FBI systems have reportedly been breached for the second time in two months, this time targeting the agency's National Crime Information Center (NCIC). The NCIC is used to share information between local, state, and federal law enforcement agencies.

![Security Visual](https://www.bleepstatic.com/c/w/w-claude-code-security.jpg)

## 🔍 Overview

- The FBI is investigating an apparent breach of the NCIC system, according to CNN's reporting citing two people briefed on the investigation.
- The intrusion was reportedly discovered in recent days.
- It is unclear when the breach occurred, how the attackers gained access, or whether any information was stolen.
- The FBI has not publicly confirmed the incident and did not respond to CNN's request for comment.
- BleepingComputer contacted the FBI on Friday evening with questions about the reported breach but has not received a response.
- This is a developing story and will be updated as more information becomes available.

## ⚙️ Key details

- The National Crime Information Center is an FBI-operated database used across the United States to share information about wanted and missing persons, outstanding arrest warrants, stolen vehicles and firearms, protection orders, and other criminal justice records.
- Police officers routinely use the system during traffic stops, criminal investigations, and background checks.
- It remains unclear whether attackers accessed sensitive law enforcement records stored in NCIC or whether the intrusion affected system availability.

## 🧩 Context and Prior Incidents

- The reported breach comes two months after the ShinyHunters extortion group claimed to have breached FBI systems by exploiting an unpatched Oracle PeopleSoft vulnerability.
- The earlier attack targeted a third-party platform associated with the FBI's recruitment website, FBIJobs.gov, with ShinyHunters claiming to have stolen sensitive information.
- The FBI subsequently launched an investigation leading to the arrests of suspected ShinyHunters members, including a Canadian cybersecurity executive reportedly linked to the attacks.
- There is currently no indication that the latest NCIC incident is connected to ShinyHunters or the earlier breach.
