# Anthropic mathematician claims independent Riemann Hypothesis proof using Claude

Within a few hours of each other, two mathematicians claimed independent proofs of the Riemann Hypothesis, the most famous open problem in mathematics. Levent Alpöge of Anthropic first made his Claude-driven proof in a note released with Lean code rather than a full paper, while Alessandro Masullo released his 940-page, 100% human work-in-progress proof.

## 🧩 How it works

Levent Alpöge states that he is presenting the world with a complete argument before having mastered a massive neighbourhood around its mathematics. To avoid posting an unreadable travesty of "slop", he decided to only present the Lean code. The artifact consists solely of Lean code, which is 1.3GB worth and equivalent to approximately 250,000 pages if written out in prose. 

The work began on October 6. Eight of the 20 steps landed on November 15, and the final entry on November 16 reads "GRH." Alpöge credits Claude for the parts involving automorphic forms on higher-rank groups, an area where he says he has little mastery. As Alpöge noted, "Claude far outpaced me near the finish line. No, Claude flew, and danced, and sparkled near that finish line."

## ⚙️ Key details

- **Formalized Statement**: Elliot Glazer confirms that the formalized statement is the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH).
- **Verification**: Glazer puts the chance of a Lean bug at well under 1%. If the code finishes compiling with no extra axioms, it is probably correct, even though no human will ever have read all of it.
- **Human Alternative**: Alessandro Masullo’s proof is a roughly 940-page GRH draft. It is reported as 100% human and had been undergoing secret manual review and revision per Kevin Hartnett for the last 12 years.
- **Uncertainties**: The specific Claude model used and the degree of human steering have not been disclosed.
- **Market Reaction**: ANT stock fell about 10% on the day of the announcement.

## 💡 Why it matters

This development is the third in a trilogy of frontier math breakthroughs, following the Navier Stokes solutions and the Quasi-Riemann bounding. However, both AI-claimed Millennium Prize results will take years to verify. 

OpenAI's September Navier-Stokes claim remains a cautionary tale, as critics argued that its Lean proof did not match the natural-language result. Artificial Analysis notes that this Riemann Hypothesis claim and the previous Navier-Stokes claim are currently unverified. 

The situation highlights a distinct contrast: the AI-written solution is probably correct but incomprehensible, while the human solution is probably technically incorrect but understandable. This underscores the need for Weak to Strong alignment in the open-ended domain of controlling AI that is smarter than humans, raising questions about the No Slop Manifesto tenet that one should understand what they ship.
