# Anthropic mathematician claims AI‑assisted proof of the Riemann Hypothesis

Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, announced that he and the company’s AI system Claude have produced a proof of the Riemann hypothesis.

## 🔍 Overview
- Claim: proof of the Riemann hypothesis with Claude.
- Solution written in Lean, posted on Alpöge’s website.
- Alpöge says he texted Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei late Sunday night and delayed publication until after the IPO on Thursday.

## 🧩 Claim Details
- Quote from Alpöge’s site: “I believe I have, working with Claude, discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis.”
- He also states the method “easily extends” to the Generalized and Extended Riemann Hypotheses.
- “Claude and I have also formalized the arguments without assumptions.”

## ⚙️ Verification Status
- The proof has not been independently verified.
- Anthropic has not commented or been involved in the announcement.
- The Verge reached out to Anthropic but did not hear back immediately.

## 🏅 Prize Context
- The Riemann hypothesis is one of the seven Millennium Prize problems, each offering a $1 million bounty.
- Clay Mathematics Institute requires a solution to be published and to withstand at least two years of scrutiny with general acceptance before prize consideration.
- To date, only the Poincaré conjecture has been officially solved among the prize problems.

## 🌐 Related AI Math Efforts
- In August, Anthropic said it was working on the Riemann hypothesis with Claude but did not expect the techniques to lead to a proof.
- OpenAI announced a claimed solution to the Navier‑Stokes problem, a separate Millennium problem, after hearing rumors of competing work.
- OpenAI’s effort involved millions of dollars and 10,000 agents; the result is considered credible but lacks formal recognition and faces allegations of questionable research practices.
