# Meta Delays New Business Terms Again Amid Publisher Concerns Over AI Training

Meta has announced it may delay the implementation of its new business terms past the November 20th deadline as the company faces backlash from publishers regarding AI training practices. The company has stated it does not plan to implement these policy changes without a full consultation with industry groups and leaders, a process that may delay enforcement indefinitely.

## 🔍 Overview

*   **Original Effective Date:** October 30th.
*   **First Delay:** Postponed to November 20th.
*   **Current Status:** Likely to be delayed beyond November 20th as consultations continue.
*   **Active Terms:** Meta's existing business terms will remain in place until the updated version is implemented.

## ⚙️ Key details

The controversy surrounds updated business terms that initially appeared to grant Meta permission to train its AI models on news sites that utilize Facebook and Instagram. While Meta later removed specific language regarding "training" products on content scraped from these sites, legal experts from Nieman Lab have noted that the updated policy still does not explicitly rule out the possibility.

Meta communications head Andy Stone addressed the situation on X, stating, "We’ve heard publishers’ feedback on our proposed ads and business terms update. We’re committed to getting this right, and as we expect this consultation could extend beyond November 20, in which case we’d delay implementation of the updated terms."

## 💡 Why it matters

The proposed changes have drawn significant opposition from industry organizations and government officials who are concerned about the exploitation of content for AI development.

| Stakeholder | Position or Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| Andy Stone (Meta) | Committed to a full consultation with industry leaders before policy changes. |
| Legal Experts (Nieman Lab) | Noted that policy revisions are currently insufficient to rule out AI training. |
| News/Media Alliance | Provided pushback against the proposed business terms. |
| Sen. Marsha Blackburn (R-TN) | Stated the terms indicate Meta is attempting to exploit American creators and businesses. |
| Sen. Adam Schiff (D-CA) | Urged Meta to reverse the proposed policy changes. |
