# OpenAI releases three AI‑generated breakthroughs with expert shepherds

On Monday, OpenAI announced impressive breakthroughs in mathematics, computer science, and physics, and said it would use expert “shepherds” to decide how to share the findings.

## 🔍 Overview
- All three results concern some of the most consequential unsolved problems in their fields.
- OpenAI describes the work as extending previous efforts and breaking new ground.
- The company is working with expert “shepherds” who determine the release strategy for each result.

## 🧩 Results and Release Strategies
| Discipline | Claim | Release Strategy |
|------------|-------|------------------|
| Mathematics | Purports to resolve the Kakeya conjecture in all dimensions | AI‑generated output released; researchers free to organize their own efforts to explain the result |
| Computer Science | Claims to disprove the exponential time hypothesis | External group of researchers assembled to explain the result |
| Physics | Explores the Standard Model, quantum chromodynamics and its relationship to Yang‑Mills theory (does not address the mass‑gap problem) | Deferred to an external group of experts; no material released yet |

## ⚙️ How the shepherd model works
- Expert “shepherds” decide how best to share each breakthrough.
- Each discipline receives a distinct release approach.
- Mathematics: output released directly, leaving researchers to explain it.
- Computer science: OpenAI will assemble an external group of researchers to explain the result.
- Physics: OpenAI will defer to an external group of experts about how to release the materials.

## 💡 Why it matters
- The mathematics result targets the Kakeya conjecture, a problem linked to a recent Fields Medal.
- The computer‑science claim challenges the exponential time hypothesis, a hypothesis closely related to the P vs NP Millennium Prize problem.
- The physics work touches on quantum chromodynamics and Yang‑Mills theory, central to the Standard Model and another Millennium Prize problem.
- Experts described the findings as “huge,” “major breakthroughs,” and “significant.”
- Researchers expressed concern that only hand‑picked experts initially see the work, saying “It doesn’t make any sense that first only a few distinguished scientists can look at them.”
- Many welcomed the more manageable batch of three results compared with the hundreds previously released, but questioned the restriction of access to OpenAI‑chosen experts.
- Some argued that public announcement should also make the work available for anyone to scrutinize.

## 📚 Community reactions
- Brown University professor Brendan Hassett said the claim “looks well supported,” though he had not yet checked the Lean formalization.
- Constantin Kogler (Institute for Advanced Study) said, “I am fine with them releasing the problems as they did today,” while questioning the hand‑picked expert restriction.
- Andreas Karch (University of Texas at Austin) called the hand‑picking “a disaster.”
