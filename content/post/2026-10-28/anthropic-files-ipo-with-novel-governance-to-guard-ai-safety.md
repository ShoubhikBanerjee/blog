# Anthropic Files IPO with Novel Governance to Guard AI Safety Mission

Anthropic, the AI safety‑focused startup, announced that it will pursue an initial public offering while retaining a governance model designed to keep the company’s mission front and centre.

## 🔍 Overview
- Anthropic’s IPO is intended to "tap the vast riches of the public markets" while insisting it will not compromise its core mission of developing AI safely and for the benefit of humanity.
- The company believes a "unique and complicated governance structure" can help it resist investor pressures that other AI firms, such as OpenAI, cite as a reason to delay an IPO.

## 🏛️ Governance Structure
- Anthropic is organized as a **public benefit corporation**, which legally requires it to balance shareholder returns with its stated social mission and the interests of stakeholders such as employees and specific communities.
- Directors may also hold executive roles; the CEO Dario Amodei and President Daniela Amodei both sit on the board.
- Ordinary shareholders have limited ability to influence board composition:
  - Four of the seven board seats are appointed by a **Long‑Term Benefit Trust**.
  - The Trust consists of five financially disinterested voting trustees with expertise in AI safety, national security, public policy, and social enterprise.
  - Through a special **Class T stock**, trustees can appoint or remove directors to pursue the trust’s purpose of balancing financial interests with Anthropic’s public‑benefit purpose.
  - The remaining three board seats are technically elected by ordinary shareholders, but a separate safeguard limits their impact.
- **Founder LLC** controls 50.1 % of voting power on shareholder matters, effectively allowing the founders to block or force decisions regardless of other shareholder votes.
- Founder LLC’s initial members are the seven co‑founders: Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei, Tom Brown, Jack Clark, Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish, and Christopher Olah. (Ben Mann, who co‑leads Anthropic Labs, is not listed as a member.)

## ⚖️ Why It Matters
- The structure aims to keep Anthropic "entirely insulated from profit‑seeking pressures" because ordinary shareholders "have no ability to select the board or make business decisions."
- By concentrating board‑appointment power in the Trust and voting control in Founder LLC, the company hopes to shield its mission from the "pressures associated with being a public company."
- Legal scholars note that while the model limits ordinary shareholder influence, the founders’ own financial stakes could still affect decision‑making.
- Compared with OpenAI, whose CEO Sam Altman cites investor pressure as a reason to postpone an IPO, Anthropic is moving ahead with a governance design meant to mitigate that same pressure.
