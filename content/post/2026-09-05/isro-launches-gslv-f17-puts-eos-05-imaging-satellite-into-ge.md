# ISRO launches GSLV-F17, puts EOS-05 imaging satellite into geosynchronous orbit

The Indian Space Research Organization (ISRO) successfully launched the GSLV‑F17 rocket early Friday, placing the EOS‑05 Earth‑imaging satellite into geosynchronous orbit.

## 🔭 Overview
- GSLV‑F17 lifted off from the Satish Dhawan Space Center at 2:55 a.m. local time.
- The mission carried EOS‑05, ISRO’s first imaging platform designed specifically for geosynchronous orbit.
- ISRO posted on X: "Mission Success! GSLV-F17 has successfully accomplished its mission, placing EOS-05 into the intended orbit."

## 🚀 Key Details
| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Rocket | GSLV‑F17 (19th flight of the Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle) |
| Payload | EOS‑05 Earth‑imaging satellite |
| Orbit | Geosynchronous orbit, ~36,000 km above the equator |
| Launch site | Satish Dhawan Space Center, southern India |
| Launch time | 2:55 a.m. local time (early Friday) |

## 📡 Why it matters
- EOS‑05 provides state‑of‑the‑art imaging capabilities from geosynchronous orbit, expanding India’s Earth‑observation infrastructure.
- The launch is described as a breakthrough for ISRO’s remote‑sensing capabilities and underscores India’s growing role in the commercial space sector.

## 📆 Future Launch Schedule
- ISRO Chairman V. Narayanan said the agency aims to conduct six additional launches before the end of the year.
- The accelerated schedule highlights the agency’s intent to increase launch frequency in 2026.
