# California passes bills to force data‑center energy and water disclosures

California Governor Gavin Newsom signed seven bills on Monday that aim to increase transparency around data centers’ electricity and water consumption.

## 🔍 Overview
- Data centers are expanding across the U.S., prompting protests over potential strain on power grids and local water supplies.
- The new California legislation seeks to give communities better data and more say on how data centers impact electricity bills and water supply.

## 🧩 Legislative Details
| Bill | Requirement |
|------|-------------|
| SB 886, SB 1168, AB 2383 | Direct the California Public Utilities Commission to create separate power rates for data centers to recoup grid‑connection costs |
| AB 1577 | Requires monthly reporting on data centers’ energy consumption |
| AB 2619, AB 2469 | Require water‑use disclosures; AB 2469 also makes operators cover infrastructure‑upgrade costs |
| SB 887 | Removes categorical exemptions from the California Environmental Quality Act, ensuring data centers undergo environmental review |

## ⚙️ Why it matters
- Without clear data, scientists have found it "extremely frustrating to try to understand basic information," says Mark Specht, senior manager for the climate and energy program at the Union of Concerned Scientists.
- The disclosure rules begin next year but still won’t provide a full picture of consumption.
- Two possible outcomes are outlined: (1) ballooning electricity demand could lead to costly new infrastructure that may be overbuilt if AI demand falls, passing costs to other consumers; (2) spreading fixed grid‑maintenance costs across more customers could lower electricity rates, but this depends on data centers contributing to distribution‑system costs rather than only connecting to high‑voltage transmission.
- Water‑use data remain opaque; water providers have refused to share usage figures, and current disclosures only apply when seeking permits or business licenses, leaving year‑to‑year usage unclear.

## 📊 Perspectives
- "I’m happy [AB 1577] passed," Specht said, noting the difficulty in researching state offers.
- "I wouldn’t say definitively that data centers have increased the cost [of electricity]," he added, acknowledging the lack of numbers to demonstrate either increase or potential savings.
- As data centers’ power needs grow, monitoring their energy and water consumption becomes increasingly important.
