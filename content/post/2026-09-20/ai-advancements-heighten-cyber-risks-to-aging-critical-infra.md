# AI Advancements Heighten Cyber Risks to Aging Critical Infrastructure

The rapid development of AI capabilities is enabling adversaries to execute cyberattacks against critical infrastructure more efficiently, compounding the vulnerabilities of aging equipment designed long before modern cybersecurity threats emerged.

## 🔍 Overview
Critical infrastructure in the United States, such as the power sector, frequently relies on hardware with a lifespan of several decades, with the average nuclear reactor now approximately 44 years old. These legacy systems, often controlled by operational technology (OT) that does not support frequent software patching, are increasingly susceptible to attacks.

## ⚙️ Key details
AI is lowering the barrier to entry for adversaries by providing technical knowledge that may have been previously unavailable to them:

* AI models can analyze manuals and internal systems to help less-skilled actors understand OT protocols and networks.
* Adversaries use AI to chain vulnerabilities together and automate processes from initial access to exploitation.
* Research associates, such as Sophie McDowall of the Foundation for Defense of Democracies, note that AI allows adversaries to move faster than defenders can respond.

## 💡 Why it matters
As threats evolve, experts are emphasizing the need for defensive strategies beyond traditional IT security. Strategies include:

* Ensuring infrastructure can switch to manual operations when necessary.
* Reducing the interconnectedness of systems to limit exposure.
* Moving toward a policy of disconnecting systems that cannot be effectively protected.
