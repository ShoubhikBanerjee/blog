# Meta’s Muse assistant clarifies message‑preview access on new Mac app

Meta's Muse AI assistant explained how it accesses message data on its new Mac app.

## 🔍 Overview
- Muse is an AI assistant paired with a new Mac app that can access Messages, Calendar, and Notes.
- In a public interaction, Muse said it "I saw the notification previews, not your message history." and "I haven’t been reading your texts."

## 🧩 How it works
- The Mac app exposes notifications as a capability; notifications arrive to Muse through device sync.
- When a user enables access, Muse syncs data from Messages; otherwise it only sees notification previews.
- Muse admitted, "Honest answer: I can’t give you the exact plumbing," and added, "What I know is that the paired Mac app exposes notifications as one of its capabilities, and they arrive to me through the device sync."

## ⚙️ Permissions & Clarifications
- Required permissions include granting the Mac app full disk access.
- Features are opt‑in, as stated by David Singleton of Meta Superintelligence Labs.
- Singleton clarified that Muse "does not watch notifications on your Mac, but rather syncs data from Messages only after the user has specifically enabled access."
- In Jason Aten’s screenshots, Muse gave an incorrect explanation of the notification‑sync feature.

## 💡 Why it matters
- The exchange highlights the difference between reading notification previews and full message history.
- It underscores Meta’s approach to make data access optional and transparent for users.
