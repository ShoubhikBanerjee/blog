# Good Neighbors series spotlights rural environmental collaborations

## 🔍 Overview
- Good Neighbors is a weekly series that zooms in on six distinct parts of the United States.
- Writer Katie Hill and photographer Isabelle Zhao travel to rural communities that find ways to acknowledge differences while protecting the air, water, and land they rely on.

## 📅 Launch Details
- The series launches September 17.
- Each edition can be read online (you can read each edition here).

## 🌱 Themes Explored
- Rural communities sideline differences to prioritize imminent threats.
- Illustrated scenes include:
  - A bend in a muddy river carrying coal‑mining runoff from Appalachian headwaters toward the Potomac.
  - The view from a Holiday Inn Express room on the Permian Basin: hotel parking lot, megachurch, pumpjacks, sky.
  - An oversized equipment garage where corn and soy farmers discuss soil health, cancer, and fertilizer in drinking water.
  - A small coastal town recovering from nine 100‑year floods in seven years.
  - The sprawling Wyoming sagebrush where a herd of pronghorn crosses land they cannot tell apart (federal, state, private).
  - Municipal meetings where commercial fishermen and environmental advocates clash over liquefied natural gas plants.
  - An incalculably large desert oasis where ranchers, rafting guides, farmers, law‑enforcement personnel, and self‑described radical leftists unite against a proposed border wall.

## 📡 Data Center Controversy
- A tidal wave of data center proposals is washing across flyover country, drawing more national attention than in the past decade.
- The New York Times quoted Milwaukee‑based comedian Charlie Berens calling mass loathing of data centers “the most bipartisan issue since beer.”
- Despite losing a massive corporate sponsorship for his activism, commenters on Berens’ Instagram say they “feel healed” and “love this corner of the Internet.”

## 🤝 Community Unity
- Rural areas have a long knack for sidelining differences to address urgent threats.
- The political “horseshoe” bends back: Democrats carry guns in their trucks, Republicans fret over soil health and songbird populations, and many residents distrust the establishment.
- Top‑down attempts to “revive” or “invest in” these places often leave them in worse shape or unaffordable for the people who actually live there.
