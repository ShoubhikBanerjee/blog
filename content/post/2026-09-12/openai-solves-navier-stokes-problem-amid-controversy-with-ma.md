# OpenAI Solves Navier-Stokes Problem Amid Controversy With Mathematicians

OpenAI has announced a solution to the Navier-Stokes problem, one of the seven Millennium Prize problems established by the Clay Mathematics Institute in 2000. The development has been accompanied by disputes involving NYU professor Tristan Buckmaster regarding the company's conduct and the use of researcher data.

## ⚙️ Key details
OpenAI utilized an advanced, unreleased model to find the solution to the Navier-Stokes problem, which concerns the flow of fluids. The process involved:
- **Compute resources**: Tens of millions of dollars of compute.
- **Agent scale**: Roughly 10,000 agents.
- **Timeframe**: 88 hours.

## 🔍 Overview
Tristan Buckmaster and Levent Alpöge, a researcher at Anthropic, had been pursuing a proof for the Navier-Stokes problem. OpenAI stated it decided to test its model after hearing researchers were making progress on Millennium Prize problems. 

Controversies have emerged regarding the interaction between OpenAI and the researchers:
- **Collaboration offers**: Buckmaster alleges he was offered "unlimited compute" and the opportunity to be the sole author of OpenAI's announcement paper if he excluded Alpöge from the work. Bubeck acknowledged offering resources to help Buckmaster complete his proof or take over the writing of OpenAI's paper, stating that OpenAI has made similar arrangements with other mathematicians.
- **Competing interests**: Bubeck stated that Alpöge's affiliation with Anthropic was a sticking point, questioning how OpenAI could have an internal project involving an Anthropic employee.
- **Data usage**: Buckmaster questioned if OpenAI benefited from his use of Codex. While OpenAI spokesperson Laurance Fauconnet categorically denied that prompts from the last two months influenced the system, the company previously acknowledged it could not rule out that data derived from product use was used to improve the model.

## 💡 Why it matters
The Millennium Prize problems are considered among the most formidable challenges in mathematics, with each carrying a $1 million bounty.
