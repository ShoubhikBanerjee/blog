# eBay Restricts Access for Third-Party AI Agents Including Meta Muse

eBay appears to have blocked third-party AI agents, including Meta’s Muse, from browsing its platform. While technology companies tout AI agents to find deals, book travel, and buy goods, retailers and service providers continue to block or restrict automated agents from accessing their sites.

## 🔍 Overview

Until recently, agents like Meta's Muse were able to browse eBay to pull listings, links, prices, shipping details, and condition notes for users. Over the weekend, Muse was unable to extract details from eBay, stating it was blocked from the site. The Verge re-created the experience using other search queries and found Muse directed users to outside sites mirroring eBay listings.

eBay previously updated its user agreement to ban tools like "buy-for-me agents" and "LLM-driven bots" that place orders without human review. Responding to inquiries about third-party limits, eBay spokesperson Sybille St. Arromand stated:

> "As it pertains to third-parties, we take a selective approach to how they access and interact with our marketplace. We have restricted agent access in certain cases, including unauthorized scraping and training on our data."

St. Arromand added that eBay is having ongoing discussions with potential partners to evaluate ways third-party agents could help customers discover inventory while preserving its end-to-end marketplace experience. Meta declined to comment.

## ⚙️ Retailer Approaches to AI Agents

Retailers have demonstrated varying levels of comfort regarding third-party AI agent access:

| Retailer / Platform | Access Level | Details |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Amazon | Fully Blocked | Blocks external agents entirely from browsing or shopping. |
| eBay | Restricted | Bans automated purchasing bots; restricted browsing agents like Muse for unauthorized scraping and training. |
| Target | Partially Restricted | Allows agents to successfully browse in some instances while blocking them in others. |
| Shopify | Open | Opened its catalog of a billion listings for shoppers to set up agents to purchase items on their behalf. |

## 💡 Challenges and Adoption Rates

Integrating AI agents into e-commerce presents operational friction because retail sites are designed for human limitations rather than bot swarms. In October, The Verge found that Muse could be used to hold listings hostage on consignment site The RealReal by continually adding products to a cart, prompting The RealReal to update its bot system.

Furthermore, current consumer usage of third-party AI shopping agents remains minimal:

* **Uber**: Fulfilled only 4 rides via third-party agents in Q3 and around 500 Uber Eats orders during Halloween week.
* **DoorDash**: Fulfilled only "several hundred" orders via third-party agents out of 993 million total quarterly orders.
