# California Senator Scott Wiener Wins Congress Seat, Eyes Federal AI Regulation

California state senator Scott Wiener has won the open U.S. House seat in San Francisco, positioning him to influence federal AI policy.

## 🗳️ Election Victory
- Won a tight congressional race for former Speaker of the House Nancy Pelosi’s open San Francisco seat.
- Declared the victor and was interviewed only a few hours later.

## 🔍 AI Policy Background
- AI regulation is a cornerstone of his political career.
- Has been increasingly focused on AI in recent years, adopting concerns of the AI safety movement.

## 📜 Legislative Record
- **SB 1047 (2024)** – Authored by Wiener; set to become the first real piece of frontier AI legislation before it was vetoed by California Governor Gavin Newsom.
- **SB 53** – A lighter‑touch bill sponsored by Wiener that Governor Newsom signed.
- Both bills have inspired proposed federal legislation.

| Bill | Status | Federal Influence |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| SB 1047 | Vetoed after being poised as first frontier AI law | Inspired proposed federal legislation |
| SB 53 | Signed into law (lighter‑touch) | Inspired proposed federal legislation |

## 🚀 Congressional Priorities
- Cited a METR report suggesting Anthropic achieved recursive self‑improvement as “very concerning” and said we are “quickly running out of time to regulate AI.”
- Hoped Congress would take decisive action before the end of the year, but noted that “does not appear to be happening.”
- Calls for “a strong, empowered, nimble federal regulator,” referencing Representative Ro Khanna’s proposal.
- Emphasizes whistleblower protections, testing and auditing requirements, and a kill‑switch requirement.
- Would be eager to serve on a congressional select committee on artificial intelligence if formed.
- Says “States are, as Brandeis says, laboratories” and should remain nimble while Congress acts “in a way that is truly meaningful and that meets the moment.”

## 🤝 Funding and Industry Relations
- Received over $3 million from Public First Action, a nonprofit that supports AI regulation and receives substantial funding from Anthropic.
- Defended the contribution, writing that he was “the first lawmaker in the country to introduce AI regulation and the first to pass it and get it implemented.”
- Opponents criticized him for being “too close to the AI industry,” which he called “shockingly cynical” and a false claim.

## 📈 Future Impact
- Warns that “We’re in a moment that is very dangerous in terms of the risk of loss of control over AI.”
- Stresses the need to “slow this down” because AI is “moving very quickly, and we could sleepwalk into a disaster.”
- Says dealing with “recursive self‑improvement” is “potentially a recipe for total loss of control.”
