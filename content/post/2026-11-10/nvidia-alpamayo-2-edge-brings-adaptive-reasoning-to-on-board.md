# NVIDIA Alpamayo 2 Edge Brings Adaptive Reasoning to On‑Board Autonomous Driving

NVIDIA announced an update to its Alpamayo series with Alpamayo 2 Edge, an adaptive reasoning vision‑language‑action (VLA) model designed for real‑time operation on the NVIDIA DRIVE AGX Thor platform.

## 🔍 Overview
- Alpamayo 2 Edge runs on a 2 B‑parameter Nemotron‑based reasoning backbone from NVIDIA Cosmos 3 Edge.
- It is paired with a diffusion action expert to turn surround‑camera observations, temporal context, and navigation inputs into Chain‑of‑Causation (CoC) reasoning traces and future driving trajectories.
- The model is built for edge deployment, preserving reasoning quality while fitting the inference budget of an onboard stack.

## 🧩 How it works
| Component | Role |
|---|---|
| Alpamayo 2 Edge | Adaptive VLA model that generates reasoning only when needed |
| Nemotron 2 B backbone (Cosmos 3 Edge) | Core reasoning engine |
| Diffusion action expert | Generates driving actions from reasoning traces |
| AlpaJudge (RB & RF) | Human‑calibrated evaluation and reward model for reasoning traces |
| AlpaGym | Closed‑loop reinforcement‑learning environment |

- Adaptive reasoning reduces unnecessary token generation, conserving inference compute during routine driving.
- Multi‑resolution inputs encode surround‑view observations online.
- Speculative decoding with DFlash2 accelerates online reasoning without sacrificing quality.
- Offline distillation uses CoC reasoning autolabels generated by Alpamayo 2 Super.
- Quantization and inference acceleration recipes are provided for NVIDIA TensorRT and TensorRT‑Edge‑LLM.

## ⚙️ Key details
- Post‑trained with closed‑loop RL in NVIDIA AlpaGym, improving closed‑loop driving performance by **48.9 %** over the best checkpoint without closed‑loop RL.
- Uses AlpaJudge to evaluate reasoning traces, select higher‑value training data, and provide RL rewards.
- The inference script consumes surround‑view camera streams, navigation text, and historical ego‑motion to output CoC reasoning and trajectory predictions.

## 📈 Performance
- In early simulation tests, Edge kept its lane in **more than twice** as many runs as Alpamayo 1.5, recovered from lane offsets **~40 %** more often, and planned roughly **twice** as fast as a hand‑tuned Alpamayo 1.5 server on the same GPU.
- Zero‑shot evaluation showed a **13.6 %** reduction in displacement error (minADE) and a **46.1 %** reduction in lateral prediction error (RMSE at 3 s) compared to Alpamayo 2 Super, despite sensor differences.
- Fine‑tuning on proprietary driving data delivered an additional **44.2 %** reduction in displacement error versus the Alpamayo 2 Edge baseline.
- Within **48 hours**, Alpamayo 2 Edge ran at **10 Hz** on AGX Thor for real‑time planning.
- Early access users reported running closed‑loop on NuRec reconstructions of London street recordings within days of receiving the model.

## 🚀 Availability
- Early adopters were able to move from model receipt to first real‑vehicle tests **within a few days**.
- The repository includes an inference script and recipes for quantization and acceleration, enabling developers to integrate reasoning‑based driving into an onboard stack.
