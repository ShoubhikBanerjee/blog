# JPMorgan Chase Warns of Data Breach via Third-Party Cyberattack

J.P. Morgan Chase announced that it suffered a data breach following a cyberattack on a third-party service provider, which exposed customers' personal information and marketing data. The bank stated that its services were not disrupted and the incident is unrelated to a service disruption that occurred earlier in the week.

## 🏦 Banking Sector Impact

J.P. Morgan Chase Bank is the largest U.S. bank by assets, with about $4.1 trillion as of June 2026, and its consumer banking division reports about 78 million active digital customers. The bank's announcement on X stated, "We have learned that third parties who provide services to the firm were the target of a cyber attack." The bank further disclosed, "Based on our investigation so far, we believe the attackers accessed certain marketing data held by those vendors, which may include some customer names and email addresses."

## 💳 Payment Networks Affected

Visa and Mastercard confirmed that they were involved in third-party incidents, though both have not confirmed any impact on customers or their core payment networks.

* **Visa**: A spokesperson told BleepingComputer that the incident is "associated with an incident involving a third-party service provider in Asia Pacific." Visa stated, "Visa's systems were not involved and were not impacted. No payments data such as card numbers were affected."
* **Mastercard**: The company confirmed it was "informed of an incident at a third-party provider that resulted in the exposure of limited amount of email addresses – non-payments information – from a small subset of cardholders enrolled in a specific benefit program." A spokesperson assured that "There is no impact to the Mastercard payment network or the ability for people to use their cards."

## 🏭 Third-Party Provider

On November 11, 2026, the n0n ransomware group claimed responsibility for an attack on Qubole, a cloud data analytics company owned by Idera, suspected to be the third-party provider used by the financial institutions. Qubole is used for cloud data analytics, automated processing, real-time analysis, and reporting.

## 📊 Stolen Data and Extortion

Between October 14 and 26, 2026, the n0n ransomware group allegedly used compromised Qubole credentials to access customers' AWS environments through IAM roles configured to grant Qubole access. The group claimed to have stolen 53.9 GB across 30,743 files directly from Qubole, including customer contracts, pricing records, staff and trial-user information, and production data. Additionally, they claimed to hold nearly one petabyte of data from Qubole's customer networks. The n0n group leaked the claimed data on its extortion site on November 16, 2026, stating that their extortion demands were ignored after contacting Qubole and the affected companies.
