# TfL data breach impacts 265,000 Oyster photocard customers

Transport for London (TfL) announced that approximately 265,000 Oyster photocard customers may have had their account details exposed in a security incident discovered on November 16, 2026. The breach was identified after customers reported receiving emails from TfL's mail systems requesting them to create an account.

## 🔍 Overview
TfL operates London's public transport network, including the Underground, Overground, buses, trams, and the Elizabeth line, recording 3.53 billion passenger journeys in 2025/26. The Oyster photocard website has been taken offline, and data breach notifications are being sent to impacted individuals. The incident has been contained, and TfL has notified the UK's Information Commissioner's Office (ICO).

## ⚙️ Key Details
The data leak involved the following personal information:
- Full names
- Phone numbers
- Email addresses
- Home addresses
- Dates of birth
- Security questions and answers

TfL's assessment indicates that passwords, bank and payment card details, journey information, and the names and dates of birth of children with Zip Oyster cards were not compromised. No evidence of misuse has been found so far.

## 💡 Why It Matters
The exposure of security questions and answers is particularly concerning because these details may be used for account access restoration. Customers who received the suspicious email should ignore it and remain vigilant for further activity. Once the Oyster photocard portal is restored, all photocard customers will be prompted to change their password, security question, and answer.

| Recommendation | Detail |
| :--- | :--- |
| Account Security | Change passwords, security questions, and answers on Oyster photocard |
| Cross-Site Security | Rotate security questions used at other sites |
| General Hygiene | Change passwords on other services where the same information was used |
| Reporting | Report suspicious activity to TfL Customer Services immediately |

Oyster photocards remain valid for travel. Customers who need to renew their cards or check journey history can contact TfL's Customer Services.
