# New Poll Shows Strong Public Opposition to AI Data Centers

A new poll released Tuesday by the New York Times and Siena University confirms that AI and data centers are highly unpopular among voters. In the survey of 1,503 likely voters conducted in early September, a strong majority expressed opposition to building the infrastructure required to power artificial intelligence technology.

## 📊 Poll findings and opposition levels

* **Strong opposition:** 61 percent of likely voters said they oppose the construction of data centers to power AI technology, while only 14 percent said they strongly support it.
* **Limits versus bans:** Among the surveyed voters who oppose data centers, 56 percent said they support placing limits on them, while 38 percent preferred a total ban.
* **Historical context:** This matches previous trends, including an NBC News poll in March that showed AI polling lower than ICE, and a Gallup poll in May where seven in ten Americans opposed the local construction of AI data centers.

## 🧩 Key concerns driving opposition

Among voters who oppose AI data center construction, the poll identified several primary concerns driving their stance:

| Reason for Opposition | Percentage of Opposed Voters |
| :--- | :--- |
| Environment/water usage | 32% |
| Impact on local communities | 21% |
| General distrust or dislike of AI | 18% |
| Regulation concerns | 10% |
| Economy | 9% |

## ⚙️ Political and demographic breakdowns

* **Voter alignment:** The opposition split varies by political alignment. About 49 percent of 2024 Donald Trump voters support construction compared to 45 percent who oppose it. In contrast, 74 percent of Kamala Harris voters and 67 percent of non-voters lean toward opposition.
* **Party trust:** Neither major political party holds a significant edge on handling the issue. Out of 554 respondents, 42 percent trust Republicans, 40 percent trust Democrats, and 18 percent did not know or refused to answer.
* **Midterm priority:** Despite the high levels of opposition, AI and data centers ranked low on the list of issues determining midterm votes, registering at less than one percent for most demographics. An exception was found among younger voters (ages 18-29), where three percent chose it as their most important issue over options like the economy, foreign policy, inflation, and opposition to Trump/Republicans.
