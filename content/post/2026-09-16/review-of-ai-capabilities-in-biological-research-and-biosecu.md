# Review of AI Capabilities in Biological Research and Biosecurity Risks

A review paper submitted on September 14, 2026, analyzes the impact of artificial intelligence on biological research and the associated biosecurity risks across digital-to-physical workflows.

## 🔍 Overview
AI is reshaping biological research through several integrated technologies:

| Technology | Capability |
| :--- | :--- |
| General-purpose LLMs | Retrieve and integrate scientific information, support experimental planning, and computational analysis |
| Biological foundation models | Predict, optimize, and generate proteins, genes, and genome-scale sequences |
| Agentic systems | Coordinate multistep research tasks |
| Automated laboratories | Partially close the design-build-test-learn cycle |

## ⚙️ Key details
The review identifies a gap between digital capabilities and physical execution:
* Frontier systems have exceeded expert baselines on in-silico and screening-evasion benchmarks.
* Controlled wet-laboratory studies indicate that physical execution and tacit knowledge remain substantial barriers.
* AI uplift currently primarily affects digital rather than physical tasks.

## 💡 Why it matters
While these technologies can benefit biotechnology, public health, and medicine, they introduce biological threats throughout the pipeline, including information gathering, biological design, procurement, synthesis, testing, scale-up, and potential release.

Biosecurity risk is determined by several factors:
* The capabilities of the AI.
* The intent and expertise of the user.
* Access to materials and laboratory tools.
* Existing safeguards.

Additionally, the review notes that alignment techniques for general-purpose models transfer poorly to biological models, leading to a proposed "defense-in-depth" governance model that links capability thresholds to proportionate responsibilities.
