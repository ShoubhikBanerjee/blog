# Experian Study Finds Consumers Comfortably Delegating Financial Tasks to AI Agents

A new study released by data and technology company Experian reveals that consumers are ready to transition from using artificial intelligence for information gathering to allowing AI agents to support and execute financial transactions. The research, titled "AI in Risk: The Rise of Agentic Commerce" and conducted by Forrester Consulting, shows that more than half of surveyed consumers are comfortable letting AI agents apply for credit on their behalf.

## 🔍 Overview of the Study
Experian commissioned Forrester Consulting in July 2026 to conduct the study. The research surveyed 6,247 credit-active, digitally literate consumers across 13 EMEA and Asia Pacific markets:
* Australia
* China
* Denmark
* Germany
* India
* Italy
* Malaysia
* New Zealand
* Norway
* Singapore
* South Africa
* Spain
* Turkey

The respondents represented a balanced mix of generations and employment groups, selected based on their recent experience using digital financial services.

## 📈 Consumer Attitudes Toward AI Agents
The study highlights how consumers are shifting from utilizing Large Language Models (LLMs) to understand financial products to delegating more complex financial journeys to AI agents. Consumers are embracing these tools because they see practical value, such as saving time and making more informed choices.

| Consumer Perception / Attitude | Percentage of Respondents |
| :--- | :--- |
| Trust LLMs to compare loans across providers | 82% |
| Believe AI agents can compare more options than they can manually | 85% |
| Say AI can save them money by finding better prices or rates | 84% |
| Believe AI agents can help avoid missing details like hidden fees or contract terms | 83% |
| Believe AI can reduce decision fatigue by handling research and routine tasks | 81% |
| Comfortable with AI agents applying for credit on their behalf | 54% |

"Consumers are already changing the way they engage with financial services," said Mariana Pinheiro, CEO of Experian EMEA & Asia Pacific. 

The findings show that what began as using LLMs to better understand financial products is already moving towards asking AI agents to help them navigate complex financial journeys.

## ⚙️ Levels of Autonomy and Trust
While consumers see the practical value of AI agents, they maintain varying comfort levels regarding delegation and autonomy:
* **Rules-Based Action:** 23% of respondents would allow an AI agent to act only when certain pre-agreed rules are met.
* **Full Autonomy:** Only 5% of respondents are comfortable giving an AI agent full autonomy.
* **Trusted Providers:** 75% of respondents stated they would feel more comfortable using AI connected to a financial institution they already trust.

## 💡 Preparing for Agentic Commerce
As consumer-side AI agents begin executing more tasks, financial institutions must prepare systems to support secure interactions with lender-side platforms. Critical areas of focus will include:
* Identity verification
* Consent management
* Fraud prevention
* Explainability

To assist with this transition, Experian is offering Agent Trust, a capability designed to enable secure interactions between consumers, AI agents, and businesses. This capability is part of Experian's broader suite of advanced identity, fraud prevention, and decisioning tools designed to help organizations build trusted AI-assisted financial services.
