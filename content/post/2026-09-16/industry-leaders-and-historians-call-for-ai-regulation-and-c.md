# Industry Leaders and Historians Call for AI Regulation and Caution

Prominent technology executives and researchers are advocating for the regulation of artificial intelligence, citing long-term risks and the need for proactive government oversight.

## 💡 Why it matters
Concerns regarding autonomous systems date back several decades:
* In 1863, Samuel Butler warned that intelligent self-replicating machines could replace humans.
* In 1951, Alan Turing stated that undying machines would eventually "take control" and "outstrip our feeble powers."
* In 2000, Bill Joy described self-replicating robots as potentially more dangerous than nuclear weapons.

## ⚙️ Key details
Various industry leaders have called for specific regulatory actions and precautions:

| Person | Position/Affiliation | Stated View or Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Elon Musk | X CEO / OpenAI Investor | Called AI "potentially more dangerous than nukes" and said we must be "proactive about regulation instead of reactive." |
| Sundar Pichai | Google CEO | Stated "there is no question in my mind that artificial intelligence needs to be regulated." |
| Brad Smith | Microsoft President | Opened the door to voluntary regulation in a speech at the Brookings Institution. |
| Mark Zuckerberg | Meta Founder | Suggested his company should be regulated following the Cambridge Analytica scandal. |
| Eric Horvitz | Microsoft Chief Scientific Officer | Led researchers in 2009 to consider policies to constrain behaviors of autonomous systems. |

Additionally, a letter published by the Future of Life Institute and signed by Musk, among others, stated that if a pause on AI cannot be enacted quickly, "governments should step in and institute a moratorium."
