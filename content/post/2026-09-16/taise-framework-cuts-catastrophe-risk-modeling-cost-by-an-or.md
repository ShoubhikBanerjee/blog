# TAISE framework cuts catastrophe risk modeling cost by an order of magnitude

A new study introduces the TAISE framework, which repurposes AI weather forecasting models to generate extreme weather sequences at a fraction of the cost of traditional catastrophe risk models.

## 🔍 Overview
- Traditional catastrophe (CAT) risk models rely on costly manual construction, an approach largely unchanged since the 1990s.
- As climate extremes intensify, this creates mounting challenges to the entire risk transfer chain.

## 🧩 How it works
- TAISE repurposes existing AI weather forecasting models.
- Through self‑iterative generation, it produces continuous global atmospheric fields.
- Extreme weather events emerge from these continuous fields.

## ⚙️ Key details
- A proof‑of‑concept experiment demonstrates an order‑of‑magnitude reduction in computational cost compared with conventional methods.
- The approach captures temporal continuity and cross‑regional correlations that are absent in snapshot‑based approaches.

## 💡 Why it matters
- Findings suggest a pathway toward democratising catastrophe risk quantification.
- Enables dynamic, comprehensive portfolio assessment for insurers, reinsurers, ILS fund managers, and public‑sector risk managers.
