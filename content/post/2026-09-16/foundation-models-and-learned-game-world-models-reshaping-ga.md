# Foundation Models and Learned Game-World Models Reshaping Game Development Lifecycle

Foundation models and advances in learned game-world models are reshaping AI across the game lifecycle, moving beyond simply playing games to supporting design, development, and runtime experiences.

## 🔍 Overview
Recent systems now model players and game dynamics, support the development process, adapt player-facing experiences at runtime, and evaluate resulting artifacts. These applications are organized into six roles based on the immediate use of the AI output:

| Role | Application |
| :--- | :--- |
| Playing and acting | AI agents playing games |
| Modeling players and games | Modeling player behavior and game dynamics |
| Designing games | Supporting the design process |
| Building and maintaining games | Supporting development and maintenance |
| Generating and adapting at runtime | Adapting player-facing experiences |
| Testing and evaluating games | Evaluating resulting artifacts |

## 🧩 How it works
Cross-role connections link these different AI applications:
* Trajectories are used to train world models.
* Learned environments provide experience for agents.
* Design specifications drive executable implementations.
* Feedback from playing or testing guides revision.

## ⚙️ Key details
Despite these advances, several challenges remain regarding standardization and transferability:
* **Setting-Specific Constraints:** Control schemes, rules, engine interfaces, state representations, and player contexts often remain specific to a setting, meaning downstream claims require validation in the target setting.
* **Evaluation Gaps:** While evaluation is most standardized for bounded game playing and selected learned environments, other areas are less established, including:
    * Persistent state in learned worlds
    * Repeated software revision
    * Validated player modeling
    * Sustained runtime adaptation
    * Representative automated testing

## 💡 Why it matters
The central challenge facing the field is the ability to reuse or transfer outputs and capabilities across these six roles while re-establishing evidence for their effectiveness within specific game contexts.
