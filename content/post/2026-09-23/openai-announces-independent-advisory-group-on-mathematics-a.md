# OpenAI Announces Independent Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence

OpenAI has announced the formation of the Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence (AGMAI), an independent panel of mathematicians tasked with advising the company and other AI firms on their interactions with mathematical research and the wider community.

## 🔍 Overview

The group consists of nine members described by researchers as "celebrities in the field," including individuals with MacArthur “genius grants” and multiple Fields Medals. Members are drawn from institutions such as Oxford, Cambridge, Harvard, and Stanford. The group is hosted by the Institute for Advanced Study (IAS) in Princeton, New Jersey.

## ⚙️ Key Details

OpenAI has stated the following regarding the group's operation:

* **Independence:** The group will operate independently, with the freedom to make its advice public and offer advice the company has not requested.
* **Compensation:** Members are not paid by OpenAI.
* **Access:** The group appears it will be granted early access to OpenAI’s research.
* **Governance:** The group can change its membership as it sees fit.

## 🧩 How it works

The AGMAI will provide guidance in several specific areas:

| Focus Area | Responsibility |
| :--- | :--- |
| Emerging Results | Help assess significance and advise on the coordination of dissemination |
| Research Standards | Advise on academic and professional standards of mathematical research |
| Tooling | Advise on how tools can support mathematical research and learning |

## 💡 Why it matters

According to the group's website, the panel was launched as an independent group after OpenAI approached some members about establishing an external advisory board. Martin Hairer, a professor at the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology in Lausanne (EPFL) and Imperial College London, stated that the organization is "genuinely independent of OpenAI and any other of the so-called ‘frontier’ AI labs." He noted that, aside from "obvious confidentiality requirements," members have not signed restrictive agreements regarding public statements.

However, the development has met with some skepticism:

* Simon Machado, a researcher at ETH Zurich, questioned the selection process for the nine members, stating the matter seemed "shrouded in mystery."
* Other concerns have been raised regarding whether a group of this small size possesses the breadth of expertise required to evaluate the highly specialized knowledge found at the frontiers of mathematics.
* Francesco Fournier-Facio, an incoming professor at Heriot-Watt University, described himself as "ambivalent," though he noted that greater engagement with mathematicians "can only make things better."
