# a16z Launches Horowitz Andreessen Academy for Silicon Valley Startup Pipeline

Venture capital firm Andreessen Horowitz (a16z) is creating the Horowitz Andreessen Academy, a pipeline designed for young people to build or join a Silicon Valley startup.

## 🔍 Overview
Led by a16z with $42 million in funding, the academy is described as a "highly selective school" that does not grant any accreditation or degree. It is designed mainly for recent high school graduates.

## 🧩 How it works
The program replaces traditional academic structures with a focus on industry experience:
* **Curriculum**: Short, high-impact classes led by tech figureheads, such as OpenAI CEO Sam Altman.
* **Practical Experience**: "Co-ops" that provide students with roles at tech companies.
* **Assessment**: There are no traditional grades, tests, or homework.

## ⚙️ Key details
Students in the program are provided with specific financial and technical resources:
* More than $50,000 in compute credits
* A $5,000 travel and research budget offered by a16z

## 🚀 Availability
* **Launch Date**: The first one-year program begins in the fall of 2027.
* **Cost**: The first program will be free.
* **Capacity**: Only 50 students will be accepted.
* **Requirements**: Students must move to San Francisco and arrange their own housing.

## 💡 Why it matters
The academy follows a trend of Silicon Valley billionaires, including Elon Musk, Mark Zuckerberg, and Jeff Bezos, investing in their own schools. a16z founders Marc Andreessen and Ben Horowitz have been critical of colleges; Andreessen reportedly called the Massachusetts Institute of Technology and Stanford "political lobbying operations fighting American innovation" in a group chat with tech leaders and White House officials.
