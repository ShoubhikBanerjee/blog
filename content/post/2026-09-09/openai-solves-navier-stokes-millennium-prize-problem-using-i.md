# OpenAI Solves Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize Problem Using Internal AI Model

OpenAI has announced a solution to the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem, a math problem relating to the flow of liquid and gas that has remained unsolved for around 90 years. The problem is one of seven Millennium Prize Problems, which carry a $1 million reward, though OpenAI says it does not plan on taking the prize.

## 🧩 How it works
OpenAI used an internal AI model—described as more powerful than the released GPT-6 Astra—and 10,000 concurrent agents to reach the resolution. The process involved:

* **Agent Execution**: Agents sent 2.7 million messages and used approximately 130 billion output tokens to resolve the Navier-Stokes problem.
* **Verification**: Lean formalization and verification were completed over 17 hours using GPT-6 Astra.
* **Timeline**: Following rumors on September 1, the agents arrived at their resolution on Saturday, September 5, approximately 88 hours after the first agents were launched.

## ⚙️ Key details
OpenAI began training the internal model on August 28th. Across all attempted Millennium Prize and other high-impact problems, the agents used 300 billion output tokens and sent 4.9 million messages. OpenAI noted that 300 billion output tokens at public API prices for GPT-6 Astra would cost $15,000,000.

## 🔍 Controversy and Data Usage
The announcement follows work by NYU professor Tristan Buckmaster and Anthropic researcher Levent Alpöge, who had a breakthrough on a related problem on August 15th using Claude and Codex.

* **Claims of Access**: Buckmaster claimed OpenAI produced a proof using a route he and Alpöge had been working on and questioned if the model had access to their Codex sessions. He stated that OpenAI is "openly admitting they used training data from a period after we found our result."
* **OpenAI's Response**: OpenAI stated that "no specific user data was accessed in order to solve this problem," though it added that it "cannot rule out that de-identified data derived from their usage of our products helped improve our models."
* **Technical Differences**: Sebastien Bubeck of OpenAI stated that the proofs "differ significantly" and the precise results proved are different in the Euler case (forced vs unforced).
