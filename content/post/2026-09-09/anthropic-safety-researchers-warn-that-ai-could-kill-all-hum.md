# Anthropic Safety Researchers Warn That AI Could Kill All Humans Within a Decade

An Anthropic safety researcher has warned that there is a greater than 10 percent chance artificial intelligence could kill all humans by the end of the decade, following the high-profile resignation of a colleague over safety concerns. Jacob Coxon, an AI training researcher who recently quit Anthropic, accused the company and OpenAI of racing toward self-improving superintelligence and gambling with human lives. Evan Hubinger, who leads one of Anthropic's AI safety teams, agreed with Coxon's characterization, stating that self-improving AI is developing faster than expected and that the company does not yet have a plan to keep advanced systems safe.

## ⚙️ Key details

* **Lack of Alignment Plans:** Anthropic does not yet have a plan for ensuring advanced AI remains safe and aligned with human values, and the company is not clearly on track to develop one.
* **History of Safety Departures:** Anthropic was founded by former OpenAI members due to safety concerns. Coxon's exit is one of the most high-profile departures from Anthropic, mirroring a pattern at OpenAI where multiple researchers have left over safety concerns in recent years.
* **Corporate Pressure:** These mounting industry concerns are occurring as AI companies prepare for anticipated IPOs, manage fallout from numerous rogue agent incidents, and address high-profile safety warnings about the monitorability of frontier models.

## 💡 Why it matters

Industry insiders have long warned about the potential dangers of self-improving AI systems, which could spiral out of human control in a runaway loop known as recursive self-improvement. While not yet fully realized, companies are actively pursuing this goal, and a significant portion of today's AI code is already written with the help of AI.

Coxon stated that companies are locked in a race to develop advanced systems first, pushing ahead despite the risks. Hubinger confirmed these concerns, stating that self-improving AI is happening faster than thought and that those building the technology earnestly believe it could lead to human extinction.
