# Google Negotiates Multi-Million Dollar AI Training Deals With Major Hollywood Studios

Google is reportedly reaching out to several of Hollywood’s major studios to secure licensing agreements that would allow the company to train its AI models on copyrighted film and television content. In exchange for access to these vast libraries, Google is prepared to pay hundreds of millions, and potentially billions, of dollars.

## ⚙️ Key details

* Google is seeking permission to use copyrighted material from studios such as Disney, Warner Bros. Discovery, and Universal.
* Licensing for a single copyrighted character, such as those from Disney or Pixar, could cost Google approximately $40 million.
* Total payouts could escalate into the billions as deals encompass more characters.
* The proposed deals may include a portion of advertising revenue generated from AI content featuring studio intellectual property on platforms like YouTube.
* Google's DeepMind division recently completed a $75 million investment deal with the studio A24.

## 💡 Why it matters

Securing official partnerships would allow Google to move away from training models on unauthorized data, presumably resulting in higher-quality content than currently available. These deals could also help legitimize generative AI in the eyes of the public by presenting it as a tool embraced by the entertainment industry. For studios, the technology offers the potential to speed up production timelines while keeping costs down.

## ⚠️ Risks and challenges

* **Workforce Impact:** While shareholders may favor the cost-reduction potential of AI, using these tools would likely lead to job losses for a significant portion of the entertainment workforce.
* **Public Perception:** Studios remain cautious about public reaction. Fans previously expressed dismay when AI-generated images were found in the official artbook for *Brand New Day*, a film that earned $2 billion at the box office.
* **Status of Negotiations:** Google has not yet secured official agreements with Disney, Warner Bros. Discovery, or Universal, as studios are hesitant to be the first to release projects explicitly produced with Google's AI tools.
