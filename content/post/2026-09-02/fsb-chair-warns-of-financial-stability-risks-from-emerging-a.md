# FSB Chair Warns of Financial Stability Risks From Emerging AI Models

Financial Stability Board (FSB) Chair and Governor of the Bank of England Andrew Bailey has warned that emerging AI models pose a growing threat to financial stability and economic growth. In a letter sent on 28 August to G20 finance ministers and central bankers ahead of their meeting in Asheville, North Carolina, US, Bailey highlighted the risks of a new generation of sophisticated artificial intelligence systems.

## 🔍 Overview

The warning comes amid a complex global economic environment. Several factors are currently shaping this landscape:
* Conflict in the Middle East is driving up energy prices and fuelling inflation.
* Large-scale AI investments in the United States are adding further inflationary pressure.
* Unprecedented trade decisions by US President Donald Trump are contributing to an increasingly unpredictable economic environment.

Within this scenario, emerging AI systems are introducing new systemic risks to the global economy.

## 🧩 Key capabilities and risks

A new generation of highly sophisticated AI models is capable of identifying and exploiting digital vulnerabilities in complex systems, such as banking infrastructure and other sensitive servers. The consequences of these actions could be potentially catastrophic for the economy.

In his letter, Bailey noted:
* "Frontier AI may have the ability materially to alter the speed, scale and economics of cyber risk, which could undermine market confidence system-wide, especially due to highly concentrated third-party service providers."
* The threat environment will be "characterised by a higher volume of vulnerabilities and a faster pace of patching," requiring firms and authorities to adapt their safety systems.

## 💡 Why it matters

The implications of these advanced AI risks are inherently global and difficult to contain within single nations:
* **No national borders:** Bailey warned that "AI will not respect national borders," meaning any financial stability disruption can quickly have a global impact regardless of where it begins.
* **Jurisdictional vulnerabilities:** Differences in legal frameworks, cyber capability, resilience, and recovery capacity across different jurisdictions could have consequences far beyond the jurisdiction in which an incident originates, and may themselves become a source of vulnerability.

With these challenges ahead, the next G20 meeting is scheduled to occur in Miami, Florida on 14-15 December.
