# Rise of AI Washing Enforcement and Litigation

Regulators and private plaintiffs have increasingly targeted companies for overstating their AI capabilities, a practice known as "AI washing." Recent securities class actions against Tempus AI and GitLab highlight this trend, with allegations of misleading investors about AI functionality and monetization potential.

## 🔍 Overview
- AI washing involves overstating, mischaracterizing, or failing to disclose the realities and risks of AI capabilities.
- It is considered a form of false advertising.
- Enforcement actions have continued under the current administration, treating such misrepresentations as fraud.

## ⚖️ Key Legal Actions
- AI-related securities class actions more than doubled from 2023 to 2024.
- Two notable cases involve Tempus AI and GitLab.

### Tempus AI, Inc.
- A putative securities class action was filed on June 12, 2025, in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Illinois.
- Allegations: violations of Sections 10(b) and 20(a) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 and Rule 10b-5 thereunder.
- Tempus was accused of misleading investors about its business model, data licensing agreements, and AI capabilities from August 6, 2024, to May 27, 2025.
- The complaint alleged overstated contract values, including non-binding opt-ins and related-party transactions.
- A short-seller report by Spruce Point Capital Management on May 28, 2025, triggered a 19% stock price decline.
- The case was voluntarily dismissed on November 17, 2025, without prejudice.

### GitLab Inc.
- A putative securities class action was filed on September 4, 2024, in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California.
- Allegations: violations of Sections 10(b) and 20(a) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 and Rule 10b-5 thereunder.
- GitLab was accused of misleading investors about the effectiveness, demand, and monetization potential of its AI features from June 6, 2023, to March 4, 2024.

## 🏛️ Regulatory Context
- Regulators, including the SEC, DOJ, and FTC, have emphasized AI-related enforcement risks.
- Former SEC Chair Gary Gensler repeatedly warned against AI washing.
- Current SEC Chair Paul Atkins has signaled a preference for traditional materiality principles over AI-specific mandates.

## 💡 Why It Matters
- AI washing is increasingly treated as straightforward fraud.
- Investor enthusiasm for AI has led to heightened scrutiny of corporate disclosures.
- Enforcement actions and litigation are likely to continue as AI adoption grows.
