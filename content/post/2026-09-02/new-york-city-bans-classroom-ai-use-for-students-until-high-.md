# New York City Bans Classroom AI Use for Students Until High School

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani has announced a new policy that will ban younger schoolchildren from using AI in classrooms. The one-year moratorium, effective in the 2026-2027 school year, will impact about 600,000 public school students in 2-K through eighth grade.

## ⚙️ Key Details

The new rules introduced by NYC's Department of Education establish strict boundaries on technology inside classrooms:

* **AI Classroom Ban:** Students are kept from using AI in classrooms until they enter high school.
* **Companion Chatbots:** Chatbots designed specifically for companionship and mental support are banned across all grades.
* **Grading Restrictions:** Teachers are barred from using AI tools to grade assignments.
* **Device and Screen Limits:** Individual screen use is banned until third grade. The department is also issuing recommended screen time limits, including a maximum of 45 minutes per day for middle schoolers.
* **Device Ban Context:** These changes follow NYC's public school system introducing a bell-to-bell ban on personal internet-enabled electronic devices, including smartphones, for the 2025-2026 school year.

## 🧩 Exceptions and High School Rules

High school students will only be permitted to use AI in specific instances. To support this transition, they will be offered AI literacy classes twice a year to help them "think critically" before they come to "rely on it."

Exceptions to the ban may be granted for:
* AI tools that help students with disabilities or multilingual students.
* Particular purposes like computer science programs.
* Teachers using AI for lesson planning.

## 🧪 Limited AI Pilot Program

A "limited AI pilot" program will also be offered at up to five classes in a "small number" of high school classrooms. This program will feature tools vetted to meet safety and data privacy standards while "keeping students as the primary thinker." These tools feature set time limits for usage and include:

| Tool | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- |
| Quill | English language support |
| Edia | Math |
| Brisk Teaching | Guiding study practice |
| Playlab | Multi-subject support |
| Intel AI-Ready Schools | Multi-subject support |

## 💡 Why It Matters

NYC district leaders state that the new classroom AI moratorium is one of the most restrictive policies in the US. However, countries such as Norway, Sweden, and the Netherlands have implemented nationwide changes to address similar concerns about how technology impacts developing minds.

"Children need teachers and human connection in order to learn and grow. They need to develop skills alongside their peers, build relationships with educators, and wrestle with tough problems on their own," Mamdani said in a statement. "The tech industry wants us to believe that AI-powered early education is not only inevitable, but necessary. We do not see it that way."
