# Trump Administration Intervenes in New York Times Copyright Lawsuit Supporting OpenAI

The Trump administration has intervened in *The New York Times’* copyright lawsuit against OpenAI, filing a statement of interest in favor of the artificial intelligence laboratory. The administration is supporting OpenAI’s argument that training AI models on copyrighted text constitutes fair use, asserting that letting models train on copyrighted work will preserve American prosperity.

## ⚖️ The Government's Statement of Interest

In the newly filed statement of interest, US attorneys argued that restricting AI development would conflict with basic legal frameworks. Key assertions in the filing include:

* **Narrowing Fair Use:** US attorneys wrote that "The New York Times seeks to narrow fair-use doctrine to exclude the training of OpenAI’s large language models (LLMs)."
* **Hampering Progress:** The filing stated that "That result would be inconsistent with basic copyright law principles and severely hamper ‘the Progress of Science and useful Arts.’"
* **Scientific Breakthroughs:** The attorneys wrote that "LLMs are already helping researchers across fields achieve major breakthroughs."
* **Economic Impact:** The administration warned that "constraining LLM development under a misunderstanding of fair use doctrine would thwart such creative and scientific progress while hindering American prosperity and economic mobility."
* **The Fair Use Standard:** While the attorneys noted that "The fair-use inquiry hinges on the specific facts and uses at issue in each case," they argued that "it would be problematic — and legally incorrect — to impose broad copyright liability that would generally render training of AI models impermissible without licensing." They concluded that LLM training is "consistent with that creative ‘progress’ that is the basic constitutional objective of copyright itself."

## 🔍 Background on the Lawsuit

Originally filed in December 2023, the landmark lawsuit alleges that OpenAI unlawfully trained its AI systems on articles from *The New York Times*. The media outlet is seeking "billions of dollars" in damages from both OpenAI and Microsoft. 

This case has major implications, as it could set a precedent for other media companies concerned about AI systems training on their work. Copyright disagreements between media outlets and AI firms have intensified in recent years, leading to other active lawsuits from the Center for Investigative Reporting, Chicago Tribune, and New York Daily News.

## 🧩 Policy and Legal Context

To advance its policy objectives, the Trump administration has leaned heavily on filing statements of interest in private litigation—a strategy that one official has described as "incredibly" successful. The administration has previously made the case that AI training should count as fair use in its National AI Legislative Framework.

Additionally, Donald Trump holds a personal animus toward *The New York Times*, against which he is currently pursuing an active defamation lawsuit.
