# Google to Invest €13 Billion in AI Infrastructure and Nuclear Power in Finland

Google has announced a €13 billion ($15.1 billion) investment in AI infrastructure in Finland, marking the company's largest single investment in the EU to date. This initiative includes a massive deal to purchase nuclear power in the Nordic country over the next two decades.

## ⚙️ Key details

Scheduled for disbursement in 2027 and 2028, the investment will support Google's map, search, and chatbot services through the following projects:

* Data centers
* Electrical grid improvement
* Clean energy and battery projects

Google is expanding its existing site in Hamina and opening data center operations in three new municipalities:

| Location | Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| Hamina | Expanding site on a "remarkable level" |
| Kajaani | Opening data center operations |
| Muhos | Opening data center operations |
| Vaala | Opening data center operations |

## 💡 Why it matters

**Economic and Employment Impact**
Google claims the investment will create 37,000 jobs (7,000 permanent and the remainder during construction) and contribute approximately €3.6 billion to Finland's GDP.

**Energy Infrastructure**
To manage energy needs, Google has entered a purchase agreement for 50% of the energy output from one of Finland's two nuclear power plants for the next 22 years. Regarding this "bring your own power" (BYOP) strategy, Ruth Porat, Google President and Chief Investment Officer, noted, "This is Google's first nuclear energy deal outside of the United States."

**Operational Continuity**
Plant operator Fortum stated that this deal allows the Loviisa Nuclear Power Plant to continue operating until its licenses end in 2050; otherwise, the plant—which provides about 10% of Finland's electricity—would not have been able to operate past 2030. Following the news, Fortum shares rose 15.5% to their highest level in over four years.

**Energy Demand**
Network operator Fingrid forecasts a 40% jump in energy consumption over coming years due to Finland's data center boom.
