# New Research Examines Safety Risks of Autonomous Machine to Machine AI Communication

A new academic study by researcher R. Rajalakshmanan, published online on September 10, 2026, in the journal IJERT, explores the safety and governance challenges associated with autonomous AI-to-AI communication. The research paper focuses on what happens when autonomous software agents are permitted to communicate and coordinate at machine speed while human operators have limited visibility into their internal representations, messages, objectives, or actions.

## 🔍 Overview
The academic paper, published in IJERT (Volume 15, Issue 09, September – 2026, ISSN 2278-0181) under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License, is intended for educational and seminar discussion. The document distinguishes established technical findings from speculative claims, specifically noting that:
* It does not assume AI systems routinely create secret encrypted languages or communicate through hidden radio frequencies.
* It focuses on what is technically possible and demonstrated in research regarding autonomous agents acting with limited human supervision.
* It highlights the importance of the responsible use of AI tools in preparing and reviewing academic material.

## 🧩 How it works
Artificial intelligence has transitioned from rule-based software and statistical prediction systems to highly capable foundation models, autonomous agents, multimodal systems, and AI-enabled tools that can plan and execute multi-step tasks. 
* **AI Agents**: These are software systems that can perceive information, reason or plan, use tools, maintain state, and take actions toward a goal.
* **Agent Interactions**: These interactions may involve negotiation, task allocation, tool use, and information exchange.
* **Digital Protocols**: Rather than using mysterious transmission mechanisms, software agents exchange data through ordinary digital channels and protocols.
* **Interpretability Challenges**: The actual safety problem stems from the fact that AI agents can produce machine-generated codes, compressed representations, or learned communication conventions that are difficult for humans to interpret.

## ⚙️ Key details
The study identifies several distinct risks associated with unsupervised, autonomous machine-to-machine interactions, alongside a proposed framework to manage them:

* **Risks**: Coordination failures, goal misalignment, deception, unsafe tool use, cyber abuse, privacy leakage, cascading errors, excessive autonomy, and the concentration of decision-making power.
* **Layered Safety Approach**: To address these issues, the paper proposes a safety framework involving monitoring, access control, human approval for high-impact actions, interpretable logs, red-team testing, evaluation of multi-agent behavior, and governance mechanisms.

## 💡 Why it matters
The rapid growth of generative AI has changed human-software interaction, allowing users to describe objectives in natural language and ask AI systems to reason about the necessary steps. The paper's conclusion argues that the ultimate objective of safety research should not be to halt AI development, but to ensure that increasingly capable AI systems remain observable, controllable, accountable, and aligned with human interests.
