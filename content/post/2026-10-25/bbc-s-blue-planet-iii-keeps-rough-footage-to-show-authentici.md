# BBC’s Blue Planet III Keeps Rough Footage to Show Authenticity in AI Age

BBC’s Blue Planet III is deliberately keeping imperfect footage to prove its authenticity in an era where AI‑generated polish can be a giveaway.

## 🔍 Overview
- BBC’s Blue Planet III is looking to prove its authenticity by leaving in the less-than‑perfect shots.
- Previous incarnations of the nature series were defined by their gorgeous, crystal‑clear footage that seemed almost too good to be real.
- One dead giveaway of AI is when things look too polished and seamless.

## 🎬 Production Choices
- To counter that, Blue Planet III goes warts and all.
- Blue Planet III is keeping the rough edges in the age of AI.
- BBC’s Blue Planet III is keeping in shots that would normally end up on the cutting room floor to prove it’s real.
- Examples of shots that were previously cut:
  - “the amount of plankton and things” floating in the water would have caused them to cut a shot.
  - “we might have framed out a boat or a buoy or gone: ‘Oh, actually … the water conditions are not really good enough for this.’”

## 🐟 Notable Wildlife Moments
- The series features incredible displays of:
  - dolphins trapping fish by plugging holes with sea sponges,
  - inter‑species fish communication,
  - 200,000 crabs lining up in formation to feed.

## 💬 Executive Perspective
- White says that real ocean creatures “are probably more remarkable than AI could imagine.”

## ✨ Why it matters
- By leaving the rough edges, the series aims to show a more authentic, organic version of the oceans.
