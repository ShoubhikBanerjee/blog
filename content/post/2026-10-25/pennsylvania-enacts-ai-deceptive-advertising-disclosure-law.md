# Pennsylvania Enacts AI Deceptive Advertising Disclosure Law

On Thursday, Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro signed the Artificial Intelligence Deceptive Advertising Act into law, mandating disclosures on AI‑generated ads.

## 🔍 Overview
- Pennsylvania is one of the first states to require disclosures on AI content.
- The law applies to any ad — still image, video, audio, or text — that has been significantly modified or generated by artificial intelligence.
- Labels must appear in the same format as the ad itself.
- The requirement applies regardless of where the ad was created, so disclosures may appear in commercials aired in other states if they are expected to air in Pennsylvania.

## ⚙️ Requirements
| Ad Format | Disclosure Requirement |
|-----------|------------------------|
| Still image | Label in same format as the ad |
| Video | Label in same format as the ad |
| Audio | Label in same format as the ad |
| Text | Label in same format as the ad |

## ⚖️ Loopholes
- "Significantly modified" is a subjective standard.
- Carve‑outs exist for parody, satire, commentary, criticism, and anything that is "evidently fictional."
- The meaning of "evidently fictional" is unclear.
- The law requires disclosure only when synthetic content creates a false consumer perception about a product or service.
- What qualifies as “false” and who decides is left ambiguous.
- The office of State Senator Nick Pisciottano, who introduced the bill, did not immediately return a request for comment.

## 💡 Why it matters
- Labels must appear on TV commercials from multi‑billion‑dollar companies as well as on local flyers, such as restaurant wing‑special ads.
- Consumers receive clear information about AI‑generated or heavily modified content.
- The law could influence advertising practices beyond Pennsylvania because ads aired nationally may need to carry the label when Pennsylvania audiences are targeted.
