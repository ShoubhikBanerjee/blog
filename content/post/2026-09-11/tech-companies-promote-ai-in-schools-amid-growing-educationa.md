# Tech Companies Promote AI in Schools Amid Growing Educational Scrutiny

AI companies are currently pitching schools on the necessity of generative AI, providing resources and curricula—often pro bono—to ensure students do not fall behind in a competitive job market.

## 💡 Why it matters
According to New York Times education technology reporter Natasha Singer, this approach mirrors how the tech industry has built influence in classrooms for 15 years. In her book *Coding Kids*, Singer details how companies promoted computer science as a ticket to high-paying jobs while training students on their specific software to create future customers.

## ⚙️ Key details
Historically, major tech companies have integrated their tools directly into educational systems:

| Company | Educational Tool/Method |
| :--- | :--- |
| Apple | Swift curricula for AP Computer Science Principles |
| Microsoft | Minecraft curricula for AP Computer Science Principles |
| Google | Chromebooks and the Classroom app |
| Code.org | "Hour of Code" campaign and celebrity-led videos |

## 🔍 Current Status
While AI companies continue their push, several developments indicate increasing pushback:

* **School Bans:** New York City has banned AI use in elementary and middle schools for the coming year, and Los Angeles has implemented broader restrictions including high schoolers.
* **Hardware Shifts:** Some schools are removing Google Chromebooks.
* **Academic Findings:** Global research indicates that adding technology to the classroom often does not have a significant impact on actual learning.
* **Graduate Feedback:** Some recent graduates report feeling let down after being told to code their whole lives, only to find diminishing entry-level jobs.
