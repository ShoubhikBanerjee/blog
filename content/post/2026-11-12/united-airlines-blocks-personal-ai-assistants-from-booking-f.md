# United Airlines blocks personal AI assistants from booking flights

United Airlines announced it will not allow personal AI assistants to book tickets, citing privacy and regulatory concerns.

## 🔍 Overview
- United says it won’t let personal AI assistants book tickets on the airline, citing both privacy and regulatory concerns.
- United won’t let your AI agent book your flight.
- AI agents can’t attest that you don’t have explosives in your bags.
- While agents can still show users United flights, they won’t be able to actually complete the transaction on their behalf.

## ⚙️ How it works
- Agents are able to display flight options, but the final purchase step is blocked.
- The inability to attest that bags are free of explosives prevents agents from meeting federal disclosure rules.

## 📋 Policy details
- United is concerned that agents could expose customers’ information or make purchases they didn’t intend.
- United worries agents could “potentially skirt federal rules about the kind of information airlines need to share with customers — like having them attest that their bags don’t have explosives.”
- The company says it hasn’t yet seen many people try to use AI agents to book their flights, according to The Wall Street Journal.

## 🌐 Industry context
- Across sectors, businesses are grappling with whether to let AI agents transact on their sites, and United is not alone in blocking access for unauthorized agents.
- eBay appeared to block agents from browsing the site, and several major banks have also placed restrictions on them.
- The decision, and others like it, could limit the potential usefulness of newer personal AI assistants like Meta’s Muse and OpenAI’s Dots.

## 🔮 Future outlook
- United suggests it could reconsider its decision based on future advancements.
- “As third‑party AI agents advance, we see the opportunities they present. However, at the same time, allowing them to interact directly with a United account or booking could involve sensitive actions, and we want to make sure we have established protections for travelers first.”
- “To be honest, I don’t want to create another middleman,” United CEO Scott Kirby said at an event on Tuesday, according to the Journal.
