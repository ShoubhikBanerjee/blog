# Open-source AI paints watercolors by writing JavaScript code

On 23 August Surya Narreddi posted a beautiful video of watercolour paintings generated by a language model that writes JavaScript through **p5.brush**, a library that adds natural drawing tools to p5.js. The video went viral fast, with over **1.5 M views** at the time of writing.

## 🔍 Overview
- The model generates a program of about **150 lines of JavaScript** that paints the image.
- Output is **code**, which can be read, edited, and re‑run; the decision behind each brushstroke is visible.
- Style is shaped by a restriction to only **ten methods** of the p5.brush library.
- The resulting paintings look loose, imperfect, and handmade, contrasting with the statistically‑average pictures of typical image models.

## 🛠️ How it works
- **Training data**: a hand‑rated pool of reference images (initially close‑up flowers, later full compositions).
- **Reward design**: uses the same framing as the original blog; three reward mixes were trained and compared.
- **Reinforcement‑learning stack**: built with **TRL** and **OpenEnv**; the RL environment is provided in the repository.
- **Execution**: the generated JavaScript runs in p5.js via the p5.brush library, producing the final watercolor rendering.

## 📦 Open implementation
- The author reproduced the idea step‑by‑step, publishing:
  * Reference‑pool dataset
  * RL environment
  * Training scripts
  * Trained models
- All artifacts are open‑source; once two Hugging Face Spaces are up, the entire pipeline runs with a **single command**.
- The repository includes a “What I would try next” list that captures ideas not incorporated into the experiments.

## 💡 Why it matters
- The project revives the early generative‑AI‑art mindset where the medium and exploration were central, echoing works such as **DeepDream (2015)**, **Edmond de Belamy (2018)**, and the portraits of **Mario Klingemann**.
- By outputting editable code instead of a static image, the approach offers transparency and creative control.
- The viral response (over 1.5 M views) indicates strong public interest in AI‑generated art that retains a handcrafted aesthetic.
