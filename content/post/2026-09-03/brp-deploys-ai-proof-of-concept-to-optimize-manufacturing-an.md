# BRP Deploys AI Proof‑of‑Concept to Optimize Manufacturing and Cut Costs

BRP’s Chief Information Officer Stéphane Bilodeau explains how the company used AI to tackle a manufacturing inefficiency that traditional methods could not solve.

## 🔍 Overview
- AI strategy is defined as *solving complex problems that are worth solving*.
- The focus is on problems where *traditional approaches have not worked* and where the outcome creates *meaningful business value*.
- AI is treated as a *strategic capability* that requires strong internal teams and selective external partners.

## 🤖 AI Project Details
- The team first *spent time understanding the data and the process*.
- A *proof of concept was built within a few weeks*.
- Weekly governance asks two questions: *Is the problem still worth solving?* and *Can we solve it?*.
- If either answer is *no*, the effort stops – reasons may include insufficient data or missing technology.
- Upon completion, *finance validates the results* and the business decides on deployment.

## 📈 Business Impact
- The AI solution delivered a *significant optimization*.
- In *financial terms, significant savings* were realized *across all manufacturing sites*.

## 🛠️ Implementation Approach
- AI projects follow the broader IT principle of asking *does it work?* before scaling.
- Predictability is valued: stable systems, consistent service quality, and on‑time, on‑budget delivery.
- Success is measured by the ability to *solve the problem reliably*, not just by the sophistication of the AI.

## 💡 Why It Matters
- The initiative shows that *progress with AI is gradual* but the benefits are real.
- It acknowledges the *hype cycle* around AI and the expectation of immediate massive gains.
- It highlights a *major unknown*: AI is the first invention capable of improving itself.
- By embedding AI as a strategic capability, BRP strengthens its digital backbone that *supports every function* from design to distribution.
