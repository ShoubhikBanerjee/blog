# Organizations Shift From Single Chatbots to Centralized AI Agent Platforms

Enterprises are increasingly moving away from individual, flashy chatbot prototypes toward unified agent platforms. This transition addresses the challenge of scaling fragmented proof-of-concept projects into production-ready operations through shared infrastructure, observability, and evaluation layers.

## 🔍 Overview
Many companies now find that while individual agents are easy to prototype, they are difficult to operate at scale. To manage dozens of agent efforts, organizations are consolidating their development into central AI hubs that provide a consistent foundation, preventing individual teams from reinventing essential components like observability and deployment.

## 🧩 Strategic Focus
Organizations are prioritizing specific business areas for agent deployment, particularly those involving high-volume, repetitive, or regulated tasks:

| Focus Area | Examples of Use Cases |
| :--- | :--- |
| Back-office and Regulated Work | Claims, underwriting, invoices, tenders, procurement, payroll |
| Risk and Compliance | Security alert triage, anti-financial-crime testing, flagged-transaction verification |

## ⚙️ Key Details
- Platform Adoption: 35% of organizations surveyed utilize a company-wide agent platform or control plane as their primary use case.
- Federated Development: 16% of organizations are implementing systems where non-technical users can configure agents via low-code interfaces, while engineers manage industrialization and guardrails.
- Operational Discipline: Successful deployments rely on an LLMOps discipline that includes tracing, prompt management, and unified visibility through LLM gateways to monitor spend and policy adherence.
- Industry Example: Schneider Electric operates an internal AI Hub with 350 people managing over 60 agents. These agents support critical infrastructure and business objectives, ranging from embedding intelligence into products for energy efficiency to automating customer success workflows.
- Evolution of Systems: Teams are discovering that general-purpose agents often require re-engineering. For example, monday.com rebuilt its 'Sidekick' assistant into a layered system of subagents and bounded tools after production testing showed that simple tool expansion decreased performance.

## 💡 Why it matters
Building a central platform allows organizations to enforce standards for production, preserve data residency, and maintain security controls while enabling teams to build agents quickly. By moving toward a shared infrastructure layer, companies can effectively navigate the transition from hundreds of prototypes to reliable, production-grade AI systems.
