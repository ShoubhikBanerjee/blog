# Study Maps How Mixed Human‑AI Groups Form Consensus

A recent experiment examined how mixing large language model (LLM) agents with humans in a collaborative description game changes the way groups reach consensus.

## 🔍 Overview
- LLM agents are moving from being simple tools to active participants in human groups.
- The research asked how this growing presence reshapes consensus formation.
- Participants played a description game where shared conventions emerged through repeated rounds of random pairwise communication.

## 🧩 How it works
- Human participants and LLM agents were combined in varying proportions.
- Each round involved a random pair communicating to describe a target, allowing conventions to develop over time.
- Agent influence stems from a shared linguistic prior that places agents near one another in expression space and from relatively stable expression choices across rounds.
- Humans initially resist adopting expressions from partners perceived as AI but gradually yield to conformity pressure.

## ⚙️ Key findings
- **Three distinct regimes of consensus formation** emerged as the proportion of LLM agents changed:
  - Low agent proportions → human‑led consensus.
  - Intermediate proportions → disruption of convergence.
  - High proportions → strong consensus that shifts toward agent‑led conventions.
- The regimes differ not only in convergence strength but also in semantic grounding and communicative form:
  - Human‑led consensus is **more concrete, holistic, and grounded in shared real‑world analogies**.
  - Agent‑led consensus is **more abstract, less information‑dense, and more geometrically segmented**.

| Regime | Agent proportion | Consensus type | Characteristics |
|--------|------------------|----------------|-----------------|
| Low | Low | Human‑led | Concrete, holistic, grounded in real‑world analogies |
| Intermediate | Intermediate | Disrupted convergence | — |
| High | High | Agent‑led | Abstract, less information‑dense, geometrically segmented |

## 💡 Why it matters
- The study provides evidence that AI composition can shape the emergence, content, and perceived legitimacy of group norms.
- Agent proportion and transparency emerge as important design variables for human‑AI systems.
- Understanding these dynamics helps designers anticipate how mixed groups will develop shared language and norms.
