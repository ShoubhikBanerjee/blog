# MIT Technology Review reports AI agents still lack creative research ability

At EmTech Future 2026, MIT Technology Review highlighted new observations about the current limits of artificial‑intelligence agents and large language models.

## 🔍 Overview
- **AI agents are not yet creative enough to carry out genuinely innovative open‑ended AI research** – a key takeaway from the conference.
- **LLMs don’t reason** and many "breathless" claims about AGI or new capabilities fall apart quickly under scrutiny.
- The event featured several sessions that explored how AI intersects with other fields and the broader technology landscape.

## 🗓️ Conference Sessions
| Speaker | Focus |
|---|---|
| Yossi Matias, Vice President & Head of Google Research | How AI is beginning to reshape biology, infrastructure, manufacturing, and science, and its impact when it intersects with other fields |
| Hartmut Neven, founder and lead at Google Quantum AI | Quantum advancing alongside other frontiers and its significance when interacting with surrounding systems |
| Evelyn Wang, VP for energy & climate at MIT | Interconnection of energy, computing, infrastructure, and climate technologies |
| Cory Doctorow, bestselling author | How AI is changing the way we work and think |

## 💡 Why it matters
- The skepticism around **recursive self‑improvement** suggests that rapid AI leaps toward AGI may be farther off than hype implies.
- Ten years after AlphaGo’s historic match, **today’s AI still isn’t tapping into the machinery that made that win possible**, indicating a gap between celebrated milestones and everyday research capability.
- Start‑ups are **chasing the next big thing in LLMs**, but the community is cautioned not to be misled by a summer of AI hype.
