# AI Governance Climbs to Boardroom Fiduciary Duty

As boards push for fast AI adoption, a new question is emerging: what happens to human judgment when AI is everywhere? University of Chicago President Paul Alivisatos argued in The Wall Street Journal that students should learn to think with AI, without AI, and about AI. The shift is moving AI governance from a technical issue to a board-level priority, much like cybersecurity did a decade ago.

## 🔍 Overview

AI governance is now a fiduciary responsibility. The boardroom question is no longer "How do we deploy AI?" but "How do we scale AI innovation while protecting trust, accountability, and long-term enterprise value?" Organizations with trustworthy AI foundations move faster than those retrofitting controls after an incident.

## 🧩 Key Details

- Only 28% of talent management leaders believe they have high-potential talent in their most critical roles (Gartner research).
- AI can make a workforce highly efficient and quietly less capable of independent judgment.
- The winners of the next decade won't be the companies with the most AI tools.
- They'll be the ones whose leaders know when to trust AI, when to challenge it, and when to override it.
- Bias, misinformation, security gaps, and regulatory exposure are not solved after the fact.

## ⚙️ Governance Requirements

- Require governance models with clear ownership, auditability, and escalation paths from day one.
- Before autonomy scales, require management to show controls on agent authority, access, data movement, and runtime monitoring.
- Name decision stewards who have the authority to challenge, pause, or override AI when value, trust, or regulatory obligations are at stake.

## 💡 Why It Matters

History won't remember who adopted AI first. It will remember who deployed it responsibly — and turned governance into a competitive advantage. Trust isn't an ethical footnote anymore; it's a strategic asset. AI safety is fast becoming a board fiduciary duty. The US and China are setting up channels to contain AI-related incidents before they escalate, because AI accelerates insight and amplifies mistakes at machine speed. The next AI risk isn't what models know — it's what agents are authorized to do.
