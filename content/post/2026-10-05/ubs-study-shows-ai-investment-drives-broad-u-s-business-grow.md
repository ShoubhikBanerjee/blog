# UBS Study Shows AI Investment Drives Broad U.S. Business Growth Through Large Spillovers

Investment linked to artificial intelligence is generating a much broader boost to U.S. business investment than its direct contribution would suggest, according to UBS research.

## 🔍 Overview
- AI‑related technology investment is rising about **30% year over year**.
- Other business fixed investment is up only **0.8%**, while residential investment has **contracted 3.8%**.
- The data indicate that overall investment strength remains heavily concentrated in AI‑related spending.

## 📈 Investment Growth
- AI spending is becoming an increasingly important source of broader business investment growth.
- Every **1 percentage‑point** contribution to growth from AI‑related investment generates about **1.46 percentage‑points** of total investment growth in the following quarter.
  - **0.46 pp** comes from continued AI‑related categories.
  - **0.30 pp** stems from software and research & development.
  - **0.69 pp** flows into other sectors.

## ⚙️ Spillover Effects
- UBS estimates large spillovers from AI investment into:
  - Data centres
  - Power infrastructure
  - Computers
  - Communications equipment
  - Specialized machinery
- The spillover is especially visible in industries that supply data‑centre infrastructure, such as **gas turbines, electricity infrastructure, and utilities**.
- UBS defines its AI investment proxy across five national‑accounts categories:
  - Electrical transmission and distribution equipment
  - Special industry machinery
  - Computers and peripherals
  - Communications equipment
  - Data centres
- Those five categories together account for roughly **18% of non‑residential fixed investment** and about **2.5% of GDP**.

## 📊 Multiplier Mechanics Over Time
- The multiplier effect has strengthened historically:
  - **88 basis points** contribution per 1 pp AI capex increase in **1980**.
  - **106 basis points** in **2000**.
  - **146 basis points** by **June 2026**.

## 🌐 Notable Beneficiaries
- Beyond obvious tech suppliers, less obvious firms are benefiting, e.g., **Japan’s TOTO**. Its ceramics expertise enables production of electrostatic chucks used in semiconductor manufacturing, now accounting for **more than half of the company’s profits**.

The UBS analysis highlights how the AI cycle is fueling a broad-based surge in U.S. business investment, with sizable indirect effects that extend far beyond the core AI technology sector.
