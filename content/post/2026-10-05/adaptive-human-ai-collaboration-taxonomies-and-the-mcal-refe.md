# Adaptive Human-AI Collaboration: Taxonomies and the MCAL Reference Model

Artificial intelligence is shifting from a static decision-support tool to an adaptive collaborator that must sense context, decide when and how to intervene, and improve through repeated interaction with humans individually and in groups.

## 🔍 Overview
This paper reports a review of adaptive human‑AI collaboration literature through a closed‑loop lens.

## 📚 Review Methodology
* Iterative identification
* Staged selection against explicit criteria
* Structured extraction
* Taxonomy‑driven synthesis
* Snowballing

The authors analyzed **50 reviewed works** using this process.

## 🧩 Taxonomies
The review contributes three taxonomies:

1. **Multimodal Context Modeling** – spans from individual states to collective states such as group engagement and participation equality.
2. **Uncertainty‑Aware Intervention** – covers uncertainty sources, estimation and calibration mechanisms, an intervention repertoire that ranges from explanation modulation and deferral to group facilitation, and intervention policies.
3. **Longitudinal Co‑Adaptation and Synergy‑Oriented Evaluation**.

## ⚙️ MCAL Reference Model
The three taxonomies are integrated into **MCAL**, a dual‑timescale **Multimodal Co‑Adaptation Loop** reference model.  MCAL is instantiated on a mixed human‑robot workspace, and the authors walk every stage of the loop through one concrete setting to show what each taxonomy cell holds in practice.

## 💡 Why it matters
Meta‑analytic evidence shows that human‑AI combinations often fail to outperform the best of either partner alone, and the enabling literature remains fragmented across multimodal sensing, uncertainty quantification, reliance and delegation, facilitation, and teaming.  By organizing this fragmented work into unified taxonomies and a closed‑loop reference model, the paper provides a structured foundation for building adaptive human‑AI collaborators that can sense context, handle uncertainty, and co‑adapt over time.
