# Sen. Schiff pushes for AI regulatory agency amid White House non‑binding pledge

In 2026, Senator Adam Schiff highlighted a growing push for federal regulation of artificial intelligence, citing recent White House events and a Supreme Court decision that could shape any new AI agency.

## 🔍 Overview
- President Donald Trump gathered AI CEOs at the White House to sign a non‑binding pact agreeing to keep AI safe.
- A separate group of tech executives signed a voluntary pledge to evaluate their own AI systems.
- Schiff, a Democrat from California, opened the conversation by discussing self‑dealing and corruption in politics.
- He emphasized the need for regulation rather than relying solely on industry promises.

## 🏛️ Legal backdrop
- The Supreme Court case *Loper Bright* overturned a Reagan‑era precedent that gave agencies deference when laws were vague (Chevron deference).
- Courts now decide on such matters, which could cause any new AI regulation to become entangled in litigation.

## ⚙️ Proposed regulatory approach
- Schiff supports creating a new agency to regulate AI.
- He noted that the Supreme Court decision makes immediate litigation a real risk for new regulations.

## 🚨 National security concerns
- Advanced models, especially those that improve themselves recursively, reduce our ability to understand their decisions.
- The Pentagon’s unbridled AI use could lead to calamities; there is a call to investigate AI’s role in the bombing of an Iranian girls’ school.
- Anthropic has refused to use AI for domestic mass surveillance or fully autonomous weapons, prompting an administration effort to “kill” the company.
- When asked about these concerns, the president answered that the world need not worry because “these companies love America.”

## 📣 Why it matters
- The combination of industry self‑regulation, a potential new federal agency, and a Supreme Court shift creates a complex landscape for AI governance.
- National security implications add urgency to establishing clear, enforceable rules.
