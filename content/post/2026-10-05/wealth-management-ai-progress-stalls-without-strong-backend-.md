# Wealth Management AI Progress Stalls Without Strong Backend Infrastructure

The Wealth Mosaic’s AI in Wealth 2026: APAC conference in Singapore on 24 September highlighted that the wealth‑management industry’s rapid move from digitalisation to artificial intelligence is now hitting a bottleneck: the surrounding systems, processes and human relationships.

## 📈 Industry Momentum
- The sector has “moved quickly from digitalisation to artificial intelligence (AI)” but the next stage “may depend less on the capabilities of the technology itself than on the systems, processes and human relationships around it.”
- This theme recurred throughout the morning sessions at the event.

## ⚙️ Infrastructure Gap
- Kaushik Kadayam, Executive Director at Standard Chartered, opened the event warning that “AI is great – but AI needs a robust backend workflow and operations and technology is important.”
- He likened the situation to “lifting a lot more on the right arm and we're really bulking it up here. But the left arm is still very skimpy and skinny.”
- He said sophisticated AI can only take firms so far if “processes beneath them remain fragmented or manual.”
- Legacy systems that “lack APIs” and manual information transfers still dominate many firms.
- The uneven spread of technology means markets such as Singapore, Hong Kong and London are ahead, while “greenfield markets where technology adoption remains less advanced could offer more scope for genuinely transformative applications.”
- Risk appetite is a further challenge: client confidentiality, data restrictions, regulators, compliance and risk teams must be comfortable with the technology.
- Consequently, “much current technology investment is going towards the foundations required for transformation rather than AI alone.”
- Kadayam estimated that “around 90 percent of banking technology investment is still directed towards upgrading technology, moving to the cloud, and ‘getting the basics right’ – leaving a much smaller proportion for transformation.”
- He sees the opportunity to shift from isolated AI use cases to “end‑to‑end processes” and predicts firms will “automate the entire process front‑to‑back with AI.”
- Yet he stresses that automation will not remove the relationship element: “The fact that we still need relationship managers, and we don't have AI talking to clients, signifies that trust is the biggest win here.”

## 🤖 AI Use Cases & Caution
- Tawishi Singh, Head of Partnerships & Channels at Keenai, sees clear internal productivity gains from AI but advises a cautious rollout to clients: “Do we want to necessarily be the first one to be out there doing it? No, because I think we'd rather tread carefully than necessarily be the first one out and doing it wrong.”
- She notes that adoption takes time because of “data, risk governance, regulation, people, education, and differing levels of enthusiasm across an organisation.”
- Singh adds, “I don't care about the speed necessarily. I care that it’s accurate and achieves the results.”
- She highlights portfolio generation and analysis as proven internal uses, while emphasizing that “ultimately the human has to assess it.”
- Singh also points to risk and compliance as a productivity use case where AI can handle repetitive work, freeing specialists for higher‑value tasks.
- Yee (name only) identifies portfolio reporting, reconciliations and document review as areas where technology can deliver “substantial efficiency gains while maintaining human oversight.”
- Yee urges vendors to stop selling AI‑enabled software without first defining the pain point, asking “What is it they are actually solving for? Is this three hours saved or not?”
- He warns against merely “dressing up the same chatbots with AI conversation” when AI is added to existing processes without real improvement.

## 🤝 Human Relationship & Trust
- Kadayam stresses that firms must continue to win client trust and keep customers informed as new AI‑driven processes are introduced.
- In a fireside chat, Nicole Teo (Vice President Marketing, Lighthouse Canton) and Mark Yeo Tee Shen (Head of Investment Advisory, StashAway) discussed AI’s potential to personalise marketing and client interactions, but cautioned that excessive personalisation could erode trust if clients feel “they are being watched too closely.”

## 🚀 Path Forward
- The consensus at the conference is that the wealth‑management industry must first solidify its backend workflow, APIs and data governance before AI can deliver end‑to‑end automation.
- Successful transformation will blend robust infrastructure, selective internal AI use cases, and a continued focus on relationship‑manager‑led trust with clients.
