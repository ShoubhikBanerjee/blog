# Congress Debates Legislative Solutions for Rising AI Data Center Electricity Costs

Congress is scrambling to address voter concerns over the impact of AI data centers on residential electricity rates as lawmakers debate new legislative measures. With the data center industry rapidly expanding, regional utilities are building tens to hundreds of millions of dollars in new infrastructure, raising questions about how much of these costs will be passed on to everyday utility customers.

## ⚖️ Legislative Initiatives

Several data center and AI-related bills are moving through Congress. The primary competing measures include:

| Bill Name | Sponsor / Promoter | Key Provisions & Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ratepayer Protection Act** | Sponsored by Ohio Republican Sen. Jon Husted | Would have state utilities consider holding "large-load" customers responsible for increased costs. Passed the House in September with strong bipartisan support; opposed by Senate Minority Leader Sen. Chuck Schumer. |
| **GRID Savings Act** | Authored by New Mexico Democrat Sen. Martin Heinrich; promoted by Sen. Chuck Schumer | Aimed at protecting ratepayers; described by Schumer as having "more teeth" than the Ratepayer Protection Act because the Ratepayer Protection Act's protections are voluntary. |

## ⚙️ Rising Infrastructure and Utility Costs

The rapid growth of data centers has significantly impacted regional energy grids and wholesale electricity costs:

* **Overall Ratepayer Impact:** PJM, a regional transmission organization serving 13 states and the District of Columbia (including Northern Virginia), has an Independent Market Monitor estimating that data centers have cost their 67 million ratepayers approximately $29 billion over roughly the past two years.
* **State-Level Costs:** Maryland customers pay an estimated $168 to $216 more per year on electricity bills primarily due to the data center boom.
* **Infrastructure Demands:** Utilities are constructing new power lines, substations, and other grid-connecting infrastructure. Connor Waldoch, co-founder and chief strategy officer at Grid Status, notes, "We're talking tens to hundreds of millions of dollars [of infrastructure] that are being built by these local utilities."
* **Cost Recovery:** Jeremy L. Slayton, a spokesman for Dominion Energy, which serves Loudoun County, Virginia, stated, "We recover costs for distribution and transmission infrastructure from customers."

## 🧩 Challenges in Determining Consumer Impact

Calculating the precise cost passed on to residential ratepayers is difficult due to several variables and a lack of transparency:

* **Local Variables:** According to Lucy Qiu, a professor at the School of Public Policy at the University of Maryland, consumer impact depends on utilities' power-purchasing contracts, retail rates, regulatory decisions, the scale of upgrades, and how utilities share accounting.
* **Lack of Transparency:** Ari Peskoe, director of the Harvard Electricity Law Initiative, states that a "single, clean number" for nationwide data center costs is impossible because much of the data regarding what data centers pay is hidden behind non-disclosure agreements (NDAs).
* **Prevalence of NDAs:** A study by the University of Mary Washington found that NDAs had been signed in 80% of Virginia localities where there were existing, approved, or proposed data centers.

## 💡 Political Ramifications

The issue of data center growth and energy costs has also entered political campaigns. Ohio Republican Sen. Jon Husted is currently in a heated reelection campaign against Democrat Sherrod Brown, who lost his Senate seat in 2024. Brown has made Husted's past support of data centers in Ohio a major campaign issue.
