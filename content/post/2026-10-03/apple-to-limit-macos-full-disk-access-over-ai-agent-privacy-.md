# Apple to Limit macOS Full Disk Access Over AI Agent Privacy Risks

Apple is introducing new restrictions for the "full disk access" permission on Mac systems. This decision follows reports of risks posed by AI agents and concerns about how developers utilize high-level system permissions.

## 🔍 Overview

Full disk access is a macOS permission that provides an application with access to a user's entire system. According to Apple, this feature "largely sidesteps" standard privacy controls to enable backup applications to function correctly. However, the company noted that some developers use this access in ways that expose a user's entire system—including files, mail, messages, and browsing history—often without the user's full understanding.

## ⚙️ Key details

Apple is rolling out new controls designed to ensure that users only grant this "extraordinary level of access" through "very explicit user action." Key points regarding this permission include:

*   It grants access to files, mail, messages, and browsing history.
*   It was originally designed to support the functionality of backup apps.
*   Current implementations by some developers may put users at risk by exposing sensitive data without full transparency.

## 💡 Why it matters

The policy change follows a report from Jason Aten of Inc, who found that Meta’s Muse AI was aware of his message contents despite not having explicit permission. In response, Meta spokesperson Andy Stone stated that access to Messages is "entirely opt-in" and requires users to enable both Full Disk Access and the Messages connector.

Apple suggests that as AI agents become more autonomous and capable, the risks associated with full disk access will grow substantially. By requiring more explicit user actions, the company aims to mitigate the potential for unauthorized data exposure.

## 🚀 Availability

Apple has not yet specified a date for when these new full disk access controls will be rolled out. The company did not immediately respond to requests for further comment.
