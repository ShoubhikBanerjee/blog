# OpenAI Launches Dots Agent Platform for Pro Users

OpenAI announced its new agent platform, called Dots, earlier this week. Think of Dots as Codex, but for regular people. The platform is enterprise software that can also order your dinner. Dots have blobby, anthropomorphic avatars and customizable names.

## 🔍 Overview

OpenAI’s new agent platform, Dots, is full of cute little guys who can do your bidding. In the future, OpenAI says you’ll be able to have multiple Dots, but right now you get one.

## 🧩 How it works

The interface looks similar to Muse’s; you chat with the agent in one window and follow its work in another as it clicks around on a virtual machine.

- Your Dot’s virtual machine can access apps like Blender and GIMP right off the bat.
- You can also give it access to your own computer through the desktop ChatGPT app.
- You can call your Dot if you need to work through things out loud.

## ⚙️ Key details

OpenAI says Dots “can do nearly anything” with its cloud computer and access to apps. Testing revealed several practical examples:

- **Ordering dinner:** It almost got there, and even found a $100 promotional discount in my email that I had mass deleted, but got stuck at a “human check.”
- **Purchasing:** There’s no slick Stripe integration like Muse has to save your credit card info and use a virtual card for checkout. Dot paused to let me enter the numbers into the virtual browser; I didn’t trust it well enough to punch in my checking account number.
- **Booking a tour:** It couldn’t find the option for a free tour and entry at a coworking space, and instead offered to sign me up for a $35 day pass.
- **Logging in:** It reported that it was unable to log in to my Ikea account because it got stuck in a looping security check.
- **Restricted access:** Elsewhere on the internet, the neighborhood teriyaki spot wouldn’t allow Dot onto its ordering page.
- **Security issues:** Something about Dot’s footprint seems to get it tripped up on security checks more often than Muse or Instinct. Dot offered a link to OpenAI’s page about websites blocking its cloud browser traffic as an explanation.
- **Positive experience:** I really saw Dot come into its element when I gave it access to something fully within my control: my personal website. I hashed out the changes I wanted section by section over 10 minutes, walked away to do some dishes, and got a ping that the next iteration was ready for me to review. I had to give it a shocking number of permissions, but it worked.

## 🚀 Availability

OpenAI is offering Dots first to users on its highest-tier accounts, including the $100-per-month Pro account.

## 💡 Why it matters

Dots represents a new type of AI agent that aims to automate a wide range of tasks—from ordering dinner and booking tours to editing websites—directly from a chat interface. While it shows promise in controlled environments, it currently faces practical hurdles with security checks, e-commerce integrations, and website restrictions that limit its real-world usefulness.
