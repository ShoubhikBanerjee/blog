# UN Security Council Discusses AI Risks Amid Concerns Over Global Governance and Inequality

The United Nations Security Council recently convened to discuss the existential risks of artificial intelligence, bringing together tech CEOs and scientific advisors. However, the session has faced criticism for excluding voices from civil society and the Global South while overlooking the broader social and economic consequences of AI development.

## 🔍 Overview
The meeting addressed the safety challenges posed by increasingly powerful AI systems. While tech leaders call for a pause in development, they continue to race toward building more advanced technologies. The discussion was primarily framed through the Security Council’s peace and security mandate, which prevented a more comprehensive look at the diverse dangers AI may pose.

## ⚙️ Key details
The meeting's participants and referenced figures include:

| Name | Context |
| :--- | :--- |
| Yoshua Bengio | Co-chair of the Independent International Scientific Panel on AI and lead advocate for AI safety |
| Maria Ressa | Nobel Peace Prize laureate and panel co-chair; not invited to speak |
| Vladimír Šucha | Author of work on how AI erodes human judgment and cognitive skills |
| Mariana Mazzucato | Researcher who previously raised questions regarding AI accountability |

## 💡 Why it matters
The current approach to AI development and governance raises several critical concerns based on the briefing:

* **Representation Gap:** Apart from member countries’ representatives, no one from the Global South or civil society, nor any women, spoke at the Security Council meeting.
* **Economic Inequality:** AI reinforces existing disparities through algorithmic discrimination and unequal access to capital, technology, and computing power.
* **Human Agency:** The technology threatens to replace human judgment while eroding emotional and relational skills.
* **Social Harm:** AI impacts the mental health of young people and contributes to the violence women endure online.
* **Political Economy:** A hands-off approach to global markets has concentrated wealth among a few trillion-dollar companies. Recent political shifts, such as the U.S. withdrawal from the 15% global minimum corporate tax agreement, have further enabled tech giants to grow more powerful.
