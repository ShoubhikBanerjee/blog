# Danish Survey Finds No Wage or Hour Impact from AI Chatbot Adoption

A large‑scale Danish study of 25,000 workers in 11 AI‑exposed occupations finds that using generative AI chatbots did not change earnings or hours worked.

## 🔍 Overview
- The research links survey responses to monthly administrative records through December 2024.
- It compares chatbot users with comparable non‑users in the same occupations and workplaces.

## 📊 Survey Design
- Two representative surveys were fielded with Statistics Denmark in late 2023 and late 2024.
- Each round invited 115,000 workers; the 2024 round yielded about 25,000 valid responses from 7,000 workplaces.
- Respondents were drawn from registers, allowing linkage to earnings, hours, and occupation data for both respondents and roughly 3 million other Danish workers.
- The sample matches the workforce on age, gender, experience, earnings, and wealth.

## 🛠️ Occupations Studied
| Occupation | Exposure |
|---|---|
| Software developers | High |
| IT support specialists | High |
| Customer support specialists | High |
| Office clerks | High |
| Accountants | High |
| Financial advisors | High |
| HR professionals | High |
| Legal professionals | High |
| Marketing professionals | High |
| Journalists | High |
| Teachers | High |

## 📈 Findings
- **Headline result:** a precise null – no measurable effect on earnings or hours for either chatbot‑using workers or firms that adopt chatbots.
- 43 % of workers reported that their employer explicitly encourages chatbot use.
- Policies reported in the 2024 survey were: encouraged, allowed, not allowed, no policy, or unknown.

## 💡 Why it matters
- Earlier work (Brynjolfsson, Chandar, & Chen 2025) documented sharp drops in early‑career employment in AI‑exposed occupations, raising concerns about displacement.
- Denmark’s rapid generative‑AI uptake—on par with the United States—provides a clean window to test those concerns, and the null finding suggests that, at least through December 2024, chatbot adoption has not translated into wage or hour reductions.
