# International Study Evaluates 11 AI Systems for Gum Disease Diagnosis

On October 5, 2026, an international study led by Dr. Yaniv Mayer, senior physician and director of the residency program in the Department of Periodontics and Implants at Rambam and senior lecturer at the Technion – Israel Institute of Technology, Faculty of Medicine, was published in the *Journal of Clinical Periodontology*. The research examined, for the first time on a comparative basis, eleven leading artificial‑intelligence systems to see how they perform as auxiliary tools for diagnosing and treating gum disease.

## 🔍 Overview
- The study compared **11 AI systems** on their ability to **diagnose gum diseases** and **recommend treatment**.
- It sought to determine which systems provide **safer recommendations** and which might **endanger patients**.
- The work emphasizes that AI is becoming a **working tool for professionals** in fields such as medicine, where it can help achieve **faster, more accurate, and more efficient diagnoses**.

## 🦷 AI in Periodontics
- Artificial intelligence is rapidly integrating into medical practice, including **periodontics** (gum health). 
- The study’s results highlight that **expert oversight remains essential for safe patient care**, even when AI tools are used.

## 📊 Study Details
- **Lead researcher:** Dr. Yaniv Mayer (Senior physician, Department of Periodontics and Implants, Rambam; Senior lecturer, Technion – Israel Institute of Technology, Faculty of Medicine).
- **Publication:** *Journal of Clinical Periodontology*.
- **Date:** October 05, 2026.
- **Scope:** Comparative evaluation of 11 leading AI systems for gum disease diagnosis and treatment recommendation.

## 💡 Why It Matters
- The Technion has been **Israel's technological engine for more than 100 years**, driving innovation, technology, and entrepreneurship.
- By testing AI tools in dentistry, the study contributes to the broader effort of **transforming ideas into impact** across medicine and security.
- Findings help clinicians understand **the limits of AI**, reinforcing the need for **human expertise** to ensure patient safety.

## 🚀 Looking Ahead
- Continued collaboration between **academic institutions** (Technion, Rambam) and **AI developers** will shape how AI assists clinicians.
- Ongoing research will be needed to refine AI recommendations and safeguard against potential risks.
