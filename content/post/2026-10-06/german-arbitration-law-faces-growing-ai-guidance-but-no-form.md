# German Arbitration Law Faces Growing AI Guidance but No Formal Rules Yet

If you are looking for specific legislation or institutional guidance to tell you whether artificial intelligence (AI) belongs in your arbitration in Germany, you can stop waiting.

## 🔍 Overview
- German law does not contain explicit AI provisions in the German Code of Civil Procedure (ZPO), but existing provisions already answer the key questions.
- The EU Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) adds additional requirements that will increasingly apply to arbitration.
- Several arbitration institutions have issued AI‑related guidelines, while the German Institution of Arbitration (DIS) has not yet published specific rules.

## 📜 Legal Framework
- **ZPO Structure**: Sections 1025 et seq. are built around a personally appointed human arbitrator; section 1036(1) refers to the “person” offered the arbitrator position; section 1054 requires the award to bear the arbitrators’ signatures – a formal act only a natural person can perform.
- **Case Law**: The Federal Court of Justice (12 January 2023, I ZB 41/22) set aside an award where the appointed arbitrator did not participate, confirming that delegation of the decision‑making function to an AI tool would breach the mandatory participation requirement.
- **Public‑policy Ground**: Delegating the substantive decision to AI may also trigger the procedural public‑policy ground under section 1059(2) no. 2(b) ZPO.
- **Lawyer Duties**: German lawyers must practise conscientiously (BRAO § 43) and must not knowingly disseminate untruths (BRAO § 43a(3) sentence 2), which bars the intentional use of fabricated AI‑generated authorities.

## 🏛️ Institutional Guidance
| Institution | AI Guidance Published | Year |
|-------------|-----------------------|------|
| SVAMC | AI Guidelines | 2024 (April) |
| SCC | AI Guide | 2024 (October) |
| CIArb | AI Guideline & Template Procedural Order | 2025 |
| VIAC | AI Notes | 2025 |
| CIETAC | AI Notes | 2025 |
| ICC | Task force on AI (no guidance yet) | – |
- The DIS has engaged in the debate through events, a cooperation with the SVAMC announced in December 2025, and a joint submission with 15 other institutions to the European Commission on high‑risk AI classification.
- The DIS has **not** published any specific AI rules or practice note.

## ⚖️ Key Principles Across Institutions
- Arbitral tribunals must **not delegate** their decision‑making powers to AI.
- Counsel remain **responsible** for the accuracy of factual and legal submissions.
- All participants must ensure AI tools comply with applicable **confidentiality** obligations.

## 🚧 Current Gaps & Recent Developments
- **2026 ZPO Reform Bill** (adopted 10 June 2026) modernises arbitration (video hearings, electronic awards) but does not address AI tools.
- The same omission appears in state‑court litigation where the ZPO also lacks AI‑specific provisions.
- The DIS’s joint submission to the European Commission asks for a distinction between AI used in adjudicative functions and AI used for administrative case management.

## 💡 Practical Implications for Practitioners
- **No Delegation**: An arbitrator who outsources the substance of a decision to an AI tool no longer participates in the act of deciding, risking set‑aside of the award.
- **Right to be Heard**: Simply uploading written submissions to a large language model does **not** satisfy the procedural right to be heard.
- **Professional Conduct**: Lawyers must avoid presenting AI‑generated fabricated authorities, as this would breach the duty under the BRAO.
- **Technology‑Friendly Rules**: The 2018 DIS Rules already support electronic communication (article 4.1) and the discussion of IT‑based efficiency measures (article 27.4(i) and Annex 3), providing a procedural basis for careful AI use.

The landscape shows a rapid emergence of institutional guidance on AI in arbitration, yet German arbitration law itself remains silent on the matter, leaving practitioners to rely on existing ZPO provisions, professional duties, and emerging best‑practice notes.
