# Congress Moves to Tighten AI Chip Export Rules After Nvidia Spy Scandal

Following revelations about former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang’s ties to a suspected Chinese spy, Congress is pushing new measures to restrict AI chip exports.

## 🔍 Overview
- The scandal involving Jensen Huang and a suspected Chinese spy has spurred calls for tighter export controls on AI chips.
- Both Democrats and Republicans have voiced support for measures that would limit chip shipments to China.
- Lawmakers are demanding that Huang and other Nvidia leaders testify before Congress.

## 🏛️ Legislative Action
- Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer said Huang and the Trump administration need to answer to Congress.
- Senate Banking Committee Chairman Tim Scott sent letters to Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick requesting public testimony.
- House Speaker Mike Johnson noted a “growing consensus in Congress on limiting chip exports to China” and personally supports efforts to restrict China’s access to powerful AI chips from companies like Nvidia.
- The Remote Access Security Act, introduced in the House in April 2025, has regained interest, though its passage is uncertain.

## 💡 Why it matters
- Tightening export rules could curb China’s access to advanced AI hardware.
- Increased congressional oversight may lead to new reporting and compliance requirements for chip manufacturers.
- The bipartisan push reflects heightened national‑security concerns around AI technology.
