# SAP and Infosys Argue Enterprise AI Needs Unified Data and Governance

SAP and Infosys executives are advocating for a shift in how enterprises approach AI, moving away from isolated pilots toward a unified platform model that integrates data, governance, and business processes.

## 🧩 From Pilots to Enterprise Platform

For years, organizations have treated AI as a series of discrete experiments. However, as AI agents begin to move into workflows that run the business, that model is becoming inadequate. The core challenge now is rethinking how data, business processes, governance, and people fit together to turn AI into part of the enterprise operating model.

Thorsten Leiduck, SVP and global head of SAP Business AI Platform Ecosystem, notes that many customers struggle to convert interest in AI into an operationalized model because programs often start as isolated proof-of-concepts (POCs) on inconsistent and fragmented data foundations. This fragmentation leads to duplication, inconsistent performance, and no clear path to scale.

## ⚙️ Key Components of the Shift

*   **Unified Data Layer:** Leiduck argues that AI is absolutely dependent on the data foundation. Companies need a connected, semantically rich layer that reflects their business before they can reliably deploy AI across processes.
*   **Governance and Oversight:** Clear policies, access controls, monitoring, and oversight are necessary to ensure agents remain trustworthy and aligned with business requirements. Leiduck and Singh agree that the absence of governance does not speed up adoption but instead creates a "risk backlog" that can force disruptive stops and audits.
*   **Evaluation Framework:** Satinder Pal Singh, associate VP North America for SAP BAIP and SAP AI Strategy at Infosys, recommends evaluating AI use cases through three lenses: impact, feasibility, and scalability. Companies must identify where an agent can move meaningful business metrics, ensure their data and talent can support the use case, and establish whether successful applications can be replicated.

## 💡 Why It Matters

As AI moves into mission-critical workflows, the stakes change significantly. Leiduck points out that while "80% might be good enough" for everyday applications, that level of accuracy is a "very terrible result" in areas such as supply chains or financial close. The goal is to move beyond AI as a mere productivity tool to "AI as a decision-making partner" that helps leaders understand what is happening, anticipate what comes next, and determine how to respond. Customer-specific knowledge graphs and governed data are becoming critical enterprise assets in this future.
