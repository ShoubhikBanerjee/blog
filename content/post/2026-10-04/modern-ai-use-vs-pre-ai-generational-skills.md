# Modern AI Use vs. Pre-AI Generational Skills

This post contrasts the problem-solving methods of generations who lived without artificial intelligence with the modern reliance on AI tools.

## 🔍 Overview
A reflection compares the capabilities of grandmothers and mothers from previous generations with the AI-dependent habits of today. The grandmother raised seven children, ran a kuih stall, remembered every hutang in a notebook, and could predict rain by watching ants—none of this required ChatGPT, Google Maps, or algorithms deciding preferences. The mother, a teacher, owned a dictionary the size of a brick and looked up words manually. Today's generation has AI and has, according to the account, forgotten how to think.

## ⚙️ Key details
- Grandmother: Raised seven children, ran a kuih stall, tracked debts in a '555' notebook, predicted rain by observing ants.
- Grandmother's approach: If cendol was not sweet enough, she fixed it herself—no prompting an AI for sweeter, more aesthetic Korean-cafe-style output.
- Mother: Used a physical dictionary to find unknown words.
- Modern behavior: Outsources all problems to AI prompts, including research papers, Instagram captions, and birthday wishes for one's own mother.
- Example interaction: User prompts AI for 800 words on the haze; AI returns 515 words. The user then asks AI to stretch it to 800 words and to rework the tone until it is satisfactory.
- Described dynamic: Users treat AI like a Grab rider, demanding "extra spicy, extra fast, extra perfect" or mentally rating it one star.

## 💡 Why it matters
The post argues that previous generations survived without AI because they possessed "tolerance for imperfection." If a kuih was a bit lopsided, they ate it. If a research paper was imperfect, they submitted it. The modern reliance on AI is framed as a loss of independent thinking.
