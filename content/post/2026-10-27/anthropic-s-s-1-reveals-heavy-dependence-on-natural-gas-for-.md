# Anthropic’s S‑1 Reveals Heavy Dependence on Natural Gas for AI Data Centers

Anthropic’s S‑1 filing, released as the company prepares to go public, makes its reliance on natural‑gas‑fired data centers explicit.

## 🔍 Overview
- Anthropic announced a $50 billion plan to expand data centers in Texas and New York.
- The company currently leases capacity from hyperscalers but intends to build its own facilities.
- "Competition for access to electrical supply and data center locations with adequate power availability is intense," the filing states.

## ⚡ Energy Source
- Anthropic says some planned data centers will run on‑site electricity generated by natural‑gas turbines.
- The filing acknowledges that data centers are becoming increasingly reliant on “natural gas” — the fossil‑fuel industry’s term for primarily methane.
- Gas makes up the biggest chunk of the US electricity mix, 40 percent of the nation’s power generation.

## 📈 Cost and Supply Risks
- Rising natural‑gas prices and pipeline capacity constraints could increase operating costs.
- Anthropic notes that competition for natural‑gas access from hyperscalers and cloud providers could drive up gas prices.
- If regulators require Anthropic to bear more of the cost for new energy infrastructure, utility bills could balloon.
- Without a secure gas supply and pipelines, the company may need to delay or abandon planned data‑center projects.

## 🌡️ Climate and Environmental Impact
- Burning fossil fuels raises global temperatures, which Anthropic says will increase electricity and water‑cooling costs.
- A Texas‑based study found that data‑center electricity demand could wipe out decades of air‑quality gains, raising soot, smog‑forming pollutants, and carbon‑dioxide levels.
- Developers are installing gas engines on‑site to avoid grid‑connection delays, but simpler turbine technology is less efficient and more polluting.

## 🚧 Growth Constraints
- "Any failure to secure access to adequate electrical power, any legal, regulatory, or other administrative action … could adversely affect our cost structure and limit our ability to scale our compute infrastructure," Anthropic warns.
- Community opposition has blocked dozens of proposed data‑center sites across the US.
- Shortages of gas turbines are slowing data‑center construction.
