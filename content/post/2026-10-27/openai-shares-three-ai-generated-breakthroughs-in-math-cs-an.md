# OpenAI shares three AI‑generated breakthroughs in math, CS, and physics

## 🔍 Overview
- Less than a month after releasing hundreds of manuscripts with claimed solutions to long‑standing mathematics questions, OpenAI announced three new major discoveries in mathematics, computer science, and physics.
- The company introduced a new collaboration model that uses external experts called “shepherds” to guide the work in computer science and physics, while for the mathematics result the experts advised releasing the AI output for researchers to examine.
- OpenAI says it is taking new advice from scientists on how to release the results and is publishing the AI‑generated materials on GitHub.

## 🧪 What was discovered
- **Mathematics** – The model produced a proof of the local smoothing conjecture for the Euclidean wave equation in every spatial dimension (≥ 2).  The proof uses an auxiliary partial differential equation to establish Sogge’s local smoothing, and it also yields the Kakeya maximal and dimension conjectures and Stein’s spherical restriction estimates throughout the interior of the conjectured range.
- **Computer Science** – The model disproves the Exponential Time Hypothesis.  The accompanying paper gives a deterministic algorithm that exactly counts satisfying assignments of fixed‑width CNF formulas in subexponential time and polynomial space; the case of 3‑SAT refutes the hypothesis.
- **Physics** – The output includes a mathematically rigorous existence proof of certain interacting quantum field theories containing gauge fields and fermions in four dimensions, covering Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) and a unified completion of the Standard Model of particle physics.

## 👥 Shepherds & Verification
- A working group of computer scientists led by **Russell Impagliazzo**, **Ramamohan Paturi**, and **Ryan Williams** will examine and explain the computer‑science proof.
- For the physics result, **David Gross** will lead a group that includes **Michael Douglas**, **Arthur Jaffe**, **Igor Klebanov**, and **Nikita Nekrasov**.
- The verification of the computer‑science result will be guided by the same three experts (Impagliazzo, Paturi, and Williams).

## 📂 Release & Open Letter
- The AI‑generated write‑ups and Lean formalizations for the mathematics and computer‑science results were released on GitHub today.
- At the request of the verification group, all materials were made public immediately so others can examine them.
- OpenAI issued an open letter asking the community to discuss three broader questions:
  1. Expectations for early access, disclosure of conflicts of interest, and use of unpublished material.
  2. How credit should recognize both new discoveries and the work of checking, fixing, simplifying, explaining, and extending them.
  3. Ways to support students and researchers as methods change while preserving the judgment needed to question results and develop new ideas.

## ⚖️ Community & Policy considerations
- The AGMAI panel of math advisers warned against the practice of designating certain people to receive output first, citing unequal access, borrowed reputation, and biased selection.
- The panel recommended that AI labs release output on scholarly repositories, include Lean‑formalized proofs, provide information on how problems were chosen, fund people working on understanding the results, and supply other supporting details.
