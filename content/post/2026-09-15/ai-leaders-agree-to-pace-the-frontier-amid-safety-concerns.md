# AI leaders agree to “pace the frontier” amid safety concerns

When OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, Google DeepMind cofounder Demis Hassabis, and SpaceX head Elon Musk loosely agreed over the weekend to slow down AI development, skeptics spotted an ulterior motive immediately.

## 🔍 Overview
- The AI titans declared that their aim was to “pace the frontier,” signing on at least partially to a proposal for embedding third‑party auditors, regulating domestic labs, and reaching a global slowdown agreement.
- Critics argued the move was meant to "stop would‑be competitors, kneecap the open‑source movement, and avoid real legal safeguards — some dubbed it an outright “cartel.”"
- The proposal is seen as a possible substitute for regulation, though "under Trump, substantial regulation is unlikely anyway."
- Experts warned that "AI leaders aren’t the best people to lead the charge" on this growing issue.

## 📜 The “Pace the Frontier” proposal
| Step | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Embedded third‑party evaluators | embedded third‑party evaluators that can verify if a company is adhering to safety practices and commitments and report incidents |
| Coordination among frontier AI companies | coordination between frontier AI companies in democratic countries on standards and limits |
| Global coordination | global coordination between democratic governments and authoritarian governments on pacing AI development “to the extent this is possible.” |

## 🗣️ Reactions from leaders
- "I agree with Dario that we need to pace the frontier," Altman said shortly after Amodei posted about his essay on X.
- Altman also committed to having independent evaluators at the company, calling it a “great idea.”
- On Sunday, Altman said OpenAI welcomes “a federal framework that sets consistent safety requirements for frontier AI.”
- Altman noted two ways AI progress "could go very badly": losing “control of the future to AI” and ending up “in a world with too much concentration of power,” which he said can be avoided by “walking a narrow middle path.”
- "The details need working through, but the direction is correct for meeting this critical moment," he added.
- "Dario is right," Musk said, reposting Amodei’s essay.
- Hassabis pointed to a framework for an AI standards body he published in July.
- NYU’s Nick Reese said he "do[es] not think it’s all hot air."
- Apollo Research CEO Marius Hobbhahn called it “a good idea,” saying it’s "one of the best things for safety in a long time if it actually happens."
- The Midas Project’s Tyler Johnston said it was a “good sign.”
- Redwood Research CEO Buck Shlegeris called it "some great news — obviously it’s hard to know whether this is going to convert into anything real, but I’m feeling cautiously optimistic."

## ⚖️ Criticisms and concerns
- Daniel Kokotajlo warned, "There’s a serious concern that they’re not actually going to slow down," adding that they may "just bring in some external auditors, do a bunch of safety paperwork — some of which will be genuinely good — but at the end of the day, it actually won’t slow them down very much at all."
- Reese compared the gambit to the social‑media platforms’ playbook a decade ago, noting that "the hammer may not come in this administration, but I think if there were a Democratic administration after the next election, there would be a really good possibility."
- Critics described the effort as safety‑washing and warned that "they’ll just bring in some external auditors, do a bunch of safety paperwork…" without real impact.
- All agreed that there is still "a lot of work to do to make this verbal commitment a reality, particularly when it comes to making it an ironclad agreement."

## 📈 What comes next
- The three‑step proposal "calls for changes long espoused by AI safety advocates" and could become a framework for future regulation.
- Industry observers note that "the larger tech industry has spent years getting ahead of regulation by lobbying for its own preferred rules or promising self‑regulation," suggesting that genuine enforcement will be essential.
- Continued monitoring and independent evaluation will be needed to determine whether the “pace the frontier” approach translates into measurable slowdown of high‑risk AI development.
