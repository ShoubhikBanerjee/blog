# AI Trial Finds Senior Patent Lawyers Gain Judgment, Junior Gains Remain Mixed

In a three‑month randomized field experiment with 133 practicing patent attorneys, early access to Google’s AI patent‑writing assistant (now part of Gemini Notebook) lifted drafting performance for all participants but only improved professional judgment for senior lawyers.

## 🔍 Overview
- AI tools raise work quality in the short term.
- The impact on on‑the‑job learning varies by seniority.
- Senior attorneys who used AI for 90 days showed stronger judgment; junior attorneys showed no average skill gain, with scores spreading toward both higher and lower ends.

## 🛠️ Study Design
- **Participants:** 133 lawyers at 11 IP law firms that have regular, non‑exclusive business with Google.
- **Randomization:** Two‑thirds of lawyers at each firm received early access to the then‑unreleased Google Labs AI patent‑writing assistant (treatment); the remaining third received training but no AI access (control).
- **Tasks:** 
  1. **Drafting task** – after 10 days and again after 90 days, participants drafted a patent from a hypothetical invention.
  2. **Redlining task** – at 90 days, participants manually corrected a pre‑written patent containing substantive and stylistic errors; AI use was prohibited.
- **Evaluation:** Independent legal experts scored submissions on five dimensions (enforceability, accuracy, strategic ambiguity, completeness, clarity) using Likert‑scale rubrics. Grading was blinded.

## 📈 Key Findings
- **Drafting performance:**
  - +0.34 standard deviations (SD) after 10 days (≈10‑percentile‐point gain).
  - +0.38 SD after 90 days (≈11‑percentile‑point gain).
  - Gains were consistent across all five quality dimensions.
  - Improvement came from fewer poor scores and more good scores; the share of excellent scores did not change.
- **Time savings for juniors:** Drafting took 18 minutes less than the control average of 124 minutes.
- **Redlining (unassisted) performance:**
  - Overall treatment effect: +0.32 SD (≈9‑percentile‑point gain).
  - Effect driven entirely by seniors: +0.45 SD (≈13‑percentile‑point gain).
  - Juniors showed no net improvement; their scores bifurcated – more very low scores, fewer mediocre scores, more good scores, and no increase in excellent scores.

## 💡 Implications for Learning
- **Senior lawyers:** Sustained AI usage over months can translate into stronger professional judgment, the attribute that distinguishes senior from junior practitioners.
- **Junior lawyers:** Immediate productivity gains do not automatically convert into higher judgment when the AI is removed. Their learning outcomes are mixed, suggesting that AI may act as a “temporary exoskeleton” rather than a lasting skill‑builder.
- **Broader insight:** Evaluating AI’s effect on expertise requires (1) prolonged, embedded usage; (2) credible assessment of unassisted performance; and (3) blinded expert grading – all present in this study.

## 🚀 Looking Ahead
- Further research should explore how to pair AI assistance with guided supervision to help less‑senior professionals internalize judgment skills.
- Organizations may consider complementary training or mentorship structures when deploying AI tools, especially for junior staff.
