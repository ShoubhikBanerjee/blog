# Industrial AI Enters New Phase Driven by Foundation Models and Physical Systems

Industrial AI is transitioning from decades of predictive analytics into a new phase where foundation models, physical AI, and agentic AI enable the automation of complex tasks across industrial environments. While AI has been utilized in the sector for over 20 years, the recent explosion in general-purpose models has led to a 78% increase in industrial AI usage over the past two years.

## 🔍 Overview

The current development represents a shift toward autonomous systems that interact directly with physical infrastructure. Organizations are moving beyond specialized applications to leverage technologies that connect disparate data sources and automate both software and physical workflows. According to Arti Garg, chief technologist at AVEVA, these systems require new approaches to data, governance, and human oversight to manage safety and reliability risks.

## ⚙️ Key Technologies

| Technology | Function |
| :--- | :--- |
| Foundation Models | General-purpose models that enable wider leverage of advanced AI technologies. |
| Physical AI | Accelerates the capabilities of robotics and autonomous systems, including drones. |
| Agentic AI | Software-level automation that can perform complex tasks. |
| AI-Assisted Coding | Enables domain experts to build new applications for industrial sites. |

## 🧩 How it works

*   **Data Correlation:** Newer technologies connect telemetry, service logs, and engineering documents to provide operators with real-time support during problem diagnosis.
*   **Physical Operations:** Autonomous robots and drones perform inspections of high-risk machinery and gather data in hazardous environments, reducing the need for workers to enter dangerous areas.
*   **Power Management:** AI systems help manage complex power grids as renewable energy generation grows.
*   **Environmental Monitoring:** A new IEEE working group is developing a standard methodology to measure the impact of AI across energy, water, resources, and carbon.

## 🛡️ Governance and Safety

AVEVA's framework for responsible AI emphasizes that automated systems should augment rather than replace human judgment in critical decision loops. Organizations must establish clear guardrails to determine where systems can act independently and where human supervisors must remain responsible. This approach is designed to prevent catastrophic consequences for critical infrastructure that could arise from AI deployed without clarity, security, or accountability.

## 💡 Why it matters

As AI takes on autonomous roles in plants, power systems, and mining sites, organizations must rethink business processes to establish appropriate safeguards. The goal is a model of automation that supports experienced workers, allowing them to apply their expertise in ways that make industrial operations safer, more efficient, and more sustainable.
