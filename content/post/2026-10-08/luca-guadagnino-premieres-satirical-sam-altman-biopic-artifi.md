# Luca Guadagnino Premieres Satirical Sam Altman Biopic Artificial at New York Film Festival

Director Luca Guadagnino has premiered his satirical biopic *Artificial* at the New York Film Festival. The dark comedy chronicles the factual events surrounding the rise of OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, his brief ouster from the AI lab, and the power dynamics of those around him. Based on real-world documentation, lawsuits, and leaked communications, the film explores the theme of playing God and the pursuit of power in the tech industry.

## 🔍 Overview

The narrative of *Artificial* sticks remarkably close to factual events without relying heavily on fabricated characters or made-up scenes. The film opens with a shot of San Francisco’s Golden Gate Bridge, which serves as a metaphor for building all-powerful AI systems. Throughout the movie, Altman uses the bridge as a tool to convince and control his cofounder, Ilya Sutskever. The story tracks the intense power struggles within the organization, depicting how key figures interact as the lab grows.

## ⚙️ Key details

The film features a prominent cast portraying key figures from the history of OpenAI:

| Character | Played By | Portrayal and Key Attributes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sam Altman** | Andrew Garfield | A manipulative, power-hungry psychopath who lies easily, uses others to get what he wants, and uses physical contact to control people. |
| **Ilya Sutskever** | Yura Borisov | The idealistic, naive hero and ingenious mind behind the lab who cannot stomach lying and is reluctant to accumulate power. |
| **Elon Musk** | Ike Barinholtz | A widely beloved, busy visionary oozing with "millennial cringe" who wears sunglasses, says "epic," and starts drumrolls on tables. |
| **Greg Brockman** | Cooper Hoffman | An ultra-loyal bro and unquestioning right-hand man to Altman, interested mostly in money and power. |
| **Mira Murati** | Monica Barbaro | A brilliant but easily swayed outsider. |
| **Geoffrey Hinton** | Mark Rylance | The wise "godfather of AI" who foresees the power struggles and pitfalls ahead. |

## 🧩 How it works

The movie is structured in chapters defined by real-world milestones:

*   **Early 2000s:** Ilya Sutskever meets Geoffrey Hinton at the University of Toronto.
*   **2012:** The state of AI technology before it was auctioned off to Google for upwards of $40 million by the end of the year.
*   **March 2013:** Sutskever and Hinton join Google during an idealistic Silicon Valley era of nap pods and free merch.
*   **2015:** A dinner meeting in Menlo Park between Sutskever, Altman, Brockman, Musk, and others.
*   **2017:** An OpenAI system wins a game of Dota 2 against a human champion, prompting Altman to jump into Sutskever's arms for a hug.
*   **The Ouster:** The film takes viewers beat by beat through Altman's infamous firing.

## 💡 Why it matters

*Artificial* highlights the stark contrast between those driven by scientific ideals and those driven by power. In one scene, a man representing the "AI doomer" cohort approaches Sutskever in a park to warn him of how superintelligent AI could go badly, stating, "The question’s not, ‘How could this go wrong?’ It’s, ‘How could it not?’"

The film also highlights Altman's manipulative nature through specific interactions, such as his physical grip on Sutskever when convincing him to tell his first lie. Additionally, the film recalls a 2008 quote from Y Combinator cofounder Paul Graham regarding Altman: "You could parachute him into an island full of cannibals and come back in 5 years and he’d be the king."
