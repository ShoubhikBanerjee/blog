# Asia‑Pacific hospitals address shadow AI and governance as digital maturity rises

Robots delivering supplies through hospital wards after hours and clinicians finding new ways to bring AI into everyday work were highlighted at HIMSS26 APAC in Singapore, where health‑tech leaders gathered to discuss digital health, artificial intelligence and the challenges facing hospitals across the region.

## 🔍 Overview
- The event explored how standards, collaboration and governance are building a more connected health system across Asia‑Pacific.
- Conversations covered HIMSS's global maturity models, the rise of shadow AI and what it takes to transform a health system at scale.
- The HIMSS maturity models break digital transformation into eight stages, from a starting point (Stage 0) to fully digital, integrated operations (Stage 7).
- A hospital in Taiwan was recently validated at Stage 7, showing the importance of peer‑led support and cross‑border learning.

## 📊 Digital Maturity Framework
| Stage | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 0 | Starting point |
| 7 | Fully digital, integrated operations; a milestone often linked to greater efficiency and data‑driven care |

## 🛠️ Shadow AI & Governance
- **Shadow AI** is when clinicians or administrators use AI tools outside approved systems, a risk highlighted as innovation moves faster than oversight.
- Lily Liu, Divisional Director, Digital Health at Western Health, described shadow AI as staff using AI tools without the knowledge of their IT or governance teams.
- Liu advocates a governance approach that **educates** rather than restricts, saying “The moment you hear governance, you think no or never or later.”
- She adds, “I hope that people will take from my session that governance can be utilised, can be there to support you as well.”
- Instead of a fixed list of approved technology, governance can help staff manage risk and make better decisions.

## 🌏 Regional Challenges & Collaboration
- The region’s diversity adds complexity; each country faces issues shaped by its own population, geography and health‑system design.
- In Australia, connecting states, territories, hospitals, primary care and private clinics is a challenge.
- Taiwan is highly digitised with an integrated national platform, yet still seeks better connectivity.
- Standards for digital infrastructure are applied consistently, whether a hospital is in Melbourne, Australia, or Taichung, Taiwan.
- As more hospitals reach advanced digital maturity, peer‑led support and cross‑border learning become as important as the technology itself.

## 🚀 What This Means for Care
- Lasting change comes from linking every part of the care journey—from specialist clinics and regional hospitals to aged‑care and ambulance services.
- Even in countries with world‑class digital infrastructure, connection remains the key to better patient outcomes and smoother operations.
- Education‑focused governance enables staff to use AI responsibly while managing risk.
