# World Bank Update Outlines AI Productivity Potential and Infrastructure Barriers in Africa

The World Bank’s October 2026 Africa Economic Update indicates that while artificial intelligence offers promise for the continent’s workers and businesses, it alone cannot bridge the productivity gap. Sub-Saharan Africa’s growth is projected at 4.3% in 2026, up from 4.1% in 2025, but per capita income growth remains low at 1.8%. With over 620 million people expected to enter the labor force by 2050, the report emphasizes creating productive work at scale rather than focusing solely on headline GDP growth.

## 🔍 Overview

Economic pressures in the region are increasing, making productivity improvements more urgent. The World Bank outlook includes the following fiscal and economic figures:

*   **Median Inflation:** Projected to rise from 3.7% in 2025 to 5.5% in 2026.
*   **Public Debt:** Currently stands at approximately 57% of GDP.
*   **External Debt Service:** Has doubled as a share of revenue, rising from 9% in 2017 to 18% in 2025.

## ⚙️ Key details

African countries do not need to develop frontier AI systems to benefit from the technology. The immediate opportunity lies in adopting affordable, locally relevant tools that operate on ordinary devices and low-bandwidth conditions. These tools are currently being utilized in agriculture, education, healthcare, logistics, and small businesses. Developer activity is a key indicator of this momentum:

| Country | GitHub Developer Accounts | Growth since 2020 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Nigeria | 1.8 million | Tenfold growth; ranked 11th in EMEA |
| Kenya | 666,020 | Follows Nigeria in total accounts |
| Ghana | Not specified | Nearly eightfold growth |

## 🧩 How it works

Broad adoption of AI is dependent on prerequisites including devices, electricity, and data access. Current infrastructure and affordability gaps present significant challenges:

*   **Infrastructure Access:** Only 12% of households in the poorest income quintile have both a phone and a grid connection, compared to 54% among the wealthiest.
*   **Internet Affordability:** Mobile internet in Sub-Saharan Africa is the least affordable in the world relative to income. A basic data package costs twice the United Nations’ affordability target of 2% of average monthly income.
*   **Handset Costs:** An entry-level handset costs the poorest fifth of the population about three-quarters of a month’s income.

## 💡 Why it matters

AI is only effective when users can assess outputs and apply them to real-world problems. To ensure broad benefits, the report suggests that governments and employers prioritize job-specific training for safe and effective AI use. Without intervention, large companies with reliable connectivity may use automation to increase productivity, while informal businesses and rural workers remain excluded. Public procurement, schools, and community health systems could help provide practical AI applications to those unable to purchase them independently. Additionally, clear data-protection laws are essential to mitigate risks such as reproduced biases and the exposure of personal or health information.
