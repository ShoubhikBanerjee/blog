# Study Finds Reflective AI Use Boosts University Student Grades

A recent study from the University of Eastern Finland examined how university students use generative AI in take‑home exams (2023‑2024) and whether different usage patterns affect academic performance.

## 🔍 Overview
- 891 students participated; 159 reported using AI after disclosing its use.
- AI use was allowed during the exams, and the period captured a rise in AI applications and awareness.
- The research was published as *Self‑regulated approaches to students’ generative AI use in higher education* (Hakola et al., 2026).

## 🛠️ Types of AI Use
- **Instrumental use** – delegating assignments to AI and submitting AI‑generated responses with little or no critical reflection or revision.
- **Ideational use** – employing AI to generate ideas and perspectives for an assignment.
- **Reflective use** – first creating content independently, then asking AI for feedback or to identify missing perspectives, using AI as a learning support.

## 📊 Impact on Grades
| Approach | Typical Behavior | Relative Grade Outcome |
|----------|------------------|------------------------|
| Instrumental | Rely on AI‑generated answers without reflection | May be lower than students who do not use AI |
| Ideational | Use AI for ideas and perspectives, but trust them heavily | Leads to better outcomes than instrumental use |
| Reflective | Produce own work first, then seek AI feedback | Highest grades, outperforming both other AI users and non‑users |

> “This is why it is important to teach students how to use AI effectively and purposefully, without using it as a substitute for their own work or thinking, so that it becomes a useful part of their learning and competence development,” says Associate Professor Outi Hakola.

## 💡 Why it matters
- The study emphasizes that AI is **neither inherently good nor bad**; its impact depends on *meaningful* use.
- Reflective use aligns with responsible AI practices and supports student performance.
> “Based on our findings, we see reflective use of AI as one possible path towards using AI in a way that is both responsible and supportive of student performance.”

## 📅 Future Outlook
- The researchers note that the study took place while AI was gradually becoming part of university teaching.
- Hakola suggests investigating in about five years how AI has become integrated into students’ learning processes.
> “This is an overly simplistic view of a complex issue.”

The findings challenge the public assumption that students use AI in a single way and highlight the need for education on effective AI integration in higher‑education curricula.
