# Block launches Dessa, a speech-to-speech AI trained solely on human conversation

Jack Dorsey’s Block announced an AI model called **Dessa** that is built from scratch using only human conversation and operates as a true speech‑to‑speech system.

## 🔍 Overview
- Dessa is described as a *"true speech-to-speech model, and everything it knows came from human speech"*.
- The model is showcased with chats that can feel a little awkward and uncanny, highlighting its attempt at natural back‑and‑forth dialogue.

## 🧩 How it works
- Training data: **only human spoken conversations** – no written text was used.
- The team likens the approach to how babies learn to talk without ever seeing a word written down.
- They state, *"We wanted to see if a model could too. It can. It’s just very hard."*

## ⚙️ Key details
- Runs locally on a **MacBook Pro M4 Max (or better)**.
- In a shared example the model initially mistakes the speaker for someone named *"Ronaldo"* before settling into a mostly natural exchange.
- Demonstrations include:
  - A voice‑based guessing game.
  - A discussion about Christopher Nolan’s films that drifts into an unexpected tangent about Bryan Singer’s Superman movies.
  - A conversation about a surprise trip to Paris, which the model answers with what sounds like teary gratitude.
  - An interaction with OpenAI’s GPT‑Live‑1 voice model where Dessa appears more natural.

## 🚀 Availability
- Early access will be given to **a small group of AI safety researchers**.

## 💡 Why it matters
The Dessa team cites three motivations:
- **Curiosity (aka fun)** – exploring whether a model can learn speech the way a human baby does.
- **Better understanding between humans and AI** – aiming for a more natural conversational interface.
- **Safety** – current voice AIs flatten speech to text, miss timing, and cannot detect emotion. The team warns of scenarios like *"being in serious pain while a voice AI has no idea and just keeps going"* and positions Dessa as a step toward fixing that problem.
