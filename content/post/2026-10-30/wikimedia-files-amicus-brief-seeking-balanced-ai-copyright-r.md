# Wikimedia Files Amicus Brief Seeking Balanced AI Copyright Rulings

The Wikimedia Foundation has filed an amicus brief in the high‑profile AI copyright lawsuits involving The New York Times, OpenAI and Microsoft. The nonprofit argues for a nuanced legal approach: training large‑language models (LLMs) on copyrighted content should be protected as fair use, while chatbots that directly reproduce that content to users should require a license.

## 🔍 Overview
- Wikimedia filed the brief on Friday, weighing in on a set of lawsuits that pit major publishers against OpenAI and Microsoft.
- The filing does **not** take a side for either the publishers or the AI companies; it instead pushes courts toward a middle ground.

## ⚖️ Legal Nuance
- **Training vs. Regurgitating**: Using copyrighted material to train LLMs is presented as a transformative, fair‑use activity.  
- **Output that Substitutes the Original**: When an AI system pulls a passage and returns it to a user, effectively supplanting the original work, Wikimedia says that should require a license because the output is not sufficiently transformative.
- **Licensing Requirements**: If AI companies use Wikipedia‑licensed content, they must credit the source and keep any remix or adaptation under the same Creative Commons license.

## 🤖 Impact on AI & Wikipedia
- **Benefits of LLMs for Wikipedia**: The models can help editors detect vandalism, spot copyright violations, identify doxxing, and flag articles needing improvement.
- **Risks to the Community**: AI services that scrape and reproduce Wikipedia content can siphon traffic away, weakening the volunteer community that maintains the site.
- **Potential Outcomes**:
  - A *publisher‑favored* ruling could let copyright holders “gatekeep” even innovative uses of their work.
  - An *AI‑favored* ruling could become “a green light to snub licensing terms” and fragment knowledge flow.

## 💬 Wikimedia Leadership Statements
- Stephen LaPorte (General Council): “A ruling that does not apply fair use principles carefully could threaten the sustainability of free knowledge, and stifle the development of open source technologies, ultimately impacting access to information for everyone in both cases.”
- Bernadette Meehan (CEO): “We are taking a position in this case to defend the free and open human knowledge that Wikipedia depends on.”
- The brief also notes that Wikipedia is created through a collaborative process that requires a strong foundation of open and accessible human knowledge.

## 📄 Key Points from the Brief
- Wikimedia sites are “one of the most critical sources of data for all artificial intelligence and machine learning training.”
- It alleges OpenAI infringed copyright by scraping Wikipedia to build training datasets.
- OpenAI and Microsoft argue that LLM use is fair use; OpenAI calls regurgitation “a rare bug,” while Microsoft cites analysis showing Copilot rarely reproduces full sentences, “hardly undermines the transformative purpose of LLM training.”
- The brief warns that a nuance‑free victory for either side could harm the open web and the future of free knowledge.
