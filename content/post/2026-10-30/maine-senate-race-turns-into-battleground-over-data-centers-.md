# Maine Senate Race Turns Into Battleground Over Data Centers and AI Policy

Maine’s high‑profile Senate race has become a flashpoint for the clash between rapid data‑center expansion, AI industry influence, and local voters worried about jobs, utilities and the future of federal policy.

## 🔍 Overview
- Republican Sen. Susan Collins was defending her record in the final debate when an audience member interrupted her, which she brushed off by tying the man to Democratic challenger Troy Jackson.
- The rapid expansion of resource‑hungry data centers has emerged as one of the state’s most volatile campaign issues just days before the election.
- Maine’s Senate race is projected to be the most expensive per‑capita in U.S. history, with Democrats spending about $81 million and Republicans $114 million.
- Much of that cash is linked to the data‑center and AI industry that Mainers have been fighting for over a year.

## 💰 Campaign Finance
- Major tech companies and private‑equity firms tied to data centers and AI have invested millions in the midterms to win over skeptical locals.
- The influx of industry money has turned the race into a test of which party will control the Senate and shape federal data‑center and AI policy.

## 🏗️ Data Center Controversy
- In April, the Maine Legislature passed what would have been the nation’s first statewide moratorium on large data centers; Governor Janet Mills vetoed the bill ten days later.
- A September poll by UMass Lowell and YouGov found that 76 % of Mainers oppose building a data center in their community, with 58 % strongly opposed.
- Towns and cities across the state enacted temporary bans on data‑center projects this summer.
- Maine and other rural states are prime targets for new data centers because they offer cheap land, vacant industrial mills, and ample water from rivers and waterways.

## 🤖 AI Policy Stakes
- The race’s outcome will help determine which party controls the Senate and will influence federal data‑center and AI policy, affecting construction nationwide.
- Molly White, researcher behind Tech Influence Watch, notes that voters link data‑center growth to rising utility costs and that the AI‑industry playbook mirrors the cryptocurrency industry’s 2024 tactics.

## 🌲 Historical Context
- Maine’s traditional economy has centered on lumber, timber, shipbuilding, commercial fishing, textiles, and pulp‑and‑paper manufacturing.
- The timber industry contributed roughly $8.3 billion to Maine’s economy in 2024, but Trump‑era tariffs and rising operating costs have caused mill closures and layoffs.

## 📣 Voices from the Field
- Mark Brewer, chair of political science at the University of Maine, says former mill towns are eager to rebuild tax bases but remain cautious because of past boom‑and‑bust cycles.
- Nicholas Jacobs, director of the Public Policy Lab at Colby College, says residents question whether to trust the companies promising data‑center projects or the government facilitating the deals, citing concerns about opaque ownership, limited long‑term employment, and wealth extraction from rural communities.
- Economic research indicates most data centers create virtually no net employment after construction, despite promises of long‑term job creation.
