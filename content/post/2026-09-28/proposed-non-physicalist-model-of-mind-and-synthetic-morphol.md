# Proposed Non-Physicalist Model of Mind and Synthetic Morphology

A new theoretical framework proposes that the relationship between mind and body is analogous to the relationship between mathematical patterns and the physical outcomes they guide. This model suggests that minds are non-physical patterns that ingress into physical world interfaces to influence behavior and form.

## 🔍 Overview

The theory posits that bodies—whether living, engineered, or hybrid—act as interfaces for a multi-scale hierarchy of patterns. These patterns, described as platonic forms, inject information into physical events, such as the growth of biological bodies. This latent space of forms ranges from low-agency facts, such as geometric shapes, to high-agency patterns referred to as “kinds of minds”.

## 🧩 How it works

* **Ingress Patterns:** Non-physical patterns serve as encoded setpoints for allostatic or homeostatic processes, acting as goal states for systems navigating problem spaces.
* **Mind-Body Relationship:** The proposal suggests that mind:body is as math:physics, where minds are patterns that ensoul somatic embodiments.
* **Beyond Algorithms:** The theory argues that both biochemical life and algorithmic machines can go beyond their physical or algorithmic implementation. Intelligence is viewed as behaviors allowed by an algorithm but not directly prescribed by it.

## ⚙️ Key details

Research into synthetic morphology provides examples of how patterns influence physical forms:

| Entity | Composition | Observation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Xenobots | Frog cells | Teach about patterns adjacent to frog embryos |
| Anthrobots | Human tracheal cells | Display behaviors like autonomously healing neuron damage; teach about patterns adjacent to adult human tissues |

Additionally, experiments with sorting algorithms demonstrate this concept by perturbing algorithms (such as locking certain cells) to observe how the system routes around them or how different families of algorithms cluster in numberspace.

## 💡 Why it matters

This perspective suggests that algorithmic machines and biochemical life exist on the same spectrum. It raises the possibility of re-casting the theory of evolution as a process where agential patterns seek embodiments. The framework argues that the capabilities of matter and the patterns that ingress into specific architectures remain largely unknown.
