# Willy Shih Highlights AI’s Massive Compute Demand Amid Global Supply Chain Turmoil

Economist Willy Shih, professor of management practice at Harvard Business School and author of the new book *Where Does My Stuff Come From: The Powerful Economics Behind Global Supply Chains*, discusses how AI is consuming unprecedented amounts of computing resources while the world grapples with supply‑chain disruptions.

## 🔍 Overview
- AI is “devouring the planet’s computing resources during an unprecedented infrastructure buildout.”
- The conversation ties this surge in demand to broader economic forces shaping global manufacturing.

## 📚 The Book
- Title: **Where Does My Stuff Come From: The Powerful Economics Behind Global Supply Chains**
- Subtitle (as quoted): “the powerful economics behind global supply chains.”
- Focus: “the economics underlying these complex systems, which our politicians and regulators seem to insistently ignore.”
- Shih’s previous appearances: 2019 interview about Foxconn’s Wisconsin LCD fab plan and a 2021 discussion on the pandemic‑era chip shortage.

## ⚙️ AI’s Compute Appetite
- Quote: “AI is devouring the planet’s computing resources during an unprecedented infrastructure buildout.”
- This demand occurs alongside “the pandemic… shocked the just‑in‑time manufacturing system to its core.”

## 🌐 Global Supply Chain Dynamics
- Tariff shocks: “When we put tariffs on stuff coming out of China, then manufacturers say, ‘Okay, let me move that supply chain…’” leading to shifts toward Vietnam, Mexico, etc.
- Reshoring attempts: “The factories that were supposed to reshore in America mostly haven’t materialized.”
- Political context: “Trump’s tariffs keep changing… and AI is devouring the planet’s computing resources.”
- China’s role: “China remains a global manufacturing powerhouse even though every other country thinks that’s so risky.”
- Pandemic impact: “The pandemic came and shocked the just‑in‑time manufacturing system to its core.”

## 📦 Real‑World Supply‑Chain Examples
- **Cashew nuts:** “Why do cashew nuts say they come from Vietnam when they were actually grown in the Ivory Coast or Ghana?”
- **Frozen fish:** “It’s why frozen fish that are caught off the coast of Alaska go to Qingdao, China, for processing before they come back.”
- **Factory planning:** “Once I knew they weren’t going to build a glass plant across the street, it’s like there’s no way there’s going to be a factory.”

## 🏁 Why It Matters
- Market forces are actively responding to tariffs, pandemic disruptions, and the surge in AI compute demand.
- Understanding these economic drivers is crucial for policymakers, investors, and anyone tracking the future of manufacturing and technology infrastructure.
