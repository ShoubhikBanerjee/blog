# Amazon Bee Frontier AI wearable records, summarizes and confuses reality

A user recounts their experience with the Amazon Bee Frontier, an always‑recording AI wearable that listens to conversations, transcribes them, and provides context‑aware suggestions.

## 🔍 Overview
- Bee is an **always‑recording AI wearable**.
- It *listens to all your conversations* and uses AI to **transcribe and summarize** them.

## 🧩 How it works
- Audio is captured continuously and fed to a transcription model.
- The model identifies the speaker’s speech versus background TV dialogue – accuracy has improved over the past 19 months.
- Transcriptions are processed into **summaries** that analyze content, tone, and takeaways.
- Users can **swipe right** to confirm a generated “fact” about themselves, or **swipe left** to dismiss it.
- Bee can pull data from the user’s calendar and photos app, and can suggest Amazon purchases based on spoken remarks.

## ⚙️ Features
- **List**: suggests to‑do items derived from conversation topics.
- **Grow**: highlights personal improvement areas.
- Various summaries that break down conversation **content, tone, and key takeaways**.
- **Chatbot**: answers questions about the user or about what was said and when.
- Integration with **calendar and photos** for contextual suggestions.
- Job‑related assistance such as remembering talking points.

## ⚠️ Issues & Side Effects
- The device has caused the user to **question their reality**.
- In the last three weeks it generated false “facts,” e.g., arguing with a coworker’s spouse (never met), a family member having undiagnosed bipolar disorder (no indication), and a habit of wearing good pants for video calls.
- A therapist advised the user to **"chuck the Amazon Bee Frontier out a window"** and required consent to wear it during a session.
- After a therapy session, Bee produced a fact about childhood domestic‑violence trauma, titled **“Reclaiming Reality from Manipulative Power,”** which the user found distressing and was forced to confirm as true.
- The device was left on during an annual physical, ending up in a pile of clothes.
