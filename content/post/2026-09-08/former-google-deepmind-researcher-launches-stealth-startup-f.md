# Former Google DeepMind Researcher Launches Stealth Startup for Humanoid Robot Training

Former Google DeepMind researcher Danijar Hafner has launched a new startup in San Francisco's SoMa district to build AI that enables humanoid robots to navigate unfamiliar environments. Currently operating in stealth mode, the venture aims to solve the challenge of deploying robots in human spaces by training them using simulated physical realities.

## 🔍 Overview

Hafner’s brand-new startup is currently housed in an office with no name on the door. The physical space contains humanoids of various shapes and sizes, which are imported from China and hang like marionettes from racks in the center of the office. 

The primary objective of the venture is to continue Hafner's longtime work in enabling AI to successfully navigate environments that were not encountered during its training. This capability is critical for deploying robots in everyday human settings, such as a home, where a robot must handle a floor plan and furniture layout it has never seen before.

## 🧩 How it works

To bypass the traditional, real-world trial-and-error training usually required in robotics, Hafner relies on model-based reinforcement learning:

* **World Models:** Hafner develops AI models designed to emulate physical reality.
* **Simulated Training:** Agents are trained within these world models, treating them as real-world simulations to learn how to act.
* **Direct Execution:** This approach allows the agents and the physical robots they control to execute massively complicated tasks without requiring physical training trials.

## ⚙️ Key developments

Over several years, Hafner has honed and proved his approach by pitting agents trained within his world models against popular video games and physical reality.

| Project or Agent | Capabilities and Achievements |
| :--- | :--- |
| **PlaNet** | A model that allowed agents to execute actions by planning ahead. |
| **Dreamer 2** | The first agent to achieve human-level performance playing Atari 2600 games using a world model. |
| **Dreamer 3** | The first agent to solve the Minecraft Diamond challenge, successfully mining in-game gems on its own. |
| **Dreamer 4** | Learned to mine diamonds from an offline dataset of recorded gameplay videos, without ever interacting with the game directly. |
| **DayDreamer** | Used the Dreamer algorithm to let robots operate themselves in novel environments and react to new experiences (such as being pushed over) without any specific training. |

## 💡 Background

The 31-year-old researcher has a long history of working with leading AI organizations:

* **Early Interest:** Growing up in a rural town in northeastern Germany with classical musician parents, Hafner learned programming from a neighbor and took online AI courses during high school.
* **Academic and Research Roles:** While studying engineering as a second-year undergraduate at the Hasso Plattner Institute in Potsdam in 2015, he secured a student researcher role at Google Brain.
* **Industry Experience:** He went on to hold a dozen internships and positions at Google Brain and Google DeepMind across the UK, Canada, and the US.
* **Collaborations:** During his career, he worked alongside industry legends including Geoffrey Hinton, who is often referred to as one of the godfathers of AI, and Ashish Vaswani, coauthor of the research paper "Attention Is All You Need."
* **Industry Reception:** Timothy Lillicrap, one of Hafner’s former managers and coauthors at Google, describes him as a "standout among standouts."

Hafner left Google DeepMind in the fall of 2025 to launch this new startup, marking the physical embodiment and next evolution of his work.
