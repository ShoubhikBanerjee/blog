# Google DeepMind Launches AlphaGenome Atlas to Map Genetic Variants

Google DeepMind has introduced AlphaGenome Atlas, an AI-powered platform that provides a predictive map of every possible single-letter DNA change in the human genome. This dataset is designed to help researchers understand how genetic mutations impact molecular biology and contribute to disease.

## 🔍 Overview
The human genome consists of roughly three billion letter pairs, and there are approximately nine billion potential single-letter substitutions. AlphaGenome Atlas serves as a comprehensive catalogue of these variants, offering predictions on how they may affect the body at a molecular level, such as alterations in protein production.

## 🧩 How it works
The platform leverages the AlphaGenome model, which was trained using public databases of human and mouse genomes to recognize patterns between DNA changes and biological processes. 

| Feature | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| AlphaGenome Atlas | A 1-petabyte dataset of 9 billion single-nucleotide variant predictions. |
| AlphaGenome Variant Impact (AVI) Score | A single number summarizing the predicted impact of each genetic variant. |
| AVI Feature Attributions | Data linking AVI scores to specific biological processes like RNA splicing or gene expression. |
| DNA Sequence Motifs | A collection of over 2,500 recurrent DNA sequences and their locations. |

## ⚙️ Key details
- The platform covers both coding regions (2% of the genome) and non-coding regions (98%), where most trait-associated variants reside.
- AlphaGenome Atlas is approximately 30 times larger than the AlphaFold Database.
- Researchers can use the AVI score to rank variants and identify those that warrant closer study in rare disease research.

## 🚀 Availability
AlphaGenome Atlas is available for academic research today through a web portal, the AlphaGenome API, and as a skill in the Antigravity agentic development platform. Commercial use on Google Cloud will be available soon.
