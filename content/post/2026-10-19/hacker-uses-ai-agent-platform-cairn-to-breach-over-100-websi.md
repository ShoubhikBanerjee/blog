# Hacker Uses AI Agent Platform Cairn to Breach Over 100 Websites

A hacker breached more than 100 websites using an army of AI agents that largely operated on their own, according to the AI security firm Norforge.

## 🔍 Overview
- The attacks targeted companies in the United States, Canada, Australia, and Japan.
- Reuters identified victims that included **John Deere**, **SharkNinja**, and **Cummins**.
- Norforge described the attacker as the “most active” among the exposed Cairn instances it discovered.

## 🧩 How it works
- **Cairn** is an open‑source AI platform that calls itself a “general‑purpose problem‑solving engine.”
- Users define a start and end point for AI agents, which then document their progress as they move forward.
- While designed for legitimate problem‑solving, Norforge notes that the same tool can be repurposed to orchestrate cyber‑attacks.
- The hacker set “preferred intrusion tactics” in fewer than 100 Chinese characters, and successful breaches took about **23 hours**.

## ⚙️ Key details
- **Scale**: More than 100 compromised sites; over 20 exposed Cairn instances deemed “likely malicious.”
- **Human involvement**: The AI agents carried out the attacks with little involvement from the human hacker.
- **Company responses**:
  | Company | Reported impact |
  |---|---|
  | John Deere | No indication that sensitive information was accessed |
  | SharkNinja | Breach affected a standalone site with product support information; threat contained |
  | Cummins | Investigating a potential cyber‑attack on a website operated by a third‑party vendor |
- Norforge is working to have the malicious Cairn instances taken down.
- Cairn’s creator, @le1xia0, stated the platform “was never a ‘hacking tool’” and that misuse is a human decision.

## 💡 Why it matters
- The incidents illustrate how advanced AI tools can be weaponized to conduct large‑scale cyber‑attacks with minimal human direction.
- As AI capabilities grow, the line between legitimate automation and malicious misuse becomes increasingly blurred, heightening concerns for cybersecurity professionals.
