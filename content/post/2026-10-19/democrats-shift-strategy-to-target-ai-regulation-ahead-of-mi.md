# Democrats shift strategy to target AI regulation ahead of midterm elections

Democrats are preparing to address AI as a major political issue ahead of the November midterm elections, with polls predicting they could win back control of at least one chamber of Congress. This shift would allow committee leadership to set agendas, call CEOs to testify, and demand documents regarding the industry’s inner workings. Democratic strategists suggest the issue is ready for political capitalize because of widespread public concerns regarding how the technology impacts jobs and education.

## 🔍 Overview
While President Donald Trump has adopted an accelerationist approach and opposes most regulation, Democrats are positioning themselves to press the industry on tax credits for data centers and safety accountability. Strategists argue that the party is well positioned to capitalize on negative sentiment toward AI because the opposition's anti-regulation stance has limited their flexibility. Current campaign ads are already featuring AI issues, particularly in response to local concerns over data center development.

## ⚙️ Key details
*   **Tomorrow Together Super PAC:** Launched by Rob Flaherty and Karthik Ganapathy, the group started a $4 million ad blitz in battleground House races attacking Republican candidates as out of touch on AI.
*   **Leading the Future (LTF):** This pro-AI super PAC spent nearly $45 million over a year, though donor Ron Conway now reportedly regrets funding the group.
*   **State Leadership Actions:** California Governor Gavin Newsom issued an executive order for an AI "kill switch," and Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro has reversed course against data centers.
*   **Proposed Regulatory Agenda:** Potential measures include embedding independent researchers in labs, requiring upfront safety checks, and imposing liability on tech companies for discrimination or impacts on children.

## 💡 Why it matters
The focus on AI reflects a broader strategy to connect with voters on issues like job displacement and corporate accountability. Lawmakers are considering demands that AI companies help pay for the impacts their technology has on constituents' lives. According to Democratic strategist Jesse Lehrich, the issue is a story of politicians and industry interests that fits the current national vibe, offering a significant political opportunity for the party if they choose to seize it.
