# FCC approves SpaceX to launch up to 1 million AI‑chip satellites under Starmind

The FCC announced Wednesday that it has authorized SpaceX to deploy up to one million AI‑chip‑equipped satellites under the company’s Starmind initiative.

## 🔍 Overview
- FCC approval for a full AI satellite constellation.
- First such approval; Google and Blue Origin are also pursuing similar concepts.

## 🧩 How it works
- Starmind will capture solar energy in space to power AI workloads on Earth.
- Data will be sent to Earth via high‑speed lasers that link to the Starlink network.
- Chips for the satellites will be produced at SpaceX’s upcoming Terafab facility.

## ⚙️ Key details
- Deployment deadline: at least one satellite must be in orbit by 2033.
- Full constellation target: 1 million satellites by 2040.
- FCC deferred SpaceX’s request to use “graveyard” orbits above 600 km, citing insufficient disposal information.

## 🚀 Timeline & Competing Efforts
| Company | Project | Recent development |
|---|---|---|
| SpaceX | Starmind | FCC approval; deployment schedule 2033‑2040 |
| Google | Project Suncatcher | Launched its first AI server satellite last month |
| Blue Origin | Project Suncatcher | Will perform the first dedicated heavy‑lift launch for the project in 2029 |

## 💡 Why it matters
- Provides a regulatory pathway for large‑scale AI compute in orbit.
- Could extend AI processing capacity while using solar power captured in space.
