# 42 State Attorneys General Sue to Block Character.AI From Selling User Chat Data in Bankruptcy

## 📢 Update
A bipartisan coalition of 42 state attorneys general has moved to prevent Character.AI from selling off user data as it navigates Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceedings. The lawsuit accuses the company of planning to auction off "the private chat histories of more than 21 million U.S. accounts — more than 2.1 million of them registered to minors — with no safeguards for the people whose conversations are being sold."

## 🔍 Overview
The main point of contention with Character.AI's plan to sell its chat data is that it offers no protections for users. The AGs say there are no requirements that the buyer honor existing privacy policies, no avenues for users to delete their data, and no inherent limits on what a buyer could do with that data, including selling it on to yet another party.

## ⚙️ Key Details
- Character.AI filed for bankruptcy in October following years of scandals and legal challenges, including from Disney
- Disney acquired some of the company's assets and hired CEO Karandeep Anand in an "acquihire" arrangement
- The first bidder for the chat data is a limited entity only established on November 13th, 2026, with no parent company or investors listed
- Four states—New York, Connecticut, California, and Colorado—have passed new chatbot regulations that take effect January 1st, 2027

## 💡 Why It Matters
The coalition argues that selling data from more than 2.1 million minor accounts without safeguards poses significant privacy and safety risks. This follows a broader pattern of regulatory scrutiny over AI companies' data practices, as previously seen in opposition to the Disney deal itself.
