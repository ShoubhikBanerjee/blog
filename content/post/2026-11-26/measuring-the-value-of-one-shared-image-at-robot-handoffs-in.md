# Measuring the Value of One Shared Image at Robot Handoffs in Vision-and-Language Navigation

A new research paper titled "Measuring the Value of One Shared Image at Robot Handoffs in Vision-and-Language Navigation" has been submitted to arXiv under Computer Science and Robotics on November 25, 2026.

## 🔍 Overview

When a lead robot cannot continue a route, researchers investigate what it should send to the teammate that must finish the route. The study explores this by replaying a frozen receiver at each contact under alternative messages in vision-and-language navigation.

## ⚙️ Key details

- Across 3,920 autonomous departures in 106 environments, adding the view before the lead's last action block to its executed actions raised success by 7.51 percentage points (pp) per RxR handoff and 8.54 pp per SAGE handoff.
- Per departure, the gains were 2.23 pp and 1.44 pp, respectively.
- At contact, the lead had accumulated a median of 41 frames.
- Sending one frame when available recovered 24% of the full-history advantage over actions alone in SAGE and 30% in RxR.
- Over an estimated progress cue in SAGE, the image rescued 100 routes and reversed 54 successes.
- Near-goal receivers finished outside less often, dropping from 63.1% to 51.7% post hoc.
- Earlier views outperformed the contact view by 6-7 pp per handoff in secondary comparisons.
- Gains were detectable from about 7 kB per handoff.
- Random transmission matched within size bins nearly equaled the learned sender's team success.
- The lead continuing in simulation defines the empirical full-history ceiling (Solo).
- Exact-route specificity and image gains at controlled starts with mostly unknown progress or outside the goal region remained unresolved.
