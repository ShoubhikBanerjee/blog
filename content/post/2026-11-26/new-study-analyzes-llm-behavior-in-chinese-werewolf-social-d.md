# New Study Analyzes LLM Behavior in Chinese Werewolf Social Deduction Games

Researchers have analyzed how large language models behave in social deduction scenarios, examining 5,404 public utterances from 150 Chinese Werewolf games played by 12 different models. The study compared ordinary wolves with ordinary villagers to isolate role-based differences, finding that wolf utterances scored higher on both certainty and uncertainty after adjusting for model and length differences. Three human annotators rated 392 utterances across four behavioral dimensions, while five LLM judges achieved 60.8%-70.3% accuracy on a shared evaluation set. Additional experiments with generation probes showed increased certainty and emotional intensification when models were prompted with a wolf identity but asked to speak as villagers. The findings suggest these patterns reflect role-associated language rather than verified lying, highlighting the need for explicit comparison groups and lexicon sensitivity checks in game-based deception evaluation.
