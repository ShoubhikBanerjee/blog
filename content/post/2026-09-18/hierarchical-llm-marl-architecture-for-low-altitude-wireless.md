# Hierarchical LLM-MARL Architecture for Low-Altitude Wireless Network Coordination

Researchers have proposed a hierarchical hybrid architecture combining large language models (LLMs) and multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) to manage heterogeneous unmanned aerial systems in low-altitude wireless networks (LAWNs).

## 🔍 Overview
Low-altitude wireless networks serve as infrastructure for unmanned aerial systems supporting concurrent services in shared three-dimensional airspace. These environments involve strong coupling between connectivity, mobility, and shared network resources, creating a dynamic non-cooperative game where coordination objectives and operating conditions evolve over time.

## 🧩 How it works
The proposed framework utilizes a dual-loop structure:

| Loop | Component | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Outer Adaptation Loop | LLM-assisted game orchestration | Interprets operator intent and service requirements to reconfigure resource priorities and objectives. |
| Inner Loop | Decentralized MARL policies | Executes parameter-conditioned policies under the configured game. |

## 💡 Why it matters
Conventional learning-based and optimization controllers often rely on predefined objectives, which limits their ability to adapt autonomously to changing resource priorities and service requirements. The proposed framework allows for coordination among heterogeneous services and adaptation to evolving conditions without the need to retrain underlying MARL policies, as demonstrated in a logistics-monitoring case study.
