# Kevin O’Leary’s Utah “Wonder Valley” Data Center Project Stalls After Political Backlash

## 🔍 Overview
- Kevin O’Leary announced plans to build the world’s biggest data center in Utah.
- The project is technically called **Stratos**, but it is more prominently known as **Wonder Valley**.

## 📏 Key Details
- **Size:** 40,000 acres of land.
- **Power:** Nine gigawatts, more than double the average power usage of the entire state of Utah.
- Intended as an AI campus, billed as the largest data center in the world.

| Name | Alias |
|------|-------|
| Stratos | Wonder Valley |

## 🏛️ Political & Legal Fallout
- The proposal upended Utah politics and created a political firestorm that stopped the project.
- Approval was pursued through the **Military Installation Development Authority (MIDA)**, a state agency that can override local land‑use decisions and act like a municipal government for its project areas.
- MIDA’s authority would have accelerated permitting, zoning, and other regulations.
- Local residents were kept in the dark, leading to anger that ultimately halted the development.
- O’Leary called his critics “Chinese spies” on Fox News, resulting in a defamation lawsuit; Fox News later issued an on‑air apology.

## 🔄 Aftermath
- The land originally slated for Wonder Valley is now being used for a Microsoft data center.
- The episode highlights broader themes: the AI industry’s infrastructure boom, bipartisan backlash to such build‑outs, and the challenge of local democracy that even massive financial resources cannot easily overcome.
