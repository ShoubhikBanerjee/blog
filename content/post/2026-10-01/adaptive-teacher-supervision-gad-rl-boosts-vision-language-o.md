# Adaptive Teacher Supervision (GAD-RL) Boosts Vision-Language OCR Performance

## 🔍 Overview
- Vision‑language models can rewrite anomalous text in images into linguistically plausible expressions, which harms the faithfulness of OCR transcription.
- Sequence‑level task rewards and local teacher guidance complement each other, but a fixed teacher’s guidance loses effectiveness as the student model improves, both across training checkpoints and across response groups with different task rewards.

## ⚙️ How it works
- **GAD‑RL** (Adaptive Guidance Regulation for Reinforcement Learning) adjusts teacher supervision during joint post‑training based on the student’s current task performance and local output distributions.
- A **frozen teacher** conditions on reference transcriptions and on prefixes generated by the student.
- The method **disables distillation** for response groups that contain an output with a task reward of at least **0.95** and **continuously attenuates** the distillation strength as the group‑mean reward rises.
- It also **weights the forward KL** term by the student’s probability of the teacher’s top‑1 token, reducing the impact of local auxiliary updates when the student gives low support to that candidate.

## 📊 Results
- On the **Qwen3.5‑2B** model, GAD‑RL attains **59.92% Micro Recall** on **CHAOS‑Bench**, outperforming:
  - **GRPO** by **8.45** percentage points,
  - **GRPO+OPD (fixed‑weight)** by **4.43** percentage points.
- The same model reaches an **Overall score of 91.18** on **OmniDocBench v1.6**.

## 💡 Why it matters
- By dynamically scaling teacher supervision, GAD‑RL maintains effective guidance throughout training, preventing the degradation observed with static teachers.
- The approach yields measurable gains on both micro‑level recall for OCR‑specific benchmarks and macro‑level performance across diverse document tasks, demonstrating that adaptive supervision can improve both fidelity and generalization in vision‑language systems.
