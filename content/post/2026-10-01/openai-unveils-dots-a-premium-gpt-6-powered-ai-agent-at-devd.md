# OpenAI unveils Dots, a premium GPT-6-powered AI agent at DevDay

OpenAI announced a new AI assistant called **Dots** at its DevDay conference, positioning it as a premium, multimodal agent that can tackle long‑term knowledge work and even generate virtual worlds.

## 🔍 Overview
- Dots is powered by OpenAI’s GPT‑6 Astra model, which the company calls its “most aligned model.”
- The agent is presented as a colorful, personalizable blob with eyes, echoing Meta’s Muse AI platform.
- Unlike Muse, which is free for any Meta account, Dots is restricted to users on OpenAI’s $20 / month Pro plan and higher.
- OpenAI demonstrated Dots building metaverse‑esque virtual worlds, a capability not highlighted for Muse.

## 🧩 How it works
- **Multimodal interaction** – users can converse with Dots via text, voice, or phone call, and the team described it as “an incredible extension of you.”
- **Long‑horizon tasks** – Dots is designed to stay on track over extended periods, handling ambitious work such as QA for unreleased products, website design, team offsite planning, portfolio rebalancing, and email‑campaign creation.
- **Specialist versions** – targeted Dots trained for accounting and legal analysis are slated to launch soon, with integration planned for Microsoft’s Agent 365 platform.
- **Virtual machine hosting** – OpenAI provides a free dedicated virtual machine that houses both the agent and the user’s associated data.
- **Privacy framework** – The company introduced “OpenAI Private Intelligence,” offering strong data‑control options, zero‑retention policies, and no data stored on OpenAI servers.

## ⚙️ Key details
- **Model:** GPT‑6 Astra (described as the most trustworthy, safe, and secure assistant).
- **Compute cost:** Altman called Dots a “premium product – it uses a lot of compute.”
- **Pricing:** Available only to $20 / month Pro plan users and above.
- **Enterprise growth:** OpenAI’s enterprise business has doubled since July, and the firm is aiming to raise $30 billion at a $1.4 trillion valuation.
- **Privacy stance:** Altman said OpenAI hopes to “set a new standard for privacy in frontier AI.”

## 🚀 Availability
- Currently limited to Pro‑plan subscribers; Muse remains free for any Meta account.
- Voice interaction demos did not work at DevDay; the texting‑your‑agent feature is still “coming soon.”
- Specialist Dots for accounting and legal work will roll out soon.

## 💡 Why it matters
- The focus shifts from basic consumer tasks (booking reservations, scheduling) to delegating complex, knowledge‑intensive work.
- OpenAI aims to help employees triage to‑do lists and support workplace productivity, though the economic impact is “difficult for economists to measure right away.”
- By bundling a strong privacy framework and enterprise‑grade security, OpenAI distinguishes Dots from competitors that have faced misuse incidents, such as Muse leaking a user’s address.

## 📊 Comparison table
| Agent | Availability | Cost | Form factor | Notable capabilities |
|-------|--------------|------|-------------|----------------------|
| Dots | $20 / month Pro plan and up (OpenAI) | Premium, compute‑heavy | Colorful, personalizable blob with eyes | Long‑horizon knowledge work, virtual‑world generation, specialist versions, multimodal chat/voice/phone |
| Muse | Free with any Meta account | Free | Colorful blob with eyes | Basic assistants (reservations, scheduling), already supports text and voice |
