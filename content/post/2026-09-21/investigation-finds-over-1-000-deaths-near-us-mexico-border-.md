# Investigation Finds Over 1,000 Deaths Near US-Mexico Border Surveillance Towers

A 15-month collaboration between MIT Technology Review and Times of San Diego has produced the first comprehensive map and analysis of deaths occurring near US Customs and Border Protection (CBP) surveillance towers along the US-Mexico border.

## 🔍 Overview

Journalists examined the failures of border surveillance technology and the stories of people who died in the borderlands. The investigation found more than 1,050 people died within range of border surveillance towers between 2015 and early 2026. This includes more than 110 deaths within range of modern autonomous towers from Anduril since 2021.

## ⚙️ Key Details

* **Scope of Analysis**: The study analyzed nearly 600 towers and cases dating back to 2015.
* **Findings**: Deaths were found within the advertised range of nearly two-thirds of the analyzed towers.
* **Terrain Analysis**: Topographical analysis showed that while some towers have sight of as little as 10% of their advertised area, most deaths did not occur in blind spots.
* **Investment**: The US has spent billions on the "virtual wall" and is set to spend $1 billion more to triple its size by 2034.

## 🧩 Methodology

To build the dataset, investigators merged records from the Electronic Frontier Foundation, No More Deaths, and Humane Borders with records from over a dozen agencies. 

* **Texas Data Collection**: Because Texas lacks a national protocol or online record-sharing initiative, researchers filed records requests to sheriffs in 17 counties beginning in July 2025.
* **AI Integration**: For the high volumes of records received from Hidalgo, Webb, and Kenedy Counties, the team used Anthropic’s Claude via API to inspect case reports and extract coordinates of where remains were found.
* **Verification**: A case was considered for analysis if investigators could confirm the person was believed to be crossing the border, where the remains were found, and roughly when they died.

## 💡 Why it Matters

Despite the use of AI-equipped towers designed to automatically detect and track people, many deaths remain unnoticed by technology:

* In 2024, 18 people died in one small stretch of desert in range of three Anduril towers.
* One individual died 360 feet from the closest surveillance tower and was first spotted by landfill employees.
* Another woman died near an Anduril tower in a location where terrain analysis indicated the tower should have had clear sight; her decomposed body was spotted weeks later by agents.
* The Government Accountability Office (GAO) reported in 2017 that some data collected by agents was unreliable, citing instances in Texas where agents credited towers for apprehensions in areas where no such towers existed.
