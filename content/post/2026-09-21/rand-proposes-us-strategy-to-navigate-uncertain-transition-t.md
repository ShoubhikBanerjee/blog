# RAND proposes US strategy to navigate uncertain transition to superintelligence

RAND has published a lengthy paper outlining strategies for the US to secure geopolitical advantage while navigating the uncertain path toward superintelligence. Because the future of AI development remains unpredictable, the report argues that the US should prioritize a "Freedom of Action" strategy to maintain options for ensuring both national advantage and human survival.

## 🧩 The Freedom of Action Strategy
To maintain this strategic flexibility, the report identifies four core pillars:

* Build a human-AI ecosystem: Invest in AI safety, develop tools for human agency, share technology benefits, prepare for societal disruption, and shape ecosystem incentives.
* Build an AI-security architecture: Gain visibility into frontier AI systems and compute, create technology to verify agreements, and build regulatory expertise.
* Adapt legacy national security enterprises: Overhaul institutions to capitalize on AI capabilities and manage associated changes.
* Invest in capacity: Provide decision-makers with information, diffuse positive AI uses, create crisis-response "break-glass" plans, and increase AI literacy.

## ⚙️ Strategic Archetypes
RAND classifies potential US approaches into seven strategies across three families:

| Strategy Family | Strategy Name | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Coexistence | Dominance | US leads AI development and suppresses others. |
| Coexistence | Co-Development | US leads a consortium to co-develop safe superintelligence with shared governance. |
| Coexistence | Preparedness | US mobilizes efforts via voluntary commitments and phased deployment. |
| Denial | Moratorium | US pursues a verifiable global halt to development above specific thresholds. |
| Denial | Deterrence | US halts its own frontier development and uses national power to stop foreign programs. |
| Denial | Continuity of Society | US creates hardened, self-sustaining settlements to ensure survival. |
| Acceleration | Acceleration | Relies on markets and rapid iteration to produce safety as a byproduct of utility. |

## 💡 Why it matters
The report emphasizes that effective AI strategy requires significant financial investment, even if that spending is primarily focused on preserving optionality. Currently, the US appears to favor an "acceleration" approach, which the report compares to focusing solely on engine power while neglecting essential safety features like brakes, headlights, or seatbelts.
