# GitHub Builds AI-Powered Conference Badge for Hands-On Learning

## 🎮 Playful Hacking at GitHub Universe

For the last four years at GitHub Universe, we’ve had hackable conference badges. What started as a simple e-ink display for your name and job title morphed into a labor of love for Martin Woodward, VP of Developer Relations. This year, the badge evolved into a device with a color screen, rechargeable battery, accelerometer sensors, and touch-sensitive gaming controls.

## 🤖 AI Accelerates Code Creation

According to the Octoverse report, 40% of pull requests were started by agents by September. That’s a big jump from the 17% we saw in March, which was already a pretty high number. AI can turn our ideas into working code faster than ever before.

## 🏃 Learning Through Building

At the GitHub Universe closing keynote, Martin wanted to teach himself machine learning, so he built himself a pedometer onto his conference badge. He used Copilot to collect movement data from the badge’s accelerometer, trained a simple machine-learning model in his browser, and deployed it to the badge. Then he walked around to test whether it could tell the difference between walking, standing still, and fidgeting.

## ⚖️ Balancing Innovation and Open Source Values

We’re seeing maintainers get absolutely hammered with AI-powered contributions to their repositories, which adds a lot to the maintenance burden. As Martin explained in the keynote, “Open source is the freedom to inspect, to remix, and to share without asking for permission. But it’s not the freedom to impose your will on others.”

## 🎮 Gaming on the Badge

We answered the perennial question on our badge, “But can it run Doom?” Why yes, yes it does.
