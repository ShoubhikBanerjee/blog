# Venture-backed startups use AI and FOIA requests to map government spending data

Venture-backed startups have raised over $100 million to collect data from thousands of government agencies, using AI to turn that information into “signals” that help businesses sell to governments. This trend has altered the landscape of public records requests, shifting from annual volume to high-frequency, specific data collection.

## 📜 The Shift in FOIA Requests
The US Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) of 1966 established citizens’ right to know what their government is doing, with every state now having its own version in law. Historically, commercial requesters have dominated this system. Margaret Kwoka, a law professor at Ohio State University, documented this trend in her 2016 study “FOIA, Inc.,” finding that commercial requesters accounted for 79% to 96% of public records requests at federal agencies like the Environmental Protection Agency and the Food and Drug Administration.

In Panton, Vermont, town clerk Kyle Rowe observed this change firsthand. Panton used to receive a request every year or so from SmartProcure for “all purchasing records.” Last year, those requests started coming quarterly. Subsequently, different companies began making more specific requests, such as records for every purchase related to emergency road flares. In Chautauqua County, New York, a local paralegal was hired this summer after seeing requests double, an increase the county attorney blamed on automated activity.

## 🏢 Key Players and Funding
Several companies have built their businesses around this model, raising significant capital to scale their operations:

- **SmartProcure (GovSpend):** Pioneered the model 15 years ago with a standardized approach for collecting government purchasing data. The company sends roughly 180,000 requests per year.
- **Starbridge:** Has raised $52 million, including a Series A led by Craft Ventures, to build the equivalent of a “Bloomberg Terminal for public sector data.”
- **Pursuit:** Backed by investors including Bill Gurley and Jack Altman, it has raised $25.5 million to help companies identify likely government buyers using an “industrial-scale FOIA operation.” It also sends roughly 180,000 requests per year.
- **PinPoint Analytics:** Analyzes construction bid data. Its chief operating officer, Mark Zurada, stated the company sent as many as 2,000 requests per day while building its dataset, settling closer to 500 today.
- **NationGraph:** Serves midsize vendors, such as janitorial service providers, and claims to send about 10,000 requests per day on behalf of clients.
