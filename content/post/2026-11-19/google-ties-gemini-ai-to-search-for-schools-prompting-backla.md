# Google ties Gemini AI to Search for schools, prompting backlash from officials

Google announced that starting next year, schools will not be able to disable Gemini-powered AI features without also losing access to Google Search, a change that has drawn sharp criticism from elected officials and major school districts.

## 🔍 Overview
- Google vice president of education Chris Phillips told *The New York Times* that "Search and AI are deeply integrated," and schools turning off AI features "will not have access to Search."
- The policy shift means Gemini app, Gemini Notebook, and Search will be off by default until schools decide which services to enable, but they cannot keep Search while disabling the AI components.
- New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani and Senator Bernie Sanders labeled the move "extortion."
- Both New York City and Chicago public schools have threatened to seek alternative search providers if Google does not offer a version of Search without AI.

## ⚙️ Key details

### Google’s stated changes
- Piloting "Guided Learning capabilities in Search" this month to walk students step-by-step through complex problems instead of giving quick answers.
- Updating controls next year so Gemini app, Gemini Notebook, and Search are off by default until schools choose which to enable.
- Building experiences "in partnership with educators" and listening to ensure products support students and teachers.

### School district responses
| District | Response |
|---|---|
| New York City Public Schools | Chancellor Kamar H. Samuels: "If Google doesn’t reconsider their position, we will explore other search options." |
| Chicago Public Schools | Told Google that if it cannot provide Search without AI, "the district will look to other search solutions." |

## 🏫 Political and educator concerns
- Mayor Mamdani recently announced a policy barring 2-K-8 students from using AI in the classroom.
- Educators worry about AI’s effect on students’ critical thinking skills; some studies show students who use AI generally perform worse on tests.
- Concerns raised about mental health effects of children developing relationships with chatbots at a young age.
- Google spokesperson Laura Breen stated: "We hear parents and teachers’ concerns about students using AI to shortcut learning, and we share the same goal: help students learn."

## 💡 Why it matters
- Google is deeply embedded in U.S. classrooms through Google Classroom, Chromebooks, and other services, making a fully Google-free transition difficult for districts.
- The integration of AI into core search functionality removes the ability for schools to opt out of AI while retaining essential search tools.
- The standoff highlights growing tension between AI deployment in education and pedagogical concerns about critical thinking and student wellbeing.
