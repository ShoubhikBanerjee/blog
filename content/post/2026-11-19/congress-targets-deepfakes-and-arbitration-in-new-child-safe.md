# Congress Targets Deepfakes and Arbitration in New Child‑Safety Bills

Lawmakers are moving to give victims of online harm new legal tools before the end of 2026, focusing on deepfake creators, forced arbitration, and platforms like Roblox.

## 🔍 Overview
- Lawmakers are trying to pass new protections for victims of online harm before the end of 2026, targeting deepfake creators and online platforms that block users from filing lawsuits.
- Congress is scrambling to provide legal recourse for deepfakes and online grooming.
- The session ends in a little more than three weeks.

## 📜 Legislative Actions
- **Senate Judiciary Subcommittee Hearing** – Called for ending forced arbitration for online harms, targeting Roblox for using arbitration clauses on minors.
- **Hawley‑Durbin Bill** – Would render arbitration agreements unenforceable for disputes over alleged child sexual exploitation or “child cyber injury.”
- **Blumenthal‑Britt‑Booker Bill** – Similar approach, explicitly covering data‑privacy violations.
- **Defiance Act** – Sponsored by Reps. Alexandria Ocasio‑Cortez and Laurel Lee; would let victims of non‑consensual intimate deepfakes sue creators for damages.

## 🏢 Targeted Companies
- **Roblox** – Facing about 180 lawsuits in a consolidated case over its alleged role in failing to protect children. The platform enforces forced arbitration clauses even for users as young as 10.
- **Tech companies and perpetrators** – Could become vulnerable to a new wave of legal challenges if the bills pass.

## 🗣️ Voices & Quotes
- “The best thing we can do in terms of enforcement is we can open up the courthouse doors,” Sen. Josh Hawley (R‑MO) told reporters after the Roblox hearing.
- Hawley called the jury system “corruption‑proof” and “an incredibly big hammer.”
- “Safety has been central to Roblox since we launched. We require age checks for chat, place users under 16 in age‑based accounts with protective defaults, and block images and video in chat,” said Roblox’s global head of public policy Nicky Jackson Colaço.
- Paris Hilton: “Some people might have thought that was the end of my fight. But when I make a promise to survivors, I keep it.”
- Hilton noted that over 50 million people have been victimized by sexually explicit deepfakes and that “strangers have created over 100,000 AI‑generated explicit images and videos portraying me.”
- Law professor Myriam Gilles: passing either proposal “would have a profound impact.”

## 📊 Bills at a Glance
| Bill | Sponsors | Primary Aim |
|------|----------|------------|
| Hawley‑Durbin Arbitration Bill | Sen. Josh Hawley (R‑MO), Sen. Dick Durbin (D‑IL) | Make arbitration agreements unenforceable for child sexual exploitation/​cyber injury disputes |
| Blumenthal‑Britt‑Booker Bill | Sens. Richard Blumenthal (D‑CT), Katie Britt (R‑AL), Cory Booker (D‑NJ) | End forced arbitration; explicitly cover data‑privacy violations |
| Defiance Act | Reps. Alexandria Ocasio‑Cortez (D‑NY), Laurel Lee (R‑FL) | Allow victims of non‑consensual intimate deepfakes to sue creators |

## ⚖️ Why It Matters
- Litigation has become one of the more successful ways for survivors of online harm to seek change.
- Ending forced arbitration could expose platform practices to public discovery, something currently hidden under arbitration.
- If the Defiance Act or arbitration bans pass, creators of harmful deepfakes and platforms that enable them could face increased legal and financial liability.
- The push reflects broader congressional frustration with missed deadlines on child‑safety, privacy, and consumer‑protection bills, even as the Take It Down Act begins to address deepfakes.
