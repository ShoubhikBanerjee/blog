# Global Bank Evaluates Ten Agentic AI Platforms for Financial Services Workloads

An industrial evaluation programme run by the innovation laboratory of a global bank has assessed ten agentic AI platforms across three financial-services workloads. This "bakeoff" evaluated systems spanning open-source frameworks, proprietary products, and hyperscaler ecosystems to address how enterprise platforms are currently procured and analyzed.

## 🔍 Overview
Agentic AI platforms—systems in which LLM agents plan, invoke tools, retain memory, and coordinate to change the state of enterprise systems—are being procured faster than they can be measured. While public agent benchmarks score models in fixed harnesses, enterprises buy integrated platforms where orchestration, memory, governance, and tooling layers dominate outcomes, largely relying on the strength of vendor demonstrations.

## ⚙️ Key details
A cross-functional panel assessed ten platforms on nine capability dimensions across three financial-services workloads:
* Counterparty credit risk
* Retrieval-augmented generation
* Process automation

Analyses of the evaluation results showed that:
* Scores separated the platforms into three tiers that are stable under alternative weightings.
* Workload-specific strengths were surfaced that aggregate scores hid.
* A secondary analysis revealed a halo effect, with Cronbach's alpha = 0.98 across nominally distinct dimensions and rank correlations >= 0.93 with the total for eight of nine dimensions. This indicates that demonstration-based scoring measures presentation as much as capability.

## 💡 Why it matters
To turn enterprise platform evaluation from structured judgment into measurement, a new "gate-then-score" evaluation protocol is proposed. This protocol consists of:
* Four layers: capability, execution reliability, governance gates, and business value
* Trace-level reliability metrics estimated over repeated runs
* An autonomy-readiness scale
* A reproducibility protocol
