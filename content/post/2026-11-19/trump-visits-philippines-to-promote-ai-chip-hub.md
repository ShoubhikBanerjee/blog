# Trump visits Philippines to promote AI chip hub

President Donald Trump was greeted by beauty queens and protesters as he landed in the Philippines, where he aims to promote a proposed AI chip hub.

## 🔍 Overview
- Trump was greeted by five beauty queens upon arrival.
- A child handed him small US and Philippine flags.
- A band played "YMCA" as dancers performed.
- Trump is eyeing the Philippines to build chip fabs and data centers.
- Hundreds of protesters packed streets outside the airport, bearing signs and a prop casket.

## ⚙️ Key details
- The proposed hub covers 1,620 hectares (4,000 acres) and includes chip factories, mineral processing plants, and data centers.
- The US-led Pax Silica initiative aims to secure chips and critical minerals, with at least two dozen countries signed on.
- The Philippines and US are expected to sign a framework agreement for the Pax Silica Economic Security Zone by the end of the year.
- The Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA) expects an economic boom from the project, with potential data centers on site.
- Human rights advocates warn of displacement of Indigenous residents and small farmers.
- The Aeta Hungey tribe has been trying since 1994 to secure an official certificate recognizing its ancestral domain.
- The BCDA can offer leases lasting up to 99 years to foreign developers.
- The BCDA expects to rake in close to $1 billion USD from lease income over the first 25 years.
- The BCDA was in talks with around 30 companies interested in setting up shop in the AI hub.
- An American energy company is conducting a feasibility study into building a liquefied natural gas (LNG) line and plant.
- LNG is primarily methane, a smog-forming pollutant that is an even more potent greenhouse gas than carbon dioxide.
- The Philippine government wanted to invite Nvidia, TSMC, and Apple.
- Most of the companies interested were "high tech" manufacturing firms.

## 💡 Why it matters
- The project aims to insulate the US from China's dominance in manufacturing semiconductors.
- The potential displacement of Indigenous residents and small farmers raises human rights concerns.
- The proposed hub could significantly impact the local environment and economy.
