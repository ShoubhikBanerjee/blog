# Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) Shapes Content for AI Answer Engines

Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) is the emerging practice of preparing web content so that AI‑driven answer engines can retrieve, quote, and verify it as a source.

## 🔍 Overview
- GEO "covers every measure that makes a website retrievable, quotable and verifiable for AI answer engines such as Google's AI search, ChatGPT, Perplexity or Gemini".
- Modern search experiences often show a summarised AI answer first, with classic results below (e.g., Google’s AI Overviews and AI Mode).
- The main AI answer systems include:
  - Google AI search (AI Overviews, AI Mode)
  - ChatGPT with integrated web search
  - Perplexity
  - Google Gemini
  - Microsoft Copilot (built on Bing)
- These systems differ in the index they retrieve content from and how clearly they identify their sources.

## 🧩 How GEO Works
1. **Passage preparation** – The answer engine selects *individual passages*, not whole pages. Each passage must "stand on its own" and make sense without surrounding context.
2. **Retrieval crawler access** – The crawlers that feed AI answers are *not the classic Googlebot*; they index content specifically for generative use.
3. **Entity consistency** – Company name, description, and contact details should be identical everywhere so that the model can reliably identify the entity.

## ⚙️ Key Details
- SEO, AEO and GEO describe three stages of the same development:
  - **SEO** aims at a position in the results list.
  - **GEO** aims at being *cited as a source* inside an AI‑written answer.
- GEO can give visibility "in a place where you would otherwise not appear at all".
- GEO does **not** reliably increase traffic; a prominent AI answer can actually reduce clicks to the original site.
- Promises that GEO will boost traffic are unfounded.
- AI answer engines follow a similar source‑selection sequence: they retrieve documents, pick individual passages, combine them into an answer, and link some sources. If a site does not appear in the leading results for a sub‑question, it is usually not considered as a source.

## 📉 Impact on Website Visits
- Expect fewer visits to your website when your content is used as a quoted passage in a top‑level AI answer.
- While GEO raises the chance of being named as a source, it does not guarantee additional clicks.

## 📚 Managing Access and Training
- Widely used generative crawlers (as of October 2026) include:
  - GPTBot, OAI‑SearchBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google‑Extended.
- Blocking specific bots produces targeted effects:

| Bot                | Effect of Blocking                                                                 |
|--------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| GPTBot             | Prevents content from being used for model training                               |
| OAI-SearchBot      | Prevents content from appearing in ChatGPT search                                 |
| Google-Extended    | Excludes content from generative AI features without affecting normal indexing   |

- To stop content from being used for training **and** still appear in AI answers, the two cases must be handled separately.

## 🛡️ What GEO Does Not Do
- GEO does not quote whole pages; only self‑contained sections are used.
- GEO does not guarantee more traffic; a top‑level answer can actually reduce clicks.
- Selling GEO as a traffic‑generation solution is misleading.
