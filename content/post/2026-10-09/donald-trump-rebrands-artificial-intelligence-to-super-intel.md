# Donald Trump Rebrands Artificial Intelligence to Super Intelligence via Executive Order

Donald Trump has launched a campaign to rebrand artificial intelligence as "super intelligence," issuing an executive order last week to forbid the use of the term "AI" wherever possible. This move follows a United Nations speech where Trump argued that the word "artificial" makes intelligence seem "fake," subsequently labeling those who resist the name change as "THE ENEMY."

## ⚙️ Key details
*   An executive order issued last week mandates the replacement of "AI" with "Super Intelligence" across administrative agencies.
*   The US Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) has been renamed the Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence (CAISSI).
*   Justice Department press releases now utilize the updated terminology, including the phrase "Super Intelligence-Assisted Music Streaming Fraud."
*   Trump has replaced his "fake news media" epithet with the term "artificial media."
*   The administration is prioritizing the acceleration of development, including the construction of more data centers, while dismissing safety risks as overblown.

## 🧩 Rebranding effects
| Original Name/Term | New Name or Status |
| :--- | :--- |
| Artificial Intelligence | Super Intelligence |
| Fake news media | Artificial media |
| SpaceXAI | SpaceXSI (promised by Elon Musk) |
| AI Safety Institute / CAISI | Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence (CAISSI) |
| OpenAI | Name remains unchanged (per Sam Altman) |

## 💡 Industry and legal impact
Industry figures have responded to the shift, with Elon Musk promising to rename SpaceXAI to SpaceXSI and Jeff Bezos noting that "artificial is not a very flattering thing." Jensen Huang has also adopted the term, and Sam Altman praised it despite OpenAI keeping its original name. However, the rebranding faces legal hurdles as the administration cannot bar Congress from using "artificial intelligence" in existing laws. Administrative agencies must still cite original terminology in court when referring to legislation like the Take It Down Act. Additionally, Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-VT) previously used the term superintelligence in a different context when co-sponsoring the Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act in early September.
