# New Algorithm Enables Open‑Ended Scientific Discovery via Possibilistic Reasoning

An arXiv pre‑print titled **Open-ended Scientific Discovery with Possibilistic Reasoning** was submitted on 8 Oct 2026 to the Computer Science > Artificial Intelligence category. The work tackles autonomous scientific discovery with large language models (LLMs) by framing it as an open‑ended, hypothesis‑driven process that must remain statistically valid as new evidence appears.

## 🔍 Overview
The authors identify a gap in existing anytime‑valid methods: while they can handle data‑dependent hypotheses, they do not address the deeper challenge of open‑ended discovery, where the best discovered hypothesis may still be the best of a limited set, and even fundamental background knowledge may need revision.

## 🧩 How it works
- **Problem formalization** – The task is defined as **Abductive Autonomous Scientific Discovery (AASD)** and modeled with **possibility theory**.
- **Abductive utility** – A computable metric introduced to quantify discovery progress.
- **Possibility frontier search** – The first algorithm designed for AASD. It preserves anytime validity and, under suitable conditions, attains \(\varepsilon\)-optimal abductive utility asymptotically.

## ⚙️ Key details
- Generates and tests hypotheses adaptively as evidence accumulates.
- Maintains statistical validity throughout the discovery process.
- Supports revision of background knowledge such as physical laws when new findings emerge.
- Demonstrated strong performance on both synthetic benchmarks and real‑world scientific‑discovery tasks.

## 💡 Why it matters
- Provides a principled framework for truly open‑ended scientific inquiry with LLMs.
- Offers a measurable way (abductive utility) to track progress toward better explanations.
- Bridges the gap between hypothesis generation and rigorous statistical testing in autonomous discovery systems.
