# AI Generates Genetic Blueprints for Microscopic Viruses, Prompting MIT Tech Review Roundtable

On Friday, October 16, 2026, MIT Technology Review will host a subscriber‑only roundtable titled “A Conversation With the Creator of AI‑Designed Viruses,” featuring AI reporter James O'Donnell and Stanford bioengineering PhD candidate Samuel King.

## 🔍 Overview
- **Event:** Roundtables – A Conversation With the Creator of AI‑Designed Viruses
- **Date:** Friday, October 16, 2026
- **Time:** 18:30 BST / 1:30 pm EDT / 10:30 am PDT
- **Access:** Available only for MIT Technology Review subscribers
- **Host:** James O'Donnell, senior AI reporter
- **Guest:** Samuel King, Bioengineering PhD Candidate, Stanford University/Arc Institute, named an Innovator Under 35

## 🧩 How it works
- In 2025, Stanford University PhD student Samuel King used a generative AI model to propose genetic blueprints for microscopic viruses.
- The work does **not** constitute an example of AI‑generated life, but it represents a preliminary step toward that possibility.

## 📅 Availability
- The conversation will be streamed live on October 16th at the times listed above.
- Participation is limited to MIT Technology Review subscribers.

## 🎙️ Speakers
| Speaker | Role |
|---------|------|
| James O'Donnell | AI Reporter |
| Samuel King | Bioengineering PhD Candidate, Stanford University/Arc Institute |

## 💡 Why it matters
- The session poses the question “Can AI design new life forms?” and explores how generative AI can assist in designing viral genetic material, hinting at future directions in synthetic biology.
