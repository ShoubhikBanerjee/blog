# USA Today Sues OpenAI for Over $250 Million Over Copyright Infringement

USA Today Co., together with several local newspapers it owns, has filed a lawsuit against OpenAI alleging that the AI company copied “hundreds of thousands” of articles to train its models.

## 🔍 Overview
- USA Today is the latest publisher to sue OpenAI.
- The lawsuit seeks damages of more than **$250 million**.
- The filing claims OpenAI’s use of the content has caused “real and continuing” harm to the outlets.

## ⚖️ Legal Claims
- OpenAI allegedly **did not ask for permission** to use USA Today’s articles.
- The plaintiff asserts OpenAI “took” the content to **build products worth hundreds of billions of dollars**.
- The complaint states that OpenAI’s commercial success **rests on large‑scale copyright infringement**.
- The core allegation is that OpenAI copied **hundreds of thousands** of articles for training its AI models.

## 📄 Parties Involved
- **Plaintiff:** USA Today Co. (including its local newspapers)
- **Local outlets named in the suit:** The Tennessean, Indy Star, The Columbus Dispatch, The Oklahoman, and several others.
- **Other publishers currently suing OpenAI:**
  - The New York Times
  - The Intercept
  - CNET owner **Ziff Davis**
  - CBC/Radio‑Canada
  - Encyclopaedia Britannica
  - Merriam‑Webster
  - The Seattle Times
  - A coalition of **nearly 400 local newspapers**

## 📈 Why It Matters
- The case adds to a growing **string of copyright lawsuits** against OpenAI.
- Plaintiffs argue that the alleged infringement harms the news industry’s ability to monetize content.
- OpenAI has **not responded** to a request for comment from The Verge.

*The outcome could influence how AI developers source and use copyrighted material.*
