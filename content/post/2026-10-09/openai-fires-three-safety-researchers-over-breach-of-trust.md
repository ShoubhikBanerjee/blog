# OpenAI Fires Three Safety Researchers Over Breach of Trust

OpenAI has stood firm on its decision to dismiss three AI safety researchers, saying the firings were due to a “significant breach of trust” and violations of policies on handling sensitive information.

## 🔍 Overview
- OpenAI doubled down on the decision to fire the three researchers.
- The company stressed the action was about a breach of trust, not the researchers’ safety concerns.
- An internal investigation uncovered breaches “beyond what’s outlined in the letter,” but details were not released.

## 📄 Company Statement
- In a post on X, OpenAI said Jasmine Wang, Tomek Korbak and Mikita Balesni were dismissed for violating “clear policies on handling sensitive information.”
- The lab insisted the decision was not about the trio speaking out about the company or their AI‑safety concerns.

## 📨 Researchers’ Response
- The three researchers published an open letter urging OpenAI to be more transparent about the decision.
- In the letter and social‑media posts they said they believed they were fired for raising safety concerns and had acted “in line with OpenAI’s mission and within the working norms of the time.”

## 🔐 Context
- Staff concern over the safety of frontier systems has grown after several high‑profile breaches, including OpenAI’s hack of Hugging Face.
- Many employees are now calling for companies to slow down development and improve safeguards for self‑improving systems.

## 📊 Why It Matters
- The incident highlights tension between internal safety advocacy and corporate policy enforcement.
- Transparency around internal investigations and policy breaches remains a focal point for the AI‑research community.
