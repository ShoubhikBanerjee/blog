# McDonald's Japan Confirms Data Breach via Smartphone App Database Access

McDonald's Japan has disclosed a data breach after attackers accessed a database containing sensitive information for users of the company's official smartphone app. The exposed information includes users' product purchase history, IP addresses, mobile device location data, and customer management IDs. The company confirmed that the exposed data does not include names, email addresses, phone numbers, home addresses, or credit card information. A follow-up investigation found no service disruption or misuse of official apps as a result of the incident. McDonald's Japan is working to invalidate any credentials used for unauthorized access, investigate the relevant systems, and report to the Personal Information Protection Commission. This is not the first data breach to affect McDonald's in recent years, with the fast food giant disclosing a breach in June 2021 after attackers breached its systems and stole information belonging to employees and customers from the US, South Korea, and Taiwan. In 2017, McDonald's also patched a cross-site scripting (XSS) security flaw that affected its official website and exposed customers' plain-text passwords. Last month, Burger King Japan also disclosed a major data breach after attackers stole 11.18 million customer records that were later leaked online. The two incidents affecting the two Japanese fast food chains add to a large wave of cyberattacks impacting high-profile Japanese companies since the start of the year, including Japan Airlines, entertainment system maker Daiichi Kosho, cloud and digital infrastructure firm IDC Frontier, Japanese publishing giant Nikkei, cloud and data center service provider Sakura Internet, taxi operator Nihon Kotsu, telecommunications giant KDDI, and many others. Macnica researcher Yutaka Sejiyama has logged 288 cybersecurity incidents affecting Japanese organizations involving personal information theft or exposure since the start of the year. Sejiyama found that attackers are now probing websites and APIs for access-control, configuration, and authentication weaknesses, and exploiting known (n-day) vulnerabilities to find other victims.
