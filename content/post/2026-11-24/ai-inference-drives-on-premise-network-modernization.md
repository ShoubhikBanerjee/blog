# AI Inference Drives On-Premise Network Modernization

As enterprises rush to deploy AI, network infrastructure is facing unprecedented strain from AI inference workloads, prompting a shift back toward on-premise and near-premises architectures.

## 📈 Network Impact of AI

- AI agent tasks generate an estimated **450% more network traffic** than the same tasks done by human workers
- Training AI requires server-to-server data center architectures, while inference calls for low-latency connectivity close to users
- Inconsistent latency can lead to AI model failures, increasing queue wait times and saturating GPU loads

## 🏢 Enterprise Readiness Challenges

- As few as **half of enterprises believe their current networks** cannot handle AI’s bandwidth and latency demands
- The traffic increase from agentic AI, combined with inference moving on-premises, is exposing bandwidth bottlenecks in legacy underlay infrastructure

## ⚙️ Infrastructure Shifts

- AI inference is **pushing infrastructure back on premises**, reversing years of cloud-first consolidation
- **Inference typically requires real-time processing** and is sensitive to latency, making hosting such capabilities on distant hyperscale clouds neither economical nor practical
- Large enterprises may need on-premises or near-premises inference, forcing new approaches to data center architecture, connectivity, and physical infrastructure

## 🛠️ Modernization Strategy

- Treating network modernization as an **ongoing strategy rather than a one-off project** helps organizations adapt to disruption and uncertainty
- A holistic approach with strong architectural governance, reusable design templates, and retained internal expertise enables adaptation without rebuilding infrastructure from scratch
- **Network-as-a-service (NaaS) and AI-driven observability** can improve efficiency, reduce complexity, and offer benefits like faster provisioning and lower costs

## 🌍 Global Compliance Pressures

- **Data sovereignty and sovereign AI requirements** are creating infrastructure challenges for global enterprises
- Regulators are passing laws requiring data and AI processing to remain within national borders, necessitating significant new investment in geographically distributed infrastructure
