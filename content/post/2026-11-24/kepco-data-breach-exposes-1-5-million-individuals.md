# KEPCO Data Breach Exposes 1.5 Million Individuals

A threat actor breached the internal network of Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO), exposing details of more than 1.5 million individuals.

## 🔍 Overview
- KEPCO, South Korea’s main electric power company, detected an attack on November 20.
- The breach exposed customer, employee, and contractor data.
- The attack was detected when unauthorized access was confirmed to KEPCO ON and SRM systems.

## ⚙️ Key details
- The following data was exposed:
  - For 650,487 KEPCO ON customers: names, ID information, phone numbers, addresses, bank names, bank account numbers, account holder names, card issuers, card numbers, cardholder names, and electricity charge and billing information.
  - For 426,827 company representatives: names, ID information, email addresses, and company addresses.
  - For 442,934 KEPCO employees: names, affiliation, employee ID number, mobile phone number, email address, company phone number.
- The data breach resulted from “advanced technologies as AI,” but specific details about the initial access method or attack chain were not shared.
- KEPCO is notifying exposed individuals directly, has filed a report with the data protection authorities, and is coordinating with government and financial authorities to block abuse.

## 💡 Why it matters
- Potentially exposed individuals are warned that the exposed information could be used for targeted phishing and impersonation.
- They are advised to report any attempts to the police or Korea’s Internet and Security Agency.
- It is recommended to avoid installing unknown apps, entering information into suspicious forms, and sharing sensitive data or verification codes over calls or texts.

## 🔗 Attribution
- A threat actor using the “Noiem” alias has claimed the attack and advertised the breach on a hacker forum on November 19.
- South Korea’s Personal Information Protection Commission separately confirmed that KEPCO received an email from a hacker claiming data theft on November 19.
- While these claims match the available evidence, attribution to Noiem remains unconfirmed.
