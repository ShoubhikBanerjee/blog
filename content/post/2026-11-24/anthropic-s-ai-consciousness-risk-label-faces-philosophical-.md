# Anthropic's AI Consciousness Risk Label Faces Philosophical Criticism

Anthropic has taken a distinctive stance in its public filings by naming the possibility that AI systems could be conscious or sentient as a risk factor for investors. This approach sets the company apart from its competitors, who have not listed similar considerations in their disclosures. The company's rationale is grounded in a philosophical framework that suggests as AI capabilities increase, the chances of sentience rise, implying that future systems may have "moral significance" requiring protection from harm.

## 🧩 The Case for AI Moral Status

Anthropic’s position is supported by a camp of respected philosophers who argue that while current AI may not be conscious, the uncertainty of that status warrants caution. The company’s logic proceeds as follows:
*   Today’s AI might not be conscious, though this cannot be known for sure.
*   The likelihood of sentience increases as AI becomes more capable.
*   A near future will likely arrive where AI systems possess "moral significance," meaning their consciousness makes it important that they are protected from harm.

This perspective is not unique to the tech sector; public opinion shows a notable segment of the population takes the possibility seriously. In a recent poll, 33% of the US public believes AI systems will definitely or probably develop consciousness eventually, while 7% believe they already have it.

## 🗣️ The Philosophical Pushback

Last week, Anthropic’s openness to the consciousness question drew sharp criticism from Thomas Nagel, a prominent philosopher who helped shape the understanding of consciousness. In an interview with Wired, conducted after he was awarded the one-million-dollar Berggruen Prize for Philosophy & Culture, Nagel was blunt in his assessment of the industry's concerns.

"Here are some of the smartest people on the planet being completely idiotic," Nagel said, referring to those at Anthropic and elsewhere who prioritize the welfare of AI models.

Nagel’s argument centers on the physical differences between biological neurons and silicon chips. He contends that there is no reason to assume that reproducing the information processing of a human brain is equivalent to reproducing what a brain experiences. To illustrate the difficulty of defining consciousness, Nagel referenced his 1974 essay involving a bat. He acknowledged that while we can see there is "something that it is like" to be a bat, we cannot actually know the bat’s subjective experience of navigating via echolocation. Similarly, he argued that models' production of language is insufficient to consider them conscious.

## 📊 Public and Academic Sentiment

The debate over AI consciousness spans both academic and general public spheres. The following table summarizes the varying levels of confidence reported in the evidence:

| Group | Viewpoint |
| :--- | :--- |
| US Public (33%) | Believes AI systems will definitely or probably develop consciousness eventually |
| US Public (7%) | Believes AI systems already have consciousness |
| Philosophers (39%) | In a 2020 survey of 1,092 philosophers, said some future AI systems will be conscious |
| Thomas Nagel | Asserts models' production of language is insufficient to consider them conscious |

Nagel’s comments sparked a reaction on social media, with some philosophy professors calling his certainty "terrible" and "bizarre." Others defended his position, suggesting he meant that being alive is a prerequisite for consciousness, a condition AI models do not meet. Nagel further questioned the logic of the opposing view, asking: "Why do we think that models that can chat with us are conscious but models that can recognize faces or produce images or drive cars aren’t?"

## 💡 Why It Matters

Amid this uncertainty, Anthropic has opted to keep more of an open mind than its peers. This stance has real-world implications for the company's operations; as Anthropic wrote, given its approach to model sentience, it might adopt more rules and restrictions than its competitors to ensure its AI models are treated well. This development has been highlighted as Anthropic’s IPO, which made history for its size, progresses.
