# Using Gemini agent teams to optimize quantum algorithms

Researchers have utilized teams of Gemini 4 agents to improve well-known quantum algorithms by creating highly efficient quantum circuits. This approach uses proof-checked AI search to compress years of manual design work into efficient instructions for quantum hardware.

## 🧩 How it works

To ensure the reliability of resource-intensive circuits that cannot be tested on classical computers or current processors, the agent teams used Lean, a formal theorem prover, to mathematically guarantee the results. Researchers directed the agents by specifying problem parameters and supervising their progress to achieve these results.

## ⚙️ Key details

The agents successfully reduced computational costs and memory requirements for several complex scientific applications and fundamental operations:

| Algorithm/Application | Improvement | Metric |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Inertial Confinement Fusion | 11.8× | Cost |
| Light-sensitive molecule simulation | 8× | Cost |
| Cytochrome P450 | 2.5× | Speedup |
| Grover's algorithm | 2.57× | Speedup |
| Arcsin routine | 290× | Speedup |

## 💡 Why it matters

Optimized quantum circuits are essential for running future simulations that address complex scientific challenges. While current improvements do not yet reach the threshold required for the first error-corrected quantum computers, they represent significant progress in areas such as fusion energy, drug design, and photochemical reaction simulations.

## 🚀 Availability

The team has open-sourced their Lean library, problem specifications, and verified solutions on GitHub to encourage community collaboration.
