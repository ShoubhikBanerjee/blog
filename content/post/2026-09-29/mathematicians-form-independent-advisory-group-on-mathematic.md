# Mathematicians form independent Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence

On September 21st, a group of elite mathematicians announced the formation of the Advisory Group on Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence (AGMAI) via a guest post on the blog of UCLA professor and Fields Medalist Terence Tao.

## 🔍 Overview
AGMAI is an independent body composed of nine mathematicians at the top of their field. The group's purpose is to advise OpenAI and other frontier AI labs on the "review and communication of emerging results."

## ⚙️ Key details
* **Formation:** The group formed after OpenAI approached some eventual members about an advisory board; the members subsequently decided to strike out independently and invite others to join.
* **Independence:** Martin Hairer, a professor at Imperial College London and EPFL, stated the group receives no financial, technical, or other support from OpenAI and that members are not paid by the company.
* **Governance:** Members have not signed agreements restricting their actions or speech, beyond standard confidentiality requirements necessary for advanced research access.
* **Scope:** OpenAI noted that AGMAI will not be responsible for advising the company on how to pace internal progress on mathematics.
* **Collaboration:** The group is open to working with other frontier AI labs and has already begun conversations with some.

## 💡 Why it matters
The formation of the group follows a period of community suspicion after OpenAI's solution to a Millennium Prize problem led to disputes over scooping, credit, and the treatment of mathematicians. Since solving the Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize problem, OpenAI claims an unreleased internal model has resolved more than 100 long-standing open problems.
