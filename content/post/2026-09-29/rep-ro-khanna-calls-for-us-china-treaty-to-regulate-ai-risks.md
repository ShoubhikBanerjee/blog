# Rep. Ro Khanna Calls for US-China Treaty to Regulate AI Risks

Representative Ro Khanna (D-CA) is calling for a treaty between the United States and China to prevent artificial intelligence from "wreaking havoc on the world," citing a lack of urgency from leaders in both nations to manage the technology's risks.

## ⚙️ Key details

Khanna has sent letters to the Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI) and several Chinese AI companies. His requests include:

*   **For the ODNI:** An assessment of how the US would respond to an AI lab losing control of its bots and an evaluation of the Chinese government's approach to catastrophic AI risks.
*   **For Chinese AI Companies:** Documentation regarding efforts to achieve "superintelligence" and recursive self-improvement (RSI), which is when AI systems can improve on themselves.
*   **Safeguards:** Information on "kill switches" and whether companies would commit to inspections by a non-governmental agency if a treaty were established.

## 💡 Why it matters

Khanna seeks a treaty that would ban RSI and monitor frontier AI labs, arguing that the drive for profit should not determine safety rules. He believes the US can drive these standards because many Chinese models are distilled from US models.

This move follows a meeting between President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping. While Khanna noted it was constructive that they agreed to a dialogue, he described it as "not nearly adequate" and a "branding exercise."

## 🔍 Divergent Perspectives

*   **Support for Treaty:** Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-VT) has similarly called for a US-China AI treaty. Leading US AI companies have also called for coordination with China to slow development.
*   **Skepticism:** House China Committee Chair John Moolenaar (R-MI) has expressed skepticism that the Chinese government would adhere to such an agreement.
*   **Executive Position:** President Trump has been resistant to most agreements, stating on Truth Social that regarding "Super Intelligence (SI)," he wants to "leave it exactly where it is," noting that this is also China's position.
