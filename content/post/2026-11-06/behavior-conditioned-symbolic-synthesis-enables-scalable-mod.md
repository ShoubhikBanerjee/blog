# Behavior-Conditioned Symbolic Synthesis Enables Scalable Modeling of Human and Machine Reasoning

Symbolic models offer a powerful account of cognition, but their scope has historically been limited by the need for hand‑engineering and computational tractability. A new study shows how advances in artificial intelligence can scale symbolic modeling and help overcome these engineering limitations.

## 🔍 Overview
The authors introduce **Behavior‑Conditioned Symbolic Synthesis (BCSS)**, an approach for automated search of interpretable, deterministic symbolic programs that **verifiably reproduce behavior**. BCSS is applied to model both human and machine behavior across hundreds of challenging reasoning problems.

## 🧩 How it works
- BCSS automatically searches for symbolic programs that match observed behavior.
- The resulting programs are deterministic and interpretable, allowing direct verification against the original behavior.
- The method is employed on a large suite of reasoning tasks, covering both people and black‑box AI systems.

## ⚙️ Key details
- **Historical limitation**: symbolic models required extensive hand‑engineering and faced computational tractability issues.
- **AI‑enabled scaling**: modern AI techniques make it feasible to synthesize symbolic models at scale.
- **Empirical findings**:
  - Both people and machines tend to **oversimplify** problem structure.
  - They often adopt **partial rules** that explain only part of their observations.
  - They **misinfer dependencies** between problem features.
- **Resource modulation**: the identified signatures are affected by the resources available to the reasoner; higher reasoning effort predicts greater simplification and explanation by machines.
- **Satisficing link**: the shared patterns resemble satisficing mechanisms in both humans and machines.
- **General utility**: BCSS provides a framework for interpreting and comparing rule‑based reasoning across humans and black‑box AI systems.
