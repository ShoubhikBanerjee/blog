# Tau Robotics Deploys Remotely Piloted Humanoid Cleaning Robots in San Francisco

I watched a humanoid robot clean my apartment. It felt like a taste of the future.

## 🤖 Overview
- Tau Robotics is sending humanoid robots to clean San Francisco homes.
- The robots are piloted remotely by humans who watch what’s happening via cameras mounted in the robots’ heads and wrists.
- While cleaning, the robots collect data that Tau will use to train more independent descendants.

## 🛠️ How it works
- Human pilots perform motions on a control interface; sensors transmit those movements to the robot, which copies them (kinematic guidance).
- Tasks that need kinematic guidance include making the bed and folding laundry; these produce especially valuable training data.
- The robots communicate with Tau’s headquarters through a 3D‑printed “head” with antennae.
- A short signal transmission delay can affect coordination, causing the robot to walk “strangely.”
- Overheating of the remote‑operator’s equipment can cause the robot to stall.

## ⚙️ Robot specifications
- Height: four‑and‑a‑half‑foot‑tall.
- 3D‑printed head with antennae for communication.
- Two 3D‑printed claw “hands” (human‑like hands were found not durable enough).
- Grippy socks to avoid scratching hardwood floors.
- Each robot wears a Tau Robotics shirt.

## 📋 Capabilities
- Can vacuum and mop without any human help.
- With kinematic guidance, can make the bed and fold laundry.
- In a later visit, cleaned a kitchen, tackled a toilet, and re‑attached a cleaning wand three times by poking it into place.
- Struggles with stairs, often climbing with a “drunk” gait and occasional backward stumbles.

## 💰 Pricing
- $30 for an hour‑long cleaning with a single robot (initial offering).
- $60 per weekly cleaning with two robots (current rate).
- $100 for a one‑time visit (current rate).
- Even at $30 per hour, the robots are thorough but slow, making self‑cleaning potentially more efficient.

## 🚧 Limitations & Incidents
- Stair navigation is unreliable; the robot can stagger backward.
- Overheating can cause the robot to stall; in one case the remote‑operator’s equipment shut down.
- Without remote assistance, the robot cannot clean.
- The robot is slow, and watching it for the entire visit may not be an efficient use of time.

## 🚀 Future outlook
- Tau’s long‑term goal is to make fully autonomous cleaning robots a reality.
- Data collected during current remotely‑piloted cleanings will be used to develop more independent descendants.
