# OpenAI bans NDAs with officials and pledges full electricity costs for data centers

OpenAI announced a set of new community commitments for its upcoming compute campuses after voter backlash against data‑center expansion.

## 🔍 Overview
- Voters expressed strong opposition to data‑center projects in recent midterm elections.
- OpenAI responded with a pledge not to use non‑disclosure agreements (NDAs) with public officials.
- The company also added several other transparency‑and‑cost commitments for its future compute campuses.

## 📜 New Commitments
| Commitment | Details |
|------------|---------|
| No NDAs with public officials | OpenAI will not use NDAs with public officials, allowing only narrow protections for legitimate security information, trade secrets, or proprietary/competitively sensitive information. |
| Full electricity cost coverage | OpenAI will pay “the full cost” of electricity it uses and the infrastructure needed to serve its compute campuses. |
| Job transparency | The company will publish the number of jobs it expects to create and will report on hiring progress. |
| Local compact | Every future compute campus will have a “local compact” that includes these commitments and reflects local priorities. |

## 📈 Context
- Many data‑center projects have come under scrutiny for relying on NDAs to hide discussions between developers and local officials.
- In October, Amazon announced it would also stop using NDAs with government agencies on data‑center projects, showing a broader industry shift.

## 💡 Why it matters
- Removes secrecy that has historically shielded data‑center negotiations from public view.
- Aligns OpenAI’s infrastructure expansion with community concerns expressed in recent elections.
- Sets a precedent for other AI and tech firms to adopt similar transparency practices.
