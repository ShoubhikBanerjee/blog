# DelegateEval Framework to Assess Personal External Service Agent Platforms

A new evaluation framework called **DelegateEval** has been introduced to assess platforms that host personal external service agents.

## 🔍 Overview
- A *personal external service agent* is defined as an agent authorized and continuously managed by a specific individual to accept client requests, deliver work, and provide ongoing support using that person's knowledge, experience, professional methods, and service boundaries.
- Such agents could allow individuals to serve more clients with less direct involvement and generate economic value at scale.

## 🧩 How it works
DelegateEval is described as a **platform‑level evaluation framework** for agent systems that may combine large language models, reusable skills, protocols such as MCP, and executable tool environments. The framework is built around **eight evaluation dimensions**.

## ⚙️ Key details
- **Ease of creation** – how readily new agents can be built on the platform.
- **Service discovery and matching** – how clients find suitable agents and are matched to them.
- **Delivery quality** – the correctness and completeness of work produced.
- **Expertise inheritance** – how an individual’s knowledge and methods are transferred to the agent.
- **Service continuity** – the ability of the agent to maintain ongoing support over time.
- **Learning and improvement** – whether the agent can develop capabilities through experience.
- **Reliability and governance** – safeguards and oversight for trustworthy operation.
- **Efficiency and value** – economic feasibility and resource efficiency of the service.

The authors present the framework and an experimental design, but note that **complete datasets, executable environments, a unified scoring protocol, and cross‑model results remain to be developed and empirically validated**.

## 🚀 Availability
The framework and experimental design are publicly presented, yet the supporting data and tooling needed for full evaluation have not been released.

## 💡 Why it matters
- Enables people with diverse backgrounds to turn expertise into ongoing services that deliver verifiable work.
- Supports the development of capabilities through experience while aiming to stay economically feasible.
- Provides a structured way to measure whether a platform can help individuals scale their services with reduced direct effort.
