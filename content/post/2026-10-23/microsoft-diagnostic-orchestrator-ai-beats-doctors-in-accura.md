# Microsoft Diagnostic Orchestrator AI beats doctors in accuracy tests

Microsoft has published a study in *Science* showing its Diagnostic Orchestrator AI system can diagnose conditions up to four times more accurately than human doctors.

## 🔍 Overview
- The tool is marketed as an AI‑powered diagnostic assistant that outperforms generalist physicians.
- Results appear in a peer‑reviewed paper published Thursday in *Science*.
- Microsoft compiled the test cases into its own benchmark, **SDBench**, which it plans to open‑source.
- The research is framed as part of Microsoft’s broader ambition to create a “medical superintelligence” that can exceed the best human‑led healthcare systems.

## 🧩 How it works
- Named **Diagnostic Orchestrator**, the system uses multiple AI agents that reason through symptoms, medical histories, and test results.
- Agents decide which diagnostic tests to order and synthesize findings, mimicking the workflow of a panel of medical experts.
- The system is powered by OpenAI’s GPT‑5; the first version was built when GPT‑4.1 was the most advanced model available.
- Subsequent testing suggests that adding more agents preserves benefits even as underlying models improve.

## 📊 Performance
- On cases drawn from hundreds of reports in *The New England Journal of Medicine*, the system achieved **~83 %** diagnostic accuracy.
  - Generalist physicians using references achieved **~36 %**.
  - Generalist physicians without references achieved **~22 %**.
- On emergency‑department cases from the Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center database, the system scored **80 %** accuracy versus an average of **47 %** for human physicians.
- The AI also tended to reach diagnoses at a lower estimated cost than human counterparts.
- In head‑to‑head tests, the system outperformed other frontier AI models operating on their own, though the paper does not include newer models such as GPT‑6 Astra, Meta’s Muse Wonder Full, Google’s Gemini 4 Argon, or Anthropic’s Opus 5.5.

## 🚀 Availability & Roadmap
- Microsoft acknowledges a long road before wide deployment.
- The company plans to begin real‑world testing at Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center “in the next couple of months,” according to AI health lead Dr. Dominic King.
- Neither King nor Microsoft co‑founder Mustafa Suleyman gave a firm commercialization timeline.
- Microsoft stresses a responsible rollout, noting the high regulatory and compliance requirements in medicine.
- Suleyman predicts “extremely impressive deployments of intelligent systems in production” next year, but reiterates that human clinicians will retain a central role.

## 💡 Why it matters
- Diagnosis is only one component of Microsoft’s health vision; the company also cites goals around coordinating clinical care, making treatment decisions, and handling the human side of medicine.
- Demonstrating higher accuracy and lower cost on historical case data provides a proof‑of‑concept for AI‑augmented clinical decision support.
- The emphasis on multi‑agent reasoning suggests a design philosophy that may scale to other medical tasks while keeping human oversight.
