# Synthetic-to-Real Adaptation Lowers Error on Nepali-English Code-Switched ASR Benchmark

Researchers introduced a new benchmark for Nepali‑English code‑switched speech and a two‑stage synthetic‑to‑real adaptation pipeline that improves automatic speech recognition performance.

## 🔍 Overview
- Nepali‑English conversational speech poses severe ASR challenges due to script confusion (mixing Devanagari and Latin) and high error rates at language‑switch points.
- Collecting and annotating human code‑switched audio is expensive.
- The authors curated a benchmark of 13,044 spontaneous podcast utterances (15.45 h total), split into 10,214 test and 2,830 development utterances, and audited the data to remove machine‑translation leakages and acoustic loops.

## 🧩 How it works
- **Stage 1 – Supervised Fine‑Tuning (SFT):**
  - Fine‑tunes a multilingual foundation model on 0.95 h of synthetic speech generated by a multi‑speaker, dual‑engine TTS pipeline.
- **Stage 2 – Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):**
  - Applies reinforcement‑style optimization with transcription and script rewards to further adapt the model to real speech.

## ⚙️ Key results
- Synthetic SFT reduces Mixed Error Rate (MER) from **23.40 %** to **21.52 %** (‑1.88 pp) and Switch‑Point Error Rate (SPER) from **35.89 %** to **28.77 %** (‑7.12 pp).
- GRPO yields statistically significant tweaks on 173 test utterances (1.69 % of the test set), improving English script confusion (SCR_en) from **6.45 %** to **6.36 %** (p ≤ 0.001) and SPER to **28.70 %** (p = 0.022, clustered).
- Ablation shows an auxiliary script‑reward term matches MER‑only optimization because synthetic rollout groups have near‑zero script variance.
- On monolingual benchmarks, SFT improves Nepali MER by **2.71 pp**, stabilizing at **18.42 %** after GRPO (a 2.38 pp gain over baseline) while keeping English lexical WER nearly unchanged (3.38 % vs. 3.33 %).

## 🚀 Availability
- Verified transcripts, timestamps, episode metadata, and reconstruction scripts are publicly released.
- The paper appears on **alphaXiv**; code, data, and model assets are accessible through platforms such as **DagsHub**, **Hugging Face**, **Gotit.pub**, **ScienceCast**, and **CatalyzeX Code Finder for Papers**.

## 💡 Why it matters
- Demonstrates that a small amount of high‑quality synthetic speech can provide the primary cross‑domain transfer to spontaneous human code‑switched audio.
- Reduces dependence on costly human‑annotated code‑switched corpora while delivering measurable error‑rate improvements.
- Provides a publicly audited benchmark that can spur further research on multilingual and code‑switched ASR.
