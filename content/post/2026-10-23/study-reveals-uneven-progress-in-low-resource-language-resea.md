# Study Reveals Uneven Progress in Low‑Resource Language Research

The paper *Measuring Research Progress and Linguistic Inclusion in Low‑Resource Languages* was submitted to arXiv on 21 Oct 2026. It analyses the growth of low‑resource language (LRL) research and introduces a new evaluation tool, the Linguistic Inclusion Framework (LIF).

## 🔍 Overview
- Focus: Quantify research attention to LRLs and assess linguistic inclusion across language and speaker populations.
- Corpus: 1,158 peer‑reviewed open‑access papers published between 2020 and 2025.
- Venue: Computer Science > Computation and Language (ACL Anthology).

## 📊 Research Growth
- Annual publications rose from **52** to **415** (a 5.4× increase in ACL Anthology share).
- Growth is concentrated on already represented languages rather than expanding coverage of under‑represented languages.

## 📈 Findings
- Languages spoken in Asia account for **64.1 %** of speakers among the LRLs studied but receive only **46.7 %** of the language‑representation share (≈0.7× their proportional share).
- European languages receive **5.3×** their proportional share of representation.
- Sub‑Saharan Africa and Southeast Asia show the lowest language‑coverage ratios at the country level.

## 🧭 Linguistic Inclusion Framework (LIF)
The LIF measures inclusion in four dimensions:
1. **Research growth** – volume of publications.
2. **Representation expansion** – addition of new languages.
3. **Representation continuity** – sustained attention to existing languages.
4. **Distributional equity** – alignment of representation with speaker populations.
Applying LIF at the continental level shows no region scores well on all four dimensions, indicating that research growth alone is not a reliable metric of equitable progress.

## 🌍 Regional Disparities
| Region | Speaker Share | Representation Share | Ratio to Proportional Share |
|--------|---------------|----------------------|-----------------------------|
| Asia   | 64.1 %        | 46.7 %               | 0.7×                        |
| Europe | –             | –                    | 5.3× (relative to proportional share) |

## 📚 Implications
- The analysis highlights a mismatch between speaker populations and research focus.
- It suggests the need for targeted efforts to improve coverage of under‑represented regions, especially in Sub‑Saharan Africa and Southeast Asia.
