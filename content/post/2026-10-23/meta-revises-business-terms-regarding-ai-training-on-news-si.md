# Meta Revises Business Terms Regarding AI Training on News Sites

Meta has updated its business terms to remove specific language suggesting the company could freely train its AI models on news sites, following backlash from the publishing industry.

## ⚙️ Key details

Initially, terms for business accounts going into effect October 30th stated that Meta “may use any content” from websites associated with a business account to “develop, research” and “train” Meta’s products. Following industry backlash, Meta removed the word “train.”

## 💡 Why it matters

Legal experts argue the revisions may not sufficiently prevent Meta from scraping news sites for AI training:

* **Ambiguous Language:** Matt Topic, a media and intellectual property attorney at Loevy & Loevy, states the revised terms still allow Meta to use scraped content to develop any “business product.” He notes that “develop” is broad enough to include “train” and that “business product” is not a defined term.
* **Scope of Scraping:** James Grimmelmann, a professor of digital and information law at Cornell Law School, says the changes do nothing to restrict language regarding content “associated with your products or services,” which could still allow Meta to scrape entire sites.
* **Blocking Instructions:** Experts previously suggested the original terms could allow Meta to argue its commercial terms supersede blocking instructions like the robots.txt file.

## 🔍 Meta's Position

Meta spokesperson Daniel Roberts has stated that:

* The update “is not about scraping news sites.”
* The updates “don’t give us new rights that supersede” the preferences of content owners.
* Content owners can still use robots.txt to signal their preferences.
