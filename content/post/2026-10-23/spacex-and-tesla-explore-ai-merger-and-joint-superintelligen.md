# SpaceX and Tesla Explore AI Merger and Joint Superintelligence Projects

SpaceX and Tesla have officially created special committees to explore a merger while simultaneously collaborating on superintelligence software development. The move comes as SpaceX’s AI‑related revenue surged in the latest quarter.

## 🔍 Overview
- SpaceX and Tesla have formed special committees to discuss a merger!  
- Elon Musk said on the recent SpaceX earnings call that “there’s a lot of merit to combining” SpaceX and Tesla.  
- A quoted statement notes, “There’s a lot to be gained from cooperating on developing superintelligence software.”

## 📊 Financial Snapshot
- In its Q3 earnings on Tuesday, SpaceX reported **$7.56 billion** in revenue from the artificial intelligence — sorry, super intelligence — division.
- The subcategory **“SI solutions & infrastructure revenues”** (which includes Grok, Cursor, and the neocloud business) jumped to **$7 billion**, more than three times the previous quarter.
- **$608 million** of that increase came from Cursor being “stapled on.”
- Analyst Gil Luria (DA Davidson) said most of the remaining $7 billion is likely from renting compute to Anthropic and Google, with Grok contributing less.
- For comparison, Anthropic’s leaked S‑1 shows **$16.3 billion** in revenue in the first half of the year from its frontier models.
- Tesla’s Q3 results were weak, described as “earnings sucked — a second consecutive quarter of declining profits.”

## 🤝 Joint AI Initiatives
| Project | Description | Current Status (per filings) |
|---|---|---|
| **Terafab** | Chip‑making venture in Texas intended to supply Musk’s companies with chips; Intel is a partner. | “In the very early stages… we and Tesla have not finalized a variety of details…including financial terms, intellectual property rights, and the ultimate term of our collaboration.” |
| **Macrohard** | AI project that lets people simulate software already made by Microsoft. | Not mentioned in the last 10‑Q; details remain undeclared. |

- SpaceX’s S‑1 from May notes both Terafab and Macrohard are early‑stage collaborations with no finalized terms.
- Intel’s CEO reiterated commitment to the partnership but clarified Intel is not obligated to remain part of Terafab, and the S‑1 warns a definitive agreement may never be reached.
- Intel’s Thomas Hannaford did not respond to inquiries about any interim signing.
- On a recent SpaceX investor call, Musk claimed Terafab will reach **volume production in the second half of 2028** and will have its **first test chip in May or June 2027**.
- When asked whether orders for cutting‑edge equipment had been placed, Musk did not answer.

## 🏭 Existing Business Ties
- SpaceX bought **$131 million** of low‑selling Cybertrucks from Tesla.
- SpaceX has previously held Tesla bonds.

## 📈 Why It Matters
- The revenue surge indicates SpaceX’s AI division is scaling quickly, positioning the company as a significant player alongside established AI firms.
- Formal merger discussions could align the two companies’ massive capital resources, potentially accelerating joint AI hardware and software development.
- Early‑stage projects like Terafab and Macrohard show a strategic intent to control both the compute stack (chips) and application layer (software simulation), though concrete agreements remain unsettled.

*All statements are drawn directly from publicly disclosed earnings calls, filings, and analyst commentary.*
