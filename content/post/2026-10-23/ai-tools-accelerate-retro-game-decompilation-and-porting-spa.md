# AI tools accelerate retro game decompilation and porting, sparking community turmoil

Retro video games are popping up everywhere lately – often in places they weren’t designed for. Examples include Halo running in a web browser, *Wind Waker* on the iPhone, and *Super Smash Bros.* in mixed reality.

## 🤖 AI’s Role in Decomp & Porting
- New AI coding tools let hobbyists break games down to their building blocks and rebuild them for hardware they were never meant for.
- Decomps and recomps (short for decomposition and recomposition) are forms of reverse engineering that traditionally took years; AI has cut those timelines dramatically.
- AI enables “much faster” work when the developer knows what they want, leading to a surge of new ports on Reddit, X, and GitHub.

## 📈 Recent Surge & Examples
| Game | New Platform |
|------|--------------|
| Halo | Web browser |
| The Legend of Zelda: *Wind Waker* | iPhone |
| Super Smash Bros. | Mixed reality |
| Command & Conquer: Generals Zero Hour (open‑source) | iPhone & iPad |
| The Legend of Zelda: *Ocarina of Time* (using community decomp) | Mobile |

- Chris Sotraidis, an AI consultant who works on game ports as a hobby, saw the *Generals Zero Hour* port and then started a handful of similar ports, including a mobile *Ocarina of Time*.
- Within a few weeks he was juggling a dozen projects, noting that this level of productivity “wouldn’t happen without the use of AI.”

## ⚠️ Preservation Concerns
- Traditional decomp projects let users pull a game apart to understand how it works; AI‑generated ports can skip that learning step.
- The ability to quickly “finish” a decomp with AI means the underlying programming decisions may be lost, making faithful restoration harder.
- More than 90 % of classic games are considered “critically endangered,” so the speed‑first approach risks long‑term drawbacks.

## 🛠️ Community Impact & Shutdown
- A community‑focused website that tracked decomp projects shut down, citing AI’s impact.
- The site’s owner (pseudonym *Encounter*) wrote:
  > “It is now clear that, with the right guidance, human input, and code reviews, AI can expedite the decompilation process in a significant way.”
- The same statement added that the community aspect of decompilation has been broken, with “bad actors” abusing AI to fork projects without meaningful contribution, tearing apart the community fabric.

## 🌐 Why It Matters
- AI has turned niche terms like *decomp* and *recomp* into headline material and opened experimentation to non‑programmers.
- At the same time, the rapid, low‑detail nature of many AI‑driven ports clashes with preservationists’ goals of both keeping games playable and understanding their original construction.
- Epic Games’ shift to “AI‑accelerated development” for an upcoming Disney collaboration illustrates how major studios are also embracing AI, reinforcing its growing influence across the industry.
