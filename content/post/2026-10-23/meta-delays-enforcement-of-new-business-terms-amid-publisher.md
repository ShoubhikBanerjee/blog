# Meta delays enforcement of new business terms amid publisher AI-scraping concerns

Meta announced a delay to the rollout of its revised business terms after worldwide publisher pushback over possible AI‑training scrapes of news sites.

## 🔍 Overview
- The original effective date was October 30.
- Enforcement has been pushed to November 20.
- Delay is intended to give Meta time to explain what is changing and what is not.
- Publishers warned the terms could let Meta scrape news sites for AI training.

## 📜 Business terms details
- The draft terms appeared to give Meta permission to freely scrape news sites for AI training.
- Meta later removed explicit language about “training” its products, but legal experts said the change may still allow scraping.

## 🗣️ Publisher and regulator response
- The News/Media Alliance sent a letter to Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg demanding revisions, citing “severe harm” to publishers.
- Digital advertisers and publishers in the European Union and the United Kingdom urged regulators to block the new terms.
- Publisher backlash worldwide prompted Meta to delay the terms entirely.

## 🤝 Meta's statement
- spokesperson Daniel Roberts told The Verge the November 20 start “allows more time for us to explain to our partners and stakeholders what’s changing and what’s not.”
- Roberts added the delay will let Meta detail “how Meta uses web crawlers and the fact that content owners will still be able to use robots.txt to signal preferences.”

## 💡 Why it matters
- Scraping news content for AI models could affect revenue and control for publishers.
- Robots.txt remains a tool for publishers to signal crawling preferences.
- Regulatory attention in the EU and UK highlights growing scrutiny of large platforms’ data practices.
