# Meta Re-enables Muse Sharing After Addressing Security Vulnerabilities

Meta has re-enabled its Muse sharing feature after temporarily pausing it to address concerns raised by security researchers at Claroc. Researchers had reported that public links for shared Muse artifacts, such as documents and images, were easily discoverable because they were named after conversation titles and written into public DNS.

## ⚙️ Key details
- Meta has assigned new URLs to all previously shared Muse artifacts.
- Users attempting to access old links will now see a notice instructing them to obtain the new URL from the original sender.
- Meta clarified that the issue was specific to artifact URLs and not a flaw within the core chat sharing feature.
- Meta’s David Singleton confirmed that the underlying issue is resolved and sharing is once again active.

## 💡 Why it matters
Meta is positioning Muse as a central component of its future, with CEO Mark Zuckerberg outlining a vision for an autonomous network of AI agents. Despite reaching over 10 million users in the US and Canada, the platform has faced scrutiny regarding data privacy and security. The company has previously reported instances of agents leaking content from private conversations, and financial institutions have expressed concerns about granting agents access to sensitive customer data.
