# ONE Data Launches Development Finance Observatory AI Platform

ONE Data, an initiative of the ONE Campaign, launched the Development Finance Observatory on October 14, 2026 during the World Bank‑IMF Annual Meetings. The AI platform is supported by The Rockefeller Foundation and Google.org and aims to give free, rapid access to global aid, lending and debt data.

## 🔍 Overview
- The Development Finance Observatory is an AI‑powered data analyst that brings together fragmented development finance data in one place.
- It is described as “a world‑class data analyst in your pocket.”
- The platform is built on Google’s Data Commons and supported by the Rockefeller Foundation’s independent charitable affiliate RF Catalytic Capital (RFCC).

## 🧩 How it works
- The platform unifies isolated datasets—from the International Monetary Fund, World Bank, Organisation for Economic Co‑operation and Development, and other sources—into a single AI‑ready foundation.
- Users can query the data with natural‑language questions instead of navigating raw spreadsheets, turning analyses that previously took months into minutes.
- Every number is traceable to a specific source, preventing AI‑hallucinations.
- Calculations and visualizations that used to take months now take minutes.

## ⚙️ Key details
- **Launch date:** October 14, 2026, Washington, D.C.
- **Funding:** $4 million in combined funding from ONE Data, Google.org and The Rockefeller Foundation (announced in January).
- **Technology partner:** Built on Data Commons by Google with support from RFCC.
- **Initiative:** Part of ONE Data, which “transforms fragmented, inaccessible development data into trusted, actionable insights for policymakers, campaigners, journalists, and citizens.”

## 🚀 Availability
- The platform is publicly available, offering free, rapid access to aid, lending and debt data for governments, journalists, think tanks and the public.
- Users can ask natural‑language questions and receive answers in seconds with confidence that the answers are correct.

## 💡 Why it matters
- The observatory gives leaders a clearer picture of aid, debt, private capital and other financial flows, enabling faster, smarter decisions about where limited resources can have the greatest impact.
- As the development finance landscape is “being rewritten in real time,” transparent, accountable data is essential for building a more effective system.
- Quotes from stakeholders:
  > “We believe policy makers — who make decisions that impact millions of people’s lives– deserve world class insights.” said David McNair, Managing Director of ONE Data.
  > “We built the Development Finance Observatory so that policymakers, journalists and citizens can get the answers they need in seconds — with the confidence that those answers are right.
  > “Data Commons was built so organizations like ONE can focus on solving the world’s biggest challenges without worrying about data storage and APIs,” Prem Ramaswami, Head of Google Data Commons, said.
  > “The development finance landscape is being rewritten in real time, and we cannot build a better system without understanding how money is actually moving,” said Mike Muldoon, Chief Executive Officer of RFCC, The Rockefeller Foundation’s independent charitable affiliate.
