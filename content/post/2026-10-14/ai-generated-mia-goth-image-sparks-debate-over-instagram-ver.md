# AI‑generated Mia Goth image sparks debate over Instagram verification and labeling

Earlier this month, an AI‑generated photo of actress Mia Goth wearing a T‑shirt that reads “MY EX IS MY BIGGEST FAN” and set to Beyoncé’s “Single Ladies (Put a Ring on It)” went viral on Instagram. The picture was posted by a fan‑run account (miagoth_) that carried a blue verified checkmark and had over a million followers, causing many to assume it was authentic.

## 🔍 Overview
- The post spread across the web and was liked by hundreds of thousands, including people in the entertainment industry.
- Entertainment outlets such as OK! and People reported on the image as if it were genuine.
- Goth’s representatives confirmed the image was AI‑generated and that the account had been asked to be removed.

## 🛡️ Verification & Confusion
- The miagoth_ account was labeled as fan‑run but displayed a blue checkmark, a symbol that for years signaled an official, authentic entity.
- Meta’s verification program now allows any account that pays for a blue checkmark to display the badge, even if it is not run by the person depicted in the content.
- Christine Tripicchio, a representative for Goth, told The Verge: “Mia Goth does not have ownership of, nor does she engage in social media on any platform. We have filed multiple complaints with Instagram to remove the verified fan account which continues to post counterfeit, AI images of Mia Goth.”

## ⚙️ Platform Response & Policy
- Meta removed the miagoth_ account, with spokesperson Alex Dziedzan stating it was removed for impersonating a public figure.
- Rather than ban AI‑generated content of real people, Meta labels some generative‑AI material.
- Photorealistic AI videos of people may require an AI label, but Meta’s rules carve out an exemption for still images.
- Meta executives have indicated that AI‑generated content will be a key part of how users engage on the company’s platforms moving forward.

## 📚 Related Incidents
- In 2024, Scarlett Johansson said OpenAI used a sound‑alike for the voice of ChatGPT after she declined to work with the company.
- The same year, Taylor Swift endorsed Kamala Harris for president, saying AI‑generated images of her posted by Donald Trump made her realize she needed to “combat misinformation” around her voting intentions.

## 💡 Why it matters
- The blue verification badge can mislead audiences about the provenance of AI‑generated media.
- The exemption for still images leaves a large gap in the platform’s ability to flag synthetic content that can spread quickly.
- High‑profile misuse of AI‑generated media underscores growing concerns about misinformation, deepfakes, and the need for clearer labeling standards across social networks.
