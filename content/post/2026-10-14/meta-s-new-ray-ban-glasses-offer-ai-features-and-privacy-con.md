# Meta's New Ray-Ban Glasses Offer AI Features and Privacy Controls

Meta has launched new Ray-Ban glasses with AI features and enhanced privacy controls. These glasses come in two styles: a retro Clubmaster look or a Wayfarer-esque Burbank style.

## 🔍 Overview
- The glasses feature AI capabilities powered by Meta's Muse Spark model.
- They offer privacy controls and data opt-out options during setup.
- Users can disable the wake word and use a customizable button for voice commands.

## 🧩 How it works
- The glasses can access messaging apps, Google Calendar, and email to assist with scheduling and correspondence.
- They can provide local recommendations and turn-by-turn audio directions.
- Features like live translation need to be manually started.

## ⚙️ Key details
- The glasses do not have a native way to record conversations.
- They are equipped with open-ear headphones.
- Sound quality is decent but struggles at louder volumes.
- The trebles can get tinny, and bass can sound distorted.
- There is inevitably sound leakage.

## 🚀 Availability
- The glasses are available for $350.
- A camera-equipped model is recommended for low-vision and blind users.
- The FDA-cleared hearing assistance feature is not yet available for hard-of-hearing users.
- The Meta Ray-Ban Display may be more useful for deaf users due to its live captioning support.

## 💡 Why it matters
- The glasses offer a good pair of open-ear headphones.
- The enhanced privacy controls and data opt-out options are more prominent than in previous Meta glasses.
- The AI features, though limited with the current Muse Spark model, promise more capabilities with the full Muse AI model arriving later this year.
