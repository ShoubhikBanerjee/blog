# Microsoft Launches Xbox Labs to Explore AI‑Driven Game Modding

Microsoft announced a new Xbox Labs group to experiment with AI‑powered decompilation and recompilation of games, turning emerging ideas into experiences for players and developers.

## 🔍 Overview
- Xbox Labs is described as “a small group of game makers, engineers and scientists whose job is to turn emerging ideas into things our players and developers can experience.” – Xbox CEO Asha Sharma
- The group will focus on rapid emergence of mods that use AI models to remix games in ways rarely seen before.
- Microsoft has already seen mods such as skateboarding added to *Call of Duty* and *Halo* running in a web browser.

## 🤖 AI in Game Modding
- AI models are helping modders decompile, recompile, and merge code from different titles.
- Sharma notes that “coding became the first major workload for agents because they can write, run, and test software,” and that games share many of those same properties.
- Players spend **more than 72 billion hours a year** on Xbox games, roughly five times the estimated time spent writing code worldwide.
- Microsoft plans to share any tools and learnings from Xbox Labs with the broader developer community.

## ⚙️ Experiments & Findings
- Recent weeks have seen classic PC builds translated to run on Apple Silicon and ported to browsers via WebAssembly and WebGPU, the latter now approaching 90 % global browser support.
- Modders are achieving results similar to Microsoft’s Xbox backward‑compatibility program—running old games on modern hardware—*without* the business‑model restrictions Microsoft traditionally faces.
- Some of the largest games have been remixed into the source code of another, challenging long‑held assumptions about the tether between software and hardware, the boundary between creation and play, and how people discover and experience games.

## 💡 Why It Matters
- “While it is hard to predict the future of gaming, it is easy to see the possibilities in making great games easier to access, giving old games new life and allowing more players to become creators and co‑creators.” – Sharma
- These outcomes benefit players even when they challenge existing business models.
- The work hints at a future where modern games could be decoupled from their intended hardware, potentially just “a model or two away” from further breakthroughs.

## ⚖️ Legal & IP Considerations
- The rapid decompile‑and‑recompile activity raises questions about unauthorized use of Microsoft’s IP.
- The company did not comment on potential IP violations before publication.
- While modding is “fun,” there is uncertainty about how publishers will react if modern console games appear where they shouldn’t.
