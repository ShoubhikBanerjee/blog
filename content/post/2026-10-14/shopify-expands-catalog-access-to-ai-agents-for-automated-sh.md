# Shopify Expands Catalog Access to AI Agents for Automated Shopping and Checkout

Shopify announced Tuesday it will allow shoppers to configure AI agents to purchase products on their behalf, giving these agents access to a catalog of over a billion listings. This development marks an expansion of the company’s agentic commerce ambitions, moving beyond a previous exclusive partnership with Meta’s Muse agent. Users can now utilize any AI agent for browsing and purchasing without a specific partnership integration.

## 🔍 Overview
Shopify is marketing the new feature as a "personal shopper" that can act on a human's behalf. The agent has access to a catalog consisting of items located both on and off Shopify storefronts. This move follows an earlier partnership announced in September with Meta to allow the Muse agent to browse and purchase items.

## 🧩 How it works
To enable automated shopping, customers must connect an AI agent to their account on Shop, a Shopify-owned platform designed to consolidate orders and track packages. 

*   **Integration:** The agent connects directly to the user's Shop account.
*   **Checkout:** Transactions occur via the one-click Shop Pay feature within the conversation with the agent.
*   **Order Management:** Any orders placed by the agent are funneled back to the Shop app for tracking.

## ⚙️ Key details
Humans remain in control of the agent’s behavior through specific rules and required authorizations:

*   **Budget Guardrails:** Users can set spending limits for the agent.
*   **Shipping Preferences:** Users can instruct agents to only display products with fast shipping.
*   **Purchase Approval:** Buyers are required to approve each purchase before it is finalized.

## 💡 Why it matters
E-commerce has emerged as a significant battleground for AI development, with companies like Google and Amazon also investing in tools where the end goal is an autonomous agent purchasing items for users. This shift has also sparked conflict between major commerce players and AI companies, such as the ongoing dispute between Amazon and firms like Perplexity and Meta over whether agents should be permitted to shop on a customer's behalf.
