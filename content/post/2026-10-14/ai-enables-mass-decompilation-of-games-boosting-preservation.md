# AI Enables Mass Decompilation of Games, Boosting Preservation and Raising IP Concerns

On 14 October 2026 AI began mass decompiling games, a shift that the community describes as “both awesome and scary.”

## 🔍 Overview
- "AI is mass decompiling games."
- "(via) Indie game developer Popcar talks about the recent wave of AI‑powered decompilation of existing games, enabling weird mashups between existing games, and producing a boom in game preservation projects to get old games working on new hardware (often in a browser using WebAssembly)."

## ✅ Positive outcomes
- "This is also a big win for preservation."
- "Old games can be ported, modified, enhanced, and made much more easily accessible than before."
- "With console makers going all in on digital and games becoming entirely unplayable due to servers shutting down, this is fantastic news."
- "We could own software we bought regardless of what the company thinks."

## ⚠️ Concerns
- "Most people already realized the scary part by now."
- "If AI can decompile games and software, then the entire tech industry is about to explode."
- "Anybody can decompile other software and use it any way they'd like, and nobody can stop them."
- "Companies can steal from competitors."
- "Your paid game or app can be open‑sourced against your will."
- "This isn't exclusive to old video games, we can apply this to any software."

## 💡 Why it matters
- The technology offers a powerful tool for preserving legacy games and keeping them playable on modern platforms, especially as original servers shut down.
- At the same time, the same capability threatens software owners by making it trivial to copy, modify, or republish proprietary code without permission.
- The dual nature of the development has sparked lively discussion about balancing preservation benefits with intellectual‑property protection.
