# Google Gemini 4 Argon Demonstrates Video-to-Simulation-to-Real Control

Google’s Gemini 4 Argon model showcase a pipeline that turns raw video into a physics‑based simulation and then into real‑world robot actions.

## 🔍 Overview
- Argon converted a human cornhole video into a physics model and simulation, then trained Apptronik’s Apollo humanoid to make seven consecutive throws.
- Google presents this as a controlled video‑to‑simulation‑to‑real demo.

## 🧩 How it works
- **Video capture** – a consumer‑level video of a human performing the task is fed to Gemini 4 Argon.
- **Physics modeling** – Argon extracts a physics model from the footage.
- **Simulation** – The model runs in a virtual environment to generate motion trajectories.
- **Real‑world transfer** – The trajectory data trains the Apollo humanoid, enabling it to replicate the task.

## ⚙️ Demonstrations
- **Cornhole throwing** – The Apollo robot performed seven consecutive throws after the video‑to‑simulation pipeline.
- **CAD animation** – Sundar Pichai showed Gemini 4 Argon turning a raw Pixel CAD file into an exploded‑view animation with lighting, camera choreography, and audio.
- **Pagoda construction** – Chetaslua demonstrated Gemini 4 Argon building a detailed pagoda.

## 🚀 Availability
- Gemini 4 Argon is offered through the Gemini API, Antigravity, Gemini apps, Google Search, and Google Cloud channels.

## 💡 Why it matters
- The demo illustrates a closed‑loop workflow that bridges visual perception, physics‑based simulation, and direct control of a humanoid robot, highlighting progress toward more seamless AI‑driven physical manipulation.
