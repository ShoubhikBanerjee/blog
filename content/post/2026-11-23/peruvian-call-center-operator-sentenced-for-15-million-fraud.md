# Peruvian Call Center Operator Sentenced for $15 Million Fraud Scheme

A Peruvian call‑center operator, Carla Magaly Alcedo Mendoza, was sentenced to eight years in prison for running a multi‑year fraud scheme that stole $15 million from more than 30,000 Spanish‑speaking U.S. residents.

## 🔍 Overview
- Alcedo managed Peruvian call centers linked to fraud and extortion from Jan 2013 to Dec 2018.
- The operation targeted Spanish‑speaking Americans, first offering free services or products, then impersonating U.S. government officials and lawyers to demand payment.
- Victims were threatened with court action, arrest, and immigration consequences in unsolicited phone calls.

## 🗓️ Timeline
- March 2023: Alcedo arrested in Peru.
- April 2025: Extradited to the United States.
- Pleaded guilty to conspiracy to commit mail and wire fraud after an 18‑count indictment.
- Received an eight‑year prison sentence.

## 💰 Impact
- Defrauded over 30,000 Spanish‑speaking U.S. residents of $15 million.
- "The scheme spanned several years and thousands of victims made payments to Alcedo. Collectively, the scheme was responsible for defrauding more than 30,000 Spanish‑speaking residents of the United States out of $15 million," the Department of Justice said.
- "These criminals preyed on hardworking Americans, using fear and intimidation to steal millions of dollars. They deliberately targeted Spanish‑speaking communities, impersonating government officials and threatening innocent people with arrest and legal consequences," added Acting US Attorney Yara Lorenzo Klukas.

## ⚖️ Legal outcome
- Alcedo was the tenth defendant extradited from Peru and sentenced in this scheme.
- Ten other Peruvian call‑center owner‑operators and four distribution‑center owner‑operators have been convicted so far.

## 📊 Broader context
- June 2026 FTC warning: Americans lost $3.5 billion to impersonation scams in 2025, with losses nearly tripling since 2020 and $920 million stolen by government impersonators.
- April 2025 FBI Internet Crime Report: U.S. victims lost nearly $21 billion to cyber‑enabled crimes in 2025.
