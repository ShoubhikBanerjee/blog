# Authorities Warn More Major Tech Outages Likely as AI-Driven Upgrades Create System Vulnerability

Recent weeks have seen a cascade of high-profile disruptions—including a nationwide FAA ground stop, a multi-day Outlook outage, and a JPMorgan Chase banking outage—prompting US intelligence and tech leaders to warn that more such events are inevitable.

## 📢 Official Warnings

Both Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton and Microsoft President Brad Smith have stated that the American public should expect continued disruptions.

- **Jay Clayton (CNN):** “We haven’t seen the last of the major tech outages.”
- **Brad Smith (CBS):** “I think we have to be prepared, frankly, for the coming months because this isn’t the type of thing that just gets done in a couple of weeks … The whole information infrastructure of the world is in effect being upgraded right now.”

## 🔧 Why Outages Are Increasing

Microsoft President Brad Smith explained that AI is accelerating the discovery of system vulnerabilities.

> “AI has made it easier to find flaws, and then you can either use that knowledge to patch the code, or if you’re a bad actor, you can use that knowledge to try to hack into systems.”

Recent attacks have targeted government and commercial systems, including the USDA, JP Morgan, and major supermarkets.

## 🛡️ What Authorities Recommend

Clayton emphasized that the public should be prepared for significant investment in two areas:

- Upgrading aging infrastructure
- Expanding cybersecurity measures

## 🚨 Outlook for the Coming Months

Authorities agree that any major technological shift—especially one involving widespread changes in computing and applications—will inevitably bring instability. Whether caused by AI-assisted cyberattacks or human error during system patching, more outages appear inevitable as the global information infrastructure undergoes rapid modernization.
