# DistroKid Removes Music Following Universal Music Group AI Slop Lawsuit

DistroKid has begun unceremoniously removing songs from its platform without providing notice or explanations to affected artists. The distributor confirmed to The Verge that these takedowns are a direct response to claims made by Universal Music Group (UMG), which filed a lawsuit in September alleging that DistroKid has created an "AI-slop pipeline." While the company states it is responding to UMG's claims, artists report that original, non-AI works are being caught up in the purge.

## ⚙️ Key Details
* DistroKid is taking down songs in response to a lawsuit filed by UMG in September.
* Amanda Ferri, DistroKid’s VP of Artist Services, stated that a "very small number of recordings" have been removed and that the company "strongly dispute[s]" UMG's allegations.
* DistroKid aims to minimize disruption while protecting the ability of artists to release music, despite reports of removals occurring without warning.

## 🧩 Impacted Artists
Multiple musicians have reported the removal of original works that do not utilize AI, often with no communication before or after the tracks were taken down.

| Artist | Impacted Work | Reported Issues |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| McGwire | Six songs | Included a licensed cover and a marketplace beat; one song was flagged for a sample released two years after the track itself. |
| Insane Ian | Original song | Removed with no warning and no explanation provided by the company. |
| King Chase | Entire album | An album online for 3-4 years without incident or copyright strikes was removed. |

## 💡 Why it matters
The purge has highlighted significant frustrations regarding DistroKid's transparency and support systems:
* Artists report having to contact the company themselves to find out why their music was removed, often navigating "lengthy circular conversation[s]" with bots before reaching a live agent.
* Music producer Jesse Cannon stated that DistroKid "gave up on customer service about two years ago," treating it as a "waste of profits" due to their high market share.
* Former employees reportedly told Cannon that the company does not provide a mechanism for artists to have recourse or a warning to debate takedown claims.
* Much of the artist frustration stems from a lack of communication and the difficulty of contacting DistroKid agents.
