# Pfizer‑Astellas Launches Independent Medical Education Grant for Bladder Cancer Care

The Pfizer–Astellas Independent Medical Education Grant supports U.S.‑based organizations that wish to develop educational projects on guideline‑concordant care for muscle‑invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) and locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma (la/mUC).

## 📋 Eligibility
- Projects must be conducted in the United States.
- Applicant must be a U.S.‑based organization (individuals and independent practice groups are not eligible).
- Eligible organizations include:
  - Medical, nursing, allied‑health, and pharmacy professional schools.
  - Healthcare institutions of any size.
- The project/program lead must be an employee or contractor of the requesting organization and act as the applicant or authorized designee.
- The requesting organization must be legally able to receive funding directly from Pfizer Inc.

## 💰 Funding Details
- **Funding range per project:** $10,000 – $230,000
- **Maximum award:** $230,000
- **Estimated total RFP budget:** $900,000
- **Project duration:** 18–24 months

## 📅 Timeline
| Milestone | Date |
|---|---|
| RFP release | September 30, 2026 |
| Application deadline | November 10, 2026, 11:59 PM ET |
| Grant notification | November 30, 2026 |
| Project start (anticipated) | February 2027 |
| Project end (anticipated) | July 2028 |

## 🖥️ Application Process
- Submit applications through Pfizer’s **MARCIE** grant management system.
- Complete all required sections of the online application.
- Upload the full proposal in the designated **Proposal/Protocol** field.
- For additional information on the Pfizer Competitive Grants Program and current oncology RFPs, visit the **Pfizer Competitive Grants Program** page.
- To access the submission portal, use the **Pfizer Grant Requester Portal**.

## 🎯 Program Focus
- Enable healthcare professionals to apply new and existing evidence for optimal MIBC and la/mUC care.
- Emphasize guideline‑recommended first‑line treatment, peri‑operative therapies, adverse‑event management, multidisciplinary collaboration, and shared decision‑making.
