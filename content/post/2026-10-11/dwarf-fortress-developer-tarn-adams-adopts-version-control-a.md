# Dwarf Fortress Developer Tarn Adams Adopts Version Control After Years Without It

Dwarf Fortress creator Tarn Adams has adopted version control tools, bringing an end to years of developing the game's complex codebase without source control.

## 🔍 Overview

Despite the inherent complexity of the Dwarf Fortress codebase, the project ran for years without version control. Statements from creator Tarn Adams trace how his approach to source control shifted over a decade of development.

## ⚙️ Key details

Tarn Adams commented on his use of version control across three distinct periods:

- **March 2013:** "I don't use version control -- I didn't like the feeling of having the code get committed into a black box thingy with no immediate upside"
- **March 2016:** "I don’t even use version control. If you don’t know what that is then you’re not gonna yell at me. If you even know what version control is you’re gonna be like, ‘You don’t use version control? You don’t use source control? What is wrong with you? How can you even work? I’m just not cut out for it, in a sense. It’s been 15 years, so the reasons have change over time, but that’s my current understanding of it."
- **March 2023:** "I've got Discord channels to check and version control stuff to use now, so the process is definitely a little more involved"

## 💡 Why it matters

The March 2023 update was shared during an interview about the Steam release of Dwarf Fortress, which made the developers millionaires. The transition marks the adoption of version control tools and Discord channels into their active workflow.
