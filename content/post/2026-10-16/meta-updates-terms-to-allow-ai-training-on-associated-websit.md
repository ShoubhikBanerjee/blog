# Meta Updates Terms to Allow AI Training on Associated Websites

Meta has updated its terms of service to grant itself permission to crawl and scrape websites associated with business accounts for AI training. The new terms, which go into effect on October 30, allow Meta to use outside content to "develop, improve, research," and "train" its AI products.

## 🔍 Overview

The policy applies to business accounts that promote their content across Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, and Messenger. It allows Meta to crawl all websites 'associated' with a business account. This includes domains, web pages, or URLs that are made available through the business's use of Meta's products and services.

## ⚙️ Key Details

*   **Authorization:** The terms authorize Meta to use automated software (such as web scrapers, bots, crawlers, and user agents) to access, index, cache, analyze, collect, retrieve, or crawl domains associated with products or services.
*   **Usage:** Meta may use any content from such domains to provide, develop, improve, research, train, and maintain the integrity of its Meta Company Products and services.
*   **Enforcement:** Businesses that do not agree to Meta's new policies "may stop all access or use of our Products for business or commercial purposes."

## 🧩 Legal and Industry Context

*   **Robots.txt:** Meta spokesperson Daniel Roberts states there is "no change to how Meta uses web crawlers" and claims content owners can still use robots.txt flags to indicate their preferences.
*   **Expert Analysis:** Legal experts tell Nieman Lab that this could allow Meta to argue its commercial terms supersede other blocking instructions, like a site's robots.txt file. This could potentially allow Meta to bypass a licensing agreement and freely train its AI models on content posted by online publishers.
*   **Existing Deals:** Meta has followed OpenAI in striking AI licensing deals with CNN, Fox News, USA Today, and People Inc., but these new terms could give the company access to a much broader swath of content.
*   **Professor Quote:** James Grimmelmann, a professor of digital and information law at Cornell, tells Nieman Lab that for news publishers, this clearly represents permission to Meta to train AI on any content from their sites and possibly to deploy some of that content, even in competition with the publishers themselves.

## 💡 Why It Matters

*   **News Sites:** The new policy may allow Meta to train AI on news sites for free.
*   **Meta's Stance:** Andy Stone, Meta’s vice president of communications, writes in a post on X that this change isn’t meant to target news sites, stating the update is designed to pull accurate information about products for sale on third‑party websites when making on‑platform ad and content recommendations.
*   **Cost Implications:** Last month, Meta launched a new Meta One subscription that requires businesses and creators to pay to include links in posts and reels. Nieman Lab notes that "Meta has begun charging users to post links to the same content that it will be permitted to crawl for free."
