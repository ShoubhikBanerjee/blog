# Global Survey Reveals Widespread Concern Over AI Job Loss and Inequality

A global survey conducted across 37 countries has gathered data on public sentiment regarding the impact of artificial intelligence on employment and daily life.

## 🔍 Overview
The survey questioned 42,151 people from February 8th to May 13th. Findings indicate a general trend of concern regarding the societal impacts of AI:

* **General Sentiment:** A global median of 41 percent of respondents feel equally concerned and excited about AI, 37 percent are primarily concerned, and 13 percent are primarily excited.
* **Economic Impact:** Respondents are generally more likely to say AI will increase the gap between rich and poor.
* **Employment:** In 34 of the 37 countries surveyed, people believe AI is more likely to lead to job losses over the next 20 years than to create new jobs.

## ⚙️ Key details
Concerns regarding AI vary by region and age demographic:

| Region/Demographic | Detail |
| :--- | :--- |
| Australia | 76 percent worry about AI impact |
| South Korea | 76 percent worry about AI impact |
| United States | 71 percent worry about AI impact |
| Older people | More likely to be concerned about AI's impact on daily life than younger people |
| Younger adults (18–34) | Most likely to have mixed feelings about how AI will shape the world |

Additionally, concern among younger demographics has risen sharply over the past year in the US, Sweden, Poland, Japan, Australia, and Brazil. In the US and Sweden, these younger groups are now as worried about AI-driven inequality and job loss as older generations.

## 💡 Why it matters
These public concerns align with warnings from industry leaders, such as Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, who warned that AI could eliminate half of entry-level white-collar jobs.
