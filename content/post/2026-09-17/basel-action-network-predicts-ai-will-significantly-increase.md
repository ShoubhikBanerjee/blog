# Basel Action Network Predicts AI Will Significantly Increase Global E-Waste

The nonprofit Basel Action Network (BAN) published a paper today predicting that AI will contribute significantly to the growth of global electronic waste through 2050.

## ⚙️ Key details
BAN predicts global e-waste will triple to 211 million metric tons per year by 2050, attributing 15 to 20 percent of that garbage to AI. The organization's findings include:

* **Estimated AI Waste:** Between 2025 and 2050, AI-related electronic equipment is expected to add between 395 to 617 million metric tons of e-waste.
* **Annual Impact:** This breaks down to approximately 8.6 million to 13.1 million metric tons of equipment retired annually due to AI.
* **Data Center Capacity:** BAN expects 70,000 metric tons of e-waste per gigawatt of data center capacity. It cites a McKinsey projection that total capacity could reach 219GW by 2030.
* **Scope of Waste:** BAN includes power supply and distribution, cooling systems, backup power, and networking equipment, noting that studies focusing only on servers and accelerators miss about 87 percent of a data center's electro-mechanical infrastructure.
* **AI Waste Contagion:** This category includes telecommunications infrastructure and personal devices that may be replaced earlier due to AI advances.

## 💡 Why it matters
Currently, less than a quarter of the 68.3 million tons of e-waste created annually worldwide is formally collected and recycled. Most e-waste ends up in "informal" collection where burying or burning equipment exposes workers and the environment to toxic materials such as chromium and lead.

By 2050, the volume of waste could be enough to fill 23 million shipping containers, which would circle the world six times if lined up. BAN notes that the US has more data centers than any other country but has not ratified the Basel Convention intended to limit the international trade of hazardous wastes; investigations have found US recyclers still ship e-waste abroad, where it often enters "backyard recycling."
