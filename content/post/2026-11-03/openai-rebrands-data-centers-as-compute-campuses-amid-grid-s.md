# OpenAI Rebrands Data Centers as Compute Campuses Amid Grid Strain Concerns

OpenAI has begun quietly renaming its data centers as "compute campuses" while introducing a new initiative intended to address local power grid concerns. The shift in terminology debuted alongside the announcement of the company's "Grid Protectors Program."

## 🔍 Overview
OpenAI introduced the term "compute campus" in an announcement for its Grid Protectors Program, starting with an upcoming site in Effingham County, Georgia. The initiative aims to address concerns regarding the strain that AI infrastructure puts on local power grids, specifically during emergencies and extreme weather. Axios reported that OpenAI planned to rebrand data centers to "AI compute campuses" as part of a policy and public relations push following the midterm elections in anticipation of potential regulatory hostility.

## ⚙️ Key details
- **Terminology Shift:** Across the roughly 900-word announcement, the term "data center" appeared twice, outnumbered by the new term "compute campus."
- **Power Commitments:** OpenAI promised to "pay the costs we create" and "help communities keep the lights on."
- **Facility Design:** OpenAI stated its new compute facilities differ from older data centers, claiming the new campuses allow the company to remain flexible in power demand.
- **Search History:** A search of OpenAI's website revealed no previous uses of the term "compute campus."

## 💡 Critical reception and industry comparison
Critics raised concerns that altering terminology avoids addressing the root issues of infrastructure development. Elmore called the announcement "woefully tone deaf," stating that changing the name does not solve environmental and climate concerns or fix the issues caused by large construction projects.

| Company | Infrastructure and Grid Commitments |
| --- | --- |
| OpenAI | Debuted "compute campus" terminology, launched Grid Protectors Program, and promised flexible power demand and coverage of created costs. |
| Anthropic | Pledged to cover 100 percent of power grid upgrades, invest billions in community projects, protect local water supplies, and refrain from asking public officials to sign NDAs. |

OpenAI did not respond to requests for comment regarding its decision to change its terminology or its motivations behind the move.
