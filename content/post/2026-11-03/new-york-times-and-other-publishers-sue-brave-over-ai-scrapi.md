# New York Times and Other Publishers Sue Brave Over AI Scraping and Paywall Bypass

## 🔍 Overview
Several major news organizations, including The New York Times, have filed lawsuits against Brave Software, the company behind the Brave web browser. The suits allege that Brave unlawfully scraped copyrighted articles to create a searchable “Brave Index,” then sold access to that index to generative‑AI firms while also allowing users to bypass paywalls.

## 📜 Allegations
- Brave copied and distributed publishers’ copyrighted content without permission.
- The company offered a “Paywall blocker” that lets users access premium articles for free.
- Brave monetized its index of scraped sites by giving AI companies paid access to real‑time online content.
- Publishers were not compensated for the alleged copying, distribution, or the paywall‑bypass feature.

## 📣 Statements
- “While we believe in the ethical and responsible use and development of AI, Brave has flouted established copyright law and ignored repeated requests by The Times to stop its infringing conduct,” said Graham James, NYT spokesperson (quoted to The Verge).
- “We filed this lawsuit to hold Brave accountable for stealing our content to power its own products and to provide it to some of the most prominent and well‑resourced generative AI companies in the world,” the NYT statement continued.
- Attorney Steven Lieberman, representing the New York Daily News, Chicago Tribune, and Orlando Sentinel, said: “AI giants are now desperate to avoid getting caught red‑handed stealing news from publishers, so Brave Software is selling them a back door to the journalism they know they have no right to.”
- “Buying stolen goods secondhand doesn’t make them legitimate. Brave must be held responsible for laundering pirated reporting for the AI industry,” added Lieberman.

## 📅 Update (Nov 2)
The lawsuit filed by the New York Daily News, Chicago Tribune, and Orlando Sentinel was clarified to be a separate action from the NYT case. The attorney’s statement on behalf of those three publications was also added.
