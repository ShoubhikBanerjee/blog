# OpenAI Pilots Grid Protectors Program at Georgia Compute Campus

OpenAI announced it will pilot a "Grid Protectors Program" at an upcoming data center in Effingham County, Georgia. The initiative aims to position its AI data centers—which the company now calls "compute campuses"—as grid resources that can assist power utilities during peak periods and extreme weather events.

## 🔍 Overview
As public sentiment toward data centers has worsened across the United States, OpenAI is framing its power infrastructure as a protective community resource rather than a polluter. Opposition has mounted nationwide against massive data center developments, which residents report are frequently approved with little or no local input.

## ⚙️ Key details
- **Grid Protectors Pilot**: At its Effingham County, Georgia location, OpenAI promises to free up to 1,000 megawatts of power during emergencies or extreme weather, enough to power air conditioners in around 700,000 homes.
- **Rebranding Efforts**: OpenAI has switched to calling its data centers "compute campuses" in an effort to separate from their poor public reputation.
- **Stargate Expansion**: OpenAI is partnering with Oracle and SoftBank to build "Stargate" data centers in multiple locations across the country.
- **Utility Arrangements**: OpenAI states it will continue working with policymakers and utility services to make arrangements for future campuses to reduce electricity demand when the grid is under strain.

| Milam County, Texas | Planned Stargate compute campus with Oracle and SoftBank |

## 🧩 How it works
OpenAI pitches power plants supplying its compute campuses as resources that utilities can call on when communities need power most. According to OpenAI's announcement:
- "Electricity demand also rises and falls with the seasons, with different regions facing their biggest peaks at different times."
- "Our compute campuses need power year-round."
- "With the right agreements, that steady demand can help spread the grid’s fixed costs across more electricity sales, while flexibility lets us reduce demand when the system is busiest."

## 💡 Public opposition and environmental impact
The company's announcement arrives as community opposition and regulatory scrutiny intensify across several states:
- **Local Protests**: In Utah, residents protested after Box Elder County commissioners signed off on a data center expected to consume more than double the state's peak electricity demand in 2025 and spread hot air into the surrounding desert valley.
- **Air Quality Risks**: A study by the Houston Advanced Research Center (HARC) noted that powering proposed Texas data center projects could undo decades of air quality gains over just five years.
- **State Legislation**: Several states, including Tennessee, Florida, Oklahoma, and Oregon, have enacted laws to prevent AI companies from passing energy costs on to residents, alongside enacting moratoriums and other restrictions.
