# DOJ and Commerce Resume Investigation into Megaspeed Amid Nvidia Scandal

The Justice and Commerce Departments announced they are investigating Megaspeed, a Singapore-based data center company, after reports suggested the investigation had been halted last year. This renewed scrutiny follows the resignation of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, who reportedly had an affair with former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, alleged to be a Chinese spy.

## 🔍 Overview

- The Department of Commerce initiated an investigation into Megaspeed in 2024 for potential export control violations, triggered by suspicious activity in sensitive semiconductor diversion.
- In May 2025, Commerce Department investigators requested records from Nvidia concerning direct or indirect sales to Megaspeed.
- In June 2025, Nvidia placed Megaspeed on its “no sell” list.
- Agents from the Bureau of Industry and Security, in conjunction with Singaporean law enforcement, conducted an on-site end-use check at Megaspeed and interviewed CEO Alice Huang in June 2025.

## ⚙️ Key Details

- Earlier this month, the New York Times reported that a Commerce Department investigation into Megaspeed “came to a sudden halt” late last year after investigators received an email from Nvidia’s general counsel. The email claimed Commerce Department official Jeffrey Kessler gave Nvidia permission to reach out.
- Following the report of the halt, the case was “quickly” handed over to the Department of Justice, which took the lead role in the criminal investigation.
- The Justice and Commerce Departments state they will bring the full extent of the law against Megaspeed for any criminal conduct that may have occurred.
- The scandal has raised questions about national security and sparked scrutiny of Nvidia’s handling of chip exports to China and Megaspeed’s possible role in moving Nvidia’s chips.
