# Sam Altman clarifies remarks on memory and local AI

Sam Altman has addressed the context of his previous comments regarding personal computer memory and data centers, clarifying that his remarks about centralizing memory usage were misinterpreted.

## 📢 New Clarifications

Speaking onstage at Semafor’s Silicon Valley & The World summit on Monday, Altman stated that he wants people to have a lot of RAM and that everybody should be able to buy a lot of RAM for their own computer. He also noted that “home tinkering is great.”

These comments follow his appearance on the Cheeky Pint podcast, where he said it’s “quite terrible” for everyone to have RAM in personal computers rather than in data centers. Altman previously stated on X that the original remarks had been “taken out of context” and reiterated his desire for users to have “so much ram.”

## 🔍 The Context

- **Memory Usage:** Altman suggested that “share this memory” and explained that if the world is in a memory supply shortage, it might be optimal to have memory working 100 percent of the time rather than sometimes off in someone’s edge device.
- **Misinterpretation:** Altman stated, “That comment, which I think got misinterpreted, but there is a true part of it, which I will stand by,” explaining that he was trying to discuss the optimal use of limited supply, though he noted that people having their own at-home inference is a tradeoff.
- **Supply Chain Issues:** Altman commented that OpenAI has “been constrained by different things at different times,” including memory, rack assembly, data center capacity, or “some random component.”
- **Supply Shortage:** He noted that “a few years ago we started saying the world needs to build a lot more memory supply chain in particular,” but “the world did not build out that much,” resulting in DRAM prices potentially going up “5x, 7x, something like that.”
- **Jensen Huang:** Altman also commented on Jensen Huang’s resignation as Nvidia’s CEO, saying he “doesn’t know enough” to say if Huang should be reinstated.

## 💡 Why It Matters

AI companies have been buying up the world’s memory supply for data centers, leading to ongoing price hikes on everything from phones to game consoles. Altman’s clarifications highlight the tension between the need for centralized data center memory and the value of personal computer hardware for local inference.
