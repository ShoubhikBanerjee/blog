# Constellation Energy Unbothered by Democratic Concerns Over Data Center Development

Constellation Energy, America’s largest operator of nuclear power plants, has dismissed market fears that the recent Democratic midterm victories will slow down data center development. CEO Joe Dominguez described the company as "unbothered" by the political shift, arguing that the market overreacted to election results based on fear that Democrats would not let data centers get built.

## 🔍 Context of the Market Sell-off

Many Democrats who won control of the House and Senate in this month’s midterm elections ran on anti-data-center campaigns. This political shift triggered a historic $2.5 trillion sell-off in AI-adjacent stocks, which extended to the energy, construction, and materials sectors. 

Up until a few years ago, many of Constellation’s nuclear power plants were in severe financial distress and faced the risk of early shutdown. However, the data center development boom and its massive demand for electricity have reversed the nuclear industry’s fate. Constellation is now signing major deals with Big Tech companies to provide power to data centers, including:
*   **Microsoft**: Signed a deal to fund the revival of a nuclear plant at Three Mile Island in Pennsylvania (the site of what the Nuclear Regulatory Commission calls the “most serious accident in US commercial nuclear power plant operating history”)
*   **Amazon and Google**: Constellation signed major contracts in the third quarter to provide power to their data centers

## 💡 Bipartisan Support and Economic Impact

Nuclear energy has enjoyed bipartisan support for years. The US Department of Energy classifies nuclear as a “clean” energy source because operating a reactor does not directly produce carbon emissions, making it appealing to Big Tech companies balancing aggressive data center development with ambitious climate goals.

Dominguez noted that the market scare was not lost on Democrats, who do not want to be the reason the economy takes a nosedive by halting data center development. He pointed out that Virginia and Illinois, both led by Democratic governors prior to the election, have seen a significant amount of data center development. 

In Virginia, the most vocal critics of data centers have actually been Republicans. Senator Richard Stuart, a conservative Republican, has aggressively pursued data center regulation. Last month, Virginia Governor Abigail Spanberger, a Democrat, denied Stuart’s request for a special session to address a concerning new report from the Virginia Department of Environmental Quality regarding the state’s strained groundwater supply amid rampant data center growth.

## 🏛️ Legislative Action

The Senate is expected to vote on a sweeping bill, co-sponsored by two Democrats, marketed as a way to speed up permitting for new power plants and transmission lines to connect data centers to the grid faster. 

*   **Provisions**: New Mexico Senator Martin Heinrich, one of the bill’s backer, highlighted provisions designed to protect utility customers from rising rates and ensure Big Tech pays a fair share of costs.
*   **Critiques**: Researchers at Columbia University SIPA’s Center on Global Energy Policy called the bill’s protections “mixed,” noting they do not account for wholesale capacity and energy prices, which is a principal way data center demand can lead to higher household bills. Senate Democrats are divided, with critics arguing the bill fast-tracks projects at the expense of public input and environmental oversight, potentially leading to a massive overbuild of energy projects that Americans will end up paying for.
