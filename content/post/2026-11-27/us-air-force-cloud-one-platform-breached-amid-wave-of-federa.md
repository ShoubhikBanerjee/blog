# US Air Force Cloud One platform breached amid wave of federal cyberattacks

Hackers have reportedly breached the US Air Force's Cloud One computing platform, adding to a recent wave of cyberattacks affecting US government agencies, including the FBI, CIA, and Treasury Department.

## 🔍 Overview
The intrusion was discovered within the past month during an upgrade of the cloud platform, disrupting application migrations and software updates. According to one source who spoke to Reuters, the hackers exploited an unidentified software vulnerability to gain access to the platform, potentially exposing sensitive military operational and logistics information. US Cyber Command has reportedly been called in to assess the impact, with the attackers, scope of the attack, and whether any data was stolen currently unknown. The Air Force declined to comment on the security or operational status of the affected systems, stating that it continuously takes measures to protect its information, systems, and mission capabilities.

## ⚙️ Key details
Cloud One is an enterprise cloud computing platform launched in 2017 and managed by the US Air Force. It allows Department of Defense agencies to host and manage applications supporting military operations, personnel management, logistics, and maintenance.

## 🛡️ Related Federal Breaches
On November 21, it was revealed that hackers reportedly breached the FBI's National Crime Information Center (NCIC), a nationwide database used by local, state, and federal law enforcement agencies containing information on wanted and missing persons, outstanding arrest warrants, stolen vehicles and firearms, and other criminal justice records. This intrusion followed two months after the ShinyHunters extortion group breached FBI systems by exploiting an unpatched Oracle PeopleSoft vulnerability in the agency's recruitment website, FBIJobs.gov.

Earlier this week, hackers also reportedly breached an open-source intelligence (OSINT) analysis system at the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA). The incident also affected the Office of the Director of National Intelligence (ODNI) and the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency (NGA). Hours after the CIA breach was reported, the US Treasury Department's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) disclosed that it was investigating unauthorized access to its systems. It is unclear whether any of the recently disclosed federal government breaches are connected.

## 🧩 How it works
The CIA breach was reportedly conducted using the ARTEX AI-powered penetration testing platform. ARTEX is an open-source penetration testing framework that uses AI agents to automate various stages of a cyberattack, including discovering and exploiting vulnerabilities, escalating privileges, and stealing data. The framework integrates with external AI models from Anthropic Claude, OpenAI GPT, DeepSeek, Grok, and others, allowing attackers to conduct autonomous attacks against organizations with minimal human intervention. According to the FBI, some ARTEX-powered intrusions have progressed from initial targeting to privileged access in less than three hours.
