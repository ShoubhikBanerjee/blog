# Development of Agentic Warfighting Units for Department of Defense Missions

A task force operating in a Maryland basement has developed a formation of agentic warfighters designed to collect intelligence, develop missions, and execute against targets 24/7 within a commander's intent.

## 🧩 How it works
An agentic warfighter consists of a model, scaffolding, and intent:
* **Model:** A set of learned numbers (or weights) that provide the reasoning and capability to fight.
* **Scaffolding:** Tools, memory, and planning loops that wrap the model to create an agent.
* **Command:** Agents are placed under a human commander with a specific mission and intent to form an agentic warfighting unit.

## ⚙️ Key details
* **Hardware:** The basement operation utilizes six graphics cards and a stack of Mac minis to host dozens of agents.
* **Philosophy:** The formation follows a mission command philosophy characterized by centralized planning, decentralized execution, and clear commander's intent.
* **Mission:** The unit's mission is denial, which includes acquiring underpriced AI hardware before scalpers hoard it for sale to China.
* **Command Structure:** Commanders are expected to own every authority and risk, as agentic warfighters do not follow the same ordered relationship of answerability as human subordinates.

## 💡 Why it matters
Recent evaluations indicate a shift in agent capabilities:
* In 2023, coin flips determined if models could complete tasks taking human experts four minutes; current capabilities now push past a 16-hour time horizon.
* Anthropic’s Claude Mythos Preview successfully solved a 30-hour robotics task six for six and wrote exploits equivalent to a well-paid expert's workday, though it failed a ten-second task and a cryptanalysis challenge.
* A June 2026 national security memorandum ordered a rewrite of Directive 3000.09, Autonomy in Weapon Systems, to ensure autonomous systems respect the chain of command.
