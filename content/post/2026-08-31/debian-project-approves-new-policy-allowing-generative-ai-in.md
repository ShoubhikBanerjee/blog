# Debian Project Approves New Policy Allowing Generative AI in Linux Development

The Debian Project has voted to allow developers to use AI tools in their contributions to the Linux distribution’s development, maintenance, and documentation. The new policy acknowledges that the responsible use of AI can improve productivity while clarifying that the project neither endorses nor prohibits the use of generative AI tools.

## 🔍 Overview
The decision follows a vote by project developers who considered several other proposals, including options that would have banned contributions made using AI tools. Under the new policy, generative AI is neither exempt from nor subject to special rules beyond the standards already expected of Debian contributors.

## ⚙️ Key details
Debian has established specific guidelines for how AI-assisted work must be handled within the project:

* **Maintained Standards**: All contributions must satisfy the same standards of quality, correctness, maintainability, and legal compliance, regardless of the tools used.
* **Contributor Responsibility**: Using a generative AI tool does not diminish a contributor’s responsibility for the work they submit.
* **Review Requirements**: Contributors are expected to understand, review, test, and modify AI-assisted output before incorporating it into the distribution.
* **Human Oversight**: The project states that blindly accepting or uploading AI-generated material without human review is inconsistent with established development practices.
* **Disclosure**: Debian encourages contributors to disclose if a contribution was made with AI assistance, but it does not require them to do so.

## 💡 Why it matters
The policy change has caused friction within the community. One contributor has indicated he is quitting the project and is no longer interested in Debian following the vote. This reaction mirrors a similar backlash faced by Ubuntu developer Canonical earlier this year regarding its own stance on AI tools.
